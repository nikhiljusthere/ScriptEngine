**Every Nen Vow Made in Hunter x Hunter Explained**

1. **Chain Jail (Kurapika)**

Kurapika is a Conjurer, and his weapon of choice is one of the most unique in the world of Nen — five chains, each one extending from a finger on his right hand. Since these chains are created through his Nen, he has complete control over their size, their length, and their movement. While each chain has a specific purpose, several of them were crafted with one clear goal: to exact revenge on the Phantom Troupe for wiping out his clan. Capturing each Troupe member would be the first step toward that goal, and for that, Kurapika needed something capable of holding enemies with terrifying strength. Conjuring an unbreakable chain through Nen alone is impossible, but Kurapika’s mentor taught him a crucial workaround — the use of Nen Conditions. These are rules or requirements applied to an ability to boost its power or make it possible in the first place, especially if the user doesn’t have the raw skill needed to create it otherwise. Conditions are grounded in the idea of equivalent exchange: to gain more power, you must give something up. Many abilities in Nen require meeting specific conditions before they can even be activated. But there’s another layer that can push an ability far beyond its normal limits — self-imposed Restrictions. These are stricter limitations that the user voluntarily places on themselves, and the tighter and riskier the restriction, the stronger the ability becomes. Limitations have been shown to strengthen an ability even more than standard conditions, since they directly reflect the user’s resolve. The greater the sacrifice or risk, the greater the boost in power. When you officially commit to following these conditions and limitations, it's called making "vows" or "contracts." Nen itself is a manifestation of willpower, so taking on dangerous or even life-threatening risks is a way of proving absolute commitment to the ability. Using this principle, Kurapika developed his middle-finger chain: Chain Jail. This chain, ending in a sharp hook, was made with one purpose only — to capture members of the Phantom Troupe. Kurapika set a strict Limitation that the ability could only be used on the Troupe. But he didn’t stop there — he added an even more dangerous stipulation. If he used Chain Jail on anyone outside the Phantom Troupe, he would die. He sealed this vow by piercing his own heart with his Judgment Chain. This life-or-death clause dramatically increased the chain’s strength, since it put his own life at stake every time the ability came into play. To prove its power, Kurapika deliberately went after the physically strongest Troupe member, Uvogin, and managed to restrain him after a fierce battle. The chain’s conjuration speed is so fast that he can completely bind a distant target in half a second. Once it wraps around an opponent’s limbs and body, movement becomes impossible. On top of that, Chain Jail forces the captured target into Zetsu, cutting off their aura flow and shutting down their ability to use Nen. The only way to escape is through brute force — but even Uvogin, with all his raw strength, couldn’t break free. Kurapika is a natural Conjurer, but he also possesses something no other Nen users have — the Scarlet Eyes. Except for his ring-finger Dowsing Chain and index finger Steal Chain, all of his other chains, including Chain Jail, can only be used when his eyes turn scarlet. This condition has been part of the ability from the start, further amplifying its power. How the Scarlet Eyes work and what they bring to Kurapika’s abilities is a story on its own, and that’s what we’re gonna talk about next.

2. **Emperor Time (Kurapika)**

The Kurta Clan has this incredible trait where their eyes turn a brilliant red when they get angry or emotional. This transformation brings a massive boost in physical strength that's pretty mind-blowing. Even as a kid, Kurapika could take on three grown adults when his eyes went scarlet, outpacing and overpowering them like it was nothing. But this power comes with a serious downside \- when those scarlet eyes activate, Kurapika completely loses control. All his restraint and logical thinking just vanish, leaving him in a completely primal state. In his normal state, he has total control over these chains \- their size, length, and movement all bend to his will. But when those scarlet eyes ignite, everything changes dramatically. His aura explodes upward and he enters Emperor Time, a state that transforms his very nature as a Nen user. Instead of being limited to Conjuration like most people are stuck with their birth category, Kurapika shifts from his natural Conjurer type to become a Specialist. This transformation is absolutely game-changing. During Emperor Time, Kurapika can use every single Nen category at 100% efficiency. To understand how incredible this is, you need to know about the three components that make up Nen mastery: Level, Force, and Accuracy. Your Level in each category stays fixed \- it represents your raw skill and understanding. Force determines how much power you can put behind an ability. Accuracy affects how precisely you can execute techniques. Normally, people can only reach 100% Force and Accuracy in their birth category, while opposite categories might only reach 40%. During Emperor Time, Kurapika's Levels stay the same, but his Force and Accuracy shoot up to 100% across every category. So if his Conjuration Level is 10, his Emission Level might only be 4, but he can now use Emission abilities with the same Force and Accuracy as a natural Level 4 Emitter. This makes him incredibly versatile and dangerous. Emperor Time also serves as the activation requirement for most of his chains. Only the Dowsing Chain and Steal Chain work without his eyes turning red. But even these abilities get major upgrades during Emperor Time. The Dowsing Chain normally needs direct contact with a target to detect lies. With Emperor Time active, Kurapika can use it through video recordings, as long as he's met the person before. This expansion of range and application makes his lie detection far more practical for investigation work. The Steal Chain becomes even more terrifying during Emperor Time. Normally, it can steal abilities by piercing targets with its syringe, and Kurapika keeps those stolen abilities even if the original owner dies. But when combined with Emperor Time, Kurapika can summon a dolphin-like Nen Beast equipped with syringes on its fins. This creature doesn't just steal abilities \- it analyzes them too, giving Kurapika detailed information about their name, functions, and conditions. After stealing an ability, he can either use it himself or loan it to other people, including those who can't normally use Nen. The syringe from Steal Chain injects the ability directly into them. Stealing abilities like this is far beyond standard Conjuration — it’s firmly in the realm of Specialization. His little finger’s Judgment Chain uses both Emission and Manipulation to deliver a deadly verdict — it can pierce the target’s heart and enforce an unbreakable condition on their life. His thumb’s Holy Chain taps into Enhancement-type Nen to heal severe injuries, even something as serious as a broken arm, in seconds. With Emperor Time, Kurapika wields all of these chains with maximum effectiveness, creating a Nen skill set so complete it can feel almost unfair. But nothing in Nen comes without a price. This represents a different type of Nen condition \- not a penalty that activates when rules get broken, but a constant toll that has to be paid for using the ability. These toll-type conditions don't regulate how an ability gets used or punish rule violations. Instead, they extract a price simply for accessing the power. Unlike limitation penalties that can shut down Nen abilities when violated, toll conditions function more like a continuous payment system. And Emperor Time exemplifies this perfectly.

3. **Adult Transformation (Gon)**

Gon is an Enhancer, which means he was basically born with incredible strength and healing abilities that most people can only dream of. Under Biscuit's intense training, he expanded his skills into at least two other Nen types \- Emission and Transmutation. He pushed these abilities far enough to create actual working techniques in his Jajanken ability, which is pretty impressive for someone so young. Initially, Gon showed more talent in Emission than Transmutation, but by the time he encountered Kite in the NGL, his Transmutation skills might have actually surpassed his Emission abilities, judging by how his combat techniques developed over time. Despite his cheerful, innocent appearance, Gon is actually one of the most complex and psychologically intense characters in the entire series. His simple-minded approach to life becomes a serious weakness when his emotions get triggered. He loses all rational thinking and becomes completely tunnel-visioned on whatever's bothering him. And nothing has ever pushed him over the edge quite like what happened to Kite. When the Chimera Ant Royal Guard Neferpitou killed Kite, something inside Gon completely shattered. Kite represented everything Gon never had \- a father figure who was actually present, a mentor who cared about him, someone who filled the void left by Ging's absence. Discovering Kite's corpse being used as nothing more than a training puppet was the final straw that broke Gon's mental state completely. The guilt hit him first. He blamed himself for being too weak to protect someone he cared about. This self-hatred sent him spiraling down a dark psychological path where he had to face some truly disturbing aspects of his own personality. All that rage and frustration eventually focused like a laser beam on Neferpitou, the one responsible for Kite's death. Standing face to face with a Royal Guard who is ready to kill him, he made a desperate decision. In a moment of complete emotional breakdown, he formed the ultimate Nen vow. The condition was simple but devastating \- he would sacrifice all of his future potential, all of his natural talent, everything he could ever become, in exchange for enough power to defeat Neferpitou right now. He literally traded decades of growth and development for a single moment of overwhelming strength. What happened next was one of the most jaw-dropping transformations in anime history. Gon's body began changing, growing into the adult form he might have achieved after decades of intensive training. His physical strength exploded beyond comprehension. His Nen abilities amplified to terrifying levels. His aura output and reserves increased so dramatically that even Neferpitou started genuinely fearing he might pose a threat to Meruem himself. The most incredible part was that Gon pulled this off with virtually no knowledge about how Nen vows actually work. The only information he had came from Kurapika's brief explanation of his own abilities. Yet somehow, through sheer emotional intensity and desperation, he managed to create one of the most powerful Nen contracts ever seen. In his transformed state, he became an absolute monster in combat. His speed and reflexes let him easily dodge Pitou's Terpsichora attacks, even when they were pushing themselves beyond their normal limits. A single Ko-enhanced kick was enough to launch Pitou dozens of meters into the air and make them cough up blood. This was the same Royal Guard who had previously taken hits from Isaac Netero without showing any significant damage. When Gon unleashed his adult-form Jajanken, the attack blasted Pitou up the side of a hill and knocked them completely unconscious. But Gon wasn't done. The rage that had been building up inside him demanded complete destruction. He  began smashing the incapacitated Ant repeatedly with continuous Jajanken attacks until their skull literally shattered into pieces. Even when Pitou's post-mortem Nen allowed them to fight back as a corpse, it wasn't enough. Gon finished them off with the most explosive Jajanken technique ever witnessed. But the price for this power was exactly as severe as promised. Pitou had correctly guessed that only an extraordinarily gifted individual could pull off such a transformation by willingly throwing away their entire future and compressing their whole life into a single moment. They predicted that Gon would never be able to use Nen again, and they were right. After the Royal Guard's death, Gon's body immediately returned to its normal child form and he collapsed into a coma. His condition rapidly deteriorated as his body began to decay. He required constant life support just to stay alive, and doctors expected him to die without some kind of miracle intervention. That miracle came in the form of Nanika, whose wish-granting abilities managed to save Gon's life and restore his physical health. However, even after making a complete recovery, Gon remained completely unable to see or use aura. According to Ging, Gon had simply "returned to normal" and should consider himself lucky for escaping with relatively minor consequences. Right before Gon finished off Pitou, Killua had feared that the more aura Gon used during his transformation, the worse the backlash would be. This suggests that rather than requiring Gon's immediate death, the transformation probably shaved away a portion of his total lifespan equivalent to the massive amount of power he unleashed while transformed. The exact Nen types involved in Gon's transformation remain unknown, though many speculate it represents an extreme form of Enhancement taken to its absolute limits. And whether Gon will ever regain his Nen abilities is still a complete mystery.

4. **Skill Hunter (Chrollo)**

Chrollo Lucilfer is one of the most dangerous Specialists in the Hunter x Hunter universe, and his Nen ability perfectly matches his reputation as the leader of the Phantom Troupe. Skill Hunter represents everything about Chrollo's nature as a master thief \- it's literally designed to steal other people's Nen abilities and add them to his personal collection. He conjures a book called Skill Hunter that serves as his repository for stolen abilities. When he successfully steals someone's Nen technique, that person loses access to it completely while Chrollo gains the power to use it whenever he wants. Each stolen ability gets stored on its own page within the book, creating a growing library of techniques that makes Chrollo incredibly versatile and unpredictable. But this incredible power comes with strict rules that must be followed exactly. He can't just walk up to someone and steal their ability on a whim. The theft process requires four specific conditions to be met within a one-hour timeframe. First, he must personally witness the target's Nen ability being used with his own eyes. He can't rely on secondhand information or descriptions \- he needs to see the technique in action to understand how it works. Second, he has to engage the victim in conversation about Nen and get them to answer his questions. This requirement forces him to be somewhat social and interactive during the theft process, which fits with his manipulative personality. Third, the victim's palm must physically touch the handprint on Skill Hunter's cover. This is probably the trickiest condition since it requires direct physical contact, meaning he needs to get close enough to his target without raising suspicion. Fourth, all three previous conditions must be completed within sixty minutes. This time limit prevents him from taking weeks or months to slowly work on a single target. Once these conditions are satisfied, the ability transfers to his book and disappears from the original owner's arsenal. To activate a stolen technique, he conjures Skill Hunter and flips to the relevant page. The book must stay open in his right hand while he uses the ability, which means he can only access one stolen technique at a time. When he needs to use a stolen ability that requires both hands, he conjures a bookmark called Double Face to get around this problem. This bookmark lets him keep access to whatever ability is on the page where he places it, even when the book gets closed and he's not holding it anymore. It's basically a loophole for one of Skill Hunter's main restrictions. With both hands free, he can mix hand-to-hand combat with his stolen abilities, making him way more dangerous in close quarters. Plus, if he keeps the book open to one page while using the bookmark on a different page, he can actually use two stolen abilities at the same time. The bookmark is part of the book itself, not something he stole from someone else. The stolen abilities retain all their original characteristics, including the creator's emotions and personal limitations. He discovered this firsthand when he couldn't use Order Stamp on corpses, even though he personally views dead bodies as just objects. The ability still carried its original owner's perspective that corpses shouldn't be manipulated. Another major limitation is that stolen abilities vanish from the book if their original owner dies. This creates an interesting dynamic where Chrollo might actually want to keep his victims alive to maintain access to their powers. However, post-mortem Nen changes this rule \- if someone's Nen continues functioning after death, Chrollo can still use their stolen ability. Expert Nen users can sometimes figure out Chrollo's conditions through careful observation. Zeno demonstrated this during their encounter, correctly guessing that Chrollo needed to fulfill four or five specific requirements before stealing an ability. During his intense battle with Hisoka, Chrollo revealed another aspect of Skill Hunter \- the ability to borrow techniques from willing Nen users rather than stealing them permanently. Before the fight, he borrowed Gallery Fake from Kortopi, which lets him create perfect copies of objects by touching them with his left hand and conjuring the duplicate with his right. He also borrowed Black Voice from Shalnark, allowing him to completely control targets by sticking an antenna into them and manipulating them through a phone. After defeating Hisoka, Chrollo called Shalnark to arrange returning Black Voice, suggesting that borrowed abilities can be given back to their original owners. The exact conditions for returning abilities remain unclear, but borrowing probably requires meeting the same strict requirements as stealing. Skill Hunter possesses an additional feature that makes it even more dangerous \- the ability to upgrade itself. Chrollo has mentioned needing to enhance his book to steal certain specific abilities, and this upgrade process requires him to steal items classified as "national treasures." This condition creates an interesting side quest where Chrollo must target extremely valuable objects to unlock his ability's next level. The exact nature of these upgrades remains mysterious, but they likely involve removing some of the current limitations or conditions. Maybe upgraded Skill Hunter won't require the original owner to stay alive, or perhaps it could eliminate some of the four theft conditions entirely. Chrollo believes these upgrades are necessary to definitively defeat Hisoka in their ongoing conflict. As a Specialist, Chrollo faces certain limitations when using stolen abilities from other Nen categories. Techniques that fall far from Specialization, like Enhancement abilities, become much harder for him to use effectively. The stolen powers run on his own aura rather than their original owner's energy, which means he performs best with Specialist, Conjuration, and Manipulation techniques. This category limitation might explain why Chrollo is so interested in upgrading Skill Hunter. An enhanced version could potentially grant him full proficiency across all Nen types, removing the natural disadvantages that come with being a Specialist.

5. **Pain Packer (Feitan)**

Feitan holds the number two spot in the notorious Phantom Troupe. He specializes in Transmutation-type Nen, and his signature ability Pain Packer works in the most backwards way imaginable. Most Nen users take on the risks after they’ve already powered up their abilities. But Feitan flips that around — his Nen ability won’t even activate unless he takes on the risks first, making them the very requirement for using it. The ability only kicks in after Feitan gets hurt and absolutely loses his mind with rage. The activation requirement may suggest that it is counteractive. Pain Packer feeds off damage and fury. The worse Feitan gets beaten up, the more devastating his counterattack becomes. His aura output skyrockets because he's willing to take massive risks, and those risks translate directly into raw power. Once he's taken enough punishment and reached that breaking point of anger, Pain Packer activates. The ability made its debut during Feitan's brutal showdown with Chimera Ant Squadron Leader Zazan. Things got nasty when Zazan snapped his left arm. Feitan started muttering curses in his native tongue, completely unhinged with rage. That injury combined with his explosive anger checked all the boxes for Pain Packer to activate. What happened next was insane. He then transmuted his aura into blistering heat, with the temperature directly tied to how much damage he'd absorbed during the fight. His Rising Sun technique takes things to another level entirely. Feitan shoots a ball of concentrated aura into the air, and it morphs into a miniature sun that incinerates everything in its path. While wearing his armor, he stays completely safe from the heat and gets a steady oxygen supply. Zazan got slowly reduced to ash while the entire battlefield turned into an inferno. 6. **Cat’s Name (Camilla Hui Guo Rou)**

When it comes to counteractive Nen abilities that only kick in after the user takes a hit, nobody pushes that idea further than Camilla Hui Guo Rou.  This Second Prince of the Kakin Empire takes part in the Succession Contest, and her approach to combat is completely unhinged. While Feitan needs damage and rage to trigger his ability, Camilla takes this concept up to the extreme by requiring her actual death. This thing grabs the killer with its paws and starts draining their life force, completely destroying their body in the process. The extracted life energy flows to the tip of the beast's tail, making it glow bright white. Then the cat creature pours all that stolen life essence straight into Camilla's mouth, healing every injury and bringing her back to life. This ability taps into post-mortem Nen, making it incredibly potent and turning Camilla into someone practically impossible to eliminate permanently. She first showed off this wild technique when she decided to take out First Prince Benjamin. She dropped dead instantly, but then her Nen ability kicked in, consuming the soldier completely to restore and revive her. She then barged into Benjamin's quarters and tried provoking his soldiers into shooting her with a gun. But Benjamin saw right through her strategy and commanded his troops not to attack. 7. **Crazy Slots (Kite)**

Kite is a skilled Hunter and student of Ging Freecss. His early meeting with Gon became a major driving force behind the young boy's determination to become a Hunter. As a Conjurer, Kite can create multiple constructs, but his signature ability Crazy Slots makes him one of the most unpredictable fighters around. Crazy Slots gives Kite access to nine different weapons, each numbered one through nine and paired with a clown figure that selects which weapon to conjure. Like other conjurers who strengthen their abilities through restrictions, Kite sets his own unique limitation and condition. The weapon he receives gets chosen completely at random, which means he cannot choose a weapon of his liking. And once summoned, he has to use it before getting another roll. No take-backs, no re-rolls if the weapon sucks for the situation. This restriction supposedly boosts the power of whatever weapon appears. The random nature of his weapon selection completely changes how Kite approaches combat. Most fighters can rely on their preferred weapons and develop strategies around their strengths. But Kite gets whatever fate decides to give him. Some weapons work better against specific opponents, but his massive arsenal becomes more of a gamble than an advantage. He basically throws strategy out the window and relies on his skills to make any weapon work in any situation. Kite might be talented enough to adapt to most situations, but going up against strong opponents with a randomly selected weapon could mean the difference between life and death. The randomness factor severely limits his ability to strategize, no matter how experienced he is as a Hunter. Despite these obvious risks, the sentient clown making the weapon choices seems to have decent judgment. Out of the three times Kite summoned weapons, only two appeared in actual combat, and both matched their situations perfectly. Both weapons fit their circumstances, though Kite complained about getting bad rolls each time while the clown got visibly upset about his complaints. The randomness system includes one major exception. A mace-like weapon appears specifically when Kite desperately wants to survive. According to Ging, this emergency weapon might have triggered Kite's eventual reincarnation as a Chimera Ant. The full potential of Crazy Slots remains a mystery since Kite lost access to his Nen abilities after being reborn as a female Chimera Ant. Nobody knows if this transformation changed her Aura Type, whether she could relearn her old techniques, or if she can even use Nen anymore. The complete scope of what Crazy Slots could accomplish might never be revealed.

8. **Hakoware (Knuckle)**

Knuckle Bine works as a Beast Hunter and serves as Morel's apprentice, bringing serious conjuring skills to the table. This guy has an incredible mind for numbers, cranking out complex calculations instantly without making mistakes. His mathematical genius translates perfectly into his ability to convert aura into numerical values, which becomes crucial for his Nen ability Hakoware. Hakoware kicks in the moment Knuckle lands a hit on someone. Once he connects with his target, he conjures an indestructible Nen Beast mascot called the Amortizing Power Redirector, or APR for short. This thing latches onto his opponent and stays there as long as Hakoware remains active. The concept behind this ability gets pretty wild \- Knuckle basically becomes an aura loan shark. When APR attaches to someone, Knuckle lends his aura to his enemies rather than damaging them. If they can't pay him back by landing hits on him, the number displayed on APR keeps climbing to show how much aura they owe, complete with interest. Every time interest gets added, APR announces "It's time, adding interest." Any attacks they land on Knuckle return aura to him and lower their debt counter. During this period, Knuckle becomes completely immune to physical damage from that opponent. The flip side means Knuckle's attacks only add more aura debt instead of causing harm. Stronger attacks mean more aura gets lent or returned, though both fighters can still get knocked around despite taking no actual damage. APR charges a brutal 10% interest rate on all borrowed aura, adding it every 10 seconds. The catch is that victims need to stay within 50 meters of Knuckle for the counter to keep running. Step outside that range and the timer pauses until they get back in range. Knuckle can use Hakoware on multiple people simultaneously and track their locations no matter how far away they go. As the debt piles up, APR physically grows larger to match the amount owed. While APR can't be destroyed, victims have three options for getting rid of it. They can find an Exorcist to remove it, though this burdens the Nen remover considerably. They can force Knuckle to deactivate APR himself. Or they can knock Knuckle unconscious or eliminate him entirely. If none of these options work, APR sticks around and keeps adding to the debt. When someone's debt exceeds their remaining aura capacity, they go completely bankrupt. Bankruptcy triggers APR's transformation into the Individual Ren Suppressor, or IRS \- a demon-like mascot that shadows the debtor for exactly 30 days. During this entire month, IRS forces them into continuous Zetsu state, completely blocking their ability to use Nen and leaving them defenseless against any threats.

9. **Countdown (Genthru)**

Genthru goes by the nickname "the Bomber" and takes center stage as the main villain during the Greed Island arc. This Conjurer has mastered the art of blending his natural abilities with Emission and Manipulation to create his terrifying Nen ability called Countdown. Countdown works through a twisted activation process that requires two specific conditions, though Genthru can complete them in any order he wants. First, he needs to physically touch his target while saying "Bomber" out loud. Second, he must explain both of his abilities completely and in the right sequence. Interestingly, demonstrating his abilities counts as explaining them, which is why Genthru didn't need to break down how Little Flower worked when he activated Countdown since his targets had already seen those weaker bombs in action. Once both conditions get met, Genthru conjures a bomb inside his victim and the countdown timer begins at 6000\. The challenge lies in actually getting close enough to pull this off while Genthru actively tries to avoid capture. Genthru's sneaky nature shows through his ability to detonate bombs manually at any moment, even while the countdown continues ticking. He pulls this off by touching thumbs with his two accomplices Sub and Bara, with all three saying "Release" simultaneously. 10. **Rental Pod (Leol)**

Leol was a Chimera Ant Squadron Leader who made the smart choice to rejoin and pledge his loyalty to Meruem after the Queen died. In return for his allegiance, he got hooked up with a Nen ability courtesy of Royal Guard Shaiapouf. Eventually, he developed his own signature technique called Rental Pod. Just like Chrollo's ability-stealing power comes with its fair share of conditions to jump through, Leol's ability-borrowing trick operates under its own set of conditions. Rental Pod lets him temporarily borrow other people's Nen abilities, but he's gotta work for it. Two specific conditions need to be met for each ability he wants to get his hands on. First condition is pretty straightforward \- he needs to either know what the ability is called or witness it in action. Second condition gets a bit more personal \- he has to do the ability owner a legitimate favor and get them to acknowledge they owe him one. Usually involves some variation of "You owe me, alright?" to seal the deal. Once Leol checks both boxes, the owner's name, ability name, and usage count all get automatically logged into his I.O.U. Dispenser, this nifty little gadget that looks surprisingly similar to an iPod. Just like with Skill Hunter, there's a catch though \- if the original owner dies, their info gets wiped from the machine and that ability becomes permanently unavailable. When Leol wants to actually use a borrowed ability, he operates the dispenser to print out a receipt, then tears it up. This gives him exactly one hour of access to that power. During that window, the original owner can't use their own ability. He put this system to work when he borrowed Flutter's Satellitonbo and Super Eye abilities to track down human intruders around Peijin, following Pitou's direct orders. At this point, Leol was planning to stay loyal to Meruem indefinitely, at least until the day he might end up owing the King a favor so he can borrow his ability. 25. **Moonlight Act (Longhi)**

Longhi works as a Royal Bodyguard for Prince Tubeppa and gets assigned to take Kurapika's Nen lessons alongside Maor. When Tubeppa orders them to negotiate some kind of peace deal with Kurapika who is guarding Prince Woble, Longhi decides to use her secret Nen ability Moonlight Act to broker an alliance. Her ability revolves entirely around the concept of Nen contracts and their associated rewards or punishments. The ability lets her create binding agreements that can either reward people for following the terms or penalize them brutally if they break the contract. She transmutes her aura into the form of an aura pen and aura paper to write and sign these deals. Before she can get anyone to sign anything, she herself faces a mandatory truth-telling requirement that creates serious vulnerabilities. She must reveal her entire true plan and all relevant information to the other party, including personal info about herself and how her ability operates, before proceeding with any contract. This restriction prevents her from deceiving people into signing bad deals, but it also means she has to expose potentially damaging secrets. When approaching Kurapika for the alliance, Longhi drops a massive bombshell to fulfill this requirement. She reveals that she's actually the daughter of Beyond Netero, and that she's just one of many children Beyond had who were raised by different Kakin officers before being deployed as Royal Guards for the princes. She speculates that Beyond planted one of his own children among the princes themselves and wants to ensure his kid wins the succession contest. After explaining her background, Longhi details how Moonlight Act functions. The ability allows her to loan her powers to others or restrict their actions through limited-term contracts. The signature must be completely voluntary \- any coercion or trickery invalidates the entire contract. She also has to explain every single detail of the contract terms before anyone can sign, which means no hidden clauses or surprise conditions. The contract Longhi proposes with Kurapika contains multiple specific terms that create both protections and potential problems. Neither side can interfere with or attack the other side's prince, queen, or guards until 9 a.m. on Sunday. The contract automatically renews for another week if a Tubeppa representative stays present in Prince Woble's room. Moonlight Act only activates to enforce penalties if Kurapika's side violates the terms, with the punishment being a full week of forced Zetsu. Healing and support actions don't count as attacks, which prevents medical aid from triggering violations. The contract includes protective clauses for both sides. Tubeppa's team can't unilaterally terminate the deal unless they discover actual malicious acts from Woble's side, and Longhi promises never to arbitrarily interpret Woble's actions as hostile. These terms prevent either side from breaking the contract over misunderstandings or minor infractions. Longhi adds a personal stipulation that creates additional risks and rewards. Woble's side must identify which prince is Beyond's child before either Tubeppa withdraws or only two princes remain alive. If they discover that none of the princes are actually Beyond's children, Moonlight Act immediately activates to reward Woble's side instead of punishing them. The personal stipulation comes with its own complications. She promises to compensate him with potentially a one-time use of Moonlight Act itself, though the penalties remain limited to the week-long Zetsu punishment. The mandatory explanation requirement means Longhi can't hide the fact that forced Zetsu is currently her only known penalty method. This gives potential contract signers complete information about the risks they're taking, but it also limits her negotiating power since people know exactly what they're facing if they break the terms. Kurapika ultimately agrees to sign Longhi's contract and collaborate with Tubeppa, but only on the condition that Woble and her mother Oito accompany him when he visits Prince Tubeppa's room. This acceptance creates a binding agreement that puts both sides under Moonlight Act's enforcement mechanism for the duration specified in the terms.
