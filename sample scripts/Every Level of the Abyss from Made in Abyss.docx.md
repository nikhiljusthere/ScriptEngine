**Every Level of the Abyss from Made in Abyss**

* **First Layer**

The First Layer, also known as the Edge of the Abyss, sits 1,350 meters below the surface. It’s basically the shallow end of the world’s most terrifying pit. Compared to the nightmare fuel that waits further down, this area is relatively chill. In fact, it’s the only part of the Abyss that could even remotely be called “safe.” The Abyss Curse here is more of a gentle slap than a full-on gut punch — think dizziness and a bit of nausea, like you spun around too fast or rode the teacups after a burrito. Most Delvers make their descent through the official entrance which is the Gate to the Netherworld. But of course, there’s always the shady route where you can sneak in through the Wharf without drawing too much attention and maybe skip the paperwork. Right above it all is the town of Orth, kind of like the Abyss’s welcome mat. To help with the descent, there’s a gondola system built into The Grand Pier which lowers people down about 580 meters. That takes you pretty close to a rocky outcropping known as the Stone Ark, around 600 meters deep. At about 300 meters down, there’s something called the Burial Tower where you’ll find hundreds of skeletons dating back anywhere from 2,000 to 6,000 years. They’re all posed with their hands clasped like they were praying when they died. Now, the environment in this layer doesn’t really shift all that much. It keeps a soft, perpetual sunset vibe in terms of lighting, which is oddly peaceful. Wildlife here is mostly harmless. You’ve got creatures like Demonfish and Hammerbeaks floating or flapping around doing their thing. There’s also the Silkfang, which is slightly less cute since it spits a poison that can paralyze you. But occasionally, predators from deeper levels wander up here looking for a snack. That includes monsters like the Crimson Splitjaw and the Sakawatari. As for loot? Don’t expect to get rich here. Most of the artifacts you’ll find are pretty meh — third or fourth grade. But hey, some are weird enough to be cool. There's the Princess Bosom, also known as the “Boob Stone” because it’s soft and squishy like — you know. There’s the Rock Top, which floats and shapeshifts when spun. Then you've got the Star Compass, which always points to the Abyss like a creepy little GPS, and the Sun Sphere, which emits a bright light when activated. Because it’s relatively low-risk, this layer is kind of a training ground for rookie Delvers or the Red Whistles. They come here to cut their teeth, search for artifacts, and hopefully not get eaten. One major event that went down here is this is where Reg was first discovered by Riko. After using his Incinerator to rescue her from a wandering Crimson Splitjaw, Reg passed out, and Riko dragged him back to Belchero Orphanage. Around 250 years ago, someone discovered a sealed envelope in the First Layer containing some ultra-rare, high-level abyss knowledge allegedly from the Seventh Layer. Aside from the Gate, the Gondola, the Burial Tower, the Stone Ark, and the Abode of Trees and Fossils where Riko first bumped into Reg, other notable locations in the First Layer include the Guiding Tree, Ominaki Falls, Seat of the Waterfall, Stargazing Hill, Twisting Crag, and Wuthering Windmill. So yeah, the Edge of the Abyss is where things start. It’s deceptively calm, sprinkled with ancient mysteries, weird wildlife, beginner-friendly loot, and just enough danger to keep things interesting.

* **Second Layer**

The Second Layer of the Abyss, also known as the Forest of Temptation, sounds kinda enchanting but don’t be fooled. This is where the Abyss stops playing nice and starts showing its teeth. We’re talking full-on nightmare jungle, 2,600 meters beneath the First Layer, and miles away from anything resembling mercy. This layer is a game-changer. The moment Delvers cross into it, the Curse of the Abyss goes from “mild motion sickness” to “your limbs go numb while you puke your guts out.” Headaches, intense nausea, and that oh-so-pleasant pins-and-needles feeling all kick in when you try to ascend. Basically, once you go down here, going back up isn’t really an option unless you're tough as nails — or have a death wish. And speaking of death wishes: if a Red Whistle somehow ends up down here, the higher-ups don’t even bother sending a search party. It’s instantly marked as a suicide mission. That’s how harsh the Abyss gets, this early on. The landscape here does a hard pivot from the First Layer’s soft lighting and gentle terrain to rainforest with insanely huge vegetation. Giant lily pad-like plants dominate the upper half of the forest. These monster plants are massive enough to provide shelter but also block your view, making navigation a confusing mess. Thankfully, their branches always point toward the Abyss’s center, so if you get turned around, you can use those as a natural compass. There are also Amagiri sprouts growing along their stems which are handy navigation tools for lost Delvers. Then there's the Inverted Forest which is as wild as it sounds. This area is dominated by powerful updrafts and wind currents that are so strong they turn the whole ecosystem upside down — literally. Water flows upward. Trees hang from the “ceiling.” Platforms float in weird patterns, and you’ve gotta jump across them like a deranged obstacle course. The Force Field — the thing responsible for the Curse — is actually weaker here, which means two things: it’s darker and colder, and it’s slightly less curse-y, which is nice... relatively speaking. Because of the milder force field, Delvers have built a resting station here in a massive tree called the Giant Porta Tree. This became Seeker Camp, run by none other than the White Whistle legend herself, Ozen the Immovable. Her crew, the Subterranean Bandits, holds things down from there and offers a rare safe-ish zone in this otherwise cursed green hell. Now, let’s talk wildlife — and not the cute kind. The Second Layer is crawling with monsters that feel like they crawled straight out of someone’s worst dreams. You've got the Corpse-Weepers, which literally mimic the cries of their victims to lure in rescuers, only to devour them too. Then there are Inbyos, gibbon-like beasts that are super territorial and absolutely not afraid to throw down. Next up, Ottobas — giant hippopotamus-like, human-swallowing horrors. And don’t forget the Urikouri, those flying scavengers with long, fleshy tongues they use to strip flesh from bone like they’re unwrapping candy. In terms of loot, the Forest of Temptation actually holds some serious First-Grade Artifacts. One of the most infamous is the Curse-Warding Box. Early Delvers thought this thing could nullify the Curse if you got inside it. Except that’s not what it does. It doesn’t protect you from anything. The person inside still suffers the Curse — and often dies — but then comes back to life... only to beeline toward the Abyss’s center before dying again. Riko’s the only known exception to this twisted miracle because she lived — somehow. Another hot item is the Thousand-Men Pins. These tiny little needles supposedly give the strength of a thousand men — per pin — when stabbed into your body. Ozen is rumored to have around 120 of these lodged in her flesh. That would definitely explain why she can toss boulders around like beach balls. Outside the Inverted Forest, you’ll find other eerie-sounding points of interest like Heaven’s Waterfall, Rohana Fountainhead, Sleeping Bed of Mushrooms, and the Wind-riding Windmills. And just to make things even more fun, there’s also a mysterious virus lurking in this layer — the Tachikiri Fever. It’s a nasty, unknown disease that can wreck Delvers. Thankfully, Blue Whistle Delvers and above get vaccinated for it before coming down, so at least there’s some protection from that particular horror.

* **Third Layer**

Welcome to The Third Layer of the Abyss, also known by its super inviting name: The Great Fault. If you thought the Second Layer was bad with its upside-down forests and monster birds, the Third Layer basically goes, “Hold my Baracocha fruit.” This place is basically a narrow, terrifying, 4-kilometer-deep vertical shaft — a massive, dark gorge that drops straight down into the unknown. Unlike the relatively spacious layers above, this shaft is tight. Like, if you have claustrophobia, maybe just stay in Orth. According to the Abyss Map, this layer has one of the narrowest diameters in the entire Abyss structure. Now, descending the Great Fault isn’t a simple case of rappelling down a cliff with your favorite grappling hook. The only viable way down is through a chaotic network of caverns, tunnels, and holes that twist and wind their way through the rock walls. These passageways are a mix of natural formations and old routes likely carved by ancient Delvers, forgotten civilizations, and the local residents called the Neritantan. Lurking in these tunnels and crevices is a whole ecosystem of wildlife ranging from harmless to extremely deadly. This layer is particularly famous for its aerial predators, most notably the Madokajack. These flying fiends will absolutely swoop down and scoop you up if you're not careful. Then there’s the Crimson Splitjaw — yeah, that same nightmare noodle from the First Layer. Turns out it's strong enough to break into caves in this layer to go hunting. And let’s not forget the Amakagame, which dwell in the cave systems. They’re not exactly hunting humans but they can be a threat. Their main diet is the rodent-like Neritantan, so humans aren’t their go-to snack. Their stomach acid smells like Baracocha fruit — which Neritantans love. So, they basically weaponize scent as bait. The smell wafts through a hole in the top of their body and attracts unsuspecting prey, which they then swallow whole. Most of the inner caverns house non-aggressive residents but there are exceptions. Every now and then, a Madokajack decides to turn a large cave into a cozy nest. When you hit around the 6,750 meter mark, things get even weirder. The walls of the central shaft start sprouting spiky eggs stuck along the rock like someone went a little wild with nature’s version of thumbtacks. These eggs hatch into Kazura Squid, which eventually drift down into the even more dangerous Fourth Layer, where they become part of that biome’s aerial chaos. Now, let’s talk The Curse. At this point, the Curse of the Abyss is really flexing. You don’t just get the usual nausea and numbness from before. Now you’re also dealing with vertigo, vomiting, visual and auditory hallucinations, and a general “my brain is melting” vibe. It’s a full sensory breakdown waiting to happen, especially on the climb back up. But it’s not all nightmares and hallucinations. The Third Layer also offers up a pretty cool artifact called the Thinking Stone, which is used to make a tool called the Gentle Knock. Once calibrated to the user, the Gentle Knock can be mentally controlled. The tip morphs based on the user’s thoughts and emotions. It has pincers that can force things open, and the control unit is tucked away on the opposite end. Very handy if you're stuck in a place where everything wants to kill you. There are also some quirky and ominous-sounding points of interest down here including the Baracocha Corridors, Cumulonimbus Point, Rumbling Grounds of the Strong, Ghostly Roots, and the Tallowstone Layer. And here’s one last cool fact: starting from this layer and continuing deeper, the light in the Abyss doesn’t come from the surface anymore. It starts glowing from below. Yep — the light from the lower layers is brighter than the sunlight above. That’s your cue that the lower layers are crazier in any sense.

* **Fourth Layer**

The Fourth Layer of the Abyss — known as the Goblets of Giants — is where the line between breathtaking beauty and sheer physical horror is thinner than a Fog Weave thread. First things first, the Curse of the Abyss hits a whole new level here. Instead of just the nausea or hallucinations from earlier layers, now you’re getting full-body pain and bleeding from every orifice. So if you’re heading down, pack some gauze and a high pain tolerance. The environment is like a fever dream of a rainforest, only everything wants to kill you. This place is packed with massive concave vine platforms called Flat-creepers. These enormous discs (we’re talking 1 km wide kind of enormous) stretch out across the layer, capturing water from the air and forming shallow, natural pools. Only those pools are home to Orb Piercers which are basically biomechanical porcupine nightmares with a murder record longer than some wars. Orb Piercers are straight-up terrifying. Each one claims an entire Flat-creeper as its turf, and they guard it like a paranoid landlord. Their venom is slow-acting but causes massive swelling and weakness, so much so that amputation becomes the best-case scenario. And while they seem rare, their kill count is absurd. Over a hundred Black Whistle Delvers have lost lives or limbs to just one. Because of the high concentration of warm water and intense humidity, the whole layer feels like you're in a steamy greenhouse. Sometimes the water vapor even creates a dense fog, which, combined with the refracted blue light from The Force Field, gives the area an eerie, otherworldly glow. Every 2,000 years, something truly wild happens. The Flat-creepers' prey-trapping abilities fade and the canopy starts thinning, letting in more light. That triggers the lower plants to go berserk, race to grow taller and dominate the landscape. Basically, the whole environment resets itself and turns into a completely new jungle. Because of this ever-changing layout, no one's even bothered trying to map this place out aside from a few key landmarks. One such landmark is Nanachi’s Hideout which is tucked away in a quiet spot far from the central shaft. It’s special because it’s practically free of the Curse, making it one of the few safe-ish zones in the entire layer. Then there's the Garden of the Flowers of Resilience, found at a chilling 9,000 meters deep. This breathtaking field is filled with Eternal Fortunes, the signature flower of the Abyss. They're beautiful, haunting, and symbolic. But unfortunately, beauty never comes without some lurking horror in the Abyss. Enter the Amaranthine-Deceptors — insects from the Sixth Layer that’ve made their way up here. These bugs are sneaky devils. Their wings mimic Eternal Fortunes, letting them hide in plain sight. And when you get too close, they latch on, inject larvae, and turn your body into a mobile buffet for their babies. Now, on to the treasures — because even in this hellish jungle, there are reasons why Delvers keep pushing forward. First up, there’s the Fog Weave, a Third Grade Artifact that looks like a floating piece of mist. It’s incredibly sensitive to movement — so light that even your breath can shift it. Nanachi used this to explain how The Force Field flows, since it behaves the same way, making it super useful for visualizing invisible death energy. Then there’s the infamous Blaze Reap. This oversized pickaxe isn’t just for mining — it’s filled with something called Peace Phobia, which is basically the “explode everything it touches” setting. It smashes, it detonates, and it looks extremely cool while doing it. But the real crown jewel here is the Unheard Bell, a Special Grade Artifact with straight-up time-stopping powers. Here’s how it works: it stops time for everyone except the user, within a radius defined by how loud the bell rings. The longer the sound reverberates, the longer time stays frozen. But here’s the twist — the bell doesn’t make any sound anyone else can hear. Only the user can hear the chime. Its waves don’t behave like normal sound waves either. Lyza and Ozen were the only ones who managed to retrieve it.

* **Fifth Layer**

The Fifth Layer, ominously named the Sea of Corpses, marks a major threshold in the Abyss. It’s the final level from which Delvers can realistically return to the surface with their sanity and humanity still intact. Because of the extreme risk, only Black Whistles and White Whistles are permitted to venture this far, and even among them, survivors are few. Despite being only 1,000 meters deep which is the shallowest layer vertically, it’s also the widest, stretching out to a scale that dwarfs even the city of Orth by several magnitudes. The terrain is a sprawling frozen wasteland surrounding an eerie, massive sea — hence the name. At the center of this layer lies a vast, ancient sea filled with primeval creatures and the bones of long-dead lifeforms. But this isn’t just any body of water. Beneath the visible surface, there’s a thick, slimy water layer that keeps the sea suspended, held in place by the Abyss's mysterious Force Field. Scattered throughout are frost formations or large structures made of crystallized water. These can be temporary or permanent, the latter referred to as “water bastions”. One particularly important feature is a large central waterfall which allows Mail Balloons from deeper layers to ascend through it, making it one of the few functional methods of vertical transport in the Abyss. Here, the Curse of the Abyss manifests as extreme sensory deprivation. Victims lose awareness of their surroundings and bodily sensations. While ascending from this layer, Delvers may not even notice they’ve broken bones or suffered injuries. Riko herself once failed to realize she had broken her own teeth or tumbled down stairs while under its effects. Interestingly, the boundary between the Fifth and Sixth Layers begins just below the sea’s surface. Because of this, much of the sea technically overlaps with the Sixth Layer, while the Fifth primarily contains the surrounding landmass, surface waters, and a major base of operations. At a depth of 13,000 meters, the deepest point above the sea, sits Ido Front — a forward operating base established by the infamous White Whistle, Bondrewd. Originally an ancient ritual site, Ido Front now functions as a hub for exploration into the Sixth Layer. Powered by the constant rotation of a giant whirlpool, Ido Front includes the Altar of the Absolute Boundary, an elevator-like mechanism that allows passage to lower layers. However, it only responds to Life-reverberating Stones, meaning it can only be activated by specific White Whistles, and even then only by the stone’s designated owner. The Sea of Corpses hosts both peaceful and deadly creatures. On the gentle side, we have species like the Hamashirama and Meinastilim. But on the other end of the spectrum are the Stingerheads — giant, seven-tailed scorpion-like predators. Standing over two meters tall, they are the apex predators of the Fifth Layer. Each of their tails is filled with a flesh-melting venom, and they’re known for consuming the internal organs of their prey after liquefying them from within. One of the most haunting aspects of the Fifth Layer is the presence of the Narehate — beings who have survived the Curse of the Sixth Layer, but not without being physically or mentally altered. A lot of these tragic cases came out of Bondrewd’s brutal experiments down at Ido Front. He used children as test subjects, transported them down into the sixth layer, and when they inevitably transformed into Narehate, he dumped them in a spot called the Miniature Garden of Dawn, like some twisted science fair reject bin. But there are exceptions. Mitty, for instance, is a special case. She's nearly indestructible, doesn’t need food or water, and can shrug off any toxins like it’s nothing. The Fifth Layer is rich with relics, many of which belong to Bondrewd’s personal arsenal including the Canopy Unto Dawn (a set of armor designed to enhance combat efficiency), Far Caress (a relic that can manipulate countless tentacles), Gangway (emits a beam of light that reflects and targets enemies), Shaker (made from Curse Steel that inflicts the Abyss’s Curse upon piercing), and Sparagmos (generates a mysterious blade of energy). Other powerful artifacts found here include the Life-reverberating Stones which are used to forge White Whistles, the First Grade Artifact Third Works resembling a skeletal arm that becomes a functional extension of the user’s body when connected, and the Special Grade Artifact Zoaholic that allows the user to spread their consciousness across multiple living beings. While incredibly powerful, this ability eventually fractures the user's mind and renders them invalid.

* **Sixth Layer**

We now dive into the Sixth Layer of the Abyss, aka the Capital of the Unreturned. The name isn’t just for dramatic flair; it’s literal. Once a Delver descends to this depth, they never make it back. It’s called “The Last Dive” for a reason. At around 15,500 meters deep, this place is the real point of no return. Now let’s talk about the Curse here. The Sixth Layer hits Delvers with a level of suffering that’s basically indescribable. If you try to ascend after reaching this depth, you don’t just get a nosebleed or a headache — you mutate. Most people devolve into a writhing, mindless heap of flesh. All cognitive function — gone. All that’s left are the basest survival instincts. Sometimes, people just die instead... which, honestly, might be the kinder option. In rare circumstances, there’s a loophole. You might get off easy if someone else takes on the Curse for you. Instead of turning into a fleshy horror, you keep your mind — and get a funky new body with animal traits like fur or claws. Bondrewd calls this the “Blessing.” The only two confirmed cases of this bizarre phenomenon are Nanachi and Bondrewd himself. Now, about the terrain — this place is home to the ruins of an ancient, city-sized civilization that’s just sleeping under layers of time. It might even be the legendary Golden City whispered about in Orth. Though it’s called golden, the whole place is coated in a strange crystalline substance. No one’s sure if the city was built that way or fossilized over time. Either way, the ruins are wild — tilted, upside down, sideways — like a broken snow globe scattered across the abyss. As you might expect, extremely dangerous creatures are everywhere. The further you go, the more bizarre and deadly things get. Somewhere in this chaos, the Village of Iruburu stands out like a dark fairytale. Iruburu is home to a special type of Narehate which is totally different from Bondrewd’s failed experiments back in the Fifth Layer. These villagers chose to become Narehate, thanks to an artifact called the Cradle of Desire. Once transformed, they’re bound to the village for life. They can’t leave, but in return, they get physical forms shaped by their deepest wishes, plus the village’s protection. There’s even a sub-species of Narehate called Juusou, who are born as Narehate rather than transformed. Outside the village, the landscape doesn’t get any friendlier. There are jagged ruins, treacherous cliffs, and fields of Eternal Fortunes. You’ll also find the habitat of the Emperorshell, a massive, armored creature living past the 14,000-meter mark, where flaming iron rain casually pours from above. If you head roughly 300 meters shy of the Seventh Layer, you’ll stumble across an outpost run by the Hail Hex Delvers, led by Srajo, one of the White Whistles. There’s also a landmark there called The Eye, a mysterious place marking the boundary between the Sixth and Seventh Layers. As for the local fauna, some of the terrifying residents include the Amaranthine-Deceptors, Pseudowater (a parasitic liquid that slowly turns its victims into stone), and the Turbinid-Dragons whose poisonous scales double as armored plating. Because this layer is completely cut off from everything above it, it’s teeming with strange lifeforms and dangerous relics that no one’s catalogued yet. That brings us to the artifacts including the Cradle of Desire which infuses to the user and grants their wish, the Mermaid Burp which creates gas, and the Popple Gun which fires Popping Fodder, a relic-based ammo that explodes like a shotgun blast.

* **Seventh Layer**

The Seventh Layer, also called the Final Maelstrom, is the deepest known part of the Abyss, and it's where things really start feeling less like an ecosystem and more like a fever dream wrapped in a cryptid mystery novel. Not much is known about this layer — and that’s not just dramatic exaggeration. According to Srajo, the only official record of the Curse of the Seventh Layer comes from a sealed letter discovered all the way back in the First Layer — and that was 250 years ago. That’s the only documented instance of the Curse here. Whatever else is known about this place is passed down in total secrecy— through verbal transmission only, from one White Whistle to another. No written records. No academic papers. Just cryptic warnings from the few who made their Last Dive and never returned. Among the many strange rumors is talk of a mysterious artifact called the Ring of the Essence. Only a select few White Whistles are said to have seen it, and its purpose is still a big question mark. Along with that, there are claims of powerful, enigmatic entities called the Gatekeepers said to lurk somewhere along the descent to the bottom. And it gets weirder. Faputa, through her bond with the soul in Lyza's White Whistle named Doni, managed to confirm the existence of both the Ring of the Essence and a mysterious, still-unidentified being that has some connection to Reg. Now let’s talk environment. There’s this bizarre current of super-cold gas that snakes along the Force Field bands. It’s cold enough to frost anything it touches, which gives the whole layer this eerie, glimmering vibe. Combine that with heavy rain and surprisingly lush vegetation, and it feels like the Abyss just threw the rest of the food chain out the window and started fresh. The landscape itself is a chaotic patchwork of cliffs, floating land-bridges, and twisting cave systems that doesn’t follow traditional physics. Then there’s the phenomenon called Katchaba, which straight up disables your senses. It’s like the Abyss decides you don’t need to hear, see, smell, or feel anymore. If you think you'll be lonely in here, you’re wrong. Say hello to the Mysterious Beast, the Seventh Layer’s most famous and fearsome resident. This creature can gobble up a full-grown human like it’s nothing. It hides by manipulating your senses, including the ability to block detection via the Force Field itself. So it’s basically invisible unless it wants to be seen. But that’s not all. It can regenerate incredibly fast, shrugging off damage like a boss. It even uses the Force Field to fly, navigating in full 3D space like the Curse doesn’t even apply to it. Oh, and remember that blue pendant Riko found back near where she discovered Reg, after their run-in with the Crimson Splitjaw? That trinket is heavily implied to have originated from the Seventh Layer. Reg sees it in a flashback while in this layer — held by an enigmatic figure referred to only as Girl-type. Who this unknown figure is, what their connection is to Reg, and why that pendant matters — those are all threads still waiting to be unraveled.
