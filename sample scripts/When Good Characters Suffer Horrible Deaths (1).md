**When Good Characters Suffer Horrible Deaths**

1. **Jiraiya (Naruto Shippuden)**

Jiraiya's story begins as a student under Hiruzen Sarutobi, training alongside two names that would shape his entire life — Tsunade and Orochimaru. The three grow into elite shinobi and eventually earn the title of the Legendary Sannin after surviving a battle against Hanzo of the Salamander, During the Second Great Ninja War, Jiraiya ends up in Amegakure and comes across three orphaned kids trying to survive in the ruins — Nagato, Konan, and Yahiko. Instead of leaving them behind, he stays for three years and trains them, teaching them everything he knows about ninjutsu and survival. At the same time, he carries the weight of a prophecy told by the Great Toad Sage, which speaks of a student who could either save the world or destroy it. Jiraiya starts to believe that Nagato, with his rare Rinnegan, might be that child. Eventually, he leaves them behind, trusting that his teachings will be enough, but the world they live in doesn’t give second chances that easily, and war continues to shape their lives in ways Jiraiya can’t control. Back in Konoha, things don’t exactly get easier. Orochimaru falls into darkness and begins conducting forbidden human experiments. Jiraiya discovers this firsthand and confronts him, but when the moment comes to stop him, he hesitates and lets him escape. That decision sticks with him for the rest of his life, becoming one of his biggest regrets. By the time he reappears in the story, Jiraiya has built a reputation that feels all over the place in the best way. He’s a legendary shinobi, a wandering sage, and at the same time, a shameless pervert who somehow manages to turn that into a bestselling writing career. Underneath all of that, he remains incredibly perceptive and deeply caring, especially when it comes to the people he chooses to protect. That becomes obvious when he meets Naruto Uzumaki during the Chunin Exams. Jiraiya immediately notices two things: Naruto is the jinchūriki of the Nine-Tails, and Orochimaru has placed a seal on him that disrupts his control over that power. Jiraiya removes the seal and begins training him, and what starts as a mentor-student relationship slowly turns into something much closer to family. He pushes Naruto hard, sometimes in ways that feel extreme, like forcing him into a life-or-death situation just to unlock the Summoning Technique, which leads to Naruto successfully calling upon Gamabunta. As they continue traveling together, Jiraiya teaches him the Rasengan, a technique created by Minato Namikaze, Jiraiya’s former student. That connection adds another layer to everything, because Jiraiya knows Naruto is Minato’s son, even though Naruto doesn’t know it yet, and every lesson carries that unspoken weight. After Sasuke Uchiha defects from Konoha, leaving Naruto devastated, Jiraiya takes him away for two and a half years of training. During that time, he becomes the closest thing Naruto has to a parent, guiding him not just in combat, but in how to keep going despite loss. When they return, Jiraiya leaves Naruto in the care of Kakashi Hatake and heads out on his own mission to follow leads on the Akatsuki. Intelligence points to their leader operating from Amegakure, now ruled by someone known as Pain. Jiraiya infiltrates the village alone, fully aware of the risk. Before leaving, he secretly sends away Gerotora, a toad carrying crucial information about Naruto’s seal, making it clear that he is preparing for the possibility that he might not return. Once inside the village, he gathers intel and learns that Pain defeated Hanzo, the same shinobi who once pushed the Sannin to their limits. Things intensify when he encounters Konan, now grown and fighting on the opposing side, confirming that the past he left behind has come back in a way he never wanted. Then he faces Pain, and the fight that follows pushes him to his limits. Jiraiya enters Sage Mode, combining his strength with the elder toads Fukasaku and Shima, and throws everything he has into the battle. As the fight continues, he realizes that Pain is not a single person but a group of six bodies, each connected through the Rinnegan and sharing vision, which makes fighting them far more dangerous than he initially expected. During the battle, he takes heavy damage, including losing an arm, but he keeps analyzing, adapting, and searching for the truth behind Pain’s identity. Jiraiya deduces he’s up against his former student Nagato since the first Pain he runs into, the Animal Path, has the Rinnegan. His confusion deepens when he recognizes one of the bodies as Yahiko, one of the children he once trained, which confirms that something far deeper is going on. Before he can fully act on that discovery, Pain overwhelms him, crushing his throat and impaling him with black chakra receivers. Even in that state, he refuses to let everything end there. He pushes himself one last time and manages to encode a message about Pain’s secret onto Fukasaku’s back, ensuring that the information will reach Konoha. That message becomes the key to understanding that the real enemy is not among the six bodies, but controlling them from elsewhere. As he sinks into the water, he reflects on the many things he believes he failed to accomplish, from Orochimaru’s fall to the losses of people he cared about. In his final moments, he realizes that the child of prophecy he had been searching for all along isn’t Nagato, but Naruto, and he finds peace in that conclusion. He dedicated his life to protecting people, teaching his students, and chasing a world that could move beyond endless conflict, and just when he comes closest to seeing that vision take form, his story is cut short.

2. **Band of the Hawk (Berserk)**

The Band of the Hawk didn’t start as anything special. It was just a small group made up of thieves, outcasts, and random wanderers who somehow ended up following one guy — Griffith. A silver-haired dreamer with ambition of ruling his own kingdom pulled people in whether they understood it or not. Most of them had nothing going for them, and then suddenly, they had someone worth believing in. You had Pippin, a former coal miner built like a tank, quiet most of the time but terrifying once a fight started. Then there was Judeau, a circus performer turned soldier, sharp, reliable, and always looking out for everyone else. Corkus came from a gang of thieves, loud and rough but loyal when it counted. And Casca, who climbed her way up from nothing and became a commander through pure grit and skill. Then Guts showed up. He was already making a name for himself by taking down strong opponents like Bazuso before even crossing paths with Griffith. Griffith challenges him, wins, and immediately brings him into the group. From there, Guts rises to become the Raiders Captain, and for over three years, he leads the Band of the Hawk through battle after battle. That’s when things really take off. They build a reputation so deadly that people start calling them the Grim Reapers of the Battlefield. And it’s not just a cool nickname, they actually live up to it. Their success reaches the top, and even the King of Midland brings them into a war that had been dragging on for a hundred years against the Tudor Empire. The Band of the Hawk delivers. They reclaim the fortress of Doldrey, effectively ending the war, and suddenly these former nobodies are being rewarded like heroes. Griffith gets promoted to White Phoenix General, and the entire group is knighted as the White Phoenix Knights. These were people who used to scrape by just to survive, and now they’re standing at the top together. Everything looks like it’s finally paying off. Then Guts decides to leave. After years of fighting for Griffith’s dream, he wants something of his own. That one decision hits Griffith harder than anything else ever did. It’s the first time he loses something that actually matters to him. Guts walking away breaks something inside him that no battlefield ever could. That’s when Griffith spirals. He makes a reckless move and sleeps with Princess Charlotte, which immediately backfires. The King finds out, has him arrested, strips him of everything, and throws him into the Tower of Rebirth. What follows is a full year of nonstop torture. His tongue gets cut out, his tendons are destroyed, and the man who once led armies is reduced to someone who can barely function. While that’s happening, the Band of the Hawk falls apart. They get branded as criminals, hunted down, and forced into hiding. Their numbers drop hard, down to less than a fifth of what they used to be. The only reason they don’t completely collapse is because Casca keeps holding everything together. Still, morale keeps dropping, and it’s clear they’re barely hanging on. Then Guts comes back. The group pulls themselves together and forms a rescue team. They sneak into Wyndham, take huge risks, and somehow manage to break Griffith out of the tower. But the person they save isn’t the same. Griffith is completely broken. He can’t talk, can’t move, and can’t even react properly. The dream that kept all of them going is now just sitting there, empty. They got him back, but the version of him they followed is gone. Overwhelmed and unable to accept what he’s become, Griffith slips away from the group. While alone in a carriage, he activates the Crimson Beherit — a strange, cursed object he’s carried for years — after it gets triggered by his blood. Everything changes instantly. The sky darkens, and a solar eclipse covers the world. In a moment, Griffith, Guts, Casca, and the remaining members of the Band of the Hawk get pulled into a completely different space. It’s a closed-off, nightmarish world where the God Hand are waiting. They offer Griffith a deal. He can reclaim his dream and rise beyond being human. But the cost is every single person who ever followed him — the entire Band of the Hawk. In the most chilling betrayal in the history of anime, Griffith gladly accepts the offer. Each member of the Band of the Hawk gets branded with the Mark of Sacrifice which turns them into offerings. Right after that, the apostles show up. These creatures are massive, twisted, and hungry, and they immediately go after the branded soldiers. What follows is pure chaos. Pippin acts first. When one of the apostles goes after Casca, he jumps in without hesitation, smashing it and buying her time. More enemies close in, and he stays behind to hold them off. He plants himself there and refuses to move, even knowing how it’s going to end. They tear him apart, and it doesn’t stop there. One of the apostles uses what’s left of his body like a puppet, setting it up as bait for Guts. Guts runs toward him thinking he’s still alive, only to see the body ripped apart before he can get there. Judeau stays with Casca the entire time. His body is already wrecked, his arm barely usable, but he keeps going. He keeps protecting her and pushing forward even when he’s clearly at his limit. When he finally collapses, he still tries to keep things light and encourages her to keep moving. In his last moments, he looks at her with regret over everything he never said. Then he’s gone. Guts keeps fighting through all of it. He cuts down apostle after apostle, but they just keep coming. He finds Gaston barely alive, holds onto him for a moment, and then watches him die in one of the worst ways possible as an apostle bursts out from his body. The Band of the Hawk gets wiped out completely and gets treated like an unwilling buffet for a pack of hungry demons. And through all of this, Griffith watches from above, completely calm and untouched. That’s what makes the Eclipse hit so hard. We spend all this time getting attached to these characters, following their lives and rooting for them, only to watch them get brutally devoured just to push one man’s dream forward. In the end, only Guts, Casca, and Rickert – who wasn’t there when the Eclipse happened – are the only survivors. Casca loses sanity after she gets violated by Griffith and Guts carries everything that happened with him, fueling his thirst for revenge for the remainder of the series. For Guts, the Band of the Hawk weren’t just soldiers. They were his family. And after watching everything they went through together, it hits just as hard on anyone following their story.

3. **Portgas D. Ace (One Piece)**

Portgas D. Ace’s story starts before he even takes his first breath — and yeah, it’s already painful. He’s the son of Gol D. Roger, the Pirate King himself, the most wanted man alive at the time. When Roger gets executed, the World Government doesn’t settle for that alone and launches a full hunt for any child he might have left behind, determined to erase his bloodline completely. Before his death, Roger entrusts his unborn son to Marine Vice Admiral Monkey D. Garp, and somehow, Garp agrees to protect the child despite being a Marine. Ace’s mother, Portgas D. Rouge, takes that responsibility to an extreme by carrying him for twenty months just to avoid suspicion. When Ace is finally born on Baterilla, she gives him her surname to hide his identity, and then dies from exhaustion right after. Ace enters the world already carrying the weight of everything that came before him. Garp leaves him in the care of Curly Dadan on Dawn Island, and Ace grows up tough, angry, and constantly on edge. He learns the truth about his father early, and everywhere he goes, people talk about Roger with hatred, often extending that hatred to anyone connected to him. Ace reacts the only way he knows how, throwing fists and shutting people up, even though he personally resents Roger as well. The insults still hit him, and deep down, he struggles with the idea that his existence might be a mistake. That internal conflict shows itself clearly when he asks Garp if he should have even been born, and that question sticks with him as he grows up. Things begin to change when he meets Sabo, a runaway noble surviving in Gray Terminal, and Garp's grandson Monkey D. Luffy, who stubbornly forces his way into Ace’s life. Over time, Ace accepts both of them, and the three form a bond that feels more real than anything tied to blood. They promise to become pirates and chase freedom together, giving Ace something to hold onto. That sense of family takes a heavy hit when Sabo is shot by a World Noble and believed dead. Ace is overwhelmed with anger and grief, but he’s held back from acting on it, and instead of letting it out, he buries it. Eventually, Ace sets sail and forms the Spade Pirates, quickly gaining recognition for his strength. After eating the Mera Mera no Mi, he gains the ability to control fire, earning the name Fire Fist Ace as his reputation grows. His rising threat level puts him directly in the sights of the World Government, but before they can act, Whitebeard steps in and offers him a place on his pirate crew. Ace initially resists and challenges Whitebeard repeatedly, only to lose every time, but eventually accepts his place and becomes the Second Division Commander. Under Whitebeard, he experiences a sense of belonging that slowly helps ease the doubts he’s carried since childhood. That stability doesn’t last long because one of his crewmates — Marshall D. Teach — betrays the crew by killing Thatch and stealing his Devil Fruit. Ace takes it personally and pursues Teach despite being warned not to. Their clash on Banaro Island ends with Ace’s defeat, and Teach hands him over to the World Government in exchange for a Warlord position. The Marines waste no time announcing Ace’s public execution at Marineford, turning it into a massive display meant to send a message and eliminate Roger’s bloodline once and for all. This leads directly into the Summit War of Marineford, where Whitebeard and his entire fleet launch an all-out assault to rescue him, facing off against the full might of the Marines, including Admirals, Warlords, and an army of thousands. At the same time, Luffy storms into the war after breaking into Impel Down, determined to save his brother no matter what. While Ace remains chained on the execution platform, Sengoku reveals to the entire world that Ace is Roger’s son, trying to justify the execution and sway public perception. As the battle escalates, Ace watches the people he cares about risk everything for him, and for the first time, he begins to feel that his life might actually have value. When Luffy finally reaches him and breaks his restraints, Ace is freed, and the two begin to escape as the battlefield shifts in their favor, with Whitebeard creating an opening for them to get away. However, the situation changes when Admiral Akainu provokes Ace by insulting Whitebeard and everything he represents. Ace, unable to ignore it, turns back to confront him, driven by the same emotions that have shaped him since childhood. The fight that follows pits Ace’s fire against Akainu’s magma, and the difference in their abilities quickly becomes clear as Akainu overwhelms him. As the confrontation continues, Akainu shifts his focus to Luffy, who is already exhausted and unable to defend himself, and in that moment, Ace steps in without hesitation. He shields his brother and takes a direct hit as the magma fist pierces through his torso and causes fatal damage. Ace collapses into Luffy’s arms as his life begins to fade, and in his final moments, he doesn’t express regret or anger. Instead, he apologizes and thanks the people who stood by him, finally accepting that he was loved and that his life had meaning. The question that followed him since childhood is finally answered, and he dies at twenty years old with that realization. What makes his death so painful is how much was still ahead of him. Ace had immense potential, not just because he was Roger’s son, but because of his own strength, growth, and the position he held under Whitebeard. He was still developing, still finding his place, and just starting to accept himself. All of that ends in a single moment, right when things start to look better for him. His death feels especially cruel because he was so close to escaping, so close to continuing that growth, and yet everything is cut short by one decision driven by emotion. That mix of unrealized potential and personal growth makes it hard to accept, especially for a character who spent his entire life just trying to figure out if he deserved to exist at all.

4. **Maes Hughes (Fullmetal Alchemist: Brotherhood)**

Maes Hughes starts out as a young cadet in the Amestrian State Military Academy, where he meets Roy Mustang. The two hit it off almost immediately and build a strong friendship that carries through their early military careers. After graduation, they go their separate ways for a while, but fate brings them back together during one of the darkest events in their world — the Ishval Civil War. The conflict starts in 1901 after an Amestrian soldier accidentally kills an Ishvalan child, which spirals into a seven-year war, and by 1908, Führer King Bradley orders the total extermination of the Ishvalans, sending State Alchemists like Roy Mustang and Solf J. Kimblee to wipe them out in days. While they’re used as living weapons, Hughes fights as a regular soldier just trying to survive and get back to his sweetheart Gracia, and after the war, he supports Mustang’s goal of becoming Führer to make sure nothing like that ever happens again. While Mustang comes out of that experience determined to climb the ranks and change the system from the top, Hughes takes a quieter path. He focuses on surviving and building something meaningful afterward. He marries Gracia, settles down in Central City, and becomes a Lieutenant Colonel working in the Investigations Office. Then his daughter Elicia is born, and that’s when Hughes fully unlocks his final form — a proud dad mode with zero cooldown. He shows her pictures to everyone, talks about her nonstop, and turns even the most serious military setting into a slideshow of his child. When the Elric brothers — Edward and Alphonse — arrive in Central, Hughes and Mustang step in to help them. Hughes supports their investigation into the Philosopher’s Stone, sets them up with resources, and basically becomes their go-to adult who actually has things under control. His home becomes a rare safe space where things feel normal for once, and that presence alone makes him stand out in a story full of chaos. At the same time, Hughes is incredibly sharp. While investigating the murders carried out by Scar, he starts noticing patterns that don’t sit right. Riots, uprisings, military campaigns — they’re happening in different locations across Amestris, but when he maps them out, the points connect into something horrifying: a massive transmutation circle spanning the entire country, drawn through years of conflict and bloodshed. Amestris itself is a part of a long-term plan designed to sacrifice its own people. Hughes knows he has to report this immediately, so he heads into the military records office to confirm his findings. That’s where he runs into Lust, one of the Homunculi. She makes it clear that he has learned too much to be allowed to leave and severely injures him with her extended fingernails. Hughes reacts instantly, pulls a concealed knife, and drives it into her forehead. It buys him time, which is all he needs to escape. Bleeding and running out of time, he makes his way toward the phone lines, planning to contact Mustang. On the way, another realization hits him hard — something this massive can’t exist without someone at the very top knowing about it. That means Central Command, and possibly even the Führer, could be involved. Calling from inside the military building suddenly feels like a terrible idea. So Hughes leaves and heads to an external phone booth instead, hoping to get the message out without being intercepted. He reaches the operator and starts trying to connect to Mustang, who’s stationed in East City. While he’s waiting, someone approaches the booth. It’s Lieutenant Maria Ross, or at least it looks like her. Hughes immediately notices something is off. The missing mole under her left eye gives it away, and when he calls it out, the figure adjusts its appearance in real time. That’s when Hughes realizes he’s facing Envy, a shapeshifter who has been manipulating events behind the scenes. Hughes doesn’t hesitate and goes on the offensive, slashing at Envy with his knife. The attack lands, but it doesn’t matter against a Homunculus. During the scuffle, a photo falls from his pocket and lands between them. It’s a picture of his family. Envy uses that moment to transform into Gracia, perfectly mimicking her appearance, and although Hughes fully understands that it is an illusion, the emotional weight of seeing his wife’s face in front of him slows his reaction just enough. That brief hesitation gives Envy the opening needed to fire and shoot Hughes in the chest. The receiver hangs as the call finally connects, and on the other end, Mustang answers, unaware that he’s already too late. Hughes dies before he can say a word. His body is found soon after, and the military holds a funeral where he’s posthumously promoted to Brigadier General. The moment that hits the hardest comes from Elicia, who doesn’t understand why her father is being buried and insists that he still has work to do and needs to come home. This is someone who managed to survive a horrific war, find happiness afterward, and still contribute in meaningful ways, only to be taken out right when his role in the bigger story was becoming critical.

5. **Kyojuro Rengoku (Demon Slayer)**

This is the story of a man who burned so brightly that even his ending felt like fire. Kyojuro Rengoku was born into the Rengoku family, a line of Flame Hashira that goes all the way back to Chojuro Rengoku, and his father Shinjuro Rengoku once held that same title. Everything about his background points to someone who should have had a smooth path to greatness, but his childhood takes a different turn. His mother, Ruka, passes away while he’s still young, leaving behind a message that shapes his entire life. She teaches him that those born with strength have a responsibility to protect others, and Kyojuro takes that idea and never lets go of it. After her death, his father falls apart, becoming distant and bitter, eventually giving up on both his role as a Hashira and his responsibility as a parent. Instead of guiding his sons, he leaves them to figure things out on their own. Kyojuro doesn’t let that stop him. With only a few old Flame Breathing guidebooks to work with, he trains himself and pushes forward without any real mentorship. Somehow, he climbs all the way up to become the Flame Hashira through sheer effort and determination. Along the way, he proves his strength by taking down powerful demons, including Lower Rank Two, Hairo, while also avenging fallen Demon Slayers who couldn’t finish their missions. At the same time, he takes care of his younger brother Senjuro, supporting him even when things don’t come naturally, and he continues to show respect toward a father who has long since checked out. That mix of responsibility and kindness defines him early on and carries into everything he does later. When he finally shows up in the story, his personality hits like a burst of energy. He’s loud, enthusiastic, and fully committed to whatever he’s doing, whether it’s eating food or heading into battle. He constantly praises meals like they’re life-changing experiences, and while it comes off as over-the-top, it never feels fake. That energy is genuine, coming from someone who chooses to stay positive even after growing up in a pretty rough situation. He quickly proves that there’s more behind the attitude. He trains others, supports his fellow Demon Slayers, and shows a strong sense of judgment when it comes to people. He accepts Nezuko despite her being a demon, recognizing her actions rather than just her nature, and he sees potential in Tanjiro almost immediately. That brings him to the Mugen Train mission, where dozens of passengers have gone missing under mysterious circumstances. He takes the lead on the investigation and is soon joined by Tanjiro, Zenitsu, Inosuke, and Nezuko. Once they board the train, they come face-to-face with Enmu, a Lower Rank One demon with the ability to manipulate dreams. Enmu’s power traps people in deep sleep while targeting their spiritual cores, creating a situation where most fighters would be completely helpless. Kyojuro manages to resist through sheer willpower and stays active while others are caught in the illusion. As the situation gets out of control and Enmu merges with the train itself, Kyojuro takes full responsibility for protecting the passengers, moving across the train and eliminating threats while making sure no one gets killed. By the end of that fight, every single passenger survives. Two hundred people make it out alive because he refuses to let even one of them die, even while dealing with a demon that has taken control of an entire moving train. Just as things start to settle, the situation takes a sudden turn when Akaza appears. As Upper Rank Three, Akaza operates on a completely different level, and he immediately targets the group. Kyojuro steps in without hesitation and engages him, using Flame Breathing techniques to hold him back and keep the others safe. The fight that follows pushes both of them to their limits. Akaza constantly acknowledges Kyojuro’s strength and repeatedly tries to recruit him, offering immortality and endless growth as a demon. Kyojuro refuses every time, staying firm in his belief that strength should be used to protect people, not abandon humanity. Despite already being exhausted from protecting the passengers and fighting Enmu, he continues to match Akaza’s attacks and keeps him away from Tanjiro and the others, refusing to let them step into a fight they can’t survive. He holds the line on his own, fully aware of the gap in their conditions but never backing down. As the battle reaches its peak, Kyojuro pushes himself beyond his limits and unleashes his strongest technique, clashing directly with Akaza. His blade cuts through Akaza’s body, but the exchange comes at a cost as Akaza’s attack pierces through him and deals fatal damage. Even in that state, Kyojuro doesn’t give up and uses what strength he has left to hold Akaza in place, trying to keep him there until sunrise. As dawn approaches, Akaza breaks free and escapes to avoid the sunlight, while Kyojuro finally collapses from his injuries. In his final moments, he gives Tanjiro guidance, asking him to pass messages to his family, and expressing complete trust in Nezuko after seeing her protect others. He leaves behind encouragement instead of regret, making sure the next generation has what they need to keep going. As his life fades, he finds peace in knowing he fulfilled the purpose his mother gave him. In the end, his story feels cut short in a way that’s hard to ignore. He had just been introduced, had already shown incredible strength, and had the kind of presence that made it easy to imagine him playing a much bigger role moving forward. But Kyojuro leaves behind more than just a memory. His actions push Tanjiro and the others to grow, his words stay with them, and his example continues to influence how they fight and live. Even so, it’s hard not to feel like there should have been more time to see what he could have become if he had made it past that battle.

6. **Nanami Kento (Jujutsu Kaisen)**

Kento Nanami enters the story as someone who feels completely out of place in a world full of loud, overwhelming talent. Back in his days at Tokyo Jujutsu High, he studied alongside names like Satoru Gojo and Suguru Geto, both of whom are already shaping the future of jujutsu just by existing. Nanami, on the other hand, keeps things grounded. He knows what he can do, knows what he can’t, and never pretends otherwise. During that time, the person closest to him is Yu Haibara. They take on missions together, build that quiet kind of trust, and for a while, things feel stable. Then one mission goes wrong. Haibara dies, and Nanami is left standing there after the fact, watching his friend get reduced to a report. After graduating, Nanami walks away from the jujutsu world without making a big deal out of it. He puts on a suit, becomes a corporate salaryman, and spends his days buried in work that drains the life out of him. And somehow, he prefers it. That kind of life doesn’t throw you into situations where people die next to you. It doesn’t ask you to accept that losing someone is part of the job. But even with all that distance, he never fully disconnects. The world keeps getting worse, curses keep hurting people, and deep down, he knows ignoring it doesn’t make it go away. So after four years, he comes back. When he reappears, he carries himself like a man treating sorcery as a job rather than a calling. He sets boundaries. He tracks his hours. He even places a binding vow on himself that limits his power during work hours, only to release more strength once he goes into overtime. He checks his watch in the middle of fights and fully expects things to wrap up on schedule. It sounds ridiculous at first, but it fits him perfectly. His fighting style reflects that same mindset. His Ratio Technique targets a precise weak point, dividing anything into a 7:3 ratio and striking exactly where it matters most. Eventually, he gets assigned to mentor Yuji Itadori, which is the last thing he wants. The idea of guiding another young sorcerer feels like setting them up for the same fate Haibara met. Still, he takes the role seriously, and over time, Yuji earns his respect through persistence and sheer determination. That bond gets tested during their encounter with Mahito, a cursed spirit who manipulates souls directly. Mahito lands a hit on Nanami that should have ended him instantly. But Nanami pushes through it by shielding his soul with high-level cursed energy. Then the Shibuya Incident hits, and everything falls apart at once. The entire plan revolves around sealing Gojo, and once that happens, the balance of power disappears instantly. But Nanami doesn’t panic. He takes down threats like Haruta Shigemo without wasting time, then joins forces with Naobito Zenin and Maki Zenin to fight Dagon who traps them into his domain. Nanami and Maki are getting overwhelmed by Dagon’s nonstop guaranteed-hit shikigami until Megumi jumps in and overlaps his incomplete domain with Dagon’s, canceling the sure-hit effect and giving them a chance to breathe and escape through an opening he creates. Right as they’re getting out, the revived Toji barges in from outside and goes straight for Dagon, buying them even more time to safely get out of the domain. But the real threat happens right after that, when Jogo shows up and burns through them with overwhelming force. Nanami takes the hit directly and ends up severely injured, his body already pushed far beyond its limits. And still, he keeps moving. At that point, there’s no real strategy left. He’s running on instinct and responsibility, pushing deeper into Shibuya while dealing with wave after wave of transfigured humans. Somewhere in the middle of all that, his thoughts drift. He imagines Kuantan, Malaysia, the place he wanted to retire to. Cold cocktails. A quiet beach, a slower life, something far removed from all of this. It’s a brief moment, but it says everything about what he gave up. Then he’s back in reality, still injured, still moving, still doing what needs to be done. Eventually, he runs into Mahito and realizes he doesn’t stand a chance this time. Then Yuji arrives. There’s a moment where everything slows down just enough for him to process what’s happening. Then he sees a vision of Haibara, pointing toward Yuji, like everything connects in that instant. Nanami smiles, speaks his last words simply and directly, "You take it from here." Belaf, one of the Three Sages, had recreated her using portions of his own body, turning her into an immortal source of sustenance. She was essentially the same Mitty, down to the soul of that moment, but trapped in a new and horrifying form. During a later confrontation, Belaf extended his tendrils to feed from this Mitty, and Nanachi had to accept that this second chance didn’t mean she could stay forever. They realized that Mitty’s presence was never meant to remain permanently. Nanachi chose to set her free for real this time, guiding her toward the barrier where all beings created in that village cease to exist. As Mitty faded away, Nanachi reflected on everything they had learned, promising with teary eyes that they would meet again. Mitty’s story shows a character whose heart and spirit remain unbroken even when her body is destroyed. She met her demise twice, once physically and once in her replicated form, and each time it’s heartbreaking because she gives so much of herself and never asks for anything in return.
