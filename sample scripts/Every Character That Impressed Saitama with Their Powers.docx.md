**Every Character That Impressed Saitama with Their Powers**

* **Genos**

Saitama almost never gets impressed by anyone’s powers, and that’s pretty understandable when he’s so far ahead of everyone else that most fights barely register for him. He’s used to people being shocked by what he can do, not the other way around. When you’re literally ending threats with a single punch on a regular day, it takes something unusual to get even a small reaction out of you. Still, there are a few moments in *One Punch Man* where Saitama shows real interest in someone’s strength, speed, skill, or overall performance. These moments don’t happen often, but they stand out because they show what actually catches his attention in a world full of heroes, monsters, and over-the-top abilities. And honestly, the series has a ton of impressive characters, but only a select group has ever managed to make Saitama pause, react, or mentally take note of what they’re doing. Genos is the first character who genuinely impresses Saitama, and honestly, it makes sense. He’s Saitama’s self-proclaimed disciple, but even before that, he already had a life filled with tragedy, upgrades, and nonstop battles. Genos starts out as a normal 15-year-old kid living a peaceful life with his family. Everything changes when a rampaging cyborg crashes into his town and wipes out everything in sight. The attack destroys the area, kills his family, and leaves Genos barely alive. Dr. Kuseno happens to pass through the ruined town while tracking the same cyborg and finds Genos at the edge of death. After hearing what happened, Genos asks him for the power to take revenge, and Dr. Kuseno decides to help him. From that point on, Genos becomes a cyborg built for combat, dedicating himself to defeating evil and eventually hunting down the Mad Cyborg responsible for his village’s destruction. For four years, Genos travels from place to place, taking down monsters and shady groups, hoping he’ll eventually find the one target he wants the most. Despite all his battles, he still finds no trace of the cyborg he’s searching for, but he never slows down. This is the version of Genos we meet when he first enters the series. His actual debut in the series is when he follows a trail of drained animal corpses until he reaches Z-City, where the attacks continue. In the middle of all this, Mosquito Girl is rampaging through the area. She notices that some of her mosquitoes are dying, and when she looks down, she sees a figure completely covered in bugs that suddenly get vaporized. Genos steps out with a focused expression and prepares to take Mosquito Girl down. Their initial exchange is brutal. Genos fires an incineration blast at her, but she dodges with ease, rushes him, and tears off his arm. He doesn’t even flinch and immediately counters by taking her legs. Even with the damage he takes, he stays locked in and ready to finish the fight. Mosquito Girl retreats long enough to summon every mosquito in the area, gathering an enormous swarm to power up. Genos recognizes the danger and starts charging another blast. Right at that moment, Saitama runs into the street. Genos tries to warn him, but Mosquito Girl’s swarm collapses onto both of them. Genos unleashes a massive incineration wave that wipes out the entire swarm and covers a huge portion of the city in flames. For a moment, he worries and assumes he burned the bald civilian in the process, but when the smoke clears, Saitama is completely fine aside from his clothes being scorched off. And this is the first real moment Saitama feels impressed by the young cyborg. Unknown to Genos, Saitama had been struggling earlier with a single mosquito that refused to die no matter how hard he tried. That moment also connects to a long-running gag in the series that Saitama has a ridiculous weakness when it comes to mosquitoes.  Meanwhile, he suddenly meets a cyborg who wipes out an entire city-wide swarm in seconds. Saitama sees the size of the blast, sees how cleanly Genos handles the threat, and acknowledges him with clear praise \- saying he's awesome and he's actually saved him. This is the first time in the entire story someone’s powers make Saitama react in a positive way. But that isn’t the last time Genos grabs Saitama’s attention. Later in Season 2, Genos gets another moment where his growth becomes obvious. It happens when he senses someone approaching their apartment. He reacts instantly, heads out to check, and finds Speed-o’-Sound Sonic waiting for a rematch with Saitama. Genos doesn’t want to bother his master, who’s inside dealing with Fubuki, so he decides to handle Sonic himself. Their fight kicks off fast. Sonic’s speed gives him an early advantage, but Genos matches him with durability and precision. During the exchange, Genos tries to land a strike on Sonic, but the impact incidentally hits Fubuki, who ended up in the middle of their clash while arguing with Saitama. Genos immediately tells Saitama what happened, apologizing for the mess, and then goes right back to fighting Sonic. Sonic pushes harder and throws exploding shurikens at Genos, but Genos tanks the explosions without issue. He does lose some mobility from the blast, but he stays composed and decides to increase his output. He fires up his upgrades and boosts his speed to close the gap between him and Sonic. This is where the improvement shows clearly. Genos accelerates so fast that Sonic barely avoids him, and their movements reach a level where Fubuki can no longer track either of them. The entire fight turns into bursts of speed, shockwaves, and rapid-fire movement. Meanwhile, Saitama watches from the side and reacts with a visible hint of surprise. He notes how Genos has gotten faster since their last spar. Even though Saitama has witnessed way higher levels of speed from powerful enemies before, he still acknowledges Genos’s improvement. The thing that impresses him here isn’t raw speed on its own but the huge leap Genos makes after getting upgraded. Genos constantly pushes himself, constantly upgrades his body, and constantly tries to reach a higher level. Saitama may not treat him like a student in the usual sense, but it’s clear he pays attention to how much Genos improves. Genos continues to evolve after this as he receives more advanced upgrades from Dr. Kuseno. Each upgrade pushes him further, whether it’s increasing his incineration output, improving his acceleration, or enhancing his combat stability. He gets torn apart in battles often, but every rebuild adds something new. It’s a cycle that reflects his determination more than anything else. These definitely won’t be the last times Genos impresses him. As the manga continues and Genos keeps upgrading his systems, the gap between him and Saitama won’t close, but the reactions he earns could become even more meaningful. Maybe one day he’ll get the clear, straightforward praise he’s always wanted from the person he respects the most.

* **Mumen Rider**

The next character to genuinely impress Saitama is an absolute fan favorite, and his moment is still one of the most memorable scenes in the entire series. We’re talking about Mumen Rider. Unlike Genos, he doesn’t wow Saitama with destructive firepower or insane upgrades. What grabs Saitama’s attention is something completely different: pure determination and a level of courage that doesn’t waver no matter how hopeless the situation gets. Before diving into the big moment, it’s worth going over what we actually know about Mumen Rider as a fighter. The C-class hero rarely gets to show much combat ability because he’s usually overwhelmed before he can deal any real damage, but we still see enough to get an idea of his style. His outfit suggests he focuses on close-quarters combat and speed, and he makes good use of his bike, which he named “Justice.” He often uses it to rush toward danger or even to throw it as a last-ditch attack when the situation calls for it. What really defines him, though, is his personality. He is one of the bravest and most principled heroes in the Hero Association. He refuses to ignore any crime, no matter how minor, and he refuses to back down from any enemy, even when he has absolutely no chance of winning. He puts his life on the line without hesitation if it means protecting civilians. That kind of loyalty and honesty makes him surprisingly beloved by the public, especially considering how unusual it is for a lower-ranked hero like him to earn that level of adoration. His biggest moment comes during the Deep Sea King’s attack on J-City. The Sea King announces his plan to wipe out humanity and wreaks havoc across the city. When he locates the shelter full of civilians, several heroes show up to protect everyone, but they’re all taken out almost instantly. Even Genos, who had earned an S-class ranking at that point, gets overwhelmed and nearly destroyed. Right when the Deep Sea King is about to finish off the badly damaged Genos, Mumen Rider arrives. Without hesitation, he throws his bike at the Sea King’s back. The attack does no physical damage, but it successfully draws the monster’s attention away from Genos and probably saves him from being crushed right there. From that point on, Mumen Rider goes all in. He challenges the Deep Sea King despite being completely outmatched. The Sea King tosses him aside like nothing and seems ready to move on, but Mumen Rider refuses to stay down. He gets back up and charges again, landing another desperate strike even though it barely slows the monster. The Deep Sea King hits him over and over, but Mumen Rider keeps standing up. He admits he’s too weak to reach B-Class and that nobody expects him to win, yet he still refuses to run. The civilians watching from the shelter start cheering for him, and he pushes himself even harder, launching one last attack with everything he has. The Sea King ends it with a single punch that sends him flying, but before he hits the ground, Saitama catches him. This moment is when Saitama acknowledges him. He tells Mumen Rider that he did fine and that it was a nice fight. The fight itself is completely one-sided, so on paper it doesn’t look like anything close to a “nice fight.” But Saitama sees what actually matters. He sees someone who keeps fighting even when defeat is guaranteed. He sees a hero who prioritizes protecting others even when the cost is his own life. That is the kind of strength Saitama respects. Mumen Rider not only impresses Saitama in that moment, but he also wins over the viewers. His determination becomes one of the defining emotional moments of the series. Without his effort to buy time and distract the Deep Sea King, more civilians would have died, and Genos might not have survived long enough for Saitama to arrive. That scene also reveals something important about Saitama. Even though he doesn’t relate to most heroes in terms of power, he pays attention to their resolve. He may not react to flashy abilities or massive attacks, but he respects genuine effort and the spirit behind it. Mumen Rider embodies that completely, and that’s why he earns one of the most sincere moments of acknowledgment from Saitama in the entire story.

* **Boros**

When it comes to Saitama actually being impressed by a villain’s powers, Boros is basically the gold standard. Most enemies don’t last long enough for Saitama to form an opinion. They rush him, yell some dramatic threat, and then explode into dust after one punch. So the idea of Saitama not only acknowledging an opponent but doing it multiple times is something that practically never happens. Boros is the first villain to pull that off, and his whole arc is built around finally giving Saitama the closest thing he’s wanted for years: a real fight. Boros is the leader of the Dark Matter Thieves, a group of aliens that goes around conquering worlds and wiping out civilizations. His destructive lifestyle comes from his own overwhelming strength. He’s been so powerful for so long that he grows numb to everything. Nothing excites him, nothing challenges him, and nothing even forces him to try. He ends up in the same place emotionally that Saitama is in: bored out of his mind and desperate for an opponent who can make him feel alive again. That’s when a seer on his planet gives him a prophecy — somewhere out there, on a faraway world, a being exists who can match him. That one line is enough to push Boros into a twenty-year journey across the galaxy. He wipes out civilizations, crosses endless star systems, and follows any clue that might get him closer to this opponent. This search becomes his entire purpose. It’s the only thing keeping him going. Eventually, his travels lead him to Earth. And the moment his ship appears above A-City, the Dark Matter Thieves go straight into full invasion mode. They bomb the entire city with a barrage strong enough to destroy 99.8% of it instantly. Entire districts get erased in seconds. The only structure still standing afterward is the Hero Association headquarters — and Saitama happens to be inside that building. As the heroes outside struggle with Boros’s generals and the overwhelming alien forces, Saitama decides he’s had enough and casually jumps onto the alien ship by punching a hole through it. While the other heroes fight tooth and nail just to survive, Saitama walks through the corridors like he’s looking for the bathroom. He bumps into two of Boros’s top subordinates and defeats both of them instantly. Boros feels their deaths through the ship’s monitoring system and is shocked that someone managed to kill them so easily. When Saitama finally reaches the throne room, he finds Boros waiting for him. Boros immediately explains his prophecy and why he came to Earth. He traveled for decades just to find this opponent, and he believes Saitama is the one he’s been looking for. He tries to hype up the encounter by building tension and pushing Saitama into a dramatic showdown. But Saitama listens for about five seconds before punching him into a pillar and calling him out for destroying A-City over something that stupid. This is where things take a turn. Boros survives the hit. His armor cracks apart, and for the first time, Saitama sees someone actually stand back up after receiving a punch that would’ve obliterated almost anyone else. Boros explains that the armor wasn’t decorative — it was a limiter designed to contain his overflowing strength. Now that it’s gone, he unleashes his true form, and the battle begins for real. Their fight immediately breaks away from the usual Saitama routine. Boros moves so fast that he becomes a blur. His energy output skyrockets and he launches Saitama across the throne room while he regenerates from injuries that would kill normal beings instantly. Saitama trades blows with him without ending the fight in one hit. Boros is thrilled because Saitama keeps up with him. And Saitama, seeing Boros’s stamina, speed, and regeneration, acknowledges him for the first time by saying he’s “pretty strong too.” As the fight escalates, Boros pushes his body even further. He activates Meteoric Burst, a transformation that compresses his energy and skyrockets his speed and power to extreme levels. His movement becomes almost impossible to track, and he smashes Saitama through the ship, takes him into the sky, and hits him with a knee strike powerful enough to launch him all the way to the Moon. That moment is huge for two reasons. First, Boros becomes the first character in the story to physically send Saitama off-planet. Second, it leads to Saitama casually jumping back from the Moon in a single leap, landing straight onto the alien ship without any hesitation. Boros gets even more fired up when he sees Saitama return instantly. He charges at him and throws everything he has into a nonstop flurry of attacks. Saitama stays still at first, letting the blows land without any real damage. Then he responds with a single punch that sends Boros flying. He follows up with his Consecutive Normal Punches, tearing Boros’s body apart in a rapid chain of hits. Even after being shredded, Boros stitches himself back together with his regeneration. Knowing he has reached his limit, Boros prepares his strongest move: Collapsing Star Roaring Cannon. It’s a massive energy beam with enough force to wipe out the entire planet. He fires it directly at Saitama with the intention of ending the battle and taking Earth with them. Saitama replies with his Serious Punch, not to destroy Boros, but simply to counter the attack. The shockwave alone cancels out the energy beam and overwhelms Boros’s body. His regeneration can’t keep up with the damage anymore. He collapses, defeated and unable to recover. Even in that state, he stays conscious long enough to reflect on the battle. Saitama praises him again, saying he’s really strong — a second moment of genuine acknowledgment. But Boros, sensing Saitama’s true ability, tells him he’s lying. He knows Saitama held back and that the prophecy he chased for decades wasn’t accurate after all. His “equal” wasn’t equal at all. As Boros dies, Saitama leaves the ship and returns to the ruins of A-City. Genos meets him outside and asks if he was the one who defeated the alien leader. Saitama confirms it and describes Boros as “really strong” and possibly the strongest opponent he’s ever fought — marking the third time Saitama praises him. This makes Boros the only character in the entire series so far to receive consistent, repeated acknowledgment from Saitama. He pushed Saitama harder than anyone else, lasted longer than any previous enemy, and left a genuine impact that no other villain up to that point could match.

* **Child Emperor**

The Monster Association Arc is packed with some of the wildest moments in the entire series, and it’s not just because of the constant fighting. This arc finally gives everyone a proper look at what the S-class heroes can actually do when they stop holding back. Fans waited years to see these heroes go all out, and the story delivers exactly what people hoped for. Among all the battles in this arc, one of the most surprising and genuinely impressive ones is the fight between Child Emperor and Phoenix Man. And this is also one of the few times where Saitama actually pays attention and recognizes someone else’s effort and strength. Child Emperor’s main role during the Monster Association raid is basically running the entire operation while still jumping into danger when needed. He’s the one who pinpoints the base, charts the paths through it, sets up communication, and designs the whole infiltration plan for the S-Class heroes. And even with all that on his plate, he still handles some of the most important on-the-ground tasks, using his gadgets to fight when necessary while searching for the kidnapped kid Waganma. While navigating through the maze-like base, he runs into Phoenix Man. At this point, Phoenix Man is still in his earlier form, and Child Emperor takes him down using an assortment of weapons he keeps hidden inside his backpack. Each one is engineered specifically for offense, mobility, defense, or utility, and the way he takes down Phoenix Man shows how skilled he is at using them under pressure. Once he defeats Phoenix Man, Child Emperor gets to Waganma’s jail cell and frees him. They don’t get much breathing room because the headquarters is crawling with monsters. Child Emperor defeats more enemies as they escape and starts guiding Waganma out of the base. But their situation changes when they run straight into Phoenix Man’s dead body. At first, it looks harmless, but Phoenix Man unexpectedly resurrects thanks to his costume. He also reveals something important: Gyoro Gyoro once told him the real secret behind a monster’s sudden growth. A monster’s evolution depends on surviving near-death experiences. Every time Phoenix Man brushes close to death, he becomes stronger, and that revelation makes the situation much more dangerous. Child Emperor immediately activates his energy shield, protecting both himself and Waganma. Phoenix Man launches his devastating Phoenix Explosion Beak Attack, engulfing himself in flames and breaking through Child Emperor's barrier, slamming the young hero into a wall. In response, Child Emperor activates his ultimate weapon — a massive mech called Brave Giant that erupts from underground via drilling mechanisms, assembling from multiple components while destroying nearby monsters. Despite facing this imposing machine, Phoenix Man remains unfazed and dismissive. He shifts tactics by pulling Child Emperor into Phoenix Space, a mental dimension where costume-wearing monsters exist. There, he attempts to recruit the hero, arguing he possesses qualities suitable for becoming a powerful monster. Child Emperor rejects this offer and escapes, using the exit momentum to slam Phoenix Man down. The battle intensifies as Phoenix Man transforms through multiple modes. In Prominence Hawk Mode, his flames grow stronger, knocking Brave Giant through pillars. Switching to Phoenix Fire Falcon Mode grants him enhanced speed, allowing rapid strikes against the mech before Child Emperor can react properly. Child Emperor deploys an energy whip technique called 20,000 Fold: Rope Skip Grinder, spinning it at incredible speeds to catch Phoenix Man and hurl him into a pillar. Their destructive clash continues throughout the base, demolishing walls and structures. When Phoenix Man fires Phoenix Flares, Brave Giant blocks them, but Phoenix Man becomes trapped in electromagnetic webbing Child Emperor had strategically placed earlier. Child Emperor seizes this opportunity to attack, but Phoenix Man blocks and counters with Full Power: 10,000 Degree Celsius Prominence — flames hot enough to melt straight through Brave Giant's armored fist. Child Emperor quickly shifts tactics again. After detecting a water source below, he removes the mech’s output limiters and sends both himself and Phoenix Man crashing downward. The impact triggers a massive surge of water that blasts through the area and ends up dragging Saitama along with it. Once they’re underwater, Child Emperor tries to help Saitama, thinking he’s just an ordinary person who got caught in the chaos. But the moment is interrupted when Phoenix Man returns with another transformation: Phoenix Penguin Mode. This new form lets him manipulate ice. He launches his Phoenix Antarctic Iceberg Attack, freezing the area and smashing Child Emperor through a floor. This sends the hero down to the grave of the Subterranean People, adding another layer of danger to the environment. Child Emperor tells Waganma to stay inside the barrier and includes Saitama in it too, still assuming Saitama needs protection. Meanwhile, Phoenix Man fires another energy blast at the child hero. Child Emperor tries to respond, but Phoenix Man insists it’s pointless and drags him back into Phoenix Space once more. This second trip into Phoenix Space hits Child Emperor harder than the first one. Phoenix Man tries again to convince him to abandon being a hero and become a monster, promising him a level of acknowledgment and “glory” that heroes supposedly can’t achieve. For a moment, Child Emperor actually wavers. He’s mentally pushed to the edge when Phoenix Man reveals the dark side of the Hero Association he's working with. The possibility of gaining more power tempts him, and Phoenix Man’s persistence almost gets through. But then Saitama appears inside Phoenix Space. He interrupts Phoenix Man’s manipulation by stating a simple truth: becoming a monster doesn’t provide the kind of fulfillment Phoenix Man claims it does. Right after that, Zombieman contacts Child Emperor through the communicator. He confirms that the weaknesses Child Emperor identified in the enemy robots were accurate. He also thanks Child Emperor for his contribution and expresses that he’s glad the child hero is fighting alongside them. This support hits Child Emperor emotionally, bringing him to tears as he regains his resolve. Child Emperor turns back to Phoenix Man and rejects the offer. Phoenix Man tries to force the transformation by sliding a Monster Cell through the cracks in his armor. Child Emperor spits it back out and clarifies that being a hero isn’t about wearing a suit or transforming. It’s something that comes from the inside. That’s the moment where Saitama silently starts giving Child Emperor his full attention. He doesn’t step in or take over the fight. He lets Child Emperor handle it because the kid clearly wants to finish what he started. For his final attack, Child Emperor activates Gigavolt Smash. The mech channels an enormous surge of electricity, pushing itself past its limits. Brave Giant is destroyed in the process, and Child Emperor ends up flying toward Phoenix Man. Phoenix Man tries to kill him right there, but Saitama steps in and blocks the attack. Waganma sees Child Emperor’s suit fall apart and panics, thinking the hero is done for, but Saitama assures him that Child Emperor will succeed because he’s strong on the inside. Coming from Saitama, that statement says a lot because he rarely comments on anyone’s strength unless he genuinely means it. Child Emperor then reveals the trick that decides the fight. Earlier, during the scramble, he planted a Tickle-Tickle Bug between Phoenix Man’s face and beak. The bug irritates Phoenix Man, but trying to remove it requires taking off the suit, and Phoenix Man refuses to do that. The bug continues to provoke a reaction until Phoenix Man’s body begins to glow uncontrollably. He explodes, and once the smoke clears, he reverts into Monster Chick Man, a tiny chick-like monster with almost no power. With that, the battle ends. Child Emperor stands victorious despite everything he went through. Saitama catches him before he collapses, supports him physically, and praises him for his courage and determination. Calling him a great hero is another rare moment of acknowledgment from Saitama, and it shows exactly how much he respects what Child Emperor accomplished.

* **Flashy Flash**

Another S-class hero who actually gets a nod from Saitama in this arc is Flashy Flash. Flash is already a fan-favorite for obvious reasons, but in the Monster Association arc, he gets even more attention thanks to how ridiculous his speed and technique are. He comes from the Ninja Village, which is pretty much a nightmare factory disguised as a training school. Kids there are molded into emotionless weapons, and Flash grows up in that system until he becomes the top ninja of his graduating class — the same batch Sonic came from. By age 16, he decides enough is enough. He wipes out the entire village and shuts down its operations, sparing only Sonic. Flash basically ends the Village’s corruption single-handedly, which already says a lot about his skill. Fast forward to the Monster Association Raid. Flash starts off handling things on the surface, cutting through monsters like it’s just warm-up training. After clearing out the area, he dives straight into the underground base by himself. This is where he gets his first big showcase. He eliminates several monsters at ridiculous speed before running into two former Village ninjas: Hellfire Flame and Gale Wind. Both of them turn into their monster forms thinking they have an edge now. Flash doesn’t waste a second. He uses Flash Slash and slices both of them in half so fast that neither one understands what went wrong. After that, Flash heads deeper into the base and takes out his Locator to check where he is — only to realize he lost it in the previous fight. While he’s figuring things out, he bumps into Saitama and mistakes him for an enemy. He attacks Saitama with his usual speed, expecting to end things instantly. But Saitama dodges cleanly and even counters without batting an eye. Flash tries again, and Saitama dodges again. This immediately throws Flash off because he knows exactly how fast he is. If someone can avoid his attacks twice in a row without effort, that’s not normal. Flash eventually recognizes Saitama as the B-Class hero he met during the Alien Conqueror Arc. Still, he tries to shake him off because he assumes Saitama will just slow him down. Instead, as Flash speeds off to ditch him, he realizes Saitama is casually keeping up. The two of them end up running laps around the Monster Association base because neither has any sense of direction. Flash suggests interrogating monsters for directions, while Saitama keeps telling him the easiest solution is just to call the other heroes. Flash shoots that down immediately, saying he doesn’t need help and the other heroes are handling things just fine. While wandering through the tunnels, they find Manako, a small monster who’s terrified of them but desperate enough to offer help. Flash and Saitama let her lead the way to Orochi, and the trio ride a minecart deeper into the base. Manako tries to derail the cart at a broken section of the track because she thinks she can kill both heroes and herself in the process. But when she actually sees how high the drop is, she panics and abandons that plan immediately. As they move through another area, they enter an excavation site filled with massive mining-type monsters. They barely get a chance to attack because Saitama and Flash wipe them out instantly. Flash notices Saitama fighting at a speed that feels familiar — maybe even close to what he’s used to. He’s definitely impressed but refuses to admit it out loud since he has way too much pride for that. Saitama, on the other hand, doesn’t hide what he thinks. He casually mentions that Flash “is not bad” and “kinda fast,” which, coming from Saitama, is honestly high praise. Flash obviously doesn’t appreciate the wording at all, and with his personality, there’s no chance he’s letting that slide without feeling a little annoyed. But despite that, the moment is pretty important. The fact that he openly acknowledges Flash’s speed gives Flash a rare spot among the characters who actually impress him.

* **Garou**

The Monster Association arc reaches its climactic peak when Saitama finally clashes with Garou, who’s already deep into his monsterization. At this point, none of the Monster Association baddies have actually challenged Saitama — not even Orochi, the Monster King himself, who got absolutely wrecked with just one punch. Similar to the whole Boros situation, some fans might argue that Garou's fight didn't actually pose a real threat to Saitama. But during their entire showdown, Saitama definitely acknowledged how skilled Garou was and how much the Hero Hunter had leveled up his game. By this stage, Garou’s body is mutating nonstop, swelling with new muscle, spikes, and raw power. The two take their fight into the sky, miles above the ground, trading hits with enough force to shred the clouds around them. Garou gets solid blows in, but Saitama barely reacts. Meanwhile, Garou keeps regenerating every injury Saitama dishes out, pushing his transformation even further. Garou ramps things up with his God Slayer Instant Attack, unleashing a rapid flurry of punches and sharp hand strikes so fast they blur together. He drives Saitama into the ground with enough force to bury him. Saitama tunnels back up like nothing happened, which frustrates Garou even more. Garou follows up with God Slayer Ascending Attack, landing a heavy punch to Saitama’s gut, then smashing a knee into the same spot and launching him straight into several buildings with a powerful kick. Before the dust even settles, Garou transitions into Great Fa Jin, chasing Saitama through collapsing structures and blowing apart everything in their path. When the debris clears, Saitama steps out completely unharmed. Garou, now running on a mix of pride and rage, pushes his transformation further. His form becomes even more monstrous, sprouting wings, long horns, and a more demonic silhouette. As Garou goes wild with non-stop attacks, Saitama casually points out that Garou has improved “a little.” It’s a tiny compliment, but coming from Saitama, it actually means something. Garou doesn’t take it well at all. The word “little” hits his ego like a hammer, and he becomes even more aggressive. Garou punches Saitama with enough strength to send him flying toward a volcano that’s about to erupt. He follows through immediately, striking both palms into Saitama. The force behind it completely shatters the mountain behind them. Once again, the attack does absolutely nothing to Saitama. The volcano, on the other hand, is gone — and the destruction ends up preventing a nearby city from being swallowed by lava. Saitama points out that Garou has strong hero instincts, comparing them to King’s. This is Saitama genuinely praising Garou’s tendency to save people even when he claims he wants to embody pure evil. Garou hates hearing it, especially since he detests heroes and their hypocritical ideals. So even though Saitama meant it as a compliment, Garou totally took it the wrong way and saw it as an insult. That comment also shows how much Saitama genuinely respects King as a hero, even though he knows the truth behind King’s reputation. The fight shifts again when Garou sprouts four arms, overwhelming Saitama with relentless attacks, though Saitama effortlessly dodges one-handed. Garou grabs him, transforms into a jet-like form, and unleashes Extreme Fa Jin, driving Saitama deep into Earth and shaking the planet's core. Saitama emerges unscathed, countering with Consecutive Normal Punches that shatter Garou's monstrous shell, briefly revealing his human form underneath. Garou realizes he’s losing and anguishes over how far he’s fallen short. Then everything changes. He hears a voice. When he responds, he encounters “God,” and after that contact, he returns in a completely transformed state: Awakened Garou, also called Cosmic Fear Mode. His entire body looks like a silhouette filled with constellations, galaxies, and cosmic radiation. His power spikes far beyond anything he had before. He unveils his new martial arts style, All Life Eradication Fist, and immediately launches nuclear-level attacks. His Nuclear Fission strikes erupt like mushroom clouds across the battlefield. He copies Saitama’s fighting style through Mode: Saitama, mimicking Saitama’s Consecutive Normal Punches and actually matching him hit for hit. It’s the closest anyone has ever come to keeping pace with Saitama. Their exchange escalates quickly. Garou charges a Gamma Ray Burst that blasts Saitama so hard he’s launched away. When Saitama comes back and sees Genos’s destroyed core, the mood shifts. Both fighters throw Serious Punches. When they collide, the force is so overwhelming that Blast and a whole group of other dimensional fighters are forced to redirect the shockwave into space, where it tears open the cosmos. After the explosion, Saitama and Garou are launched all the way to Io, one of Jupiter’s moons. They crash-land and immediately start fighting again. Garou unleashes a ridiculously complex series of techniques: Roaring Aura Sky Ripping Fist, Water Stream Rock Smashing Fist, Exploding Heart Release Fist, Whirlwind Iron Cutting Fist, and Nuclear Fission Gravity Knuckle. Even with all that variety and absurd destructive output, Saitama walks through it unharmed. He retaliates with Serious Table Flip, tearing apart the moon’s surface and flipping massive chunks of terrain into the air. Then he follows up with Omnidirectional Serious Punch, bombarding Garou from every angle with overwhelming force. Throughout the battle, Garou keeps activating Mode: Saitama to copy Saitama’s power. The problem is that Saitama’s strength keeps increasing faster than Garou’s ability to replicate it. The more Garou copies, the further behind he gets. When Saitama sneezes hard enough to blast away a portion of Jupiter’s atmosphere, Garou realizes how outmatched he really is. He tries one more Nuclear Fission attack to escape. Saitama follows him back to Earth instantly and shatters Garou’s cosmic form with a final blow. Defeated, Garou finally asks why Saitama won’t kill him. Saitama answers plainly: he’s a hero, and Tareo’s final request was for him to stop Garou without killing him. When Garou hears the phrase “final request,” he looks over and sees Tareo’s lifeless body. The radiation from Garou’s attacks killed him. He fully understands that he really became the Ominous Future. When Saitama asks what Garou wants to do next, Garou makes one final request. He asks Saitama to copy his martial arts technique. He believes Saitama can fully master it, even though Garou himself couldn’t perfect it despite wielding power from “God.” Saitama agrees. As Garou begins transmitting the technique to him, God intervenes. Garou’s borrowed power and life force are forcibly stripped away, and his body starts breaking down into salt. Garou tries to hold on long enough for Saitama to finish copying the technique. He then crumbles completely and dies in front of Saitama. Saitama is then thrown back in time where he witnesses every moment of their battle in reverse. As he watches Garou’s martial arts techniques in rewind, he can’t help but admire them. He even calls them “awesome” as he marvels at the sight. When he reaches the moment where Garou declares himself the Ominous Future to the other heroes, Saitama punches straight through that moment, returning to the present timeline. The present Garou looks up with no idea what’s coming. Saitama arrives instantly and hits him with a decisive punch, sending him crashing to the ground. The impact cracks his face and forces the divine power inside him to pour out. Garou loses the last remnants of God’s influence as the energy escapes from the wound. And with that, Garou’s long, chaotic path hits its conclusion. Even after everything, Saitama’s admiration for Garou’s skills is obvious. For someone like Saitama, that means Garou left a real impression.

* **Tatsumaki**

Tatsumaki’s hype starts way before she ever interacts with Saitama. In the anime, she straight-up calls down a meteor and turns the Ancient King into cosmic dust. In the manga, it’s technically a huge boulder instead of a meteor, but either way, the scene does its job. It pushes her reputation to the top of the S-Class food chain and makes it crystal clear why everyone treats her like an untouchable goddess of destruction. Then comes the Monster Association arc, where Tatsumaki basically carries half the war on her back. She crushes the Association’s base, takes down demon-class monsters like she’s speed-clearing a dungeon, and does it all while dragging injuries, stress, and a massive workload. It’s no surprise that a lot of readers walked away calling her the MVP of the whole arc. But interestingly, the moment she actually impresses Saitama happens after all of that. It goes down during the Psychic Sisters arc, where Tatsumaki ends up dealing again with Tsukuyomi — the same psychic research group that experimented on her when she was a kid. When Tsukuyomi operatives arrive at the Hero Association HQ to extract the captured Psykos, both Tatsumaki and Fubuki jump in to stop them. Things spiral fast once they discover someone in Fubuki’s faction is a traitor planted by Tsukuyomi. To make it worse, that spy secretly slipped a deadly poison capsule into Fubuki’s supplements. He threatens to detonate it inside her if Tatsumaki gets any closer. Tatsumaki uses her psychic powers to secretly locate the capsule inside Fubuki's body, reshapes it into a needle, pulls it out, and drives it into her opponent's eye in one smooth motion. After saving Fubuki, she tears into the rest of Fubuki’s group for letting a spy get that close. She’s furious because to her, Fubuki staying with weak allies only puts her in danger. When they try to explain themselves, it doesn’t calm her down at all. She prepares to put them out of hero work permanently just to keep Fubuki “safe.” That’s when Saitama intervenes and casually grabs her wrist. Tatsumaki orders him to let go and tries to blast him away with psychokinesis. It does absolutely nothing. Saitama calls out to Fubuki and her group, telling them to evacuate, and this makes Tatsumaki stop and wonder why he’s acting so familiar with her sister. She decides to test whether this bald guy deserves to be part of Fubuki’s circle and starts shaking the entire area, even affecting the building above them. Saitama gets annoyed because she’s being careless around people’s homes. He drags her against his chest and jumps out of the underground cell with her so she doesn’t bring the whole place down on everyone’s heads. Once they hit the ground, Tatsumaki slings him away with a burst of psychic force to check if he can handle her attacks. She then whips up a tornado and starts chucking massive rocks at him using the rotation. One of the rocks nearly smashes into a car, so Saitama destroys it and complains about her not paying attention. But the debris from his deflection almost hits the same car, so Tatsumaki stops it midair and mocks him for it. Things escalate fast. Her psychic blasts rip apart Saitama’s jacket, he screams about it, and Tatsumaki flicks him through building after building like it’s a warm-up exercise. Saitama scolds her for the collateral damage and tells her to return to where they started, so she grabs his head and drags him along the ground at high speed until they land back at the original spot. When they arrive, Saitama slips out of her grip and tells her it’s fine to go wild. Tatsumaki gets irritated because she feels he’s looking down on her, but he assures her he’s not restricting her. She starts panting as her stamina fades, but she admits she’s actually enjoying fighting someone who reminds her of the number one S-class hero, Blast. She tries to trap him underground, but he punches his way out like it’s nothing. That shocks her again. She tries to fling him into space, but she can barely lift him, and the strain wears her down even further. Saitama tells her she treats relationships too lightly right before she collapses from exhaustion. During her unconscious moment, she falls back into an old memory. She sees her younger self chasing after Blast, the hero who saved her from Tsukuyomi’s experiments and inspired her to become stronger. Beneath all her rough behavior, Tatsumaki still carries that childhood trauma and the fear of dragging Fubuki into the same life she had. When she wakes up, Saitama is right there, just waiting for her to recover. This is when he finally tells her what he thinks. He acknowledges how strong her psychic powers are and how much training she must have gone through to get to that level. Then he calls her “pretty tough.” She definitely feels the compliment, but she tries to act like she doesn’t care, which irritates Saitama a little. He tells her she can live however she wants as long as she stops causing unnecessary destruction. He also tells her she’ll become a great hero if she can manage that. Then he pats her on the head — something he doesn’t usually do when complimenting others. For Tatsumaki, who normally hates being touched, actually allowing the gesture says a lot. This is the moment where her opinion of Saitama shifts. She recognizes him as someone worthy of respect. Because of that, when Saitama later tells her to let the Fubuki Group stay with Fubuki, she listens. She knows it’s what Fubuki wants, and she actually backs off — even if only for now — because Saitama asked her to.

* **Blast and Empty Void**

From the beginning of the series, the number one S-class hero Blast is treated like this mysterious figure operating on a whole different level from the other S-class heroes. We get tiny flashbacks and vague mentions of him, but nothing concrete enough to piece together his actual strength. It takes years of build-up before he finally shows up during the Monster Association arc, and when he does, the story instantly makes it clear why he’s been absent for so long. While the Hero Association is busy dealing with dragons and city-level disasters, Blast has been working nonstop for the past twenty years preventing a godlike being from entering the human world. He spends his time traveling across different locations, hunting down the mysterious cubes that have the power to turn humans into Mysterious Beings. These cubes are more than just weird artifacts — they act like beacons for “God,” and anyone who touches them risks being transformed or manipulated. When Blast finally appears during the Monster Association raid, he immediately notices the residue of “God’s” energy. That alone shows how familiar he is with this being. And then there’s his combat ability. His teleportation portals don’t waver even when exposed to the insane energy output from Saitama and Garou’s near-divine clash. That kind of stability already puts him in a higher tier than almost everyone in the series. It tells us he’s operating comfortably on a cosmic scale. Blast’s physical abilities match that level too. He reacts to light-speed attacks and throws punches that literally distort space. In the moments he joins the fight, he uses energy constructs and creates gravitational distortions so strong they behave like miniature black holes. His approach to combat isn’t about overwhelming force. Instead, he focuses on sealing, redirecting, and neutralizing threats. He fights like someone who’s been dealing with cosmic-level disasters for decades, and he understands exactly how dangerous “God” is. But Blast hasn’t been working alone. Years before this, he partnered with Empty Void — the original founder of the Ninja Village. Both of them were active in hunting down the cubes tied to “God,” trying to prevent anyone from turning into another test subject for this divine entity. That partnership eventually fell apart. Void starts losing faith in Blast and decides to handle the cubes by himself. This mistake pulls him directly into “God’s” influence. The entity grants him divine power, turning him into a Mysterious Being completely under its control. Blast confronts Void after that transformation and defeats him so decisively that Void falls into a deep coma. He’s locked away inside a recovery capsule within the Ninja Village, but because he created a dimensional barrier around himself, Blast can’t track him. The barrier shields Void long enough for him to recover, and he eventually wakes up after fifteen years. Once Void wakes up, he finds Sonic’s hideout and launches an attack, but Saitama happens to be there. The fight ends instantly. Saitama drops him with a single punch and sends his body drilling straight into the ground. The moment Blast, Flash, and Sonic arrive at the scene, Saitama has already taken care of Empty Void.  A massive crack suddenly appears across the sky and forms a rift in space and time. The distortion happens because Blast strays too far from the location he’s responsible for guarding. Through the rift, two divine beasts, Byakko and Genbu, surge into the human world. They act as guardians serving “God,” and the moment they arrive, they unleash destruction on the area. They fire a gigantic energy blast that would have wiped out everyone there if not for Blast putting up a barrier that absorbs the hit. Even with the barrier, the explosion levels multiple city blocks and causes heavy damage all around. Void wakes up again, this time fully conscious and capable of fighting. He launches Dimension Slash — an attack sharp enough to slice clean through both beasts without resistance. The technique cuts through them like they’re made of paper. Blast immediately follows up with Giga Gravity Blow. He unleashes several gravitational spheres that crush the beasts with overwhelming pressure and launch their bodies into the sky. Watching Blast and Empty Void dismantle two divine guardians back-to-back, Saitama appears in awe as he takes Rover with him away from the site. The showcase of powers from Blast and Empty Void convinces Saitama that this threat is something the two of them can handle better than he can. He turns around and leaves the cleanup to the ones who've spent years dealing with cosmic intrusions. This is again one of those rare moments where Saitama acknowledges someone else’s expertise. Blast then seals the dimensional tear and sends the defeated beasts back to wherever they came from. On the ground, Empty Void’s monsterized form begins to crack apart. His human form emerges beneath the broken layers of divine corruption. Blast calls out his name, genuinely happy that Void has finally broken free from “God’s” control and returned to himself after fifteen long years. Their fight against the two Divine Beasts gives Saitama a front-row seat to the scale they’re working on, and for once, he lets someone else take charge of handling threats that can wipe out multiple cities because he knows they can do it.
