**What It’s Like to be Every Rank of Curse in Jujutsu Kaisen**

In *Jujutsu Kaisen*, cursed spirits aren't just monsters that exist to get punched by teenagers. They're born from human emotion — every phobia, every anxiety, every deeply personal fear that people carry around without realizing it has the potential to manifest into something that wants to take them out. Today, we're not watching from the sorcerer's side. We're flipping the script entirely and living the curse life, grade by grade, from the absolute jokes at the bottom all the way to the catastrophic nightmares at the top. So get comfortable, forget everything you know about being the hero, and get ready to find out what it's actually like to be every rank of curse in *Jujutsu Kaisen*.

* **GRADE 4**

Let's paint you a picture. You are a Grade 4 curse. You spawned from someone's fear of dirty public toilets, or maybe that weird creeping feeling people get when they're alone in a dark room. That's your origin story — not from a volcano or the concept of death itself, but from a toilet, so congratulations. Life at Grade 4 is less "terrifying monster" and more "pest problem that society hasn't gotten around to dealing with." You exist in the Jujutsu world the same way pesky bugs exist in someone's kitchen — deeply unwanted, occasionally spotted, and completely handled by whoever shows up with the right tool. Except in your case, that tool is a literal wooden bat. The official benchmark for how much effort it takes to put down a Grade 4 curse is a wooden bat. That's all the respect you're getting from this universe. You don't have a cursed technique. You don't have a strategy. You don't even have particularly impressive strength. What you do have is the ability to terrify non-sorcerers who've never seen a curse before, which is basically your only career highlight. Regular people can't fight you, and that makes you feel powerful right up until the moment a first-year student shows up and ruins your whole evening. Think about the curses that appeared on the night Yuji and Megumi went to that high school where Sukuna's finger was unsealed. The second that finger got exposed, cursed energy was radiating everywhere and dozens of Grade 4 curses swarmed the building, drawn in by the overwhelming energy of a Sukuna relic. That's you. You were one of those guys. You showed up, you looked terrifying to the students trapped inside, and then Megumi's Divine Dogs started tearing through your kind like it was nothing. When Yuji finally showed up and dropkicked one of your fellow Grade 4s through a window, the fight was already over before it started. Then there's the Roppongi training mission. Gojo literally used a building full of Grade 4 curses as a warm-up exercise for his students. Yuji sliced through one with a knife in the first few seconds while Nobara hammered a nail through another one's head and blew it apart with her technique. That's the Grade 4 experience in full — you are a training dummy, and you don't even get the satisfaction of making the students struggle even a little. The closest thing Grade 4 has to a fun quirk is the Fly Head. These are insect-like spirits so weak that Jujutsu High keeps them in boxes wrapped in talismans like some kind of cursed spirit gift packaging. The only thing they're used for is checking whether new people can perceive curses at all. If someone can see a Fly Head, great, they've got cursed perception. If not, the Fly Head will just sit there looking pathetic and non-threatening until someone disposes of it. Fly Heads travel in swarms, which at least gives the illusion of being a threat — but even that has its limits. The one time a Fly Head swarm actually did something useful was when Toji Fushiguro stored a bunch of them in his inventory curse and released them mid-fight against Gojo to mess with his detection. That's peak Grade 4 achievement. You weren't the threat. You were a distraction tool for an actual threat. Being a Grade 4 curse means you have no future, no growth ceiling worth mentioning, and no legacy. You appear, you terrify some civilians, and then either a first-year student, a Divine Dog, or a wooden bat finishes the chapter.

* **GRADE 3**

Moving up the ladder, you're now a Grade 3 curse. You're still on the wrong side of every fight, but at least now people treat you with mild concern instead of immediate dismissal. You were born from something more emotionally complex this time — job anxiety, social rejection, illness, the kind of low-level dread that follows people around for years. That gives you more substance, more energy, and just enough intelligence to make you genuinely annoying to deal with. Grade 3 is where curses start showing actual personality. You can fight back. You can think, at least a little. You can survive long enough for a sorcerer to look at you with something resembling concern before they put you down. The official power reference for a Grade 3 is that "if you have a handgun, you can rest easy" — which is a massive step up from the wooden bat, but still says everything about where you stand. You're not a catastrophe. You're an inconvenience with teeth. Imagine you're the Occult Club Curse from the first episode. You're strong enough to completely overwhelm Megumi — you slam him into a wall hard enough that his Divine Dogs vanish from the impact and he crashes down an entire floor. That's a legitimate achievement. You grabbed a trained Jujutsu High sorcerer and made him feel the full weight of your power. For a moment, you're winning. Megumi is beaten, bruised, and out of options, and Yuji — who at this point has zero cursed energy — tries to fight you and gets knocked down immediately. You're the reason Yuji had to eat Sukuna's finger. Without you, there's no story, so at least you can feel good about that. Then Sukuna's energy floods into Yuji, and your entire existence ends in seconds. But before that happened, you were a genuine threat. The Roppongi Curse takes a slightly different approach to being a Grade 3\. Rather than going full brute force, it grabs a child and uses him as a human shield — a calculated move that forces Nobara to freeze up and drop her weapon. That's Grade 3 intelligence in action. You're not going to overpower a sorcerer with raw strength, so you make the situation complicated. You make them hesitate and exploit their empathy. The plan actually works for a while, right up until Yuji bursts through the wall and removes the arm holding the hostage in one clean motion. The curse panics and tries to flee — because at Grade 3, survival instinct kicks in, even if the ability to actually escape doesn't always follow. Nobara uses the severed arm as a link for her Resonance technique and channels damage directly through it, exorcising the fleeing curse remotely. Smart enough to use a hostage but not smart enough to escape a first-year with a voodoo technique. Then there are Mahito's transfigured humans, who sit awkwardly in this Grade 3-to-weak-Grade-2 range. They used to be regular people before Mahito reshaped their souls, and now they're grotesque creatures under his complete control. The deeply unsettling part is that traces of consciousness remain — they cry, they beg, they ask for death, all while their bodies carry out Mahito's orders without pause. That's the Grade 3 experience taken to its worst possible extreme: powerful enough to end a normal person without effort, but still fundamentally at the mercy of something far stronger. A single transfigured human can tear through civilians without problem, and a fused version — a Polymorphic Soul Isomer — can send a Grade 1 sorcerer flying through a building. But at the end of the day, they're still Mahito's pawns. Even among Grade 3 curses, having any real autonomy is pretty rare. The Grade 3 experience overall is one of almost-but-not-quite. You're strong enough to be a real threat to untrained people and a genuine challenge for rookies. You've got enough intelligence to play games, use tactics, and create complicated situations. But every time you run up against an experienced sorcerer or a main character with something to prove, the ceiling becomes painfully visible.

* **GRADE 2** 

Grade 2 curses are where the real danger starts to announce itself. You're no longer something that gets handled before lunch. You're the reason sorcerers call for backup. Born from deeper, heavier fears — the kind that eat away at people over long periods — you come into the world with actual cursed techniques, a higher concentration of cursed energy, and enough intelligence to hold a conversation. The benchmark for your threat level is "a close call with a shotgun." You might not walk away from that fight. The sorcerer might not either. Consider Ko-Guy, the grasshopper-looking Grade 2 curse hanging around Shibuya Station during the Incident. When Yuji finds him in the middle of eating a human corpse, Ko-Guy doesn't panic or flee — he just talks. He explains that he's there to protect a curtain, announces his superiority over humans, and boasts about his jaws, arms, and eyes. That's the Grade 2 demeanor right there: confident enough to have a conversation with someone who's about to try to slay you. He's not wrong about his physical capabilities either. His grasshopper anatomy is enhanced with cursed energy, letting him zip around enclosed spaces, spit corrosive black mucus, and deliver piercing strikes with his abdomen. He extends that abdomen in a sneak attack that would impale most opponents — but Yuji blocks it with his hand and takes Ko-Guy apart in one punch. The conversation ends quickly right there. The Yasohachi Bridge Curse gives a clearer picture of what a Grade 2 is really capable of when given room to operate. This curse doesn't just show up and start throwing itself at sorcerers — it built a whole hidden domain under that bridge and waited. It takes Megumi, Yuji, and Nobara three attempts just to find the thing, and accessing it requires a symbolic act of crossing the river at night to enter the barrier. Once inside, the mole-like curse operates on its home turf, moving through tunnels it generates across the ground in a whack-a-mole pattern that's fast enough to dodge even Nobara's Resonance technique. It fights smart — it baits Megumi into attacking at the wrong moment, resurfaces behind him to counterattack, and conserves energy by switching between two tunnels instead of chasing them recklessly. The curse's own domain eventually costs too much energy to maintain, and Megumi picks up on the pattern just in time. But the fact that this thing held its own against three Jujutsu High students while juggling a domain, defending against technique-based attacks, and fighting tactically puts it firmly in a different category from the lower grades. Grade 2 life means you have enough going for you to demand respect. You can form alliances — as seen with Ko-Guy who was working directly with Kenjaku. You can build domains. You can speak and strategize. You're not cannon fodder anymore; you're a calculated threat. The limitation is that you still exist at the mercy of anything stronger than a solid first-year duo. You can dominate a battlefield full of civilians and make trained sorcerers uncomfortable, but a Grade 1 sorcerer treats you the way a Grade 2 treats Grade 4 curses. The intelligence and technique you've gained mostly just let you understand how outmatched you are before the fight ends.

* **SEMI-GRADE 1** 

At Semi-Grade 1, something shifts. You're not just a threat anymore — you're a problem. What separates you from Grade 2 is the ability to use cursed techniques with genuine effectiveness, and what that means is that going up against you alone is a decision that could genuinely end someone's career or their life. Any curse that can communicate clearly and show real intelligence sits at this level at minimum. You think three moves ahead, you wait for openings, and you hit with intent. The unnamed Semi-Grade 1 that shows up during *Jujutsu Kaisen 0* makes its entrance by immediately releasing a blinding flash that carves a hole straight through the ground. That's your hello. Toge — a sorcerer whose Cursed Speech is one of the most powerful techniques in the series — commands you to "twist," and your arm twists violently in response. The hit lands. But you're still standing. The backlash that hits Toge from using that level of output on you is enough to leave him coughing and temporarily out of commission, which tells you everything about how much cursed energy you're putting out. Yuta — fresh to his power, still figuring out Rika — has to use her energy channeled through a blade just to coat it in enough cursed energy to damage you. Even then, your skin is hard enough that a full upward slash leaves a wound too shallow to finish the job. It takes Toge getting his voice back and landing a final Cursed Speech command at full power to finally put you down. Against two skilled sorcerers working together, it took everything they had. You're the kind of threat that makes sorcerers change their strategy mid-fight, that forces improvisation, that leaves people injured even in victory. The downside is that anything above Grade 1 makes you look like the Grade 4 curses look to you. An official Grade 1 sorcerer handles you as their standard mission. And the moment a Special Grade curse or Toji Fushiguro walks into the room, you're an afterthought — as proven by the Semi-Grade 1 curse released by Principal Gakuganji during the Goodwill Event to assassinate Yuji. It gets decapitated by Hanami before it can even begin the assassination mission it was released for. That's the Semi-Grade 1 existence: genuinely dangerous, until something genuinely worse shows up.

* **GRADE 1** 

Grade 1 is the prestige tier that the entire sorcerer ranking system is built around. A Grade 1 sorcerer is literally defined by their ability to defeat a Grade 1 curse solo. That's how significant this benchmark is. You have multiple cursed techniques, refined cursed energy control that starts approaching Domain Expansion, and the raw power to destroy sorcerers who aren't paying full attention. The Finger Bearers gives you the Grade 1 experience in full. They are officially labeled Unregistered Special Grade, but let’s be real — we’re bumping them down a bit since they’re clearly nowhere near the level of the other unregistered Special Grades that actually shook the entire series. The Finger Bearers are born when a cursed spirit consumes one of Sukuna's fingers, inheriting a fragment of the King of Curses' power and becoming something fundamentally above their original level. The first one appears at the Eishu Juvenile Detention Center, slices off Yuji's left hand almost immediately, and takes hits that would end most curses without slowing down. It's only when Sukuna takes control — and then the Finger Bearer makes the catastrophic mistake of attacking Sukuna — that it gets obliterated via Malevolent Shrine. The second Finger Bearer completely overwhelms Megumi under Yasohachi Bridge, breaking his weapon, cornering him, and forcing him to test an incomplete Domain Expansion just to survive. It resists the domain using a burst of cursed energy — which is an impressive feat in itself — but that expenditure opens the window Megumi needed to land the killing blow through Divine Dog: Totality. The Rainbow Dragon rounds out this tier with an entirely different set of tools. Summoned by Geto during the Star Plasma Vessel Escort Mission after Riko Amanai's death, the Rainbow Dragon combines flight, exceptional speed, and the hardest skin of any curse in Geto's collection. It crashes through stone walls without slowing down and dominates the area around it. The issue is that "hardest skin" means nothing when Toji Fushiguro has the Split Soul Katana, which bypasses physical durability entirely by attacking the soul directly. The dragon goes down in seconds. But the fact that it forced Geto to use it as a trump card against Toji — and that its toughness was considered notable enough to be a defining characteristic — places it squarely in the Grade 1 bracket. Grade 1 life is the closest curses get to actually being respected as equals by high-tier sorcerers. At this level, you're a genuine threat that demands the full attention of the people fighting you. But you're still below the ceiling, and the closer you get to that ceiling, the more obvious it becomes that there are things out there that would end you without a second thought.

* **SPECIAL GRADE** 

You made it. You are a Special Grade curse, and everything about your existence operates at a level the lower grades couldn't comprehend. You weren't born from someone's fear of bugs or dirty bathrooms or job rejection. You came from the deepest, most ancient terror humanity collectively carries — death, disease, volcanic fire, the hatred people have for each other. By 2017, sixteen cursed spirits are officially registered by Jujutsu High, and the general institutional advice for handling one of you is "maybe cluster bombs could work." Maybe. The fact that it's a maybe says everything. You are not a mission that gets handed to a first-year. You are a national-level problem. Imagine you're Tamamo-no-Mae — Geto's most prized registered Special Grade, held in reserve through the entire Night Parade of a Hundred Demons and only summoned at the very last second alongside Maximum: Uzumaki as a two-part kill combination. You match Rika at full power, and the whole plan collapses only because Yuta stakes his own life on a Binding Vow. One desperate gamble from a kid willing to die is the only thing that stops you. The Smallpox Deity plays the game completely differently. You don't brawl — you trap. Kenjaku sends you after Mei Mei during Shibuya, and the moment you appear, your Domain Expansion locks her and Ui Ui inside before either of them can react. Coffins seal them in, a boulder drops, and a countdown from three starts ticking. If it hits one, they're infected and gone instantly. Mei Mei only survives by reverse-engineering your domain's logic mid-fight — figuring out that the sure-hit targets whoever has the most cursed energy, using Ui Ui to redirect your focus, and sacrificing a crow to land the kill. Nobody out-muscles you. They have to out-think you, and most people never get the chance. As Kurourushi, you exist purely to infest and horrify. Born from Japan's collective disgust toward cockroaches, you produce man-eating swarms that strip people to the bone in seconds, shoot insect eggs into opponents through your Festering Life Sword that hatch immediately and crawl out from inside their bodies, and blind anyone who tries to fight back with your Earthen Insect Trance. Yuta eventually disintegrates you with Reverse Cursed Technique — positive energy against a creature made entirely of negative energy — but the fear that created you doesn't disappear when your body does. Your child inherits your cursed energy and revives, which is a better legacy than most curses across every grade will ever manage. Then there's Rika — and her situation is unlike anything else on this list. You were a person once. Rika Orimoto, Yuta's childhood friend, who died in a car accident and got cursed into one of the most destructive spirits in the series by the sheer intensity of Yuta's grief. As a curse, you are classified as a national-level threat even while only partially manifested. You can crush sorcerers and non-sorcerers alike without effort, copy cursed techniques, protect Yuta from incoming attacks, and carry his injured allies to safety while simultaneously fighting. When Yuta makes a Binding Vow and stakes his life to fully release you, the energy you fire in that state overwhelms both Geto's Maximum: Uzumaki and Tamamo-no-Mae Incarnate in a single blast. The fight ends. And when it's over, you forgive Yuta for the promise he made and let your spirit pass on — because after everything, what you were was never really hatred or hunger. It was love. Unchecked, boundless, catastrophically powerful love, and even at your most destructive, that's what was driving every moment of it. Among the unregistered Special Grades, Dagon spent most of his existence as a cursed womb — quiet and formless — until the Shibuya Incident forces his evolution into his true form. Your Domain Expansion, Horizon of the Captivating Skandha, disguises itself as a serene tropical beach while automatically unleashing serpents, piranha swarms, sharks, and crushing crustaceans on anyone trapped inside. Naobito, Nanami, and Maki — three elite sorcerers — can barely stay alive in there. The only crack in your armor comes when Megumi opens an incomplete domain inside yours, and the exit he creates lets a newly-revived Toji walk through. Toji runs on zero cursed energy, which means everything your domain was built around simply doesn't apply to him, and the fight ends fast. As Jogo, you were born from the earth's fear of volcanic destruction, you can melt stone with your flames, and your Domain Expansion, Coffin of the Iron Mountain, turns the air itself into a weapon. You genuinely believe cursed spirits are the true humans and that you're fighting for a righteous cause — right up until Gojo casually opens Infinite Void and dismantles that belief in real time. Hanami pulls you out before the exorcism lands. But you come back during Shibuya and launch Maximum: Meteor, a fireball that devastates an entire section of the city, and Sukuna acknowledges it would've hurt him if it connected. You still lose. Sukuna reduces you to ash with Divine Flame, but you stared down the King of Curses without flinching, and that's a better ending than most things at any rank ever get. Finally, there's Mahito — and being Mahito is a trip. You're born from humanity's hatred and fear of each other, which makes every person who ever treated someone badly a tiny contributor to your existence, and you find that genuinely funny. Your Idle Transfiguration lets you touch souls and reshape whatever body is attached to them — stretching, twisting, fusing, or detonating them however you feel like. You heal yourself by adjusting your own soul's shape. You clone yourself. You create transfigured humans from victims and fuse souls into Polymorphic Soul Isomers. Your domain, Self-Embodiment of Perfection, connects you directly to every soul inside it, letting you reshape them the instant the domain activates — and you're skilled enough to flash it for 0.2 seconds just to land a hit without leaving yourself exposed, which is something only top-tier sorcerers like Gojo have ever pulled off. You keep growing through every fight, evolving mid-battle in ways that leave veteran sorcerers genuinely alarmed. Nanami fights you early and immediately warns that you need to be killed before you get any stronger — and he's completely right. You land Black Flashes on both Yuji and Todo, hit your Instant Spirit Body of Distorted Killing where your durability skyrockets and Yuji's punches can't move you, and you would have kept going indefinitely if Yuji and Todo hadn't coordinated their attacks with perfect timing to finally corner you. Even then, you try to escape. Your ending comes not from exorcism but from Kenjaku absorbing you through Cursed Spirit Manipulation — you don't get a clean death, you get collected. Which, honestly, might be the most Mahito thing that could've happened. Special Grade life means you get to exist at a scale where the rules that apply to every other curse barely register. You have technique, domain, goals, and the kind of cursed energy that makes sorcerers rethink their career choices before the fight even starts. The ceiling is real — Gojo and Sukuna both sit comfortably above even the strongest Special Grades — but at this tier, you're shaping the story. What you leave behind—fear, legacy, or just the memory of a fight where nobody walked away clean — sticks around way longer than the body ever does.
