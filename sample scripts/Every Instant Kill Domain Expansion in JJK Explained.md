**Every Instant Kill Domain Expansion in JJK Explained**

1. **Unlimited Void (Satoru Gojo)**

Unlimited Void is Satoru Gojo’s Domain Expansion — and easily one of the scariest techniques in *Jujutsu Kaisen*. When he activates it, both he and his targets get pulled into the Limitless itself — an infinite space that looks like outer space, filled with galaxies, black holes, and floating white fragments. It looks beautiful, but if you’re trapped inside, you’re done for. Kento Nanami once said Unlimited Void is basically a guaranteed win. The moment someone gets caught, their brain gets slammed with endless information. Every sense, every detail, every thought — all of it hits at once. The human brain has no way to process that much data, so it completely shuts down. Victims stay conscious, but they can’t move, speak, or even think straight. Inside the domain, the target’s mind tries to keep up but eventually breaks down from the overload. This is how the sure-hit effect works — the domain doesn’t need to physically hit the target. It attacks their perception itself, drowning their mind in infinite input until they freeze completely. But here’s the thing — Unlimited Void doesn’t kill instantly. It’s not a one-hit kill. It’s slow, creeping death. When the brain is forced to process infinity, it doesn’t just shut off like a switch. The body keeps functioning for a while — heart still beating, lungs still breathing — but the mind is done. Their consciousness loops endlessly, reliving every action and thought they’ve ever had. Gojo describes this as “forcing one’s actions upon them infinitely.” That loop becomes their prison. Time stretches endlessly because their mind is running millions of times faster than reality. The nervous system overloads, the brain burns out, and the body shuts down. There’s no way out once the domain fully takes hold. The only ones safe from this effect are Gojo himself and anyone he’s touching when the domain activates. That tiny loophole technically means someone could survive if they’re holding onto him, but good luck pulling that off. Unlimited Void is the pinnacle of the Limitless technique. Gojo inherited both the Limitless and the Six Eyes, and after his Star Plasma Vessel mission, he unlocked his full potential. That’s probably when he mastered his Domain. The first time he used it was against Jogo — the volcano-headed curse. Jogo’s Coffin of the Iron Mountain tried to burn Gojo alive inside a volcanic hellscape, but the second Unlimited Void came out, it wasn’t even close. Gojo’s domain crushed Jogo’s completely. Inside, Jogo couldn’t move or think. He was frozen in place, overwhelmed by the flood of information. He realized just how helpless he was, unable to even form a coherent thought. Kenjaku and Jogo knew exactly how dangerous Unlimited Void was, so when they planned to seal Gojo, they made sure to exploit that. Their idea was to use non-sorcerers as obstacles to stop him from using it at all. They figured if Gojo tried expanding his Domain in a packed area like Shibuya, the civilians trapped between barriers would be crushed instantly. Kenjaku was confident Gojo wouldn’t take that risk — and he was mostly right. When the Shibuya Incident hit, Gojo was surrounded by hundreds of transfigured humans while fighting Jogo and Mahito. He could’ve wiped out every curse in seconds with Unlimited Void, but that would’ve killed all the civilians too. So instead, he did something insane — he activated Unlimited Void for just 0.2 seconds. He estimated that a normal person could only survive 0.2 seconds inside without permanent damage. So, he used that tiny window — and it worked perfectly. Every transfigured human froze in place, their minds overloaded with half a year’s worth of information in less than a blink. The non-sorcerers dropped unconscious but were still alive, while the cursed spirits were briefly paralyzed. Gojo then exorcised every single transfigured human in 299 seconds. That’s a thousand enemies wiped out in under five minutes. The next time we saw Unlimited Void was in Gojo’s showdown with Sukuna — easily one of the most intense battles in the series. Gojo used Unlimited Void five times during their fight, constantly evolving it to counter Sukuna’s moves. Their first Domain clash happened early on. Both expanded their Domains at the same time — Unlimited Void versus Malevolent Shrine. The result was a draw. Their techniques were so refined that the sure-hit effects canceled each other out. But Sukuna had the edge with his open barrier, which gave his domain a way bigger range. Unlimited Void’s outer shell was strong inside but weak from the outside, and Sukuna took full advantage. His constant slashes tore through the barrier until it broke. Gojo managed to survive and recovered using Reverse Cursed Technique before activating his domain again. This time, he switched the structure, reinforcing the outer shell to tank the slashes. It held up for a bit, but Sukuna adapted again, using a binding vow to drop his sure-hit effect and strengthen his attacks even more. Unlimited Void shattered again but Gojo wasn’t done. He used Falling Blossom Emotion to defend himself, recovered his technique, and launched a third expansion. Drawing from what he learned in the Prison Realm, he shrank his domain’s outer shell down to basketball size, massively boosting its density. Sukuna countered by narrowing his own domain for higher output, but Unlimited Void lasted three full minutes before collapsing. In that time, Gojo landed serious damage on Sukuna, forcing both domains to fail at once. They recovered with Reverse Cursed Technique again and clashed in a fourth expansion, which ended in another stalemate. Then came the fifth and final clash. This time, Gojo was slightly faster — less than 0.01 seconds faster, actually. That tiny difference was enough for Unlimited Void’s sure-hit to trigger first. Sukuna froze for just a moment, and Gojo used that instant to land a brutal hit. The two fought for nearly three minutes before Malevolent Shrine finally broke, leaving Unlimited Void in control. For that short moment, Sukuna was completely paralyzed, unable to move or think. But before Gojo could finish him off, Sukuna summoned Mahoraga, who instantly destroyed the domain. Sukuna then revealed his trick — he’d been using Megumi’s soul to absorb the effects of Gojo’s previous Unlimited Voids so Mahoraga could adapt faster. Gojo, who had been burning out his brain and restoring it over and over with Reverse Cursed Technique, couldn’t go for a sixth round. Still, the damage was already done. Unlimited Void had burned out Sukuna’s brain, just like it did to Gojo’s, and completely shut down his ability to use Malevolent Shrine for the rest of the fight. After using his Domain five times, Gojo finally hits his limit. He drops to his knees, his brain too damaged to keep it up any longer. With both Gojo and Sukuna unable to expand their domains anymore, the fight turns into a raw, full-power clash of cursed energy and pure strength — Gojo’s Infinity against Sukuna’s shikigami from Megumi’s Ten Shadows Technique. Gojo eventually pushes through the onslaught and fires off an absolutely devastating Hollow Purple, tearing through Mahoraga and leaving Sukuna heavily injured. But Sukuna wasn’t just tanking hits — he was learning. He used Mahoraga’s adaptation as a blueprint to figure out how to counter Infinity. Mahoraga’s first adaptation changed the nature of its cursed energy to bypass Gojo’s technique, something Sukuna couldn’t normally do on his own. Then came the second adaptation — this time, it didn’t just aim at Gojo. It expanded the target itself. Instead of cutting Gojo, it cut everything — the space, the world around them. Using that as a model, Sukuna applied the same concept to his Dismantle, creating a slash that didn’t care about Infinity because it was slicing through reality itself. That world-cutting slash split Gojo clean in half, ending the fight instantly. After Gojo’s death, Yuta Okkotsu took over his body by using Kenjaku's cursed technique, transplanting his brain into Gojo's body, which eventually led Yuta to inherit Gojo’s powers. When Yuta fought Sukuna later on, he also used Unlimited Void. Both expanded their Domains — Unlimited Void versus Malevolent Shrine, round two. Yuta used the same method Gojo developed — shrinking the barrier to make it denser and harder to break. Thanks to Gojo’s training, Yuta managed to hold it for a while. But he wasn’t as refined. When he used Hollow Technique: Purple, the blast tore through his own barrier, collapsing the domain and injuring both him and Sukuna. Even then, Unlimited Void remains one of the most advanced and deadly domains ever created. It’s the ultimate mental kill. You’re given everything there is to know… and then you die knowing it all.

2. **Malevolent Shrine (Sukuna)**

Malevolent Shrine is Sukuna’s Domain Expansion, and just like its name suggests, it’s pure chaos made real. When activated, a massive shrine appears — a grotesque, demon-worshipping version of a Buddhist temple. The structure is twisted and monstrous, built on a base of bovine skulls. The roof has horns jutting out, mouths on each gable, and human skulls dangling beneath it. The four entrances are formed by gigantic mouths lined with human teeth, and there’s another smaller mouth between the two roof layers. Even the corners have short, tree-like stumps with gnarled branches, like something alive is growing out of the shrine. It’s already horrifying in that form, but Sukuna can take it even further. When the domain transforms into its alternate, more “eldritch” form, the shrine turns into a pulsing mass of flesh, horns, and teeth. Dozens of eyes stare in every direction, patches of hair-like appendages twitch across its body, and four massive horns crown its head. Two huge humanoid hands hang beneath it, and a spine-like structure extends down from its back. It’s the visual definition of cursed energy gone berserk. But the terrifying look is just the surface — the real nightmare is how the domain works. Malevolent Shrine’s guaranteed-hit effect continuously slashes everything within its range until nothing remains. It doesn’t stop until the domain disappears or Sukuna is too injured to maintain it. The ability works through two types of slashing attacks: Cleave and Dismantle. Sukuna chooses which one to use based on what he’s targeting. Dismantle is for inanimate objects, while Cleave adjusts its power to cut through anything that has cursed energy. The technique doesn’t waste time on precision — once you’re inside, you’re getting sliced apart until you’re gone. What makes Malevolent Shrine stand out from every other Domain Expansion is that it doesn’t create a closed space. Normally, a domain forms a sealed barrier — a separate pocket where the sure-hit effect can’t be escaped. Sukuna skips that part completely. His domain has no barrier, meaning it doesn’t trap targets — it simply overwrites reality in a massive radius. This unique trait creates a binding vow: because there’s technically a way to escape. But in return, Sukuna is rewarded with a much larger effective range. The slashing attacks reach nearly 200 meters in every direction, turning an entire city block into his killing zone. This also means Malevolent Shrine can’t be destroyed by normal means. Domains like Gojo’s Unlimited Void can collapse if the outer shell breaks, but since Sukuna’s doesn’t have a shell, it can’t be broken from the outside. The only real way to stop it is by injuring Sukuna badly enough that he can’t sustain it anymore. Sukuna’s mastery over Domain Expansion is on par with Gojo’s. Even among the strongest sorcerers, very few can match the refinement of Malevolent Shrine. With just two fingers worth of power, Sukuna easily summoned the domain and obliterated a Finger Bearer, a Special Grade cursed spirit, in a single moment. The spirit was completely erased — not even scraps left. Later, in the Shibuya Incident, Sukuna used Malevolent Shrine again during his fight against Mahoraga. He wanted to avoid hitting Megumi while still overwhelming Mahoraga’s ability to adapt, so he narrowed the domain’s range to about 140 meters. Inside that radius, everything was shredded. Sukuna configured his domain to use Cleave on anything with cursed energy and Dismantle on everything else. The result was devastating — Mahoraga was severely damaged, countless civilians were killed instantly, and even Toge Inumaki lost his arm from being caught on the edge of the domain’s range. During Sukuna’s legendary battle with Gojo, he pushed Malevolent Shrine to its limits. Across the fight, he expanded his domain three times, each clash showing how advanced both their techniques were. In their first clash, Sukuna and Gojo expand their domains simultaneously — Malevolent Shrine versus Unlimited Void. Inside the barrier, they were evenly matched. But outside, it was a different story. Malevolent Shrine’s slashes reached beyond Unlimited Void’s barrier and tore into its outer shell, which was weak from the outside. The barrier collapsed, and Gojo was immediately struck by Sukuna’s sure-hit slashes. During their second domain exchange, Sukuna changed the rules of his domain using another binding vow. He removed the guaranteed-hit effect within Unlimited Void’s active area and shrunk his domain’s range in exchange for more concentrated power. This tweak gave him enough output to crush Gojo’s reinforced barrier again, though it also meant he had to risk getting closer. The third exchange was even more intense. Gojo adapted his domain to strengthen its outer shell, while Sukuna refined Malevolent Shrine further to focus on destroying it. The battle turned into a war of endurance — both domains lasted about three minutes before they collapsed simultaneously. Gojo’s attacks damaged Sukuna enough that he couldn’t maintain his domain afterward, but not before Malevolent Shrine once again broke Unlimited Void’s outer shell from the outside. After that, both sorcerers briefly fought without domains, and when they finally activated them again, Gojo was slightly faster. The difference was less than a hundredth of a second, but that was enough for Unlimited Void’s sure-hit to trigger first. Sukuna was paralyzed for an instant, giving Gojo the chance to land a devastating hit. The damage left Sukuna unable to maintain Malevolent Shrine for longer than two minutes and forty seconds, and his domain collapsed inside Unlimited Void. Without the domain’s protection, Sukuna’s brain was hit by the infinite but incomplete information of Gojo’s sure-hit effect, leaving him immobile until he summoned Mahoraga to destroy the domain from outside. After the fifth exchange, Sukuna tried to re-expand his domain and close it off completely to trap Gojo inside, but it failed immediately. The damage Gojo’s Unlimited Void had done to Sukuna’s brain in under ten seconds made it impossible for him to use his Domain Expansion for the rest of the fight. Even so, Sukuna wasn’t done. As mentioned earlier, Sukuna ends up winning the fight, killing the “Honored One” with a single slash that literally cuts through the world itself. Later, after being hit by seven consecutive Black Flashes from Yuji during his final clash against the jujutsu sorcerers, he managed to form an improvised Malevolent Shrine. By altering his hand signs and using the undamaged part of his brain, Sukuna summoned the domain again, maintaining its open-barrier structure. This allowed him to include Maki Zenin, who had no cursed energy, within the domain’s effect. Because of his condition, though, Sukuna could only hold it for 99 seconds before it collapsed. When Yuta returned to the battlefield using Gojo’s body and expanded Unlimited Void, Sukuna countered with another Malevolent Shrine. But when Yuta fired Hollow Technique: Purple, the blast destroyed both domains at once — severely injuring Sukuna but also tearing through Yuta’s own barrier. That ends up being the last time Sukuna ever uses his Domain Expansion. Eventually, he’s taken down by the combined efforts of the jujutsu sorcerers. Yuji lands one final Black Flash — strong enough to knock Megumi’s body free and release his soul from Sukuna’s grip. With no vessel left to hold onto, Sukuna starts to fall apart, his body breaking down into a twisted, melting mess of flesh until there’s nothing left.

3. **Self-Embodiment of Perfection (Mahito)**

Self-Embodiment of Perfection is Mahito’s Domain Expansion, a terrifying manifestation of his cursed technique, Idle Transfiguration. He forms it through hand seals made by tiny hands that sprout from inside his mouth, and once it activates, everything shifts into a dark, oppressive space surrounded by enormous, interlocking hands. These hands clasp together in a flower-like shape, with countless arms stretching out and gripping one another to form a web that traps Mahito’s target inside. The design alone makes it clear what this domain represents — complete control over the soul. Inside Self-Embodiment of Perfection, Mahito gains full access to his technique’s true potential. Normally, Idle Transfiguration requires physical contact to alter a person’s soul and reshape their body, but within this domain, that restriction is gone. Anyone caught inside automatically has their soul exposed to him. This turns every living thing in range into a direct target for transfiguration, making the domain’s guaranteed-hit effect truly unavoidable. Even a split-second inside the domain is enough for Mahito to rewrite a person’s soul completely. Nanami once described the experience as being “in the palm of Mahito’s hand,” and that’s exactly what it feels like. Inside this space, there’s no escape or defense — Mahito’s technique reaches you the instant you’re within its boundaries. That’s why Nanami ranked it alongside Gojo’s Unlimited Void as one of the few nearly unwinnable domains. Once it activates, victory is practically guaranteed for Mahito. But that power comes with a serious weakness. The domain forces Mahito to make contact with every soul inside it, including ones he didn’t intend to touch. That flaw cost him dearly in his first major use of Self-Embodiment of Perfection. During his fight with Nanami and Yuji, Mahito was cornered and pushed to his limit. Facing death inspired him to evolve, unlocking his Domain Expansion for the first time. When he activated it, Yuji was locked out while Nanami was trapped inside, completely exposed to Mahito’s guaranteed-hit technique. But before Mahito could finish him off, Yuji broke through the domain from the outside. This forced Mahito to come into contact with Sukuna’s soul through Yuji’s body for the second time. Sukuna immediately retaliated, cutting Mahito’s body open with deep, devastating slashes. The attack left Mahito unable to maintain the domain, and it collapsed while he fell to his knees, defeated. Later, on October 21st, Mahito used Self-Embodiment of Perfection again against Kokichi Muta, also known as Mechamaru. It looked like an instant win as Mechamaru’s giant puppet collapsed. But Kokichi had anticipated the attack, using Ultimate Mechamaru – Mode: Absolute’s Technique Charge Simple Domain to cancel Mahito’s guaranteed-hit effect and fake his defeat long enough to strike back. Afterward, Mahito refined his control even further. Thanks to his battle experience against Gojo and a breakthrough during his fight with Yuji, where he landed a Black Flash, he discovered a new way to activate his domain. He learned how to trigger Self-Embodiment of Perfection for just 0.2 seconds, merging the processes of manifesting his domain and activating Idle Transfiguration into one seamless motion. He used this shortened version during his fight against Yuji and Aoi Todo. Even though it lasted less than a blink, the domain still allowed him to transfigure Todo’s arm instantly without touching him. Todo managed to stop the curse from spreading by cutting off his own transformed hand. Because the domain was active for only 0.2 seconds, Mahito avoided making prolonged contact with Yuji’s soul, which would’ve triggered Sukuna’s wrath again. Self-Embodiment of Perfection is one of the most lethal domains in Jujutsu Kaisen because its guaranteed-hit effect bypasses every physical defense. Once inside, Mahito doesn’t need to touch his opponent — the domain itself touches their soul for him.

4. **Womb Profusion (Kenjaku)**

Womb Profusion is Kenjaku’s Domain Expansion, and it’s as creepy as it sounds. When he activates it, a massive tower made of cursed spirits forms around him — all their faces mashed together like some kind of living monument. At the bottom are four bald, humanoid spirits with eye-shaped markings on their shoulders. Above them, the tower stretches upward with piles of bodiless heads stacked on top of each other, all with wide, gaping mouths. The whole thing is stitched together with fleshy arms and branches that twist around like roots. It looks more like a curse factory than a Domain, which fits perfectly for someone like Kenjaku. Once it’s expanded, everyone caught inside is instantly in danger. Womb Profusion’s guaranteed hit slams its targets with a blast strong enough to match a direct hit from Maximum: Uzumaki. It’s not clear exactly how the Domain delivers that hit — whether it’s a spiritual strike or pure cursed energy pressure — but the result is the same: an explosion that tears through everything in its range. Getting hit doesn’t mean you die instantly, though. It’s more like your body just can’t hold up after taking that much force. The shockwave wrecks your insides, shatters bones, and destroys any defense you throw up. Once it lands, you’re basically done for — it’s just a matter of how long you can stay alive after the impact. Like Sukuna’s Malevolent Shrine, Kenjaku’s Domain doesn’t form a closed barrier. Instead, it expands straight into the air around him, merging with the environment. That’s what makes it so hard to deal with — there’s no separate space to target or collapse. Even Tengen, who specializes in barriers, couldn’t easily dismantle it because there was no clear “shell” to attack. The Domain just exists in the real world, making it a nightmare to counter. Kenjaku uses Womb Profusion in his fight against Yuki Tsukumo. When Yuki hesitates to open her own Domain, Kenjaku wastes no time and activates his first. His cursed energy floods the battlefield, instantly overpowering her Simple Domain and breaking it apart in seconds. The guaranteed hit then blasts Yuki with a massive shockwave that severely injures her, even with her incredible durability. Tengen manages to interfere right after by targeting the very edge of the Domain’s effective range, dismantling it before it could finish her off. Womb Profusion shows exactly why Kenjaku is one of the most dangerous sorcerers in the series. It doesn’t just trap you — it crushes you with overwhelming force the second you’re caught inside.

5. **Coffin of the Iron Mountain (Jogo)**

Coffin of the Iron Mountain is Jogo’s Domain Expansion, created through his innate technique, Disaster Flames. When activated, it transforms the area into what looks like the heart of an active volcano — a sealed chamber of stone filled with rivers of molten rock. The walls and floor are split open with deep fissures, constantly spitting magma and heat intense enough to turn almost anyone into ash the moment they step inside. Even before the guaranteed hit comes into play, the domain itself is already a death trap. What makes Coffin of the Iron Mountain deadly is its guaranteed hit effect. Once someone is caught inside, Jogo can command the lava itself to attack with pinpoint accuracy. Every strike from the molten rock is impossible to dodge or block, since the domain automatically locks onto the target’s cursed energy and ensures that every blow connects. The heat alone is unbearable, but once the magma hits, it can melt through almost anything — cursed tools, barriers, even reinforced cursed energy defenses. Death is certain once the magma makes contact, but it doesn’t happen in a flash — it’s slow and inevitable. Jogo first uses Coffin of the Iron Mountain in his fight against Gojo. Knowing Gojo’s Infinity made him untouchable in normal combat, Jogo tries to overwhelm him by forcing him into his domain. The environment instantly fills with intense heat, but Gojo isn’t affected at all — not even his clothes burn. Jogo launches his guaranteed-hit magma attack, only for Gojo to counter it effortlessly with his own technique. Desperate, Jogo pushes his cursed energy to the limit, trying to bury Gojo in molten rock. But Gojo expands Unlimited Void, which completely overpowers Jogo’s domain. Coffin of the Iron Mountain collapses almost instantly, leaving Jogo completely outmatched. After that defeat, Jogo becomes cautious about using his domain again. When he later faces Sukuna, he doesn’t even attempt to activate it, fully aware that Sukuna’s Malevolent Shrine would crush his domain the same way Gojo’s did. Despite its raw destructive power, Coffin of the Iron Mountain is a reminder that in *Jujutsu Kaisen*, even a lethal domain means nothing if it’s up against a stronger one.

6. **Deadly Sentencing (Higuruma)**

Deadly Sentencing is the Domain Expansion of Hiromi Higuruma, a defense attorney who completely lost faith in the justice system and is one of the participants in the Culling Game. His domain reflects his character in the most literal way possible — a grim courtroom where judgment isn’t just spoken, it’s enforced. Once activated, the space transforms into a small courtroom with guillotines looming in the background and debris scattered across the floor, setting the tone for what’s about to unfold. Inside, Higuruma and his target take the roles of prosecutor and defendant. Unlike most Domains that rely on a can’t-miss attack, Deadly Sentencing operates under a different kind of guaranteed hit. Instead of forcing physical damage, it forces the target into a trial where every word and choice determines their fate. The domain enforces the rules of Higuruma’s cursed technique, meaning anyone caught inside must play by the court’s system. Violence is completely restricted, and the trial begins the moment the domain is cast. This setup is backed by a binding vow that compels Higuruma to explain how everything works. Once explained, both parties are bound to the process — no fighting, no running, just judgment. The one overseeing the trial is a shikigami called Judgeman, who serves as the centerpiece of the cursed technique. It acts as both judge and executioner. Judgeman begins by announcing the charges against the defendant. These charges aren’t made up — they’re based on actual crimes the target has committed, whether proven or not. What makes Judgeman terrifying is that it knows everything about everyone inside the domain, but this knowledge isn’t shared with Higuruma. He’s as blind as any real prosecutor would be and has to build his case based only on what Judgeman provides. The process starts with Judgeman reading out the crime, and then the defendant has one chance to respond. They can stay silent, confess, or deny the charges. If they deny, they’re free to lie, but that doesn’t mean it’ll save them. Their goal is to eliminate all doubt and earn a verdict of innocence from Judgeman. Once the defendant speaks, it’s Higuruma’s turn to make his rebuttal as the prosecutor. Judgeman provides him with evidence — a sealed envelope that only he can see. The evidence isn’t always decisive, but it gives Higuruma an angle to argue. He’s told what’s inside the envelope, but the defendant doesn’t get to know until after they’ve already made their case. Once both sides have spoken, Judgeman delivers a verdict. If Judgeman finds the defendant guilty, punishment follows immediately. The kind of punishment varies, and even Higuruma doesn’t get to decide which one is given. That randomness adds another layer of risk to the domain. The most common punishment is Confiscation. This sentence takes away the target’s ability to use cursed techniques. If they don’t have one, then it blocks their access to cursed energy entirely. It can even prioritize cursed tools if that’s what the target is using, meaning it strips away whatever power they rely on the most. Confiscation alone doesn’t kill, but it leaves opponents wide open and defenseless — a perfect setup for execution. Then there’s the Death Penalty, the most severe sentence Judgeman can give. This punishment almost always comes paired with Confiscation. When that happens, the target first loses their ability to use cursed energy or techniques. Then, Higuruma receives a weapon called the Executioner’s Sword, a transformed version of his gavel. This blade is absolute. Anyone struck by it dies instantly, no exceptions. If a person has multiple souls, only the one found guilty is destroyed. At this point, the domain becomes a guaranteed kill zone. The Death Penalty grants Higuruma the power to end a life in one clean strike — but it’s important to note that this isn’t an instant kill upon activation. It becomes certain death only after the trial concludes and the conditions are met. The target isn’t automatically executed just for being found guilty. They die the moment Higuruma lands a successful hit with the Executioner’s Sword. That distinction is crucial because the death isn’t triggered by the domain itself, but by the physical execution of the sentence. After the verdict is given, the domain automatically dispels. The punishment, however, stays in effect. Confiscation takes immediate hold, and the Death Penalty remains active until either the execution is carried out or Higuruma cancels it. But the system isn’t entirely unfair. If the defendant is found guilty without confessing, they can request a retrial. Judgeman can’t deny it. This gives the accused one more chance to change the outcome, though that rarely ends well. Higuruma gained his Domain Expansion naturally when his cursed technique was awakened by Kenjaku. His background as a brilliant lawyer made him perfect for a trial-based technique like this. He used it efficiently during the Culling Game, taking out several players with pure logic instead of brute strength. One of the most detailed demonstrations of Deadly Sentencing happened during his fight against Yuji. When Higuruma cast the domain, Yuji tried to attack him before the trial began, but his kick stopped mid-air and he was pulled back to his place. The rules of the domain forbade any violence before the verdict. Judgeman charged Yuji with unlawful entry into a Pachinko Parlor while underage. Yuji guessed that the evidence would prove he entered the parlor and decided to claim he only went in to use the bathroom. That backfired because by admitting he entered at all, he confirmed the crime. The evidence ended up being a picture of Yuji at a cash exchange, which was enough for Higuruma to build his argument. He pointed out that Yuji’s admission contradicted his claim, and Judgeman found him guilty. The sentence was Confiscation. Since Yuji didn’t have an innate technique, he lost the ability to use cursed energy instead. This weakened him badly, as even his basic energy control became unstable. Higuruma took advantage of this and quickly gained the upper hand. However, Yuji remembered the rule about retrials and demanded one. The courtroom reset, and the trial began again. This time, the charge was far more serious — mass murder during the Shibuya Incident. Yuji immediately confessed, taking responsibility for the deaths caused by Sukuna when he took over Yuji’s body. Judgeman reacted with fury, issuing both Confiscation and the Death Penalty. Higuruma received the Executioner’s Sword, which meant he could kill Yuji in one strike. But when he reviewed the evidence, he realized the murders weren’t Yuji’s fault — they were Sukuna’s. That reminder of what justice actually meant broke through Higuruma’s disillusionment. He dispelled the sword and spared Yuji, marking a rare moment of mercy inside Deadly Sentencing. Later, during the battle against Sukuna, Higuruma used his Domain Expansion again. This time, he structured it to retry Yuji’s second case but added Sukuna as a co-defendant. Because Sukuna had been inside Yuji’s body during the original crime, this setup made him legally accountable in the eyes of Judgeman. Sukuna didn’t bother defending himself and immediately admitted to the massacre. Judgeman handed down the harshest possible punishment — Confiscation and Death Penalty. This should have been a guaranteed win for the sorcerers. The plan was simple: strip Sukuna of his cursed technique and let Higuruma kill him with the Executioner’s Sword. But Higuruma made a critical mistake. Because of his inexperience with how Confiscation prioritized its targets, he accidentally confiscated Sukuna’s cursed tool, Kamutoke, instead of his cursed technique. That single error nullified the advantage and turned the situation around completely, leading to his defeat. Despite the mistake, Deadly Sentencing remains one of the most unique and terrifying Domain Expansions in *Jujutsu Kaisen*. Instead of relying on raw destructive power, it uses logic, rules, and binding vows to decide life and death. The execution may not be instant, but once the sword is granted, the outcome is inevitable.

7. **Time Cell Moon Palace (Naoya)**

Time Cell Moon Palace is Naoya Zenin’s Domain Expansion, born from his mastery of the Zenin Clan’s inherited technique — Projection Sorcery. To get how this Domain Expansion actually works, you first need to understand how Projection Sorcery operates. Originally passed down from Naobito Zenin to his son Naoya, this cursed technique splits one second into twenty-four individual frames, like film animation. Using his field of view as the projection angle, Naoya can program his movements across those twenty-four frames and execute them within that single second. The result is terrifying speed that looks completely unnatural, fast enough to blur past even special-grade cursed spirits. While Projection Sorcery is active, the technique’s rules don’t apply to Naoya alone. Anything that touches his palm is forced to obey the same “24 frames per second” law of movement. If a target moves incorrectly — basically failing to match the timing of the frames — they get frozen in place for one second. During that frozen moment, they’re completely defenseless. Even a light hit can break them out of the frame, but the impact usually leaves them with serious injuries. When Naoya pushes his cursed technique to its limit, he unfolds his Domain Expansion Time Cell Moon Palace. The inside of the domain is a pitch-black space filled with faint floating particles and a single fleshy pathway leading to a massive eye behind the caster. Within this eerie setup, the domain’s guaranteed hit function begins. It forces its targets to automatically receive films of animation frames injected into their necks. Once those films are embedded, the domain’s rule takes hold — anyone moving inside must obey the 24FPS principle of Projection Sorcery. This effect takes Projection Sorcery’s mechanics and amplifies them to a microscopic scale. Instead of freezing a person’s full body when they break the rule, the domain targets individual cells. When the cursed technique hits, each cell in the victim’s body starts following the 24-frame rule. Any motion that doesn’t align with those frames tears the cells apart, causing countless small gashes and cuts to appear all over the body. The target quickly becomes paralyzed as their body breaks down under the pressure. Trying to force a movement makes things even worse — limbs can be severed completely. The true horror of Time Cell Moon Palace is how it guarantees a hit. Once a person is inside, the cursed technique automatically embeds itself into them, meaning there’s no dodging it. The moment they fail to move according to the 24FPS rule, their own motion becomes their execution. Naoya first unleashed this Domain Expansion after becoming a vengeful cursed spirit, during his battle against Maki, Hagane Daido, and Rokujushi Miyo. He managed to trap Daido and Miyo inside the domain, and both instantly had strips of cursed film injected into their necks. At first, nothing seemed to happen, making them think the domain had malfunctioned. But when they tried to attack, their bodies were shredded by invisible cuts. Dozens of thin wounds appeared across their skin, bleeding heavily as they collapsed from the cellular damage. Miyo was completely immobilized, and Daido tried to retaliate using the Split Soul Katana. The moment he swung, his hand was sliced off, severed by the backlash of his own movement. The domain punished every action he made until he could no longer fight. However, Naoya’s Domain Expansion had one fatal weakness — it couldn’t recognize anyone without cursed energy. Since Maki has almost no cursed energy due to a Heavenly Restriction, she was completely unaffected by Time Cell Moon Palace’s rules. She could move freely inside the domain, ignoring the 24FPS restriction entirely. Taking advantage of this loophole, Maki closed in and attacked Naoya from behind, ending him before he could react.

8. **Graveyard Domain (Smallpox Deity)**

During the Shibuya Incident, Mei Mei and her brother Ui Ui encounter Pseudo-Geto on the Fukutoshin Line platform underground. Before fighting them directly, Pseudo-Geto summons a special grade curse — the Smallpox Deity — to test Mei Mei’s strength. Without hesitation, the curse activates its Domain Expansion, dragging Mei Mei into one of the most dangerous environments in the series. The inside of the Smallpox Deity’s domain looks like a graveyard. It’s quiet, eerie, and covered with tombstones stretching across the area. As soon as the domain forms, Mei Mei is trapped inside a coffin. The curse begins chanting, and a massive gravestone drops from the sky, slamming down onto the coffin. The coffin is sealed, and the Deity starts a slow three-second countdown. If the target doesn’t escape before the count finishes, they’re killed by the curse’s infection — a deadly, guaranteed kill condition. Mei Mei manages to break free before the countdown ends, shattering the coffin and avoiding certain death. She takes a moment to assess the situation, realizing that it’s been a long time since she’s actually felt her life in danger. But before she can regain her footing, another gravestone crashes down, burying her once again. The curse repeats the same process — coffin, gravestone, countdown — but Mei Mei breaks free for a second time. It’s at this point she starts piecing together how the domain works. The Smallpox Deity’s domain is built around a three-step guaranteed hit: first, it traps the target in a coffin; then it buries them under a gravestone; and finally, if they fail to escape before the three-count is over, the curse infects them with smallpox and kills them instantly. The death isn’t immediate when the domain is activated — it only becomes certain once the countdown finishes. Mei Mei realizes the real danger isn’t the infection itself but the gravestone’s weight. Being crushed multiple times could leave her immobilized, and that would make her an easy kill. She also notices that the curse seems to automatically target the person with the strongest cursed energy in the area. Two of her crows are caught inside the domain, and she decides to test her theory by increasing one crow’s cursed energy. The curse immediately locks onto it and kills it on the spot, confirming her suspicion. With that information, Mei Mei forms a plan. She has Ui Ui take over as the main target by releasing his cursed energy while she suppresses hers. She knows it’s a risky move — if Ui Ui gets hit by the gravestone twice, he might not survive. Still, he agrees without hesitation. As soon as Ui Ui’s energy flares, the Smallpox Deity reacts and traps him inside a coffin. Before the curse can complete its hand signs to summon the gravestone, Mei Mei strikes. She leaps forward and slices the curse’s hands clean off with her axe, halting the process entirely. The attack forces the curse to retreat for a moment. Mei Mei uses this time to explain that Ui Ui isn’t just there to assist — his real role is to counter domains. Inside the coffin, Ui Ui activates New Shadow Style: Simple Domain, a barrier technique designed to neutralize domain effects within a small space. With his Simple Domain, Ui Ui shields himself from the curse’s fatal sequence while Mei Mei prepares for the final strike. The Smallpox Deity reappears behind Mei Mei, its hands regenerated and ready to attack. But before it can act, her last remaining crow flies through the air and pierces straight through the curse’s body. The hit is clean and lethal, completely exorcising the curse. The crow dies instantly as part of the technique, collapsing as the domain dissolves around them. After the battle, Mei Mei explains the full scope of her technique to Ui Ui. Her innate technique is Black Bird Manipulation, which allows her to control crows infused with cursed energy. The finishing move she used against the Smallpox Deity is her extension technique, Bird Strike. It forces the controlled crow to self-destruct by overloading its cursed energy far beyond normal limits, turning it into a one-shot projectile capable of destroying special-grade curses. Mei Mei notes that no one other than Satoru Gojo has ever survived a Bird Strike. As the domain fades completely, Mei Mei and Ui Ui step back onto the platform to face Pseudo-Geto. She’s unharmed but clearly impressed by the power of the Smallpox Deity’s Domain Expansion. Unlike most domains that deal direct physical or cursed energy damage, this one kills through a ritual of entrapment and infection. The Smallpox Deity’s domain may look simple, but it’s one of the most structured and inescapable domains shown so far. By breaking that cycle multiple times, Mei Mei proved just how dangerous this kind of domain can be — one that doesn’t rely on sheer destruction, but on absolute control over life and death through its unbreakable conditions.

9. **Horizon of the Captivating Skandha (Dagon)**

Horizon of the Captivating Skandha is Dagon’s Domain Expansion, created through his innate technique, Disaster Tides. When activated, it manifests as a calm tropical paradise — a long stretch of beach lined with palm trees and shallow blue waters that go on forever. It looks peaceful, but inside this serene setting is one of the most dangerous domains where even a single mistake means getting eaten alive — literally. Dagon can trigger the domain either by forming a hand sign or by drawing a cursed seal on his stomach. Once it’s expanded, everything inside it becomes part of his territory. Within this ocean-like space, Dagon’s cursed technique goes into overdrive. His innate ability allows him to summon aquatic shikigami, but inside the domain, it evolves into a sure-hit effect called Death Swarm. Every fish, piranha, and sea creature created by Dagon automatically hits its target — no aiming, no warning, no escape. The terrifying part is how these attacks work. From the target’s point of view, the shikigami don’t swim up or charge toward them. They just appear — materializing right where the victim stands and immediately tearing into their flesh. There’s no visual cue, no buildup, and no chance to block, since the fish only exist at the exact moment they make contact. That’s how Dagon’s domain ensures a guaranteed hit. The domain itself first shows up in a more relaxed setting back when Dagon is still a cursed womb. He uses it to create a peaceful beach where he lounges in the water while Mahito and Jogo sit nearby. Even Pseudo-Geto comments on how calm Dagon’s domain feels, showing how in control Dagon is of his environment. But once Dagon evolves into a fully realized cursed spirit during the Shibuya Incident, the Horizon of the Captivating Skandha turns into a full-blown death trap. When Dagon activates it in battle against Naobito, Nanami, and Maki, the entire area transforms into his beach world. Nanami and Maki are immediately bitten by invisible piranha shikigami, bleeding before they even realize what’s happening. The auto-hit function of the domain kicks in instantly — the fish spawn the moment they “hit,” leaving no time to react. Dagon floods the entire domain with his Death Swarm, unleashing countless shikigami that attack from every angle. Nanami and Maki are quickly overwhelmed, while Naobito counters the guaranteed hits using the Zenin Clan’s Secret Art: Falling Blossom Emotion, a technique that negates the sure-hit effect of Domain Expansions. Even with Naobito’s counter, Dagon’s advantage inside his domain is massive. Every inch of the beach and water is under his control, and his shikigami seem endless. He pushes the sorcerers back and nearly wipes them out until Megumi suddenly expands his own domain — Chimera Shadow Garden — inside Dagon’s. That triggers a domain tug-of-war, where the two territories overlap and start canceling out each other’s effects. With Megumi’s domain disrupting Dagon’s sure-hit feature, the sorcerers finally have a chance to fight back. Dagon redirects his focus toward Megumi, sending two massive fish-shaped shikigami straight from his body to crush him. Nanami intercepts them by jumping across Megumi’s shadow toads and slicing them apart with his Ratio Technique. Dagon is stunned that Nanami lasted through Death Swarm for nearly a minute. Before he can react, Naobito appears behind him, moving at incredible speed, and strikes. Dagon barely manages to block the attack, only to find Maki right behind him, destroying one of his eel-like shikigami with her weapon, Playful Cloud, while Naobito crushes the other. Despite being cornered, Dagon still holds the advantage. The beach remains his domain, and as long as he maintains it, any sorcerer inside is constantly under threat of guaranteed attacks. But Megumi reveals his true strategy — he’s not trying to win the domain battle; he’s trying to open an escape route. He knows Dagon likely can’t use his domain twice in a single day, so forcing him to end it once would tilt the fight in their favor. As the group coordinates, Nanami calls the Zenins to his position. They sprint toward him as a hole begins forming in the ground beneath Megumi, leading outside the barrier. Dagon realizes too late what’s happening and rushes in to stop them, but before anyone can react, a "resurrected" Toji Fushiguro bursts through the hole and enters the domain. Toji’s entry completely changes the situation. Because he has zero cursed energy, the guaranteed-hit function of Horizon of the Captivating Skandha doesn’t register him as a target. To Dagon’s domain, Toji might as well not exist. This lets him move freely inside the ocean-like space while every attack meant for others completely ignores him. He tears through Dagon’s body in seconds, killing the cursed spirit and shattering the domain from the inside.
