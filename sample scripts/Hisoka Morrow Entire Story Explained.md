**Hisoka Morrow Entire Story Explained**

* **Introduction**

Hisoka Morrow is the super sus-yet-captivating magician of *Hunter x Hunter*. Although normally stealing the show with his battle genius and mid fight gooning sessions (show clip of hisoka being weird when he's fighting gon at heavens arena), his origins and early life have always been a mystery. That is until today as we go through Hisokas entire life from beginning to end. From a young circus performer, to the undead monster he is at this very moment. And it all starts right now

**Background**  
Hisoka doesn't talk about his past, mostly because he couldn't care less about it. What we do know is that sometime after the Kurta clan massacre, he snuck his way into the Phantom Troupe by pretending to be one of them and eventually taking over the \#4 spot. The *Hunter x Hunter Movie: Phantom Rouge* explores an original backstory of this by showing him defeating a Soul Doll copy of the original \#4 member, Omokage. But since that movie isn't canon, it's more of a bonus story than actual backstory. Around that same time, Hisoka introduced Kastro to Nen at Heavens Arena because he sensed some real potential in the guy. Not long after, he joined the 286th Hunter Exam where he casually murdered 20 people, nearly killed an examiner and got disqualified. By the time he makes his debut in the series, he’s back again for another shot at the Hunter license. As for his actual origin story, Togashi keeps it vague. The only canon hint we’ve gotten is that Hisoka named his Nen abilities after a candy brand and chewing gum brand he liked when he was a kid — suggesting his favorite treats may have been named "Texture Surprise" and "Bungee Gum" respectively. But here’s where things get interesting: Sui Ishida, the creator of *Tokyo Ghoul*, once wrote a one-shot manga exploring Hisoka’s early days. It started as an April Fool’s joke, but Togashi liked it enough to make it "kinda" official. Whether it’s canon or not is still debatable, but the story gives us a rare peek into a younger, less-polished Hisoka. The story takes place in Glam Gas Land, a flashy little corner of the world known for its over-the-top performances and constant hunger for entertainment. That’s where a young, bloodied Hisoka is found lying in the street by Moritonio, the eccentric ringmaster of a traveling circus. Moritonio is prepping for a big show at the prestigious Bar Jack Princess Hotel, and when he asks the strange kid if he knows any tricks, Hisoka casually pulls off a card trick that blows him away. From that point on, Moritonio sees raw, untapped talent — and invites him into the troupe. Not everyone’s thrilled. Abaki, one of the performers, is immediately suspicious. You don’t just find kids lying in the road without some baggage. While out in the marketplace, she and Moritonio spot a wanted poster for a serial killer dubbed “John Doe,” known for crushing his victims and constantly changing faces, earning him the nickname “Hundred Face.” The guy is a killer and a showman as he makes sure he's seen by security cams just for the attention. Back at the circus, one of the jugglers injures himself, and Hisoka steps in to fill the gap. He performs with ridiculous precision and quickly earns the spotlight. This doesn’t sit well with fellow performer Yasuda, who tries to sabotage him mid-performance. Despite the badly thrown batons, Hisoka performs well and owns the moment. Yasuda storms out, and the rest of the troupe starts training in secret to avoid giving Hisoka any more ideas. As time passes, Hisoka starts noticing strange things — like a faint shimmer around Moritonio. Turns out, it’s aura. Moritonio begins teaching him the basics of Nen and he picks it up way faster than he should. Abaki eventually explains more about Moritonio’s past: he once tried to become a Hunter, failed miserably, and never attempted it again. But he clearly still wants a connection to that world — maybe through a student who can succeed where he couldn’t. Abaki herself vows to take the Hunter Exam, too. Hisoka’s progress is insane. What normally takes a year to learn, he masters in days. He finds out he’s a Transmuter, just like Moritonio. Not long after, Abaki goes missing during a trip to town. She’s attacked by a man she believes is John Doe, and although she tries to fight back using Nen, he nearly kills her. Hisoka shows up just in time to save her, slashing the man’s eye and forcing him to retreat. Later, Hisoka performs at the Royal Glam Hotel in front of the powerful Glam Clan. The act is a hit, and the troupe gets their moment of fame. But after the show, he confronts Moritonio. He’s figured out that Moritonio is John Doe and calls him out. Moritonio admits everything: he’s obsessed with surprising people and even killed his own sister just to see her reaction. The circus was his attempt to keep those urges “controlled,” but it clearly didn’t work. Then a fight ensues. Moritonio uses his face-swapping Scarf Ace ability and traps Hisoka using magnetic bars with his Blood Magnet Nen technique. But he pulls out a trick Moritonio never taught him: he uses Gyo and spots the bars. He escapes the trap, turns the tables, and finally debuts his own Nen ability: a Transmutation-type power that mimics the properties of both rubber and gum — calling it Bungee Gum. Using it, he redirects the magnetic bars back at Moritonio and kills him. Before leaving, he changes Moritonio’s face to the same one he wore when he attacked Abaki, framing him posthumously as John Doe.

* **Hunter Exam Arc**

Hisoka makes a grand return in the 287th Hunter Exam as applicant \#44 and already infamous for causing chaos in the previous exam. From the jump, he doesn’t waste time reminding everyone why he's a problem — an unlucky applicant bumps into him, and not long after, that guy’s arms are gone. The First Phase kicks off with a deceptively simple task: follow the examiner, Satotz, through a never-ending underground tunnel which turns out to be over 80 kilometers long. And while most are gasping for air, he breezes through like it’s a light jog. Then comes the real fun: the Milsy Wetlands. This place is basically a death trap full of fog and man-eating creatures that disguise themselves as people. As they begin to cross the swamps and reach the end of the phase site, a man suddenly appears, claiming to be the real examiner and accuses Satotz of being a Man-faced Ape who infiltrated the exam to trick the applicants. He takes down most of them with no effort until Kurapika and Leorio are left. The two run in opposite directions to increase their chances and Hisoka seems impressed by the tactic. But when Leorio comes back to fight him head-on, he toys with him and ends up getting blindsided by Gon’s fishing rod when the latter came back to search for his friends. Instead of getting angry, he gets intrigued by these brave applicants. He knocks out Leorio, sizes Gon up, and declares they “pass” the test. After that, he casually walks off with an unconscious Leorio over his shoulder and slicing through any monster that gets in his way until they reach the destination. The Second Phase, run by Gourmet Hunters Menchi and Buhara, involves cooking. But things get troublesome when the strict Menchi demands sushi for the second round. Like everyone else, he fails miserably — until Chairman Netero steps in and changes the rules. Menchi admits her mistakes and takes them to Split Mountain where she demonstrates to retrieve a Spider Eagle egg. She then instructs the applicants to do the same and boil the eggs. With the revised challenge, Hisoka manages to clear the challenge and passes the Second Phase. Onboard the airship that will take them to the Third Phase site, the examiners note that Hisoka gives off a ridiculous amount of bloodlust and seems to be testing them just as much as they’re testing him. Satotz even says Hisoka is on their level. In Phase Three, the candidates are brought on top of the Trick Tower and are given 72 hours to reach the bottom. As Hisoka descends into the bottom floor, he gets ambushed by Togari, the former examiner he attacked in the previous exam. Togari is able to cut Hisoka’s shoulder with his knives but Hisoka shrugs it off  and catches the guy’s weapons mid-spin before decapitating him. Hisoka couldn’t care less who his assigned target is — he just wanders around and waits for fun to find him. Gon, who's been secretly tailing him, gets close thanks to the butterflies attracted to Hisoka's bleeding shoulder. Before Gon makes a move, Hisoka is challenged by a dying warrior named Goz, but refuses to fight someone already on death’s door. Gittarackur steps in to grant Goz his final battle and then hands Hisoka a badge. Gittarackur then reveals his true appearance and turns out to be Illumi Zoldyck in disguise. As night sets in, Hisoka finally decides it's time to move and complete the test by collecting random badges. He runs into Leorio and Kurapika again. Impressed by their growth, he accepts trading a badge for free and lets them walk away. But he still needs a kill to let off some steam — he finds Agon and ends him immediately. Right then, Gon makes his move and successfully steals his badge. Amused and impressed, he follows Gon's trail and ends up killing his actual target Geretta who happens to have targeted Gon, incapacitating him with his poison dart. He gives his badge and the boy’s badge back to him while recognizing his potential, saying he had already completed the necessary points by getting Geretta’s badge and the badges he got from Gittarackur, Kurapika, and Agon. Gon is not thrilled to owe him anything and refuses the badges but Hisoka did this (Episode 16 20:28 \- 21-02). With the one-week time limit up, Hisoka is among the 9 examinees who pass the Fourth Phase. When interviewed by Chairman Netero and asked why he wants to become a Hunter, he said that a Hunter License could give him the freedom to do whatever he wants, especially the stuff most people can’t get away with. He’s got his eyes on Killua and Gon, but says he’s in no hurry to fight them yet. He then challenges Netero, who just brushes him off like an annoying fly. The final round is a one-on-one elimination tournament. Win your match and you become a Hunter. Lose, and you still have a shot — but you’ll need to win later fights. Hisoka watches Gon's match with Hanzo closely, intrigued by how far the kid’s willing to go and forcing his opponent to surrender. * **Heavens Arena Arc**

After Gon, Kurapika, and Leorio pull off a successful “rescue mission” at the Zoldyck estate, the squad parts ways for now with a promise to meet up again on September 1st in Yorknew City. With 6 months to go before their reunion, Gon and Killua head straight to the Heavens Arena to train and earn money at the same time. Gon decides to settle the score with Hisoka first before returning home to Whale Island. But of course, Hisoka’s already a few steps ahead as he’s waiting for them at Heavens Arena. When the boys reach the 200th floor, Hisoka blocks their path using Ren, basically as a way to test them. With the two unable to break through his wall of aura, he tells them flat out that they’re not ready to step into his domain yet. So, Gon and Killua head back to learn the basics of Nen from Wing. Once they’ve got Ten down, they return, and this time Hisoka lets them through. He even throws Gon a bone — win one match on this floor, and maybe he will fight him. Gon’s fight against Gido catches his eye, especially when Gon enters Zetsu mid-battle. Then comes his fight with Kastro, a fighter still stewing over his past loss to Hisoka. This time, Kastro starts strong. He’s landing hits from impossible angles and jumping to a 4-0 lead like he’s figured him out. But Hisoka remains calm, even when his right arm gets sliced clean off. Turns out, he’s letting Kastro show his hand which turns out to be a Nen clone. With the secret out, he gets serious and uses magic tricks and sleight-of-hand mid-fight to distract his opponent. When Kastro sends his clone and severs his left arm, his supposedly lost right arm is suddenly back. This is because he tricked Kastro by hiding his severed right arm using Bungee Gum and placed it back using Texture Surprise to give the illusion that his right arm is fully restored. The moment Kastro realizes he’s in deep trouble, Hisoka finishes it. One hit to the chin using his severed left arm, then a final blow with cards to seal his victory. After the match, Machi shows up to stitch Hisoka’s arms back together. She uses Nen threads to reattach both arms, while Hisoka uses Bungee Gum and Texture Surprise to hide the damage. We also learn that Hisoka had been using In the whole time during his fight to keep parts of his aura invisible, giving him the upper hand. He was setting up every move mid-fight without Kastro ever catching on. Machi drops a reminder that the Phantom Troupe is meeting in Yorknew City at the end of August, and Chrollo’s not going to be thrilled if he ghosts them again. He shrugs it off, even tries to flirt, but Machi’s out before he finishes his sentence. Later, while showering, he casually peels off the fake spider tattoo from his back which turns out to be made with Texture Surprise. This reveals that his spot in the Troupe is just another layer of misdirection. Meanwhile, Gon clears his matches against Gido and Riehlvelt and declares he’s ready for Hisoka. They agree to fight on July 10th. Come fight day, he is practically vibrating with anticipation. At first, he barely moves as he casually dodges and blocks everything Gon throws at him. But then, Gon comes up with this move (Episode 35 20:47 – 21:25). After landing a clean punch on him, Gon approaches him and gives his badge back — and he takes it. It’s symbolic — it's Gon’s way of saying, “I’m ready now.” and Hisoka acknowledging his strength. Hisoka then cranks things up. He figures out Gon’s an Enhancer, drops the Nen personality test info and reveals himself as a Transmuter. From there, it’s all downhill for Gon. He is faster, stronger, and straight-up playing with him. Gon tries to keep his distance, but Bungee Gum reels him right back in for a brutal hit. He then messes with Gon mentally, asking him to guess when he attached Bungee Gum. None of the answers Gon picks are right because the real answer wasn’t even offered. When Gon charges him head-on, he’s so thrilled that he has to consciously pull himself back from actually killing Gon. He tanks a few hits just to savor the moment before countering with a punch. Then, while Gon argues with the referee after giving Hisoka an extra knockdown point, he sneakily attaches Bungee Gum to a rock and yanks Gon into a surprise punch, ending the match with another victory and the eligibility to challenge a Floor Master. As he walks away, he tells Gon their next fight won’t be just a match. It’ll be the real deal. To the death.

By the night of September 1st, a hot air balloon transports some members of the Troupe to the auction site. After Kurapika kills Uvogin, the Troupe sets out to hunt down the “Chain Dude” for revenge, with Hisoka keeping mum on the target’s identity. * **Succession Contest Arc: Chrollo vs Hisoka Showdown** (Ch 351 – 357\)

After getting his Nen restrictions removed by Abengane, Chrollo finally agrees to a deathmatch with Hisoka at Heavens Arena. The moment the fight kicks off, Chrollo wastes no time — he activates Shalnark’s ability, controls the referee like a puppet, and uses him to attack Hisoka. Turns out, Chrollo can now use multiple abilities at once and doesn’t need to hold his book anymore. He’s thrilled — it’s exactly the kind of challenge he lives for. Chrollo starts mixing and matching stolen powers: he uses Order Stamp and Kortopi’s Gallery Fake to turn the ref’s copy into a puppet. Hisoka quickly figures it out and disables it by decapitating it, but Chrollo’s just getting started. He throws Convert Hands into the mix and keeps the pressure high. Chrollo then casually reveals that one of the powers he’s using, the Sun and Moon, came from someone who’s already dead, which means their Nen is way stronger now. Hisoka realizes this is a problem — especially because this post-mortem Nen ability has effects that won’t disappear unless they explode. Chrollo's confidence is sky-high, but Hisoka isn't backing down. He charges back in. But things escalate when Chrollo disappears into the crowd, disguises himself with Convert Hands and uses Black Voice to make the audience attack Hisoka. With about thirty puppets rushing at him, he pulls back using Bungee Gum, but they pile up and catch him anyway. Chrollo sneaks in another hit while he’s fending them off. He fires back with a sneaky move using a severed head, and actually manages to land a hit, but it’s not enough to stop Chrollo completely. As the battle drags on, he tries to figure out Chrollo’s plan by watching which abilities deactivate. He finally catches someone he thinks is Chrollo and pins him with Bungee Gum — only to realize it was just a look-alike puppet. Chrollo had used Convert Hands again to throw him off. But Chrollo’s set a trap: with more explosive puppets waiting for him. One blast sends him flying straight into another batch of them. He tries to escape but gets knocked down into the ring, where he’s finally overwhelmed by an explosion. Next time we see him, Hisoka is dead. Or... seems to be. * **Succession Contest Arc: The “Real” Hisoka Appears** (Chapters 405 – 406\)

On the eleventh day of the voyage, Hisoka finally shows up — and he does it in classic Hisoka fashion. Not with a bang, but with a poker game. He sits down in Tier 1’s casino to play “SQ-X,” a fancy poker variant. Spectators gather to watch, buzzing about the rules while Hisoka casually draws cards twice before revealing his hand and calling the game. It’s not about the money for him — he’s just here to pass the time. He even lands a jackpot spin with triple sevens on the slot machine, but walks away without claiming the prize. As he roams the casino, he sinks into his own thoughts, thinking less about money or glory and more about deadly, intimate battles that really get his blood pumping. He reflects on why the Chimera Ant conflict left him cold as it wasn’t personal enough. Even the rumbling Nen beasts didn’t do it for him. He reassures himself that he’s “normal,” in his own twisted way. He’s not here for beasts or crowd-control spectacles. He just wants a one-on-one deathmatch with someone worthy. While he’s enjoying his solitude, Bonolenov — currently disguised as Hisoka — spots him from across the casino. Chrollo had asked Bonolenov to take Hisoka’s form as part of a plan, but now that the real deal’s out and about, sticking around like that is a death sentence. The next day, Hinrigh and Zakuro stumble across Lynch’s corpse. Zakuro was left alive, likely as a way to tip off the rest of the Mafia that “Hisoka” had been found. They both realize someone is impersonating Hisoka not just to fool others, but to disrupt the Mafia’s search and buy themselves time. Meanwhile, on Tier 3, the crowd is preparing for Prince Halkenburg’s funeral procession. Chrollo is among them, silently watching. While everyone else mourns, he’s got other things on his mind — namely, tracking Hisoka and locating Kakin’s royal treasures. He tries to use a conjured cell phone via Nen, but the signal’s weak and he can’t make contact. Frustrated, he debates whether to head up to Tier 1 despite the risks. Hisoka might be up there, but so are all the strongest players and Nen users in the succession war. If Chrollo can get his hands on them, he believes he can upgrade his Skill Hunter ability that allows him to steal some OP Nen power that can finally beat Hisoka for good. There’s a condition, though. To unlock this power-up, he first has to steal something equivalent to a national treasure before he even activates the ability. And the three sacred treasures of Kakin fit the bill. He’s convinced that Tier 1 is where the treasures are kept, protected more fiercely than even the King himself. Stealing one would give him the edge he needs in a rematch with Hisoka. Despite the looming confrontation, he isn’t rushing. He’s carefully setting the stage all while knowing the real Hisoka is somewhere close. He tries one more call, and this time the signal connects. His target has been found — but they’re somewhere beyond the Nen signal’s range, likely higher up. If he levels up Skill Hunter the way he’s planning, he could walk into that rematch with an arsenal so stacked that even Hisoka’s elastic, explosive tricks won’t be enough. Two predators. One ship. One unfinished fight. And this time, someone might actually die for good.
