**Cursed Techniques That Should Not Exist**

* **Comedian**

Comedian is Fumihiko Takaba’s cursed technique. Takaba starts out as a failing comedian, just a normal guy who gets turned into a sorcerer because Kenjaku needs more players for the Culling Game. When he was a kid, he was cold and distant. Other kids avoided him because he didn’t really know how to connect. So he started making jokes. And it worked. People laughed, got closer to him, and he finally made friends. That’s what pushed him toward comedy in the first place. But growing up didn’t make it easier. He struggles to find partners to perform with, and his career never really takes off. Before his awakening, he’s performing at a comedy club where he finishes his set to a bored crowd that barely reacts. Then he goes backstage, and his manager tears into him for how bad the show was. He tells him to quit comedy or do something else. He takes it hard, and it messes with him. Then the Culling Game happens, and he gets thrown into the worst situation possible. During the chaos, Megumi Fushiguro gets cornered by Reggie Star and Iori Hazenoki. Takaba shows up at the last second and steps in to protect him. He even taunts Reggie and Hazenoki, calling them cowards for jumping a teenager together. Takaba’s cursed energy swells so much that Reggie and Hazenoki immediately go on guard. Then Takaba hits a dramatic pose and drops a word pun involving 'Wi-Fi'. His joke falls flat but that's just the opening act for what he's about to showcase. Comedian lets Takaba manipulate reality just by thinking something is funny. He just thinks something is funny, and reality goes along with it. The effects can cover almost anything, including conjuring objects or substances from nothing, healing without Reverse Cursed Technique, nullifying damage, and forcing imaginary scenarios into reality. It’s the kind of technique that makes you stop and wonder how this is even part of the same power system. He first shows it off when he gets injured by Hazenoki’s technique. Hazenoki uses explosive body parts, and one of his explosive teeth causes Takaba’s head to bleed badly. Then seconds later, the wound is just… gone. Takaba heals himself without using Reverse Cursed Technique. It just fixes itself like it never happened. Then he takes it even further. During his first exchange with Hazenoki, he conjures a paper fan out of nowhere and uses it to swat away Hazenoki’s explosive eyeball. Near the end of the fight, he uses Comedian again and covers himself in slippery ankake sauce. Hazenoki tries to punch him, and the punch slides off like he turned himself into a human slip-and-slide. Hazenoki is completely losing his mind at this point. He realizes Takaba should have died multiple times already from the explosions. But Takaba keeps walking around like nothing happened. Then Kogane informs Hazenoki that Reggie is dead. Hazenoki immediately decides to retreat because he understands he’s up against the worst possible matchup. Not because Takaba is smarter, or stronger, or has better combat instincts but because the fight stops making sense the moment Comedian activates. After that, Takaba meets Yuji Itadori. They work together and find an unconscious Megumi, who’s being carried through the air by Hana Kurusu. Eventually, the group reaches a hotel and settles into a room. When Takaba hears why Yuji and Megumi want to stop the Culling Game, he actually decides to help. As the fight between Sukuna and Gojo is about to begin, Angel suggests Takaba should go fight Kenjaku because she realizes Comedian could bypass Kenjaku’s defenses, making him the best candidate to counter the most experienced sorcerer in the entire series. And Takaba heads into a fight that should be a death sentence. Kenjaku attacks him with cursed spirits, and Takaba uses Comedian to nullify the damage. Kenjaku feels like the attack lands, but it does nothing because Takaba sees himself as a cartoon character who can't be harmed \- much like a slapstick comedy show where he can break the laws of physics to portray a cartoonish reality. To make things even more annoying for Kenjaku, Takaba pulls him into his comedy routine and just roasts him by randomly slapping outfits on him or making embarrassing words pop up on his face. Kenjaku immediately realizes how bad this is. Even with all his experience, he still won’t be able to deal with a player like Takaba normally. Takaba makes the same Wi-Fi joke he used back when he fought Hazenoki and Reggie, and Kenjaku responds by breaking down why the joke wasn’t funny. Then the fight turns into a debate about comedy, and Kenjaku starts feeding him actual entertainment knowledge. This conversation allows the ancient sorcerer to discover the weakness of Takaba's technique. Comedian only activates if he stays confident and truly believes he’s funny. But the moment he loses faith in himself, the technique shuts off and leaves him wide open. And Kenjaku targets it immediately. Takaba loses confidence, Comedian deactivates, and Kenjaku lands a heavy blow. Then Takaba stumbles on Hazenoki's body after Kenjaku took him out. That’s the moment the situation finally hits him. This isn’t a goofy stage anymore. Seeing Hazenoki’s corpse reminds him of a fight he had in college with one of his old comedy partners. Looking back, he realizes how ironic that argument was. But now he finally remembers why he became a comedian in the first place. He stands back up, reactivates Comedian, and locks in with one goal. He wants to make Kenjaku laugh. Kenjaku instantly summons the special-grade cursed spirit Akuro-o Otake and he responds by driving a truck through it and ripping it apart. Because Comedian keeps turning his imagination into reality, and the fight starts shifting through different scenarios as he thinks them up. Kenjaku soon realizes Comedian doesn’t only manifest Takaba’s ideas. It also integrates Kenjaku’s thoughts as feedback. Kenjaku understands brute force won’t settle this. So he changes tactics and manipulates the environment into a stand-up comedy stage with hundreds of people in the audience. Now Takaba and Kenjaku appear as a comedy duo on stage, performing to the approval of the audience and judges. Takaba gets exactly what he always wanted as he becomes the comedian everyone laughs at. Tears start forming in Takaba’s eyes because he realizes it’s going to end. Kenjaku consoles him and tells him not to cry because it would kill the mood. Then they return to reality where Takaba is on the ground defeated, dressed like a corpse, and he thanks Kenjaku who smiles and tells him he cracked him up. That’s when Yuta appears and slices Kenjaku’s head off as planned. Most cursed techniques have limits, conditions, and a clear trade-off but Comedian can heal, negate damage, create objects, and reshape situations without any normal restrictions. The only real cost is Takaba’s mental state. So if he ever fully masters his confidence, Comedian becomes one of the most illegal cursed techniques in the series.

* **Private Pure Love Train**

Private Pure Love Train is Kinji Hakari’s cursed technique. Hakari is a third-year student from Tokyo Jujutsu High, currently suspended because he doesn’t play well with authority. Instead of laying low, he runs the Gachinko Fight Club, an underground venue where sorcerers beat each other senseless for money. When Yuji and Megumi approach him for help with the Culling Game, he doesn’t agree right away. He challenges Yuji to a fight and overwhelms him with ease. Yuji refuses to back down, keeps pushing forward, and that persistence flips a switch in him. He decides to help, but only if they help him too. With the aftermath of the Shibuya Incident forcing changes in jujutsu regulations, he wants to use the chaos to legalize his fight club. Megumi convinces him this is possible using his authority as the new head of the Zenin clan. With that settled, Hakari chooses to head straight into danger. He enters the Tokyo No. 2 Colony, aiming to deal with Hajime Kashimo, the player with the most points and likely the strongest one there. Inside the barrier, Hakari ends up in an amusement park with a working ferris wheel. There, he meets Charles Bernard, an awakened player whose cursed technique lets him see into the future of his opponent. Hakari’s fight with Charles is where his innate technique fully comes into play. Hakari’s cursed technique is primarily applied through his Domain Expansion, Idle Death Gamble. The domain manifests a pachinko game themed around a fictional romance manga called Private Pure Love Train. Even outside the domain, he can summon parts of the game’s visual effects, like shutter doors. Once Idle Death Gamble is activated, Hakari’s goal is simple. He needs to hit a jackpot by lining up three matching symbols featuring the characters from the manga. If he succeeds, he wins a bonus round that completely breaks the rules of combat. The jackpot reward gives him unlimited cursed energy and a fully automatic Reverse Cursed Technique for four minutes and eleven seconds. That time limit matches the length of the Private Pure Love Train theme song, Admiring You, which plays out loud during the entire bonus round. The important part is what unlimited cursed energy actually means here. He has no restrictions at all. His body heals itself automatically where damage doesn’t linger and wounds don’t matter. For those four minutes and eleven seconds, Hakari is effectively unkillable. And that’s only the start. To reach the jackpot, Hakari has to play through the pachinko game. The base chance of winning is one in two hundred and thirty-nine. That sounds bad, but the domain bends over backwards to give him more chances. All the rules of the game are instantly transferred into the opponent’s brain through the domain’s sure-hit effect. This doesn’t hurt them, but it acts as a trade-off that allows the domain to activate absurdly fast, in under 0.2 seconds. That speed makes Idle Death Gamble extremely dangerous in domain clashes. The game begins in a neutral stage. During combat, Hakari can manifest three types of visual indicators. Shutter doors can be used to shield him or crush his enemies while pachinko balls function as offensive attacks by flicking them with extreme force. Consecutive effects act as defensive indicators that rewind sequences and undo damage. These indicators come in green, red, and gold, with higher rarity increasing the chance of hitting a jackpot. Rainbow indicators or triggering four consecutive effects in a row guarantee a jackpot outright. Hakari can choose which indicators to attempt, but the outcome is still luck-based. As indicators appear, the game shifts into riichi mode, revealing two of the three symbols needed for a jackpot. From there, one of four riichi scenarios plays out, each with different success rates. The final train riichi has over an eighty percent chance to hit. Each scenario changes the environment using scenes from the manga. One example is the transit card riichi, where the characters move through a train station and need to pass the gate on time. This scenario has low odds and usually fails without rare indicators. The opponent cannot interfere with these scenarios. They are forced to watch them unfold or defeat Hakari before they end. If Hakari fails to hit the jackpot, the stage resets and the number of remaining spins decreases. This loop continues until Hakari either wins or runs out of chances. On the first successful jackpot, the domain vanishes and the music starts. Hakari gains unlimited cursed energy and automatic healing for the duration of the song. During this state, he can also receive random bonus bursts of cursed energy on top of the infinite supply he already has. When the song ends, his cursed energy returns to normal while his cursed technique is already replenished. That means he can immediately expand his domain again if he wants to. And here’s where the technique really crosses the line. The conditions of each jackpot carry over into the next domain expansion. If Hakari lines up three odd numbers, the next game starts in increased probability mode. If he lines up even numbers, the game starts with faster spins. There’s also hidden probability mode, which looks like faster spins but actually increases the jackpot chance. Against Charles, Hakari lines up three number threes and instantly defeats him with a single kick during the bonus round. He then uses that same jackpot state to rescue Panda from Kashimo. During the fight with Kashimo, his infinite cursed energy lets him tank lightning attacks head-on. Even when Kashimo lands clean hits, Hakari shrugs them off and counterattacks immediately. Kashimo severs his arm with lightning, and he keeps fighting because he knows it will heal automatically. Once the music ends and his cursed energy drops, he immediately expands his domain again. Because he lined up odd numbers earlier, the game starts in increased probability mode. Kashimo realizes Hakari will likely keep hitting jackpots, but he also knows Hakari is vulnerable while the domain is active. In base form, Hakari gets outmatched by Kashimo’s physical ability. To survive, he uses consecutive effects to rewind brutal exchanges and erase damage. The risk of failing consecutive effects increases on later domain uses, but Hakari pushes anyway. He enters riichi mode again, draws two sixes, and triggers the potty emergency riichi. He and Kashimo even interact with the scenario together, which leads to another jackpot. The music starts again and he returns to his unkillable state. Because this jackpot used even numbers, increased probability won’t carry over next time. In the next round, he survives direct lightning strikes to the head. His automatic Reverse Cursed Technique heals his brain while he expels Kashimo’s cursed energy through his nose. With seconds left, his physical output spikes and he overwhelms Kashimo in close combat. Just as the round ends, Kashimo lands a fatal lightning strike. He can’t heal it in time, but he uses the last of his strength to expand his domain again. With no increased probability, Hakari should be back to the one-in-two-hundred-and-thirty-nine odds. But Hakari immediately hits a jackpot on the first spin due to hidden probability so he heals instantly. Kashimo tries poisoning him with chlorine gas next, but Hakari’s automatic Reverse Cursed Technique neutralizes toxins without him even thinking about it. With seconds left, Hakari chooses to finish the fight instead of retreating to cast the domain again. Kashimo attempts a steam explosion meant to destroy Hakari’s arm and stop him from forming hand signs. But Hakari anticipates this and he sacrifices the arm Kashimo is targeting while he uses cursed energy to shield the rest of his body. Kashimo surfaces, exhausted and out of cursed energy, convinced he’s won. Then Hakari emerges and knocks him out cold.

* **Copy**

Copy is Yuta Okkotsu’s innate technique, and yeah… it’s as broken as it sounds. It lets him replicate another sorcerer’s innate technique and use it like it’s always been his. In a series where most people are stuck with one cursed technique for life, Yuta basically gets a whole collection. He awakened Copy as early as his first year at Jujutsu High, and even back then, it was already unfair. He doesn’t “learn” techniques through talent or training. Copy lets him use innate techniques stored inside Rika Orimoto, the cursed spirit created from his deceased childhood friend. For Copy to work, Rika has to consume part of the original user’s body. And the amount matters. If the technique is strong, Rika needs a more vital body part to satisfy the condition. If Yuta wants to avoid taking something extreme, he can place a binding vow that limits how many times he’ll use the copied technique. Fewer uses means he can copy it with less. There’s also a limitation when the target can use Reverse Cursed Technique. If the consumed body part gets regenerated, that body part loses its value and stops meeting the condition for Copy. That means Yuta loses access to the technique, since the copied “payment” no longer counts. Once Rika consumes the body part, the cursed technique gets stored inside her. Initially, Yuta can only access those stored techniques when Rika is fully manifested. That storage part is important because if he tried to keep multiple techniques in his own brain, it would overload him. Rika basically acts like an external hard drive for cursed techniques. And when he accesses a technique, he doesn’t get a watered-down version. He can use the full ability, plus any extension techniques that come with it. He can swap tools depending on the situation, which makes him unpredictable in a way most sorcerers can’t handle. If you fight someone like Nanami, you know what kind of threat you’re dealing with. If you fight someone like Megumi, you expect shikigami and shadow tricks. If you fight Yuta, you don’t even know what genre the fight is going to be. Copy also messes with people mentally. Once opponents know Yuta can copy techniques, they start focusing more on the techniques he has stockpiled instead of thinking about the original users. People also assume he “stole” techniques rather than copied them, which changes how they react in battle. And that misunderstanding becomes a weapon. Yuta believes that’s the real strength of Copy. It makes opponents misread the entire situation. He first shows Copy in his fight against Suguru Geto. Through unknown methods, he previously copies Toge Inumaki’s Cursed Speech and stores it inside Rika. When Rika fully manifests, he uses Cursed Speech by manifesting a megaphone with the Snake Eyes and Fangs sigil. With that, he wipes out several weaker cursed spirits from Geto’s arsenal. Even after the Night Parade of a Hundred Demons, when Rika Orimoto’s soul is released from Yuta’s curse, a remnant of her stays behind. That vestige is still called Rika, and she continues protecting Yuta. This version retains the original cursed Rika’s skillset and still works as storage for copied techniques. Later on, during the Culling Game, Yuta enters the Sendai Colony and immediately starts folding the strongest players in the area. He defeats the top-scoring ancient sorcerer Dhruv Lakdawalla and copies his technique. Then comes the three-way fight against Ryu Ishigori and Takako Uro. Yuta fully manifests Rika to access his stored techniques, uses Cursed Speech to immobilize Uro, then injures her further with Dhruv’s technique. That combo catches her completely off-guard because she thinks Yuta is only a Cursed Speech user with shikigami. That’s the mind game part again. Copy does the same thing again during Yuta’s duel with Ryu who assumes Yuta is still running the same kit, so he tries to finish him off while he thinks Yuta’s technique is still recovering after using Domain Expansion. But Yuta’s cursed technique has already been replenished. He takes advantage of Ryu’s assumption and manifests Uro’s technique, redirecting Ryu’s Granite Blast into the air. He was able to do this because Rika ate Uro’s arm earlier, fulfilling the condition to copy her technique. Ryu gets caught off-guard again, and Yuta follows up with Thin Ice Breaker, landing a critical hit that heavily damages him. After Gojo gets freed from the Prison Realm, Yuta takes things to an even more ridiculous level. He has Rika consume Yuji’s left ring finger, which lets him copy Sukuna’s Shrine technique. During the month of preparation, Yuta also copies Angel’s Technique Extinguishment, which is part of the plan to free Megumi from Sukuna’s control. He also copies Charles Bernard’s G Warstaff. By the time Yuta fights Sukuna, he’s walking into battle with an entire arsenal of stolen-looking abilities. Besides a fully manifested Rika, Yuta can also tap into all his copied techniques when he's inside his domain Authentic Mutual Love. His domain creates a space filled with cross-like structures and countless katanas scattered everywhere. The whole area is wrapped in ropes tied together in a way that symbolizes love. Inside this domain, he can pick up any katana and activate copied cursed techniques without restrictions. One copied technique also becomes the sure-hit effect of the domain. Even Sukuna, the King of Curses, struggles to adapt when he’s trapped inside Yuta’s domain. Not because Yuta’s techniques are always stronger, but because the constant switching creates openings. Every time Yuta pulls out something new, Sukuna needs a split second to adjust, and that’s enough for Yuta to capitalize. Another scary detail is that the effects of copied techniques can stay active even if Yuta switches to a different one. He commands Sukuna not to move with Cursed Speech, then immediately switches to Uro’s technique and hits him with Thin Ice Breaker while Sukuna is still paralyzed. Even with all of that, Yuta still can’t defeat Sukuna. He barely survives the fight, which says a lot about how terrifying Sukuna is. But Copy’s potential doesn’t stop at combat versatility. Yuta had a last-resort plan in case Gojo lost to Sukuna and died. He wanted Rika to consume Kenjaku’s body and copy Kenjaku’s technique that lets him swap his brain into other bodies. If he could copy that, he could transplant his own brain into Gojo’s body and bring his mentor back to fight Sukuna again. That plan actually happens. Takaba’s fight with Kenjaku creates the opening he needs. He catches Kenjaku off-guard while he’s still under the influence of Comedian, decapitates him, and copies the technique soon after. Later, after Sukuna cuts him in half, he uses the brain transplant technique with help from Shoko Ieiri, Arata Nitta, and Rin Amai. He later returns to the battlefield in Gojo’s body to fight Sukuna once again. Long story short \- Copy breaks the idea of identity in cursed techniques which are supposed to be personal, rare, and tied to your birth. But Yuta turns them into inventory. And if he keeps stacking options as long as he's alive, the power ceiling stops being a ceiling and turns into an open sky.

* **Sky Manipulation**

Sky Manipulation is Takako Uro’s cursed technique. Uro is a jujutsu sorcerer from a thousand years ago who gets incarnated by Kenjaku for the Culling Game. Back in her first life, she was the captain of the Sun, Moon, and Stars Squad, a group of assassins tied to the Fujiwara Clan. A man from that family gave her a name, then immediately turned around and betrayed her. He framed her for his own crime after killing another member of his clan, and Uro basically got sentenced to death because of it. So yeah, she didn’t come back to the modern era in a great mood. Fast forward to the present day. Kenjaku incarnates her on the night of October 31, 2018, and she enters the Culling Game. By November 12th, she already racked up 70 points in the Sendai Colony. And her Sky Manipulation technique is a huge reason why, since it made her one of the four players stuck in that tense deadlock where nobody could easily wipe the others out. So what does Sky Manipulation actually do? It lets Uro control the “sky” like it’s a real physical surface. She can grab the surrounding atmosphere and pull it around like it’s a solid sheet or cloak. The effect works like distortion, where everything bends and warps depending on how she moves it. And she uses it casually too. She levitates with it like it’s nothing, and she even uses it to cloak her nude body as a substitute for clothing. That’s how natural this technique is for her. It’s always active in her movement, her defense, her positioning, everything. In a fight, Sky Manipulation turns her into a walking cheat code because her defense isn’t about blocking attacks normally. She defends by distorting the shape of her opponent’s attacks. So instead of taking a hit head-on, she twists the space around it and makes it miss, bend, or lose its clean path. Most techniques rely on aiming, distance, and momentum. She basically reshapes the battlefield itself. Now, there is a limitation. The surfaces she controls can’t directly crush her opponents. She shows all of this when she fights Yuta after he finishes dealing with Dhruv and seemingly exorcises Kurourushi. She jumps him immediately, and her opening attack blasts Yuta straight through the ground before he finally comes to a stop. Then she follows up fast with a punch that gets stretched even further thanks to her cursed technique, turning a normal strike into something way harder to dodge. When Yuta tries to counterattack, she bends his arm as he throws the punch, dodging it in the most disrespectful way possible. She pushes him away right after and starts explaining that her technique manipulates space as a surface. Then Ryu crashes the moment by firing a Granite Blast at them. And even that doesn’t solve the Uro problem, because she protects herself with Sky Manipulation. Now, there is a limitation to her technique. The surfaces she controls can’t directly crush her opponents. But she covers for that limitation by creating an extension technique called Thin Ice Breaker. Because she can break the surface of the sky like it’s a thin layer of ice, she then hits her target with the impact that comes from that break. That impact slips past guards and can cause massive destruction, which means she still has a nasty offensive option even without direct crushing. Since it’s an extension technique, it’s a more concentrated offensive use, letting her deliver a focused shockwave instead of just general distortion. During the three-way fight, Yuta gets sent flying by Ryu, and while he’s still mid-air, she ambushes him again with Thin Ice Breaker and blasts him straight into a nearby building. Ryu gets furious that she interrupted their fight, so he fires another cursed energy blast at her. She responds by redirecting the blast back at him using Sky Manipulation, injuring Ryu and destroying part of the building he was standing on. After Yuta sends Rika to keep Ryu busy, he confronts her directly. Yuta goes for another punch, but she blocks it, then uses Thin Ice Breaker right in front of his fist to stop the strike and destroy the metal casing around his arm. Later, she, Yuta, and Ryu all activate Domain Expansion at the same time. Then Kurourushi comes back, interrupts everything, and the barriers collapse soon after. She tries to defend herself, but her cursed technique fails to activate because it’s burnt-out from her domain expansion earlier. And she pays for it immediately. Kurourushi hits her arm with the Festering Life Sword, severing it, and Rika consumes it. She doesn’t even get time to recover mentally because Ryu hits her with a Granite Blast and knocks her unconscious. Since Rika consumed her severed arm, it fulfills the condition for Yuta’s innate Copy technique. That means Yuta can replicate and use her space-manipulating abilities too. Yuta even uses Thin Ice Breaker on Ryu and lands a blow so strong it instantly makes Ryu spit up blood. And later, after trapping Sukuna inside his Domain Expansion, Yuta uses Thin Ice Breaker together with a katana to strike the King of Curses. The slash gets amplified, overflowing with cursed energy, and it actually breaches Sukuna’s defenses and wounds the left side of his face.

* **Séance Technique**

Séance Technique is Ogami’s innate cursed technique. Ogami is a curse user who used to kill and collect money whenever she felt like it. Séance Technique lets her contact the dead and summon either their body or their soul’s information. From there, she can transform herself or a willing participant into that deceased person. The transformation has conditions, and even those conditions are terrifying. If she wants to shapeshift into someone through a summon, she needs their corpse. And if she transforms using their body’s information, the shapeshifter can use that person’s abilities. That means this technique isn’t only copying a face, it can copy the full combat package. Skills, physical traits, and everything that made the original person dangerous. Even worse, the technique is designed to keep going properly. It’s supposed to signal its own end once the shapeshifter’s cursed energy runs out, and it can continue even if the original caster dies. So once the process starts, killing Ogami doesn’t automatically solve the problem. The technique can outlive the user, which is already a huge red flag in a world where cursed techniques are supposed to have some kind of risk attached. Ogami’s track record proves how disgusting this ability can get in the wrong hands. At one point, she killed a wealthy young girl, then impersonated her using Séance Technique just to assassinate her father. She also kidnapped and raised multiple children as her “grandchildren” after falsifying their relationships so she could use them as willing vessels for her technique. Her crime spree only slowed down because of one thing: Satoru Gojo being born. She stopped because the world suddenly had someone who could crush her. Still, she wanted her old freedom back, so during the Shibuya Incident, she and her grandson sided with Kenjaku. When she fights Takuma Ino, her grandson protects her while she recites a long incantation. Once she finishes chanting, she activates her technique and summons the physical information of Toji Fushiguro into her grandson by speaking Toji’s birth name. That transforms her grandson into Toji. After the transformation, “Toji” defeats Ino, and she orders him to kill the other sorcerers waiting at the bottom of the tower. But then things go sideways in the worst way. Toji’s personality takes over her grandson completely, letting the dead Sorcerer Killer take full control of the body. Ogami panics and backs away, confused how Toji is in control when she only summoned his body’s information. Toji explains his body is special, and it overpowered her grandson’s soul. While technically following her command to kill, Toji turns on her and kills her. And since Séance Technique can copy abilities through body information, this isn’t just some cosplay transformation. This is a functional revival that can recreate a person’s threat level almost perfectly. So yeah, if Toji didn’t choose to end himself to prevent killing Megumi, the technique could’ve created a roaming disaster that Shibuya had absolutely no plan for. In a series full of unfair cursed techniques, this one is pure nightmare fuel because it turns death into something you can still lose to.

* **Brain Transplant**

Kenjaku is an ancient curse user who’s existed for over a thousand years, and he pulled that off using his innate technique Brain Transplant. As the name implies, this technique lets him transplant his brain into other bodies and control them, even if those bodies are already dead. After he takes a new body, it leaves a stitched scar running horizontally across the forehead, and he can’t heal it because of a binding vow. That scar is basically the only proof he’s even there, and it’s wild that the one downside is just a permanent cosmetic warning label. He can undo those stitches too. When he does, he can remove the top part of his head and reveal his original brain, which is as disturbing as it sounds. And once he’s inside a body, it’s not some shallow possession either. He gains access to the host’s cursed energy, all their innate techniques, and even their memories. He doesn’t just wear someone’s face, he becomes them so convincingly that even the Six Eyes can’t detect him as an imposter, since he retains the host’s cursed energy. That detail alone makes this technique feel illegal. The Six Eyes are supposed to be the ultimate “no tricks allowed” detector, and Kenjaku still slips past it. That means he can infiltrate the strongest circles in jujutsu society, act like someone everyone trusts, and nobody has a reliable way to call him out. This body-hopping also explains how he can live for over a thousand years. He just keeps swapping vessels, assuming identities, and moving through history like a parasite with a long-term plan. And that plan is huge. His ultimate goal is to force the evolution of humanity by merging all humans in Japan with Master Tengen, an ancient and insanely powerful being. He wants to unlock humanity’s true potential for cursed energy use and create a new, chaotic, evolved form of existence. The Culling Game exists as the method to reach that goal. He created it specifically to make the merger with Tengen possible. Across generations, he recruited sorcerers from different time periods and offered them participation as a second chance at life. For a lot of them, it’s the perfect deal, since they get reborn into a killing game where they can thrive as bloodthirsty warriors. Kenjaku also has a skill nobody else has. He’s the only sorcerer who knows how to transform someone into a cursed object after death. That’s how he stocked the Culling Game with reincarnated fighters in the first place. About 400 years ago, Kenjaku transplanted his brain into an unknown person’s body. Using that vessel, he made binding vows with past sorcerers to set up the Culling Game, particularly Hajime Kashimo. At another point in the past, Kenjaku transplanted his brain into Noritoshi Kamo’s body. And as Noritoshi, he ran some of the most horrifying experiments in the series. He experimented with fusing human flesh and curses, which resulted in the Death Paintings. Then there’s the most personal and messed up use of this technique. He transplanted his brain into the body of Kaori, Jin Itadori’s partner. While posing as her, he used her body to mate and give birth to Yuji who was created to serve as Sukuna’s vessel for the Culling Game. After Geto dies at the hands of Gojo, he transplants his brain into Geto’s body and uses him as his new host. That gives Kenjaku access to all of Geto’s memories, skills, and jujutsu. So now he’s wearing the face of one of Gojo’s closest friends, and he can weaponize that emotional connection whenever he wants. But the reason Brain Transplant truly crosses into “this should not exist” territory is the technique stacking. Kenjaku doesn’t just gain the technique of his current host. His body-hopping also lets him keep innate cursed techniques from previous vessels alongside his own. That includes Geto’s Cursed Spirit Manipulation with its maximum technique Uzumaki, and Kaori’s Antigravity System. He can only maintain a limited number of techniques at once, suggested to be around three to four total including his own, but even that is ridiculous. Most sorcerers are locked into one innate technique for life. Kenjaku gets to ignore that restriction and build a custom loadout over centuries. That’s why he’s able to become the mastermind behind two of the biggest jujutsu terrorist events in modern history: the Shibuya Incident and the Culling Game. In Shibuya, his main objective is sealing Gojo with the Prison Realm, removing the single biggest obstacle to his plan. Once Gojo is out of the way, he moves to trigger the Culling Game and force human evolution through the merger with Master Tengen, using Mahito’s Idle Transfiguration as part of the setup. So yeah, Brain Transplant is scary because it breaks the rules of identity, death, and limitations. It lets one person live forever, steal the best techniques, manipulate entire eras, and rewrite the future whenever he feels like it. That kind of ability doesn’t belong in any balanced world, and Kenjaku proves it by turning it into the most dangerous long-term plan in the series.

* **Star Rage**

Star Rage is Yuki Tsukumo’s innate cursed technique. Yuki is one of only four special grade jujutsu sorcerers, which already puts her in the “built different” category. She’s also not aligned with Jujutsu High, so her reputation makes her look like the only special grade who doesn’t take missions. In reality, she just refuses to play along with the higher-ups’ methods. She’s been working toward a curse-free world in her own way, and she doesn’t care if the system likes it or not. But when Kenjaku becomes an imminent threat during the Culling Game, she agrees to cooperate with Jujutsu High, specifically to protect Tengen. Even as an independent sorcerer who hated the higher-ups, she recognizes that Kenjaku’s plan to force human evolution and absorb Tengen would destroy her goal of creating a world without curses. So she shows up, not because she suddenly changed sides, but because Kenjaku’s plan is that dangerous. And a huge reason she even has special grade status is Star Rage. She’s so secretive about it that even Jujutsu Headquarters doesn’t have details on her ability. If the people running jujutsu society don’t even know what your technique does, you’re either a mystery… or a walking disaster they don’t want to acknowledge. Star Rage mainly boosts her hand-to-hand combat to an almost unfair level. She uses it to add “virtual mass” to her body and to her shikigami, Garuda, which massively increases the force behind every strike. The key detail is that the mass she adds is imaginary, but the impact it produces is very real. So she gets the hitting power of absurd weight without the usual downside of actually carrying it. With Star Rage active, Garuda becomes a cursed tool she can wield, which gives her even more ways to apply that ridiculous force. And the technique has extra layers that make it even worse. The added mass doesn’t weigh Yuki down, so she doesn’t get slower or crushed under her own power. But it also doesn’t increase her body’s tensile strength. That means she can hit like a monster, but her body isn’t automatically reinforced to match the damage she might take. Even with that limitation, Star Rage stays unfair because the mass affects anything she or Garuda are in contact with. If she grabs you while adding mass, she can weigh you down just by holding onto you. If she adds titanic amounts of mass, her body condenses into a black hole, creating extreme density and gravity. That’s not a “strong punch” anymore. That’s a doomsday option. During her fight with Kenjaku, he summons Ganesha to target the concept behind her technique. But mass is such an overwhelming concept that Ganesha can’t even handle Yuki’s first attack. Garuda folds into a ball, and she kicks it straight through Ganesha’s head like it’s nothing. She immediately follows with a straight punch boosted by virtual mass. Her strike punches through the arms guarding Kenjaku’s face and breaks them clean in half. The impact launches Kenjaku so far that he literally punctures the circular definition of the empty barrier they’re fighting in. While Kenjaku heals, she confidently lays out how her technique works, because she knows she has the advantage as long as her body can keep up. But Star Rage starts showing its drawbacks once she gets injured. Her output gets severely dampened, and even though her body is badly damaged, the technique isn’t enough to properly weigh Kenjaku down when Garuda wraps around him. Her physical attacks also can’t bring him down anymore because her injured body can’t push Star Rage to the level it needs. So she heals herself using reverse cursed technique. The problem is that this makes Star Rage even more ineffective, and it lets Kenjaku escape Garuda completely. Once she’s healed, she goes on the offensive again. She uses Garuda like a whip and tears apart the environment with several high-impact strikes. She corners Kenjaku fast, but he figures out the crucial weakness. Star Rage doesn’t boost Yuki’s durability. He takes advantage of that and fatally wounds her. Even after taking fatal damage, Yuki tries to drag Kenjaku down with her by continuously adding more and more virtual mass to herself until she turns into a black hole. That’s her final attack, a last attempt to kill him no matter what it costs. Kenjaku survives anyway by using Kaori’s Anti-gravity System, which is honestly the only reason he doesn’t get erased right there.

* **Mythical Beast Amber**

Mythical Beast Amber is Hajime Kashimo’s innate technique. Kashimo is a sorcerer from 400 years ago, now reincarnated in the modern era inside a vessel Kenjaku prepared. He joins the Culling Game as a player for one reason: he wants to find Sukuna and fight him because he just wants the strongest opponent possible. The moment he reincarnates, he enters the Culling Game in the Tokyo No. 2 colony and immediately starts wiping out other players. In only twelve days, he kills at least forty people and becomes the first player to reach 100 points. Later, he becomes the first person to add a new rule by spending 100 out of his accumulated 200 points. His cursed energy naturally behaves like electricity, and that makes every basic punch feel like a trap. Even when he’s just reinforcing his body, every hit comes with an electric shock that tears through defenses. Blocking doesn’t save you, because the damage isn’t only coming from the impact. It’s also coming from the current ripping through whatever you thought would protect you. His real threat comes from how he manipulates charges. He can plant a positive charge on his opponent while keeping the negative charge on himself. Once the conditions are satisfied, he releases it, and the lightning connects every time. No Domain Expansion needed. It’s just guaranteed contact once he’s ready. The only downside is that he needs time to recharge after using it, but most opponents don’t even survive long enough for that weakness to matter. They’re already done by the time they realize what’s happening. Kashimo also fights with a staff that acts like a lightning rod, storing and directing his energy. It fits him perfectly since it turns his cursed energy into something even more lethal. He mixes clean martial arts with raw strength, and when he needs to, he can snap limbs without slowing down. Everything about his fighting style is built to end fights fast. But even with all of that, his deadliest weapon is still his innate technique. Once he activates Mythical Beast Amber, it reconstructs his flesh so he can manifest any phenomenon that electricity can create. The source is his cursed energy, but the result looks like he turns himself into a living electrical disaster. This technique can boost his agility by increasing the activity of the brain’s electrical signals, making his movement speed spike hard. It also lets him produce sound waves that optimize and tune into the natural frequencies of substances, and he can release electromagnetic waves that vaporize irradiated objects. It’s ridiculous, because this isn’t a single power-up. Its multiple deadly effects stacked together under one technique, all coming from the same “electricity” theme. And since it pushes the user past human limits, it can only be used once. After the technique ends, Kashimo’s body crumbles and collapses. It’s the ultimate trade. He gets a temporary form that breaks the rules, and in exchange, his body basically deletes itself afterward. Kashimo even confirms this limitation earlier when he tells Hakari that he didn’t use his innate technique during their fight because he can only use it once, and he wanted to save it for Sukuna. After Sukuna defeats Gojo, Kashimo finally gets what he wants. He immediately faces Sukuna and uses technique release to activate Mythical Beast Amber early in the duel. As the technique reconstructs his body, his appearance gradually changes to match the different electricity-based phenomena he’s manifesting. First, parts of his hair shape into horns, and electricity starts pouring from his eyes. With the agility boost from his brain’s increased electrical activity, he blitzes Sukuna and lands a full combination of punches. He follows that with a thunderous roar that blasts Sukuna with a sonic wave. Then the technique evolves again. His mouth gets reconstructed with fiery cursed energy, and that energy spreads up and wraps around his upper body. His hands transform into sharp claws, and now he can unleash electromagnetic waves as energy beams fired directly from his palms. Even with all these upgrades, Kashimo still keeps access to his signature lightning discharge, the one that guarantees a hit without needing a domain. So Sukuna isn’t just dealing with new forms of damage. He’s also still dealing with Kashimo’s original “this will connect no matter what” attack. Kashimo even uses x-ray vision to analyze Sukuna’s fully incarnated form, and the cursed energy reconstructs his face again, giving him three eyes. The technique keeps rewriting his body to match what he needs in the moment, like it’s actively evolving mid-fight. Even with that insane performance boost, Sukuna’s true form overpowers him. In the end, Mythical Beast Amber ends up as one of the most unfair “one-time use” cursed techniques in the series \- basically a self-destruct button that turns you into a walking natural disaster for a short period of time.

* **Miracles**

Miracles is Haruta Shigemo’s innate technique. Haruta is a curse user who runs with Uraume and Kenjaku’s faction of evil sorcerers. He’s also a genuinely sadistic guy who enjoys tormenting anyone weaker than him. He even admits he’s a coward, since he prefers bullying weak opponents instead of challenging strong ones. And the funniest part is, he’s almost completely clueless about how his own cursed technique even works. He’s out here committing crimes with a technique that’s quietly carrying his entire life. His technique is called Miracles, and it does exactly what the name suggests. It stores small everyday “miracles,” then releases them when his life is in danger to save him. These aren’t huge supernatural events either. They’re tiny moments of luck that happen in daily life, and the technique steals them from his memory and saves them for later. Something as basic as looking at a clock and seeing all the same digits counts as one miracle. He doesn’t even remember these moments, because the technique erases them from his mind as payment. You can actually track how many miracles he has stored. The markings under his eyes show the count, and when a miracle gets used, those markings lose their color. So every time he survives something he shouldn’t, it’s because one of those stored miracles just got cashed in. Haruta at least understands the basics, since he recognizes that his technique saves him from would-be fatal hits, even if he doesn’t fully understand the details. If he still has miracles stored, reality basically bends just enough to keep him alive. During the Shibuya Incident, Uraume orders him to keep patrolling the curtain’s perimeter and hunt anyone wearing a suit, and he agrees immediately. That job turns into a massacre, since non-combat sorcerers like assistant managers are easy prey for him. He slaughters them without effort, because he’s exactly the kind of person who looks for defenseless targets. After patrolling for a while, he runs into Nobara Kugisaki and Akari Nitta and overwhelms both of them. Once the two girls are incapacitated, he comments on how strong Nobara has gotten, then starts gloating about how strength doesn’t guarantee a win, especially against his technique. With Akari unconscious, he starts torturing her, dragging it out just because he can, while he keeps taunting Nobara. Nobara forces herself back up, and he prepares to attack again, but he stops the moment Nanami shows up. He goes after Nanami first, but he can’t even injure him. Nanami punches him and sends him flying into a wall. He ends up stunned and bleeding, and he realizes that punch should’ve killed him if Miracles didn’t activate. He tries to run, but Nanami grabs him by the head and lands another hit to the stomach. Nanami asks if he killed the assistant managers, and he panics so hard he starts apologizing on the spot. After that, Nanami launches him into another building and knocks him out cold. Later on, he wakes up and stumbles across an injured Megumi. He goes full coward mode again and lands a sneak attack, critically wounding Megumi. But his sadistic streak ends up being his downfall, because he realizes too late that Megumi is setting up a shikigami exorcism ritual meant to kill him. Megumi summons Mahoraga, then gets knocked unconscious almost immediately, leaving him alone with the worst possible matchup. Mahoraga moves in to attack him, and he is basically done… until Sukuna arrives and saves him. He survives long enough to watch Sukuna fight Mahoraga, and after Sukuna wins, he is completely stunned. He just stares at the King of Curses like he’s witnessing the rules of the universe being rewritten. Sukuna then orders him to leave, and he happily takes the escape. He thinks he survived because of Miracles. The problem is that, all his stored miracles are already burned through after his encounter with Nanami. Now he’s finally out of luck. And the moment he runs out, the technique stops being a shield. Sukuna decides to cut him in half, and Haruta doesn’t get saved this time. Miracles is terrifying because it’s an automatic survival mechanic that doesn’t care about skill, bravery, or even awareness. The untapped potential is even worse, because if someone smarter had this technique, they could manage their miracles carefully, avoid wasting them, and become almost impossible to finish off in a long fight.
