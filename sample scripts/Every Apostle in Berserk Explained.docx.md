**Every Apostle in Berserk Explained**

* **Zodd**

Zodd the Immortal is a legendary warrior and apostle feared on battlefields for centuries. Known as “Nosferatu” Zodd, he’s earned his reputation over 300 years of war, killing countless opponents then suddenly disappearing for years, only to reappear and continue his slaughter. Despite his monstrous form, Zodd is not a mindless killer. Unlike other apostles, he respects strong opponents and takes no pleasure in torturing the weak, instead viewing battle as a pure contest of strength. Those who survive a fight with him, like Guts and Skull Knight, gain his admiration and, occasionally, his support as shown when he tosses his sword to Guts mid-fight to help him defeat Boscogn. While he mostly helped Guts to keep him alive until the Eclipse, this also showed his respect for Guts as a worthy rival. In human form, Zodd is a towering warrior with extraordinary strength and durability. He first meets Guts while defending a Tudor fortress and he is actually the first apostle Guts ever had encountered in his life. When Guts injures him, he commends the swordsman for being the first man to injure him in 300 years before he transforms into his Apostle form and fights Guts at full strength. In his released form, he becomes a towering beast with massive horns, bat-like wings, and incredible power. His wings allow for both aerial assaults and quick escapes, and his apostle body can withstand brutal attacks. Despite Guts and Griffith carrying out a pincer movement on him, he easily overwhelms them but abruptly stops when he spots Griffith’s crimson beherit and warns Guts of a dark destiny before flying away. As an Apostle, it is his duty to serve the God Hand and knowing that Griffith is destined to become one, he intervenes and kills Wyald when the latter forces Griffith to invoke the Eclipse prematurely. During the fated Eclipse, Zodd confronts the Skull Knight outside of the temporal junction point as he has no interest in slaughtering the Band of the Hawk. They both engage in an intense battle before Skull Knight ultimately overpowers him. Two years after the Eclipse, Zodd pledges his loyalty to the reborn Griffith, fighting alongside other apostles in liberating Midland from the Kushan Empire. He clashes with Guts again in the Hill of Swords but ultimately retreats per Griffith’s command. Their next meeting would be in Vritannis where they would go against Emperor Ganishka. But this time, Zodd teams up with Guts to battle the emperor’s mist form as both enter a temporary truce and vow to fight some other time. They face off again in Elfhelm after Griffith takes his form from the Moonlight Boy. This time, Zodd battles Guts to distract him away from Griffith, allowing his master to abduct the unconscious Casca. 

* **Wyald**

Wyald is an apostle and the leader of the Black Dog Knights, a group of criminals serving the Kingdom of Midland. Not much is known about his life before becoming an apostle, except that he was a small, frail old man. After his transformation, he became a convict at some point. One day, the King of Midland called for all able-bodied prisoners to be gathered for war and Wyald stepped up to lead the new army, claiming he was the strongest and that the position was rightfully his. To prove his leadership, Wyald had to face off against another contender, a massive man who challenged him for the position. Their duel ended dramatically with Wyald emerging victorious by sending his opponent flying onto a spike of a tower. From then on, Wyald and his Black Dog Knights gained a notorious reputation for being the most ruthless army in Midland, which ultimately resulted in the King sending them to remote battlefields to keep them out of sight. When the Band of the Hawk rescued Griffith from Wyndham, the King ordered the Black Dog Knights to hunt them down. As they pursued the Band of the Hawk, Wyald’s group stumbled upon a farm that had assisted Griffith and they caused a lot of chaos there. Wyald and his men fell into several traps during the course of their chase and as the battle escalated, Wyald decided it was time to unleash his Apostle form where he transforms into a gigantic ape-like monster. After a fierce fight with Guts, Wyald was seriously injured and was left for dead, with scouts assigned to guard his body. However, he was only pretending to be dead and managed to surprise the scouts before crawling to the Band of the Hawk's camp where he took Griffith hostage. Wyald’s intention was to start the Eclipse early to save himself but he panicked when he couldn’t find Griffith’s beherit. At that moment, Zodd intervened and impaled Wyald with his horns to ensure that the Eclipse happened at the destined time. Wyald pleaded for mercy but Zodd rejected his reasoning and brutally ripped him into half. In the end, Wyald’s essence was drawn into the Abyss while his body reverted to the frail old man he once was.

* **Unidentified Roaming Apostle**

This unnamed Apostle is the first apostle slain by Guts after the dreadful Eclipse. A month after the Eclipse, this apostle is roaming the countryside and ends up in the middle of a puddle. Two farmers come by on a horse-drawn cart and get frustrated at him for blocking the road and looking a bit out of place. But, just then, he catches a whiff of something that interests him – the "scent" of a sacrifice, which happens to be Guts and Casca from miles away. He immediately heads toward the direction of the scent and ends up in Godot’s home. Meanwhile, as Guts is getting ready to start his revenge quest, he senses something through his Brand of Sacrifice. Suddenly, the apostle is right there in the doorway. The apostle transforms and prepares to take Guts down. Unfortunately for him, he gets the first taste of the Dragon Slayer as Guts wields the massive swords for the first time and cuts the apostle in half. In the 2016 anime, we learn more about this apostle’s past. Known as the “Keeper of the Hounds”, he once worked for Count Lansdown as a keeper for the count’s prized hounds. He was an outcast with his master deeming him as “ugly and inferior,” but the dogs never judged him and they became his only companions. One day, one of the dogs finds a beherit and brings it to him. He later met the count’s daughter who treated him with kindness to which he mistook for ya know, her liking him. So when she didn’t return his affections, his despair activated his beherit and called the God Hand to him where he sacrificed her. Now transformed, the apostle couldn’t resist his urges and ended up harming her, eventually keeping the lady’s remains as “her memory” which he displays in the mansion’s foyer where he stayed, long after it had burned down five years before Guts and Puck arrive leading to his end.

* **Snake Lord**

The Snake Lord is the fearsome apostle ruler of Koka, living large in the town’s castle and keeping the locals terrified. This hulking tyrant keeps the mayor under his thumb while demanding piles of gold and human sacrifices as his “protection fee” \- or else the entire town suffers. One night, while he’s indulging in a meal of human flesh, he hears about a stranger named the Black Swordsman who has wiped out his henchmen in a pub. During this time, Guts has begun his quest to hunt down every Apostle for revenge. The mayor assures him that the Black Swordsman has been apprehended and offers him more gold and human cattle for forgiveness. However, he dismisses the mayor's desperate offerings. Instead, he reveals his real desire is to see humanity trampled under his heel in flames. Donning his battle armor, he sets out to destroy Koka and starts tearing through the streets with his soldiers and killing anyone in his way. Guts, who had just broken out of prison, gets caught amid the chaos and takes down his minions before challenging the Snake Lord directly. The apostle accepts and wields his halberd with deadly confidence. However, Guts quickly uses his repeater crossbow to shoot the Snake Lord off his horse and smashes him into a nearby building. The furious Snake Lord transforms into his Apostle form and overwhelms Guts. He then picks him up by his arm and mocks humanity’s weakness. But Guts seizes the moment and blasts off half the apostle’s face with his hidden cannon arm before using his massive Dragon Slayer sword to cleave him in two. Reduced to a torso, the helpless apostle finally realizes who he’s up against when he spots Guts’ Brand of Sacrifice. He desperately begs for his life but it’s too late for mercy. Guts leaves the Snake Lord to die under Koka’s burning rubble to bring an end to the apostle’s reign of terror.

* **The Count**

The Count is a notorious apostle ruling over a territory that Guts visits during his epic two-year war against apostles. Before he became an apostle, the Count was a nobleman in charge of a hold, living happily with his wife and daughter named Theresia. After coming back home from a long pagan-hunt, he finds his wife in the middle of a wild, heretical orgy. In a rage, he slaughtered everyone there \- except for his wife, who had a smirk that drove him to the brink of despair. This activated his beherit which led him to the God Hand. He ended up sacrificing his wife to become an apostle and started a twisted new life, lying to everyone about her being taken hostage by heretics. After his transformation, the Count got a bit obsessed with hunting down heretics. He pretended it was about justice, but really, it was just an excuse to gather more victims for his gruesome rituals where he’d vivisect and devour them. When his physician, Vargas, found out about his dark secret and tried to escape, the Count caught him, mutilated him, and forced him to watch as he ate his own family. What’s horrifying about the Count is his ability to partially shift his body into a slug-like form and fully transform into a monstrous slug in his released form, earning him his other name “Slug Count.” The night before the fifth Eclipse, he and other apostles feast on injured members of the Band of the Hawk but bailed when the Skull Knight showed up, leaving only Rickert alive. During the Eclipse, he joined the other apostles in a banquet and helped take down Pippin. After the Eclipse, the Count resumed his heretic hunts, but things got personal when Guts showed up and disfigured his captain of the guard, Zondark. When Guts escaped with the help of Vargas, the Count didn’t take it lightly. The Count infused Zondark with part of his own power to make him a powerful spawn. He captured Vargas and publicly executed him to set a trap for Guts, who was wise to the plan. Guts eventually stormed the Count's castle, ready to settle the score. The Count’s regenerative powers gave Guts a hard time but Guts found a way to exploit his vulnerable head\! The ensuing battle was so destructive that it opened the doors to Theresia’s room, allowing her to escape for the first time in years. Guts used Theresia as a shield to catch the apostle off guard and give him the chance to sever the Count’s head. In a panic, the Count activated his beherit lying on the ground and transported everyone to the God Hand's realm. But in order to grant him a second chance at life, the God Hand required a sacrifice from the Count, and the only person he could offer was his own daughter. Ultimately, his love for his daughter is greater than his desire to live, and when he finally succumbed, his spirit was dragged into the Abyss, leaving his lifeless body behind on a pile of rubble. Guts later took the Count’s beherit as a means of reaching the God Hand.

* **Unidentified Female Apostle**

This unidentified female apostle is actually the first one introduced in the series and also the first to be defeated. She shows up during the Eclipse where apostles gather to “feast” on sacrifices for Griffith’s rise to become the fifth member of the God Hand. During the chaos, Corkus tries to convince himself that everything happening is just a bad dream, and she uses his shock to her advantage. In her human form, she’s always naked, with long blonde hair partially covering her face, and an eerie, emotionless look. She moves seductively toward Corkus so she can lure him close enough to strike. She then reveals her true form when Corkus embraces her. When we see her next, she’s already got his head in her mouth. When she shifts to her apostle form, she grows in size and transforms into a reptilian-like beast. Her mouth becomes full of sharp teeth with a long tongue, and her head takes on spike-like features, tentacles, and even some extra hands. Two years after the Eclipse, she crosses paths with Guts again, mistaking him for just another victim while Guts plays along. But as soon as she reveals her true form, Guts shoves his prosthetic arm into her mouth and uses its hidden cannon to take her down, leaving a huge hole in her head.

* **Rosine**

Rosine is one of the apostles who feasts on the wounded members of the Band of the Hawk the night before the Eclipse. When she and the Count corner Rickert, the Skull Knight swoops in to push them back. Prior to her transformation, she was an ordinary girl from a small village where, after a conflict, her father started to doubt she was really his daughter. She spent her time alone in the woods, collecting insects and pretending to be an elf to escape her parents’ fights. She even shared these fantasies with her friend Jill and decided one day to run away to a magical "Misty Valley." Before leaving, she gave Jill most of her belongings, except for a mysterious stone she'd found by the river. When Rosine’s parents finally found her, her father lashed out and scolded her for leaving. She watched in horror as he turned violent toward her mother as well. In her desperation, Rosine’s beherit activated and she ended up sacrificing her own parents, transforming her into an elf-like apostle. From then on, she began to capture children to turn them into “elves” for her own magical realm in the Misty Valley, while recruiting forest dwellers as guardians. Soon, her elves began wreaking havoc in nearby villages. Guts confronts Rosine after trapping some of her pseudo-apostles in a barn fire. She manages to poison him but is taken aback when Jill recognizes her, forcing her to retreat. Later, Jill and Guts find her again. Rosine tries to persuade her friend to join her in the Misty Valley. Guts intervenes and injures one of her wings before she takes Jill flying high above as she tries to convince her to become an "elf." However, Jill becomes reluctant to do so when she starts to see the darker reality of life among the “elves,” but Rosine comforts her while offering her a cocoon where she can transform into one. Just then, Guts arrives and sets the nest on fire and Rosine’s elves are burned in the chaos. Rosine transforms fully to battle Guts. He eventually lands a critical blow and Rosine reverts to her original form with a missing arm. In her last moments, she remembers her parents and asks for forgiveness. But before Guts can finish her, the Holy Iron Chain Knights intervene and a delusional Rosine flies off “to return to her parents.” Moments later, she falls and passes away. The Skull Knight later arrives on the scene to take her beherit.

* **Egg of the Perfect World**

The Egg of the Perfect World was a lonely outcast in the city of Albion who became an apostle. He looked like a small, flesh-colored creature resembling a beherit, with orange eyes and tentacle-like extensions to help him move around. Marked with the Brand of Sacrifice on his tongue, he watched over the city while waiting for his purpose to unfold. As a former human raised in isolation, his earliest memory being the trash dump at the Tower of Conviction where he once found a beherit as he scavenged through garbage. When he decided to spy on the humans above, he was chased away with rocks and branded a “monster.” Driven back down, he dug a deeper hole and lived by the refuse. Over time, locals unknowingly used his home as a graveyard. The weight of the bodies eventually crushed him, and in his final moment, his beherit activated and summoned the God Hand. Having no loved ones, he sacrificed “the world as it was” to create a perfect world, thus transforming into the Egg of the Perfect World. When Casca and Guts arrived, he began influencing events to fulfill his ultimate purpose \- which is to trigger the Incarnation Ceremony. He first transformed the Great Goat of the heretics into a pseudo-apostle which led to Casca’s capture, and then turned Mozgus and his followers into pseudo-apostles too. When Luca found herself near Albion's mass graves, the Egg revealed his past to her as if seeking someone to acknowledge he once existed. But he was forced to flee when the Skull Knight attacked and gave him a fatal injury. On his way to the Tower, he found the Demon Child who had been protecting Casca but was also dying. Out of compassion, the Egg swallowed the child while ensuring it could “dream in the cradle of the new world inside him.” Reaching the top of the tower, the Egg became a cocoon for the child who transformed into Griffith’s new body. The Egg then realizes his real purpose as his body cracked open to reveal the new Griffith.

* **Ganishka**

Ganishka, the ambitious Kushan emperor, dreamed of conquering the world and would do anything to secure his power. His story begins with betrayal. As the eldest son of a Kushan king, he was targeted by his mother who tried to poison him so his younger brother could inherit the throne. Ganishka survived and retaliated by killing his brother, which drove his mother to suicide. Due to his father’s lack of trust in him, he arranged his assassination and seized the throne. Once king, he believed ruling by fear and conquest was his path to power. He became a peerless warrior and led relentless campaigns that left little time for his wife and child. But ambition brought danger as his own son tried to poison him and ordered the guards to assassinate him. In the face of death, Ganishka activated his beherit  which was given to him by a wandering sadhu some time ago, and sacrificed his son to transform into an apostle. Unlike most apostles, Ganishka defied the God Hand and sought to surpass them. He saw his chance of invading Midland when the kingdom fell to disease and famine. He successfully captured Wyndham and took Princess Charlotte hostage, intending to legitimize his rule by having her bear his child. But Griffith’s Band of the Hawk launched an attack to rescue Charlotte and force him to submit himself to the Falcon of Light. As an apostle, he wielded terrifying powers. His astral form could morph into a massive mist capable of unleashing lightning strikes and covering entire cities. He could detect enemies within this fog, transform animals into monstrous pishacha, and devour anything it touched. Yet, he wasn’t invincible. The Dragon Slayer could wound him due to the sword’s connection to the astral world, and Griffith’s intense wind gusts nearly dissipated his fog form which forced him back to his weakened state. Defeated by Griffith, Ganishka retreated but was determined to return stronger. He entered a Man-Made Beherit in his desperate attempt to transcend apostlehood. As a result, he emerged as an immense, uncontrollable eldritch entity. In this colossal form, he crushed the city of Wyndham and his own troops beneath his monstrous feet while he lost every shred of sanity with each step. He could breathe torrents of fire and spawn pseudo-apostles from fallen soldiers as he became a walking disaster that threatened all he encountered. In a climactic showdown, Griffith in his God Hand form Femto, ascended Ganishka’s towering form while the Skull Knight prepares for an ambush. Griffith redirected the Skull Knight’s space-tearing sword strike toward Ganishka resulting in an astral fissure. From this opening poured the forces of the Astral World that blends reality with fantasy, and birthed the Interstice of Fantasia. Ganishka’s immense form dissolved, leaving behind the towering World Spiral Tree.

* **Grunbeld**

Grunbeld, the "Great Flame Dragon," is a fierce apostle who serves under the reborn Griffith in the Band of the Hawk. Known for his towering strength and fiery spirit, he commands the giants of the Hawk and wields devastating power. Before joining Griffith’s ranks, Grunbeld earned legendary status during the Hundred-Year War by defending his homeland with only 3,000 soldiers against overwhelming Tudor forces. Grunbeld’s origins are detailed in Berserk: The Flame Dragon Knight. Born to a noble family in the Grand Duchy of Grant, he grew up on tales of honor, told by his mother, who promised him that a glorious death in battle would lead to the Gods' Manor. As a child, he met a blind girl named Benedikte who mysteriously prophesied that he would one day become a dragon. But when Tudor forces murdered his mother, young Grunbeld was captured and trained alongside his friends Edvard and Sigur to whom he built unbreakable bonds. At some point, he found a beherit after slaying a tiger and took it to his possession. As an adult, he became a celebrated knight who led the legendary Flame Dragon Knights. Yet his rising star drew jealousy and political scheming. He faced a heartbreaking betrayal from his comrade Edvard who was under the manipulation of his father, Duke Haakon. In a brutal battle, Grunbeld tried to protect Benedikte and Sigur despite being cornered and weakened. His desperation triggered his beherit where he met the God Hand. To survive, he sacrificed his dying friends Benedikte and Sigur along with Edvard, allowing him to transform into a dragon-like apostle and tear through Tudor’s forces with vengeance to secure his place in Grant’s history. Now an apostle, Grunbeld’s powers are more terrifying. He can shatter entire ranks of armored soldiers with a single swing of his massive greathammer, and his large shield not only deflects cannon fire but also fires back cannonballs itself. His shield is also a weapon in its own right as it is equipped with hidden blades. In his apostle form, he becomes a crystalline behemoth with his skin turning into corundum, a nearly indestructible crystalline material. Apart from being stronger than steel, his dragon form even allows him to breathe fire. Standing around 9 feet tall and weighing over 300 kg in his human form, Grunbeld towers at 20 feet and over 1600 kg in his apostle form. When he was tasked to destroy the Mansion of the Spirit Tree and its caretaker, Flora, he considered it dishonorable to kill an elderly woman. Instead, he eagerly challenges Guts but is quickly disappointed upon seeing his opponent drained and wounded from his previous fights. However, when Guts dons the Berserker Armor, he is taken off guard by Guts’ sudden ferocity which forces him to transform into his full apostle form. But Guts still manages to damage him even with his nearly indestructible skin. Guts and his crew manage to escape when Flora creates a wall of fire to stop Grunbeld from chasing after them. After the establishment of Falconia, the Band of the Hawk faces an army of jotnar which later brings a massive hydra to attack the band. Zodd, Irvine, and Grunbeld team up to fight the serpentine monster but it was Grunbeld who dealt the finishing blow by incinerating the hydra with his fire breath.

* **Irvine**

Irvine, the silent shadow in Griffith’s Band of the Hawk, is a master archer with deadly precision and unmatched range. While Zodd and Grunbeld charge head-on in battles, Irvine lurks in the distance and eliminates foes with arrows that seem to pierce with superb accuracy and force, enough to dismember opponents in a single shot. In human form, Irvine can fire multiple arrows at impossible distances, each one finding its target effortlessly even when they're scattered across the battlefield. But it’s in his apostle form that his skills become truly monstrous. He transforms into a sleek, wolf-like creature fused with his bow that allows him to shoot even faster and harder. His fur generates his arrows, condensing into razor-sharp needles that unravel into thorny explosions upon impact to tear his enemies apart from the inside out. His bow, now part of his body, can fire with a ballista-like force that can shred large opponents with ease. Despite his fearsome abilities, Irvine is not all blood and fury. A quiet, introverted soul, he is often seen retreating to the woods and away from his comrades where he enjoys peaceful nights by a campfire. His one true pleasure seems to be playing the lute under the stars that gives us a rare glimpse of gentleness coming from his kind. He also reveals a surprising tenderness when Sonia asks to warm herself by his campfire, he not only allows her but also covers her with his coat when she falls asleep. In battle, however, Irvine shows no mercy. At the battle of Vritannis, he singlehandedly decimates the advancing Kushan forces by eliminating almost all their generals and creating chaos among their ranks. In the final battle against the monstrous Shiva Ganishka, he fights relentlessly to protect Sonia, maintaining his eerie calm as he methodically takes down enemies with his devastating archery.

* **Locus**

Locus, known as the “Moonlight Knight,” is a legendary apostle in Griffith’s Band of the Hawk, renowned as the mightiest of knights and an undefeated master of the lance. The tales of his unmatched skill and countless victories are so legendary that generations of young warriors grew up on stories of his heroics, yet he never bowed to any master \- until he found Griffith. Guided by a mysterious oracle to seek out the “Falcon of Light,” he arrives in Midland with a grand entrance by swiftly dispatching a squad of Kushan soldiers with one powerful thrust of his lance. Upon meeting Griffith, he pledges his loyalty and puts his absolute faith in the leader of the Band of the Hawk. Locus wields a massive lance with impressive precision and power that can cut down waves of enemies in one strike. But his prowess only grows more terrifying in his apostle form as his body fuses with his steed to form a centaur-like creature that looks gleaming with a silvery metallic sheen and outfitted with hooked armor. His lance transforms into an even deadlier, blade-like weapon, and his shield merges with his arm, making him a juggernaut on the battlefield where his unstoppable strikes are as graceful as they are lethal. In battle, he leads his lancer unit with crushing force as seen during the assault on Wyndham Palace where he easily slices through Kushan forces to create an opening. However, his unit was no match to Ganishka who takes them on in his apostle form. This distraction is part of Griffith’s plan though, as Griffith and Zodd break into the Tower of Rebirth to rescue Princess Charlotte while Ganishka is busy fighting Locus’ forces. Locus later orders a swift retreat after Charlotte is rescued. Later at the battle of Vritannis, he is seen riding at the forefront of the Band of the Hawk’s defense against the invading Kushan forces. When the monstrous Shiva Ganishka unleashes his forces, he charges into battle and fights alongside human soldiers against Ganishka’s spawn. Though he’s a devastating force in war, Locus isn’t just Griffith’s battlefield enforcer; he’s also his trusted advisor in political matters. His stoic and wise nature has earned him Griffith’s respect, making him the only apostle seen at Griffith’s side during formal occasions. 

* **Rakshas**

Rakshas, the eerie and elusive apostle assassin of Griffith’s Band of the Hawk, is a former Bakiraka clansman who lives in exile. His motives are as twisted as his form \- he claims to serve Griffith, but only so he can one day steal his head for himself. With a body that’s amorphous, Rakshas can contort, melt, and reshape himself to carry out the band’s darkest missions undetected. Sneaking up on foes is his specialty; he once slipped past Zodd’s watchful eye to dispatch Kushan scouts who dared target Griffith. His skill set goes beyond the physical as he seems to know his way around “illicit sorcery,” proving it when he lures fellow Bakiraka to the man-made beherit and explains its mysteries before vanishing into the shadows. He uses his amorphous form to conjure bladed tentacles, razor-sharp appendages, and other nightmarish weaponry. When fighting the Kushan, he goes as far as to spook their war elephants by piercing their ears with darts to send them into frenzies. During the battle with the reincarnated Ganishka, he slinks under Zodd’s wing and becomes a silent witness to the colossal clash that reshapes the world. He again blends into the shadows when he infiltrates the Kurultai council of the Kushan by lurking inside a clan leader’s body until bursting forth to unleash a storm of spikes that scatter the council in terror. Rakshas’s body holds all kinds of unsettling surprises. His head can shift to any spot which makes him hard to predict \- and harder to kill. In a duel with Silat, Rakshas’s mask is cleaved open, only for Silat to find nothing behind it. He can also turn his clothes into scythe-like blades strong enough to slice horses in half. He also seems to be oddly attached to his mask that when Silat breaks it, he is forced to retreat while visibly distressed, and feels similarly sad when Rickert damages it with a well-aimed rocket. 

* **Borkoff**

Borkoff makes his brutal debut during the horrific Eclipse, sporting a monstrous salamander-like body, armored hide and fearsome jaws. He is most notable as the apostle who tears off Guts’s left arm during the Eclipse by clamping it down with an iron bite just as Guts struggles to reach Casca amidst the chaos. With Griffith reborn as Femto and beginning his assault on Casca, Guts desperately tries to break free as he picks up a shattered sword to fight off Borkoff. However, the sword could not cut the apostle’s hide so he chose to cut his own trapped arm instead. Years later, Borkoff returns in Griffith's new Band of the Hawk, now part of the demon lancer unit under the fearsome Locus. In his human-like form, he’s a terrifying lancer armed with a lance and shield \- and, like his fellow demon lancers, has  a creepy head on his lower back. He is seen battling during the assault on Wyndham Palace where he and the rest of the Demon Lancer Unit engage Ganishka’s forces to create a diversion while Griffith and Zodd free Princess Charlotte. He then takes part in the Band’s campaign at Vritannis, clashing with Kushan forces and joining the final battle against Shiva Ganishka. With Falconia established as Griffith’s new capital, Borkoff becomes a gladiator in the city’s brutal arena named Pandemonium. There, he fights beasts for sport, such as the ogre he faces down in the arena. Though the ogre initially gains the upper hand, Borkoff’s monstrous endurance wins out and he ultimately slaughters his opponent before consuming its entrails as the crowd cheers for the gruesome entertainment.

* **Vlad Tepes**

Vlad Tepes is the twisted main villain in Berserk: The Prototype, an early version of the story created in 1988\. As you might’ve guessed, he is a direct reference to the real-life Vlad the Impaler of Wallachia. Just like his real-life counterpart, this brutal lord also has a nasty reputation of lining his roads with the skeletons of his enemies who were left impaled as a gruesome warning. With no wars left to fight, Tepes’s cruelty turns inward and he starts tormenting his own people. His most sinister pastime is abducting young girls under the guise of “maidservants,” none of whom ever return. When one girl’s body is found impaled in a river, the terrified villagers call on a lone warrior named Guts, to take Tepes down. Guts agrees which sets the stage for a deadly showdown at Tepes’s castle. Meanwhile, when a young village girl named Frikka is brought to the castle, she quickly realizes Tepes isn’t just a cruel lord \- he’s something else entirely. His cold, rotting skin and eerie, unnatural eyes give away his demonic nature. When Guts confronts Tepes, the apostle reveals his powers: he can manipulate metal and send spears flying with deadly accuracy. Then, the true horror unfolds as Tepes morphs into a towering “hog-bull” beast, with twisted horns, a huge mouth full of razor-sharp teeth, goat-like legs, and thick, rough skin. He seems unstoppable in this monstrous form. But Guts has a surprise up his sleeve \- literally. Using the hidden cannon in his prosthetic arm, he stuns Tepes and then brings his massive sword down to slice the beast in two who is shocked at his defeat by a mere human. As Tepes lies dying, he notices a strange mark on Guts’ chest. It’s the same symbol that was on the carriage that brought Frikka to his castle. Before he can think through what it means, the castle crumbles and crushes him along with the unanswered mystery surrounding that mark.
