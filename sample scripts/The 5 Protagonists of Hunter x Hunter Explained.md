**The 5 Protagonists of Hunter x Hunter Explained**

1. **Gon Freecss**

Gon Freecss grew up on Whale Island, where his aunt Mito raised him after his father, Ging, left him as a baby and disappeared for more than a decade. To protect him from the truth, Mito told Gon that his parents had died in an accident, and he believed that story for most of his childhood. Life on Whale Island was simple. He spent his days climbing trees, fishing, and befriending wild animals. Growing up surrounded by nature also gave him incredibly sharp senses, letting him pick up sounds and scents that most people would never even notice. His life changed permanently at nine years old when he was rescued from a foxbear attack by a Hunter named Kite (*1999 Anime Episode 1*). Kite revealed that he was actually Ging's student, and hearing someone speak so highly of the father Gon barely remembered sparked something inside him. Gon decided to become a Hunter himself, not because he wanted fame or fortune, but to find his father and understand why being a Hunter mattered more to him than raising his own son. To earn Aunt Mito's permission to take the dangerous Hunter Exam, Gon sets out to catch a giant fish called the Master of the Swamp (*Episode 1*). After spending an entire week refusing to give up, Gon finally reels it in, wins his bet with his aunt, and boards a ship bound for the Hunter Exam. While aboard the ship headed for the Hunter Exam, Gon meets Kurapika and Leorio, two other applicants chasing Hunter Licenses for their own deeply personal reasons. Before they even reach the official testing grounds, the candidates had to get through a few hidden screening challenges. One test looks like a simple choice between two impossible answers, but they figure out that staying completely silent is actually the correct response, since no spoken answer could possibly be right (*Episode 2*). Later, the trio realize that a couple of Navigators claiming they were being attacked by shape-shifting creatures are actually examiners testing their judgment and character. The official Hunter Exam finally begins inside a huge, dark underground tunnel with a brutal nonstop marathon (*Episode 3*). During the run, Gon meets Killua, an eleven-year-old riding a skateboard with a carefree attitude, and the two become friends almost instantly (*Episode 4*). After completing the tunnel run, the examiner leads the surviving applicants into the fog-shrouded Milsy Wetlands, where dangerous creatures and hazardous terrain test their survival instincts. Here, Gon has his first encounter with Hisoka, a bloodthirsty magician who treats the exam as a hunting ground to eliminate candidates he deemed inadequate (*Episode 5*). When Kurapika and Leorio fall behind and cross paths with Hisoka, Gon rushes back to help and intervenes just as Hisoka attacks Leorio. Although Hisoka easily overpowers Gon and nearly chokes him, he is impressed by Gon's courage and potential, declaring that all three had passed his personal evaluation and even carrying the unconscious Leorio to the finish line. The second phase is a culinary challenge judged by Gourmet Hunters Menchi and Buhara. Buhara has no problem passing everyone for their roast pork, but Menchi isn't impressed at all. She says none of the pork is good enough and fails every single applicant, causing the tension to rise until Chairman Netero shows up in an airship, calls her out for letting her emotions take over, and convinces her to give everyone another chance with a new challenge: collecting Spider Eagle eggs hanging over a massive ravine. Gon, Killua, Kurapika, and Leorio leap into the chasm, grab an egg, and successfully pass alongside 38 others.  (*Episode 6*). While resting aboard Chairman Netero's airship en route to the third phase, Gon discovers that Killua belongs to the notorious Zoldyck family of elite assassins and is one of their primary heirs (*Episode 7*). Rather than being frightened, Gon reacts with open enthusiasm, which surprises Killua. Netero challenges the two boys to a game, promising them immediate Hunter Licenses if they could steal a ball from his hands. Despite using all their agility and coordination, neither boy could seize the ball, and Netero brags he doesn’t need to use his right hand during the game. While Killua becomes frustrated and gives up, Gon refuses to quit, repeatedly hurling himself into the challenge until he executes a reckless, all-or-nothing maneuver that forces Netero to use his right hand. Although Gon still loses the game, forcing the Chairman to use his right hand serves as a moment of great personal achievement.  The third phase requires the applicants to descend Trick Tower within seventy-two hours, during which Gon, Killua, Kurapika, Leorio, and an uncooperative applicant named Tonpa are forced to form a five-person team to proceed through majority-rule doors (*Episode 8*). During a series of one-on-one matches against long-term prison inmates, Gon outsmarts his opponent in a candle-burning contest (*Episode 9*). Later, when the group is about to run out of time, he comes up with a clever solution by choosing the longer path that allows everyone to continue, then punches a hole through the wall so they could cut across to the shorter route and make it in time (*Episode 12*). The fourth phase takes place on Zevil Island, where candidates participate in a target-hunting game to collect number badges (*Episode 14*). By luck, Gon draws Hisoka's badge number as his assigned target. Gon spends days patiently stalking Hisoka through the forest (*Episode 15*), studying his movements and waiting for the exact moment Hisoka attacks another target (*Episode 16*). Using his fishing rod with precision, Gon snags Hisoka's badge, but moments later, another candidate strikes Gon with a paralyzing dart and steals his badges. After dealing with the attacker, Hisoka recovers the stolen badges and hands them back to Gon. Stubborn and prideful, Gon refuses to keep a badge he had not won entirely on his own. In response, Hisoka punches Gon in the face and tells him to return the badge only after he becomes strong enough to land a punch on Hisoka in the same manner. Gon spends the next few days thinking about everything that had happened before finding the determination to keep chasing his dream of becoming a Hunter — and one day settling the score with Hisoka. He then sets out to find his friends, eventually reuniting with Kurapika and Leorio (*Episode 17*). Using his keen sense of smell, he helps track Leorio’s target down by following the scent of the medicine she uses to protect herself and helps Leorio obtain his target's badge from a cave full of venomous snakes (*Episode 18*). The final phase of the Hunter Exam is an elimination tournament where winning just one match is enough to earn a Hunter License (*Episode 19*). That also means only one person would fail the exam, while the other eight would pass and officially become Hunters. Gon's first match is against Hanzo, a highly trained ninja who completely outclasses him in physical combat. Despite taking a severe beating, Gon stubbornly refuses to surrender regardless of the pain Hanzo inflicted. Realizing that Gon would allow himself to be broken before giving up, Hanzo concedes the match, making Gon the first applicant to pass the exam that year. Dissatisfied with winning by forfeit, Gon demands a proper rematch until Hanzo knocks him unconscious. Upon waking, Gon learns the shocking news that Killua had failed the exam after an applicant named Gittarackur revealed himself to be Killua's older brother, Illumi Zoldyck, and psychologically manipulated Killua into murdering another contestant, causing his instant disqualification (*Episode 20*). Furious, Gon confronts Illumi, breaking his wrist and demanding to know where Killua had gone. With their licenses in hand, Gon, Kurapika, and Leorio travel to Kukuroo Mountain to retrieve Killua from his family's estate (*Episode 22*). To reach Killua, the group has to train to open a multi-ton gate (*Episode 23*), endure physical strikes from a dedicated apprentice butler named Canary, and win a mind game hosted by the Zoldyck family's head butler, Gotoh (*Episode 25*). Once Killua was freed, the group parted ways, with Gon promising to return Hisoka's badge through a proper fight, while Kurapika shared that the Phantom Troupe and Hisoka would gather in Yorknew City on September 1st, prompting the four friends to promise a reunion on that date. Traveling together to earn money and train, Gon and Killua head to Heavens Arena (*Episode 27*). There, they meet a young fighter named Zushi and his master, Wing, who introduces them to the hidden art of Nen. Wing initially hesitates to teach the boys, knowing the inherent dangers of awakening aura too early in young fighters. After breezing through the lower floors, Gon and Killua reach the 200th floor, where every combatant is a proficient Nen user (*Episode 28*). Waiting at the entrance, Hisoka blocks their path using a dense wall of hostile aura. Wing arrives and explains that physical strength alone could not breach a Nen barrier without severe injury, agreeing to train them under a strict deadline before their registration expired at midnight. In his hotel room, Wing performs a risky shortcut by using his own aura to forcibly open Gon and Killua's aura nodes (*Episode 29*). The boys quickly learn Ten to wrap and contain their overflowing energy, an achievement that amazes Wing. After proving they could push forward against Wing's intense Ren, the boys return to the 200th floor and walk through Hisoka's aura barrier without harm, prompting Hisoka to grant Gon a match once he wins a match on the 200th floor. The next day, Gon enters his first Nen match against Gido, an opponent who controlled spinning tops infused with aura. Close to losing, Gon makes a risky choice to deactivate Ten and focus entirely on his physical senses (*Episode 30*), entering a state of Zetsu that he had naturally touched during his manhunt on Hisoka in the Hunter Exam. Keeping his eyes closed, Gon dodges the spinning tops for over an hour before suffering a broken arm and losing the fight. Unimpressed by Gon's reckless disregard for his life, Wing bans him from practicing Nen or fighting for two months. Once the punishment ends, Wing expands their instruction to include Gyo to focus aura into the eyes (*Episode 33*), Zetsu to suppress aura, and Ren to increase aura output. In his rematch against Gido (*Episode 34*), Gon uses Ren to overpower the dancing tops and uses his fishing rod to flip the floor tile Gido stands on, securing a victory before quickly winning another match against Riehlvelt. As Gon walks away from the arena, Hisoka is already waiting for him and finally agrees to give him the fight he's been looking forward to. Back to their training, Wing introduces Hatsu through Water Divination, revealing that Gon is an Enhancer, which fit his straightforward combat style (*Episode 35*). Following a month of Hatsu training, Wing announces that Gon had successfully passed the secret Hunter Exam.  During the much-awaited match, Gon charges directly at Hisoka, who easily repels his initial attacks. Recalling his tactic from his fight with Gido, Gon kicks up a floor tile, shatters it into flying debris, and uses the brief distraction to land a clean punch on Hisoka's face, scoring points and handing back Hisoka’s badge to him. With his obligation fulfilled, Hisoka stops holding back and reveals his Transmutation ability, Bungee Gum, quickly dominating the match (*Episode 36*). Gon is ultimately defeated by technical knockout, but Hisoka promises that their next encounter would be an unrestricted fight to the death. Knowing the gap between them remained massive, Gon leaves Heavens Arena determined to grow stronger.  Returning to Whale Island with Killua for a brief rest, Aunt Mito handed Gon a mysterious box left behind by Ging (*Episode 37*). After trying various methods, Gon uses Nen to open the outer layer, uncovering a smaller box that unlocks using Gon's Hunter License. Inside, they find a cassette tape, a memory card, and a ring bearing a unique symbol. Playing the cassette (*Episode 38*), they hear Ging's voice challenging Gon to find him, but when Ging offers to talk about Gon's mother, Gon stops the tape, declaring that Mito is his true mother. Right after, Ging's pre-applied Nen erases the recording to prevent trace tracking. Examining the memory card, Killua identifies it as save data for Greed Island, an extremely rare video game designed by Nen users exclusively for Hunters that transports players physically into the game world. Learning that 14 copies are set to be auctioned in Yorknew City, Gon and Killua set their sights on securing a copy to find clues leading to Ging.  Arriving in Yorknew City in September, Gon and Killua reunite with Leorio and organize an arm-wrestling booth to earn money, where Gon competes against numerous challengers until facing a strong opponent named Shizuku (*Episode 42*). Soon after, the group join a recruited manhunt against the Phantom Troupe following a violent raid on the auction house, hoping to claim high bounties to purchase Greed Island (*Episode 46*). While tracking Troupe members Nobunaga and Machi (*Episode 49*), Gon and Killua are captured and taken to the group's hideout. On their way, Pakunoda attempts to read Killua's memories to find the mysterious Chain User, but learns nothing because Killua is unaware of Kurapika's new abilities. Noticing Hisoka among the Troupe members, Gon keeps quiet and engages in repeated arm-wrestling matches with Nobunaga (*Episode 50*). When Nobunaga mourns a fallen comrade while swearing revenge on the Chain User, Gon openly confronts him about the Troupe's hypocrisy in grieving their own while slaughtering innocent people. Nobunaga grows fond of Gon’s spirit and keeps the two boys captive until their leader, Chrollo, returns so he can ask if they both can join the troupe. Later, Gon and Killua manage to escape captivity using a clever spatial trick. Killua tells Gon that Kurapika is actually the Chain User, something he only figured out during the arm-wrestling match. Surprised that Kurapika managed to bring down a member of the Phantom Troupe, Gon immediately contacts him to offer their help, to which Kurapika declines (*Episode 52*). After reports claim the Phantom Troupe had been killed by the Zoldyck family, Kurapika finally meets up with them and explains his Nen ability (*Episode 54*). Gon suggests he should focus on reclaiming his brethrens’ stolen eyes now that the Phantom Troupe is dead. Hisoka then sends a text message to Kurapika revealing that the corpses were fakes. Now that the situation has changed, Kurapika accepts his friends' help, targeting Pakunoda to prevent her from reading memories that could expose his identity (*Episode 55*). During the pursuit, Kurapika nearly exposes himself due to emotional turmoil (*Episode 56*), forcing Gon and Killua to step in as distractions, resulting in their second capture. Held in a hotel lobby, the boys receive a coded message from Leorio over a fake phone call (*Episode 57*). As Pakunoda scans their memories again, a sudden blackout cuts power to the building, allowing Gon and Killua to make an escape attempt before being recaptured. When the lights return, the Troupe discovers that Kurapika had successfully kidnapped Chrollo. A hostage exchange was arranged, trading Gon and Killua for Chrollo (*Episode 58*). Despite Hisoka's attempts to interfere for a duel with Chrollo, the trade executed smoothly, and Kurapika places his Judgment Chain on Chrollo to seal his Nen and prevent communication with the Troupe. After their chaotic encounter with the Phantom Troupe, Gon starts worrying about Kurapika and how consumed he has become with getting revenge for his fallen clan. Killua then reminds him that they still have their own goal to focus on: Greed Island. Realizing they could never afford a copy of Greed Island, Gon proposes getting hired by someone who already owned the game. They approach Battera, a wealthy collector hiring skilled Hunters to clear the game (*Episode 59*), and pass his screening test by demonstrating their developed Hatsu (*Episode 60*). Upon entering Greed Island, they meet another player named Biscuit Krueger, a veteran Hunter disguised as a young girl (*Episode 62*). Recognizing their massive potential, Biscuit offered to train them, revealing she was Master Wing's teacher before easily defeating a high-class criminal who later gave the boys trouble when Biscuit uses him as a live-combat training tool for the two boys (*Episode 63*). Biscuit puts them through months of intense aura conditioning, teaching them advanced techniques such as Ko to focus all aura into a single strike, Ken that combines Ten and Ren, and Ryu to dynamically distribute aura during combat (*Episode 64*). Through this training, Gon develops his signature technique, Jajanken, which incorporates Rock for a massive short-range punch, Paper for an emitted aura blast, and Scissors for a transformed aura blade (*Episode 65*). To beat the game, Gon and Killua allied with other players, including Hisoka (*Episode 68*), to face Razor, one of the game's creators, in a deadly dodgeball match for the rare Plot of Beach card (*Episode 69*). Working together, with Killua damaging his hands to hold the ball steady as an anchor for Gon's full-power Jajanken launches, they secure a narrow victory (*Episode 71*). Soon after, the alliance targeted the Bomber, a murderous player named Genthru who was killing participants for their cards. In a one-on-one duel, Gon willingly sacrifices an arm to trap Genthru and land a decisive blow (*Episode 74*). After defeating Genthru, Gon uses the Angel's Breath card to restore his severed arm, clears the final end-game quiz (*Episode 75*), and officially beats Greed Island. As his reward, Gon chose the Accompany card to travel to Ging. It turns out Ging had set the Accompany card to redirect Gon and his company to Kite if he used it, while Magnetic Force would take him straight to Ging. His reason was simple — he didn't want Gon showing up with friends when they finally met for the first time. With that, Gon and Killua are transported to Kite instead (*Episode 76*).  Reunited with Kite, they join his investigation into the Chimera Ants, an aggressive species capable of inheriting traits from consumed prey. The trio traveled into the Neo-Green Life region to destroy the Chimera Ant Queen's nest, defeating various ant soldiers along the way (*Episodes 81 \- 83*). The mission turns disastrous when Neferpitou, a newly born Royal Guard, suddenly appears and severs Kite's arm in a single attack (*Episode 85*). Recognizing the vast difference in strength, Killua knocks Gon unconscious and carries him to safety while Kite stays behind to buy them time. At the border, Chairman Netero, Morel, and Knov took charge of the operation, barring Gon and Killua from re-entering until they could defeat Morel's apprentices, Knuckle and Shoot, in the nearby town. Despite intense training under Biscuit (*Episode 86*), both boys were defeated by the experienced Hunters (*Episode 90*). Meanwhile, the Chimera Ant King is born (*Episode 91*), the Queen dies (*Episode 92*), and the King takes over East Gorteau Palace along with his three Royal Guards (*Episode 93*). Recognizing the desperate situation, Netero permits Gon and Killua to join the palace invasion squad (*Episode 94*). When Kite's body is recovered as a mindless puppet controlled by Pitou, Gon is shattered by grief and guilt, swearing that Pitou was his alone to destroy (*Episode 95*).  During the palace invasion (*Episode 111*), Gon makes his way directly to Pitou's location, only to find the Royal Guard using their ability to heal an injured human girl named Komugi (*Episode 115*). Enraged by the idea that Pitou could show compassion to a human after showing none to Kite, Gon threatens to attack (*Episode 116*). Pitou refused to fight, breaking their own arm as a gesture of non-aggression while begging for time to finish saving Komugi. Killua manages to calm Gon down, and Gon grants Pitou a single hour to complete the healing process. Once the hour expired, Gon forces Pitou to escort him to Kite's body while taking Komugi hostage to ensure compliance (*Episode 127*). When they reach the palace in Peijin, Pitou receives a call falsely claiming they’ve retrieved Komugi, freeing the Royal Guard to eliminate Gon (*Episode 130*). Upon seeing Kite’s body, Pitou admits that Kite is long dead and could not be restored. The realization destroys Gon's final hopes. And when Pitou prepares to take him down, his overflowing rage drives him into an extreme binding Nen vow (*Episode 131*). Sacrificing all of his future potential and lifespan in exchange for immediate power, Gon's body rapidly transforms into an adult form with an aura that terrified Pitou. Gon completely dominates the fight, crushing Pitou with a sequence of brutal Jajanken attacks. After he unleashes his devastating final attack on the already broken Pitou, Killua arrives at the scene and immediately recognizes him. Even after Pitou's death, the Royal Guard's corpse is reanimated by post-mortem Nen and strikes Gon, severing his right arm. Gon uses his severed arm to pin the body to the ground and delivers one final Jajanken blast that destroys Pitou completely, leaving Gon's body broken on the verge of death. Hospitalized in critical condition, Gon is kept alive by support systems while doctors declare his curse-like state incurable (*Episode 136*). Killua turns to his younger sister Alluka and her mysterious alter-ego Nanika, who has the power to grant any but comes with a terrifying, deadly curse system of trade-offs (*Episode 138*). Even with Illumi and Hisoka trying to stop them, Killua manages to get Alluka to the hospital, where Nanika fully restores Gon's body after Killua gives the command (*Episode 145*). Upon waking, Gon travels to the 13th Hunter Chairman Election hall, where he reunites with Leorio and finally meets his father, Ging. Breaking down in tears over Kite's fate who had reincarnated into a young girl's body (*Episode 146*), Gon is comforted by Ging, who tells him to apologize to Kite, not to him. Gon later meets with the reincarnated Kite (*Episode 147*), offers a formal apology, and promises that they would stand and fight side-by-side in the future. Traveling with Killua and Alluka to the World Tree where he is to meet Ging, Gon bids an emotional farewell to Killua, who pledged to spend his life traveling with and protecting Alluka. Gon climbed the World Tree and engaged in a long conversation with Ging about his motivations, the nature of Hunters, the uncharted outside world he intends to explore, and the importance of enjoying life's detours (*Episode 148*). At the end of their meeting, Gon returns Ging's Hunter License that was given to him by Kite. Shortly after, Gon realizes he could no longer perceive or use his aura (*Chapter 344*). Calling Ging for advice, his father explains that his aura nodes had likely closed, returning him to a normal person as the cost of surviving his extreme vow, and urges him to figure out what he wanted to do with his life (*Chapter 345*). Accepting his situation, Gon returns home to Whale Island, reuniting with Mito and his great-grandmother. 

2. **Killua Zoldyck**

Killua Zoldyck is born in the Republic of Padokea as the middle child of the Zoldyck family, a bloodline that has earned the title of the deadliest assassins in the world. The family lives on Kukuroo Mountain, a dormant volcano rising more than three thousand meters into the sky, with their estate hidden behind massive stone walls guarded by a monstrous beast named Mike and attendants trained to near perfection in martial arts. Outsiders know almost nothing about what goes on inside, and even a single photo of a family member can sell for millions because their identities are so closely guarded. From the moment he's born, Killua is groomed to be a weapon. His parents Silva and Kikyo, along with his older brother Illumi, put him through training that would break most adults. He gets zapped with electricity as a baby until his body can absorb insane voltage without flinching, and he's fed enough poisons and toxins during his upbringing that most substances just bounce off him now. By age three, he's already doing exercises where one mistake could cost him his life. Around this time there's also a disturbing incident involving his little sister Alluka, whose eyes and mouth suddenly go pitch black after a butler grants a series of her seemingly harmless requests (*Episode 138*). Killua is the one who calms things down, and he learns there's an entity attached to her named Nanika. Once Nanika's final request is granted, she will grant that person any wish they want. But if that person fails to fulfill her next set of requests, they face a gruesome death, along with the people closest to them. The bigger the previous wish was — like asking to become a millionaire — the more extreme Nanika's next requests become for the following person, such as demanding their liver. And if those impossible requests can't be fulfilled, the result is another gruesome death. His parents respond by locking Alluka away behind a series of vaults, treating her less like a daughter and more like a hazard to contain. Around age six, Killua is thrown into Heavens Arena and claws his way up through hundreds of floors over the following years and quits after reaching the 199th floor. By eleven, the body count behind him is already high, and his family decides he isn't acting enough like a proper killer, so his brother Illumi implants a needle in his brain designed to control his behavior through fear, forcing him to retreat when faced with a stronger opponent, and also including making sure he never tries to free Alluka. Tired of his family’s manipulation, he lashes out violently against his own mother and older brother Milluki and flees the estate, landing on the Hunter Exam more out of boredom than ambition. That exam is where the story most people know actually kicks off. He breezes through early physical trials without much effort, casually drinking a poisoned beverage just to prove it can't touch him and even asking for seconds while the guy who handed it to him stands there completely stunned. He meets Gon during the run through the tunnel in the First Phase, and their dynamic clicks almost instantly. Killua is unimpressed by basically everything, constantly bored, constantly sharp-tongued, and Gon's earnest energy is the first thing in his life that seems to genuinely interest him rather than annoy him. Through survival trials, cooking challenges, and a brutal prison-tower gauntlet, the two start acting like an actual team instead of two rivals stuck together. There's also a smaller moment on the exam's airship that says a lot about him. Chairman Netero challenges him and Gon to a simple game, take the ball away from an old man before the ship reaches its destination, with an instant Hunter License on the line. Killua throws everything he's got at it, using rapid blurring footwork to close the distance, and Netero dodges every single attempt without even needing his full strength. Realizing the chairman never once used his dominant hand or leg the entire time, Killua storms off in frustration rather than admit defeat gracefully. He later takes out his frustration on the other participants who happen to bump into him in the hallway, effortlessly defeating them with the skill of a trained assassin. The exam also gives us our first real look at how terrifyingly efficient Killua is when he's not holding back. During the Trick Tower trial, he faces off against a notorious hitman with a nasty reputation, and he ends the fight almost instantly by ripping the man's heart clean out of his chest, only to hand it back once the match is decided (*Episode 11*). Even the manhunt challenge on Zevil Island is no real challenge for him, as he easily claims his target's badge without breaking a sweat (*Episode 17*). To Killua, the Hunter Exam just wasn't challenging enough, and that overconfidence eventually came back to bite him. In the final tournament, he was matched against Pokkle, someone he could have easily beaten, but he gave up simply because he didn't feel like fighting. That decision cost him his only shot at earning a Hunter License, since his next opponent, Gittarackur, turned out to be his older brother Illumi in disguise. Just his presence is enough to paralyze Killua with fear conditioned into him since childhood. Illumi coldly threatens Gon's life purely to prove a point about Killua's supposed inability to care about anyone, and it works well enough to send Killua spiraling. He ends another candidate's life in a fit of confusion and gets disqualified, retreating home in a daze. Back at the estate where he is tortured by Milluki as punishment, his grandfather Zeno steps in, and his father Silva actually sits him down for a real conversation instead of another lesson in obedience. Silva reminds him that he belongs to himself, not to the family's expectations, and lets him choose to leave and rejoin his friends who bravely stormed into the terrifying estate just to get him back. Of course, Killua chooses to leave with his friends, promising that he'll never betray them. He reunites with the group, then later parts ways with Kurapika and Leorio before setting off with Gon to Heavens Arena to train, get stronger, and earn some money. And this is where Nen gets introduced. Early on, Killua fights a kid named Zushi who somehow keeps shrugging off hits that should knock anyone else out cold, and sensing the same aura he felt from Illumi is what convinces Killua he needs to figure out whatever secret technique is being hidden from him. Zushi’s mentor Wing eventually agrees to teach them properly, and after a rushed, risky method of forcing their aura channels open, both boys start learning the fundamentals for real. Killua turns out to be a Transmuter, meaning his aura is best suited for changing its properties rather than just boosting his body or manifesting objects. One especially satisfying moment comes when a whip-wielding opponent tries to shock him out of the ring, and Killua just grabs the whips barehanded, laughs off the current running through them, and electrocutes the guy right back until he's out cold (*Episode 34*). After Gon finally gets his long-awaited match with Hisoka, they leave Heavens Arena, say goodbye to Wing and Zushi, and head back to Whale Island to visit Mito. While they're there, they discover a box Ging left behind for Gon, which introduces them to a mysterious game called Greed Island. Convinced the game is the key to finding Ging, they decide to get their hands on a copy at the Yorknew City auction. In September, they travel to Yorknew to earn enough money for Greed Island and keep their promise to meet up with Leorio and Kurapika. After reuniting with Leorio, the three of them get caught up in the hunt for the Phantom Troupe because of the huge reward. That mission ends with Gon and Killua getting captured, but after escaping, Killua pieces together something important: the Troupe's mysterious target they call "Chain User" is actually Kurapika. With that revelation, Greed Island takes a back seat as Gon and Killua focus on helping Kurapika take on the Phantom Troupe. Things go sideways when Kurapika lets his anger get the better of him, and the boys end up captured again. Luckily, Kurapika turns the situation around by using a blackout to kidnap Chrollo. He then arranges a hostage exchange, the Troupe agrees, and Gon and Killua safely reunite with Kurapika and Leorio. After everything settles down, Killua reminds Gon that they still haven't gotten any closer to buying Greed Island because they're broke. But Gon comes up with a better idea, and after hearing the plan, Killua is all in. The Greed Island arc is where his growth really compounds. To pass Battera's screening test and earn a spot in Greed Island, Killua develops and improves his Hatsu. He channels his aura into electricity, since his body has been conditioning itself to handle voltage his whole life anyway (*Episode 59*). He electrocutes himself with a taser just to study how the current behaves, then reproduces it on his own between his fingertips. This becomes his signature tool for the rest of the series, letting him paralyze opponents, supercharge his own speed, and turn ordinary hits into genuinely lethal strikes. Training under Biscuit, Killua sharpens his electricity abilities into more defined techniques, including Lightning Palm for close combat shocks, electrocuting his opponent by touching them with both palms. He uses this technique in his fight against Sub, one of the Bombers, whom he later defeats (*Episode 73*). At one point during the game, Killua uses a transport card to leave so he can take the 288th Hunter Exam. He clears the very first phase by knocking out every other participant, earning his Hunter License almost immediately and letting him get back to Greed Island as quickly as possible (*Episode 66*). After Gon clears the game, he chooses the Accompany card as reward so he can bring his best friend, Killua, along to meet Ging. But as explained earlier, the card's hidden condition activates and sends the two of them to Kite instead. Then the Chimera Ant crisis happens. At first, Killua along with Kite and Gon, make their way through NGL with little trouble, taking down Chimera Ant foot soldiers along the way, including one that he defeats using nothing but his deadly assassination skills (*Episode 82*). But everything changes when they run into the Royal Guard Neferpitou, who gives them a brutal reminder of just how completely outmatched they really are. The moment Killua sees Kite's severed arm after Pitou's attack, he knows Gon is about to snap and immediately makes the gut call to knock Gon unconscious and drag him to safety rather than let pride cost them both their lives. Following Netero's condition, they head to a nearby town to face Knuckle and Shoot for a chance to earn their tickets back to NGL. They train relentlessly under Biscuit, who pushes Killua's mental growth by telling him that if he couldn't beat Shoot, he should leave Gon's side (*Episode 89*). Even after all that brutal training, though, the two still came up short against the seasoned Hunters. A violent wave knocks a crew member overboard, forcing Leorio and Kurapika to abandon their fight and dive after him. Kurapika realizes that the riddle is completely impossible to answer correctly and stays quiet. The screener sends them to a cabin in the forest to locate a couple who act as Navigators. Inside the cabin, they find an injured man on the floor and a woman captured by a magical beast called a Kiriko. Kurapika and Gon chase the beast into the dark forest. Kurapika notices unique tattoos on the woman's body. Leorio arrives and claims the husband is fine, but Kurapika hits Leorio across the face with his wooden sword. Kurapika reveals he knew this Leorio was actually a shape-shifting Kiriko in disguise. The Kiriko family reveals the whole setup was a test. The Kirikos fly the trio straight to the official exam hall.  Inside the exam tunnel, examiner Satotz leads the applicants on an endless running marathon through the dark tunnel. While running, Kurapika opens up to Leorio about the stolen Scarlet Eyes and his quest to track down the Phantom Troupe. Kurapika and Leorio, who witness this, realize they stand no chance against Hisoka and split up in different directions to escape. Thinking Leorio might've gone back to challenge Hisoka, he heads back too, only to find Gon kneeling on the ground in complete shock. As they run toward the Second Phase at the Visca Forest Preserve, Gon fills him in on everything that happened with him, Hisoka, and Leorio. Kurapika rounds up all the guards and uses Dowsing Chain to force confessions, discovering two guards are spies for higher queens (*Chapter 359*). Suddenly, several Guardian Spirit Beasts created by Kakin's Seed Urn ceremony flood Woble’s room (*Chapter 360*). 4. **Leorio Paradinight**

It fits perfectly with his Emitter-type Nen, which specializes in projecting aura away from the body. It's the first real look at the incredible potential he has been hiding beneath his loud personality, and the crowd absolutely loves it. Before he knows it, the random medical student who just punched Ging ends up becoming a serious candidate in the Chairman Election, finishing surprisingly high against Hunters with far more experience. His honest, unfiltered speech about wanting to use the Hunter Association to save his friend Gon strikes a chord with a lot of Hunters. Before he knows it, he ends up as one of the final contenders for the Chairman position, standing alongside Pariston Hill. But just as the final round of voting is about to begin, a fully healed Gon walks into the election hall. After their emotional reunion, Leorio immediately loses all interest in becoming Chairman and leaves with Gon instead. Before they go their separate ways, he apologizes for not being there when Gon was risking his life. Thanks to the huge support he gained during the Chairman Election, Leorio is later chosen by the Zodiacs to fill the spot left behind by Ging after he resigns (*Chapter 343*). His popularity definitely helped, but the real reason they wanted him was because they needed a doctor for the dangerous expedition to the Dark Continent and someone to assist with the Science Team. Leorio is ready to turn the offer down since he's still focused on medical school, but Cheadle tells him he'll be allowed to study aboard under her guidance. Realizing it's a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity, he accepts Ging's former seat. He then asks if there's room for one more Zodiac and recommends Kurapika, who is later recruited to fill Pariston's vacant spot. The two reunite at Hunter Association headquarters (*Chapter 346*), where Cheadle introduces them to the rest of the Zodiacs. She explains the V6, the Five Threats, and just how dangerous the Dark Continent expedition really is. When Leorio hears the mission is ranked even higher than the Chimera Ant crisis — the battle that claimed Chairman Netero's life — he can't help but feel nervous. Even so, he promises to do his best and officially joins the Science Team as one of its doctors. Once the Black Whale sets sail, he works under Cheadle in the Tier 3 medical facility, helping treat patients, carrying supplies, and following her instructions (*Chapter 359*). On the eleventh day of the voyage, he's part of the medical team treating "Prince Halkenburg" after the prince suddenly collapses (*Chapter 404*).

5. **Hisoka Morow**

You're probably wondering why Hisoka gets a spot in this lineup, so let's get into it. There are a bunch of hints scattered through the show suggesting he's secretly the fifth protagonist *of Hunter x Hunter*. He's had plenty of terrifying moments and his motivations stay questionable at best, but the more the story goes on, the more he acts like a weird support system for the main cast instead of an actual villain trying to crush them. A chunk of the fandom has already knocked him down from villain to anti-hero, and some go as far as calling him a straight-up protagonist. The birthday theory backs this up too. Leorio was born on 3/3, Kurapika on 4/4, Gon on 5/5, and Killua on 7/7. Hisoka was born on 6/6, completing the pattern like he was slotted in on purpose. Add in the official 1999 promo art ([https://www.pinterest.com/pin/hunter-x-hunter-1999-version-hisoka-killua-gon-kurapika-and-leorio--298222806551327120/](https://www.pinterest.com/pin/hunter-x-hunter-1999-version-hisoka-killua-gon-kurapika-and-leorio--298222806551327120/)) that puts him right next to the other four leads, and it really looks like Togashi has been dropping hints about this the whole time. Hisoka barely talks about his past because honestly, he doesn't care about it, and Togashi keeps that mystery locked up tight. The only confirmed detail is that Hisoka named his signature abilities after a candy and a gum brand he loved as a kid, which is why we ended up with names like Texture Surprise and Bungee Gum. Sometime after the Kurta clan tragedy, he wormed his way into the Phantom Troupe by faking his way in and eventually claiming the number four spot. Around the same period, he introduced Kastro to Nen back at Heavens Arena after spotting real potential in him. Not long after that, he joined the 286th Hunter Exam, casually took out twenty people, nearly ended an examiner, and managed to get himself disqualified. By the time the story actually starts, he's back for another shot at the license. Hisoka makes his grand entrance in the 287th Hunter Exam as applicant number 44, already infamous for the chaos he caused the year before. He wastes zero time reminding everyone why he's a problem. An unlucky applicant bumps into him and loses both arms shortly after. During the First Phase where everyone else is wheezing, Hisoka strolls through like he's taking a casual walk. Then things get messier at the Milsy Wetlands, a swamp packed with fog and shapeshifting predators. Before they can even begin the marathon, a stranger shows up claiming he is the real examiner and Satotz is secretly a Man-faced Ape in disguise. Panic breaks out, and out of nowhere, Hisoka attacks both of them with a flurry of playing cards. The fake examiner goes down instantly, while Satotz effortlessly catches every card thrown at him. That alone is enough to convince everyone that Satotz is the real examiner, and the marathon resumes. But the whole incident awakens Hisoka's bloodlust. When a thick fog rolls in, he decides to "play examiner" himself, hunting down nearby applicants and trying to take them out. Among the people caught in his rampage are Kurapika and Leorio. The two split up to boost their odds, and Hisoka actually respects the move. When Leorio circles back to fight him head-on, he toys with him and gets caught off guard by Gon's fishing rod when Gon returns to look for his friends. Rather than getting mad, he gets fascinated by their nerve, knocks Leorio out, sizes Gon up, and decides they've both passed. He then strolls off carrying an unconscious Leorio, casually slicing through any creature in his path. In the Third Phase, he runs into Togari (*Episode 10*), the examiner he attacked the previous year, and after getting nicked on the shoulder, ends the fight by taking Togari's head off and continuing on, finishing the Trick Tower descent in just over six hours and making him the first applicant to reach the bottom first. During the Fourth Phase on Zevil Island, he barely cares who his assigned target is and mostly wanders around waiting for something fun to happen. Gon manages to sneak close thanks to the butterflies drawn to Hisoka's bleeding wound from his fight with Togari. Before Gon can act, a dying fighter named Goz challenges him, but he refuses to fight someone already finished. Gittarackur, secretly Illumi Zoldyck in disguise, steps in to give Goz his final fight instead and hands Hisoka an extra badge afterward. As night falls, Hisoka finally gets moving, runs into Leorio and Kurapika again, and trades them a badge for free out of respect for how far they've come. Still itching for a real fight, he tracks down another examinee and finishes him off. While that is happening, Gon successfully swipes his badge, which impresses him enough to follow Gon's trail. On the way, he comes across Geretta, who has just paralyzed Gon with a dart and stolen all of his badges. He quickly takes Geretta out, claims the badges for himself, and finds Gon still lying there, unable to move. He then drops Geretta's head in front of Gon before handing back Gon's badge along with his own. He explains that Geretta was his target, and with Geretta's badge, the one Illumi gave him, the one Kurapika handed over, and the badge he took from the random examinee earlier, he now has enough points to pass the exam. Gon refuses to accept it out of pride, and he sends the kid flying with a right hook. With the week up, Hisoka clears the Fourth Phase along with eight others. In an interview prior to the Final Test, Chairman Netero asks why he wants a Hunter License. He admits it's basically a permission slip to do whatever he wants without consequences. He's already got his sights on Gon and Killua but isn't in a rush to fight either of them yet. He then says that if there's anyone he wants to fight more than anyone else right now, it's the Chairman himself. But Netero casually dismisses him. The final round is a one-on-one bracket, and Hisoka's first match against Kurapika ends almost instantly when he whispers something and forfeits on the spot, giving Kurapika the win. His next opponent, Bodoro, refuses to tap out even after getting completely outmatched, until Hisoka leans down and whispers something to make him surrender. That win secures his Hunter License. Before the exam wraps, he overhears Gon confronting Illumi about rescuing Killua from the Zoldyck family, and Hisoka warns Illumi to leave Gon alone, not out of loyalty, just because Gon's still his prey. Around the same time, Kurapika secretly meets with him to follow up on what he whispered to him during their tournament match. At some point, he had already learned that Kurapika is the last surviving member of the Kurta Clan and that he's hunting the Phantom Troupe for what they did to his people. He tells Kurapika that the Troupe will be in Yorknew City on September 1st for the Underground Auction, and he'll be there as well. After Gon and the others pull Killua out of his family's estate, the group agrees to reunite six months later in Yorknew City. In the meantime, Gon and Killua head to Heavens Arena to train, and Hisoka's already waiting on the 200th floor when they arrive. He blocks their path using Ren just to test where they stand, and when they can't push through, he tells them flat out they're not ready yet. The boys go learn the basics of Nen from Wing and come back stronger, and this time he lets them pass. He even tells Gon that winning one fight on this floor might earn him a shot at facing him directly. He watches Gon's fight against Gido with interest, especially once Gon starts using Zetsu mid-battle. Then comes his own fight with Kastro (*Episodes 31 \- 32*), the guy still stinging from an old loss. Kastro comes out strong, landing hits from strange angles and racking up an early lead, but he stays relaxed even after his arm gets sliced off. It turns out he was baiting Kastro into revealing that his tricky angles come from a Nen clone. Once the secret's exposed, he gets serious and starts mixing in sleight of hand to throw Kastro off. Bungee Gum lets him give his aura the properties of both rubber and gum, making it incredibly sticky and elastic. Thanks to its durability, flexibility, and strong adhesive qualities, it's an insanely versatile ability that works for offense, defense, and all kinds of tricky setups. He can activate it from his fingers, hands, or feet, and attach it to a target either through direct contact or by extending his aura from a distance, making it tough to dodge. Whenever he wants, he can command it to snap back, and the farther it's stretched, the stronger the recoil. His other ability, Texture Surprise, lets him cover smooth, flat surfaces like paper or cloth with his aura and change their appearance. It's perfect for deception, allowing him to recreate the look of over a thousand different textures and images. When Kastro's clone slices his other arm too, his original right arm mysteriously reappears, because he'd hidden it using Bungee Gum earlier and reattached it using Texture Surprise to fake full recovery. Kastro barely has time to process this before he ends the match with a clean hit to the chin followed by a card strike that finishes Kastro for good. After the fight, Machi stitches his arms back together with Nen threads while he covers the damage using his Texture Surprise trick. After paying for her service, Machi reminds him of the Troupe's meeting up in Yorknew soon, and he brushes her off with a lazy flirt attempt before she leaves. Later, while showering, he peels off the fake spider tattoo on his back, revealing his Troupe membership was just another layer of misdirection made with Texture Surprise the whole time. Gon eventually wins his rematch against Gido and his next fight against Riehlvelt. True to his word, Hisoka accepts Gon's challenge and tells him to pick whatever time works best for their fight. On fight day, Hisoka barely moves at first, casually blocking everything Gon throws at him, until Gon lands a real hit and hands his badge back as a symbol that he's ready now. He accepts it and ramps up the intensity, figuring out Gon's an Enhancer while revealing himself as a Transmuter. From there it's a one-sided beatdown, with Hisoka toying with Gon's attempts at distance using Bungee Gum to yank him right back in. He messes with Gon's head by asking him to guess when he attached the gum, offering no correct answer among the choices. When Gon charges recklessly, he has to physically stop himself from going too far, savoring the moment before landing a counter. He wraps up the match by sneakily attaching Bungee Gum to a rock and yanking it towards Gon to get the decisive point. As he walks off with the victory, he warns Gon that their next match won't be a game anymore, it'll be a fight to the finish. Come Yorknew City, he joins the Phantom Troupe as they gear up to raid the Mafia's underground auction. While part of the group heads out, he stays behind with Chrollo and a few others, listening in as Uvogin reports on the phone that the auction vault is empty as if someone tipped them, leading them to suspect a possible traitor among them. Once Kurapika captures Uvogin, Hisoka sneaks off to meet him, revealing he only joined the Troupe planning to eventually fight Chrollo, and offers intel on several members in exchange for teaming up. Their conversation gets cut short when Kurapika receives a call informing him of Uvogin’s escape. After Kurapika eliminates Uvogin, the rest of the Troupe hunts for the mysterious Chain User, and Hisoka keeps quiet about who that actually is. When Gon and Killua later get captured and brought to the hideout, Hisoka pretends not to recognize. The Troupe eventually storms the auction, wipes out the guards running it, and fakes several of their own deaths using Kortopi's cloning ability. Hisoka quietly tries to call Kurapika to tell him that the corpses are fake, though the call doesn't land in time. When Chrollo decides it's time to leave the city, tension rises within the group, so he uses Neon’s fortune-telling power he stole earlier to keep everyone in line. Hisoka's own fortune reveals he's planning to challenge Chrollo on a specific day soon, predicting several Troupe deaths in the process. To hide this, he fakes a safer version using Texture Surprise, buying himself more time while secretly feeding Kurapika the real info about the fake corpses to keep the standoff going. Once Chrollo eventually gets captured, Hisoka calls in Illumi to impersonate him back at their hideout while he follows the hostage exchange to the airport, using Gon and Killua's safety as leverage to get himself onboard. Once the trade is done, he finally corners Chrollo one-on-one. Since he was never an actual Troupe member, none of the chain’s rules is broken, and Chrollo begins to explain what Kurapika did to him, rendering him powerless at the moment. In the end, Hisoka decides he's not interested in fighting someone that is already broken, spares him, and walks away from the Troupe entirely. Later, he meets up with Chrollo, who asks for his help in tracking down a Nen exorcist currently inside Greed Island. If the exorcist can remove Kurapika's Nen restrictions on him, Chrollo promises to finally give him the fight he's been waiting for. So he enters Greed Island using Chrollo's name, hoping it'll catch the attention of any Phantom Troupe members who also made it into the game, and sure enough it works. It also catches the attention of Gon and Killua. When Gon and the others stumble across him bathing by a lake, he plays dumb about his real motives and helps them clear a major challenge involving dodgeball against the Game Master Razor. His Bungee Gum comes in clutch multiple times during the match, from redirecting the ball to catching a brutal throw that breaks two of his fingers. After the group wins, he splits off and eventually links up with the rest of the Troupe once they track down the exorcist, leaving the island with him shortly after. Following the chaos of the Chimera Ant crisis that leaves Netero gone, he resurfaces during the Hunter Chairman Election, not to vote but purely to scout new talent. He casually rates the strongest Hunters in the room like he's judging a talent show, throws in a blank ballot for fun, and stays completely unbothered by the political games everyone else is playing. He crosses paths with Illumi again, who fills him in on everything, including Killua's risky plan to save Gon using his sister Alluka's dangerous wish-granting power. Illumi wants to eliminate Alluka to avoid the risk entirely, but Hisoka's far more interested in watching the chaos unfold than playing it safe. As Killua rushes toward the hospital, Hisoka teams up loosely with Illumi, facing off against Gotoh who buys time for Killua to escape. He overwhelms Gotoh easily, exposing exactly how Bungee Gum works before finishing him off. He and Illumi continue working together to track the siblings down, eliminating anyone else sent to interfere, though Hisoka can barely contain his own bloodlust the whole time, debating whether taking out Killua or Alluka would be more satisfying either way. Once Chrollo finally gets his restriction removed, he agrees to a proper rematch with him at Heavens Arena (*Chapters 351 \- 357*). Chrollo opens the fight by hijacking the referee's body to attack, and once Hisoka breaks free, Chrollo reveals he can now stack multiple stolen abilities together instead of switching one at a time using a Bookmark. He turns the referee into a puppet, and when Hisoka shuts that down, Chrollo throws even more powers into the mix, including one post-mortem Nen ability from someone already gone, making it dangerously permanent unless triggered to explode. The fight spirals as Chrollo disguises himself in the crowd and turns dozens of spectators into puppets using the abilities of Shalnark and Kortopi that he borrowed beforehand, to swarm Hisoka. Hisoka fights through the wave using Bungee Gum as a weapon, but Chrollo rigs one of the puppet heads to detonate mid-fight, tearing off a chunk of his own. He keeps charging through one explosive trap after another while Chrollo creates more and more clones from the audience, turning them into living bombs to overwhelm him. Eventually, Hisoka gets surrounded by a massive swarm of explosive puppets. With nowhere left to escape, the final explosion finally brings him down, seemingly ending the fight for good. Machi and the others gather around his body, believing it's over. With Machi left alone to fix his corpse, his aura suddenly flares back to life. Turns out he used Bungee Gum to restart his own heart and lungs after his death, surviving through what basically amounts to post-mortem Nen, a trick he picked up by watching Chrollo use it first. He patches himself up, restrains Machi just long enough to deliver a message, and declares he's coming for every remaining member of the Phantom Troupe. Not long after, he ambushes and finishes off Kortopi and Shalnark in brutal fashion, arranging their bodies as a twisted warning before walking off. The Phantom Troupe eventually boards the Black Whale, the massive ship heading for the Dark Continent and the secret battleground for the Kakin Empire's Succession War. This time, though, their top priority is hunting down Hisoka. The Troupe splits into smaller groups and spreads across the Black Whale, searching every tier for him (*Chapter 377*). Around this time, Illumi joins the Troupe and the manhunt as well. Turns out Hisoka actually hired him to eventually come after him, turning the whole hunt into another one of their twisted games. Meanwhile, Troupe member Bonolenov starts impersonating Hisoka (*Chapter 392*) to throw off the various mafia families also hunting him, leading to a string of mistaken identities and casualties among the people searching. When the real Hisoka finally resurfaces (*Chapter 405*), he does it his way, sitting down for a casual card game in the ship's casino, completely unbothered by the manhunt closing in around him. As factions scramble to locate him and Chrollo plots his next move using stolen royal treasures to power up before their next clash, the story leaves things on a cliffhanger, with two of the deadliest fighters in the series circling each other on a ship that's about to get a whole lot smaller.
