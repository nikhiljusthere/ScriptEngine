**The 7 Deadliest Domain Expansions Ranked and Explained**

1. **7th Place: Authentic Mutual Love**

Authentic Mutual Love is Yuta Okkotsu’s Domain Expansion, and it’s basically the moment where his Copy technique goes from “already ridiculous” to “absolutely unfair.” When this domain unfolds, the environment transforms into a massive open space filled with towering cross-shaped pillars of all sizes. Every inch of the ground and the pillars is jammed with countless katanas, like someone dumped an entire armory into a cursed hellscape. Above all of this, the sky is wrapped in floating rings of mizuhiki, or intricate decorative cords from traditional Japanese knot-tying. The whole place looks calm at a glance, but everything in it is designed for nonstop technique spam. The foundation of this Domain Expansion is how Yuta uses Copy. When he casts the domain, he chooses one copied cursed technique to weave directly into the barrier itself. That technique becomes the guaranteed-hit effect. On top of that, every other technique he has stockpiled gets assigned randomly to the thousands of katanas scattered around. Only Yuta can trigger the abilities hidden inside the swords, and he doesn’t know which technique is in which blade until he actually picks one up. Each sword breaks after a single activation, but since the supply is infinite, he can keep cycling through abilities as long as the domain holds. The first time Yuta technically expanded this domain was during his fight with Ryu Ishigori and Takako Uro in the Sendai Colony during the Culling Game. They all popped their domains one after another, but between overlapping conditions and Kurourushi’s interference, none of them stabilized. Everything collapsed before fully forming, so Authentic Mutual Love didn’t get to show off at that point. The real showcase happened during Yuta’s battle against Sukuna. After exchanging a few quick blows with the King of Curses, Yuta committed and expanded his domain. For the guaranteed-hit technique, he activated Angel’s Technique: Extinguishment which negates all other cursed techniques and embedded it into the barrier. This forced Sukuna to constantly maintain Hollow Wicker Basket to neutralize the domain’s guaranteed-hit effect. Keeping that up tied down half of Sukuna’s arms and mouths, which meant his offensive options dropped significantly. Even for Sukuna, that was a huge handicap. With Sukuna locked into defense, Yuta started pulling blades. The first katana he grabbed contained Takako Uro’s Sky Manipulation technique. He combined it with Thin Ice Breaker to land a hit on Sukuna, slicing the left side of his face, though the sword naturally shattered afterward. Then he switched to another blade loaded with Dhruv Lakdawalla’s technique. Activating it summoned three small shikigami that orbited Sukuna and created a closed path around him. When Sukuna crossed that path, the shikigami cut into him, forcing him to adjust his movement. While fighting alongside Yuji, Yuta grabbed a katana imbued with Cursed Speech. With a single command, he froze Sukuna in place long enough to stop him from cleaving Yuji in half. Yuta immediately followed by smacking Sukuna away using another Thin Ice Breaker blade. As the fight continued, Yuta revealed even more surprises. At some point before this battle, he had copied Charles Bernard’s G Warstaff technique, and with it, he accessed precognitive predictions of Sukuna’s movements. Using those predictions, Yuta closed in and landed a heavy hit. He noticed that Sukuna always gave away a small opening whenever encountering a new technique for the first time, so Yuta grabbed another random sword and cloaked his thrust using Takako’s technique. Sukuna managed to dodge and grabbed the blade, but that worked in Yuta’s favor. The technique inside the sword was Sukuna’s own Cleave. Yuta immediately activated it and carved into Sukuna’s face. Later, when Sukuna decided to drop Hollow Wicker Basket so he could attempt to use his world-cutting Dismantle, Yuta pulled the biggest move in his arsenal. He triggered Angel’s extension technique Jacob’s Ladder at full power. The guaranteed-hit effect targeted Sukuna with a beam of blinding light, burning his body and weakening the curse object’s grip on Megumi’s soul. This was the peak of the domain’s effectiveness, hitting Sukuna with a technique he could neither predict nor comfortably block after dropping his protection. Eventually, though, everything collapsed once Yuta took a devastating world-cutting slash in retaliation. The domain broke apart, leaving Yuta heavily injured but having dealt massive damage. Authentic Mutual Love earns its spot as one of the deadliest domains because it turns the battlefield into a nonstop roulette of techniques — many of them already top-tier on their own. The guaranteed-hit effect locks the opponent into constant pressure, and every sword is basically a single-use, high-impact surprise attack. Yuta’s ability to chain multiple copied abilities, adapt instantly, and attack from angles his opponent doesn’t expect gives him absurd versatility. Even Sukuna, the most experienced sorcerer in history, was forced into a defensive posture because of how the domain interrupts his rhythm and demands nonstop counters. But it’s not a perfect domain. It relies heavily on Yuta’s collection of copied techniques, so its strength changes depending on what he has stored at the moment. The random distribution inside the katanas means he doesn’t have full control over what technique he’ll get next, so even though he adapts quickly, there’s always an element of luck involved. On top of that, maintaining such a huge variety of techniques and powering the domain drains a ton of energy. Against someone like Sukuna, the domain collapsed the moment Yuta suffered a serious injury, which shows how vulnerable it becomes if he takes a heavy hit. Still, Authentic Mutual Love is terrifying. One slip is enough for Yuta to pull a blade loaded with exactly the ability he needs, and that’s what makes this domain one of the biggest threats in the series.

2. **6th Place: Deadly Sentencing**

Deadly Sentencing is Hiromi Higuruma’s Domain Expansion, and it’s easily one of the most unique domains in the series. Instead of a battlefield or a killing zone, the space turns into a compact courtroom where Higuruma and his target stand across from each other as prosecutor and defendant. All around them are guillotines lined up in the background, complete with chopped-up debris underneath. The scene doesn’t try to hide what the stakes are. Once the domain activates, everything moves straight into a trial, and every action inside has to follow the rules of Higuruma’s trial-based cursed technique. This domain doesn’t use a guaranteed-hit attack. Instead, the “sure-hit” effect is forcing everyone inside to participate in a legally binding trial set by Higuruma’s technique. Violence is automatically blocked. Even if the defendant tries to dash at him the moment the domain goes up, they snap right back to their stand before they can make contact. The domain also comes with a binding vow that requires Higuruma to explain the rules to the defendant, so they’re never completely kept in the dark about what’s going on. But even with that knowledge, beating this trial isn't easy. The core of Deadly Sentencing revolves around Judgeman, the shikigami who acts as judge, jury, and executioner. The moment the domain fully forms, Judgeman starts the proceedings. First, it announces the charges against the defendant. These charges are always based on real actions the person has taken or actual crimes they potentially committed. Judgeman automatically knows everything about everyone inside, but it doesn’t share insider info with Higuruma. It only gives him what he needs to act as prosecutor. After the accusation is stated, the defendant gets exactly one chance to defend themselves. They can either stay silent, confess, or deny the charges. Denial doesn’t have to be honest. They can lie, twist details, or try to build a believable story. Their goal is simple: erase doubt and convince Judgeman to pronounce them innocent. Meanwhile, Higuruma as the prosecutor also gets one statement, but his job is to rebut the defendant using evidence Judgeman provides. That evidence appears as a sealed envelope in Higuruma’s hand. The envelope won’t be opened until the defendant already gives their answer, so the defendant can’t rely on knowing what’s inside when they build their defense. Higuruma does get the contents explained to him once he receives it, so his rebuttal is based on whatever Judgeman selected. The evidence doesn’t need to be strong or flawless — Judgeman just chooses it according to the case. After statements from both sides, Judgeman delivers the verdict. If the defendant is found guilty, Judgeman hands out a sentence. The domain hints at multiple possible penalties, but the ones we’ve seen are Confiscation and Confiscation \+ Death Penalty, with Confiscation often stacking onto harsher punishments. Confiscation temporarily removes the target’s ability to use cursed techniques. If they don’t have an innate technique, the punishment shifts to cutting them off from cursed energy entirely. If they’re holding a cursed tool, the effect targets the tool first and prioritizes confiscating it before affecting their techniques. The heaviest punishment is Confiscation \+ Death Penalty, the most extreme sentence Deadly Sentencing can issue. When this happens, Confiscation kicks in first, and then Higuruma’s gavel transforms into the Executioner’s Sword, a weapon that kills instantly with even the slightest cut. It doesn’t matter how tough the target is or how much cursed energy they have — one touch from the blade ends the sentenced soul. If the target has multiple souls or entities inside them, only the soul actually sentenced is destroyed, which keeps the execution precise. The verdict isn’t decided by Higuruma. Even he can’t predict what Judgeman will conclude. Depending on the severity of the crime, Judgeman may go softer or much harsher. But once Judgeman announces guilt, the domain dispels, and the sentence takes effect immediately. However, there’s one loophole: if the defendant is found guilty without giving an open confession, they can request a retrial, and Judgeman must accept it. This gives the defendant another shot, but with new charges. Higuruma awakened his cursed technique thanks to Kenjaku, and since he’s a brilliant lawyer, a trial-style domain fits him perfectly. He used Deadly Sentencing to eliminate several players during the Culling Game before encountering Yuji, where the domain got a full showcase. As soon as Higuruma cast the domain, Yuji tried to attack before it took effect. The domain immediately reset him back to his stand. Judgeman accused Yuji of underage entry into a pachinko parlor. Yuji assumed the evidence would catch him entering the establishment, so he tried to minimize the damage by claiming he only went inside to use the bathroom. Unfortunately for Yuji, this backfired. The evidence was a photo of him at a cash exchange on the same day. Higuruma pointed out that Yuji basically confirmed the accusation himself by admitting he entered the facility. Yuji argued that he never had a chance, but Higuruma explained that parlors and cash exchanges are separate businesses. The only thing Yuji needed to do was deny ever seeing the parlor, not explain why he was inside it. Judgeman ruled him guilty and punished him with Confiscation. Since Yuji doesn’t have an innate technique, the effect removed his cursed energy entirely. Most sorcerers rely so heavily on their techniques that losing them wrecks their combat instincts, and Yuji immediately felt that pressure. Yuji had one saving grace: since he didn’t confess, he could request a retrial. He shouted for it and got pulled back into the courtroom with Higuruma. For the retrial, Judgeman accused Yuji of mass murder during the Shibuya Incident. This time, Yuji confessed. Judgeman immediately sentenced him to Confiscation \+ Death Penalty, granting Higuruma the Executioner’s Sword. But once Higuruma examined the evidence, he realized the murders were committed by Sukuna while Yuji was unwillingly possessed. Drawing on his past as a lawyer who fought for the falsely accused, Higuruma dispelled the sword and spared him. Later, Higuruma used his domain again during the all-out fight against Sukuna. The plan was simple: redo Yuji’s second trial, but this time include Sukuna as a co-defendant. That way, Judgeman would bring up the Shibuya massacre again, letting Higuruma prosecute Sukuna directly. Since Sukuna knew the domain rules from possessing Yuji, he casually admitted guilt to push the process toward the Death Penalty. He only cared about access to the Executioner’s Sword, because he believed it would help him escape the sorcerers’ strategy. Judgeman delivered the exact verdict the sorcerers wanted: Confiscation \+ Death Penalty. In theory, this should have stripped Sukuna of his cursed technique and armed Higuruma with the one weapon capable of killing even him in a single cut. But Higuruma overlooked a crucial detail: Confiscation targets cursed techniques or cursed tools, prioritizing tools when the target is using one. Sukuna had Kamutoke on hand, and Confiscation removed the tool instead of shutting down his cursed technique. That mistake threw the entire strategy off and put everyone at a disadvantage. Higuruma strikes Sukuna with the executioner’s sword, but Sukuna reacts so fast he slices off one of Higuruma’s hands before the technique can kick in. Then he follows it up with a clean, fatal strike that puts Higuruma down for good.  Despite his loss, Deadly Sentencing is still brutal because it forces opponents into a situation they can’t brute-force their way out of. The trial starts immediately, violence is suppressed, the rules can’t be broken, and both parties get only one shot at building their case. Even if the domain isn’t inherently destructive, the consequences absolutely can be. Even the strongest sorcerers aren’t trained to win debates under stress. A single mistake — one wrong word — can mean losing all techniques or getting sentenced to death. Obviously, the domain isn’t flawless, though. It comes with clear drawbacks. Higuruma can’t control Judgeman’s verdicts. He has influence only through his rebuttal, so even he doesn’t know which punishment the target will get. So if Judgeman selects a minor offense, the sentence might be too weak to matter. Furthermore, Confiscation can target the wrong thing. As seen with Sukuna, if the defendant has a cursed tool, Confiscation hits that first. Higuruma must also abide by the domain rules. He can’t use violence either, so if the sentence doesn’t disable the enemy, he can be in real trouble. And the whole process itself requires the full cycle: accusation, statement, rebuttal, verdict. There’s no instant start-to-finish strike. Even with those limits, the domain’s structure is terrifyingly airtight. Against anyone who isn’t mentally disciplined, it’s practically an instant win. And with the Executioner’s Sword in play, Deadly Sentencing becomes one of the rare domains that can kill even top-tier threats in a single stroke, which absolutely earns it a spot on this list.

3. **5th Place: Time Cell Moon Palace** 

Time Cell Moon Palace is one of those domains that hits way harder once you understand the cursed technique behind it, so it helps to start with Projection Sorcery itself. This inherited technique is passed down in the Zenin clan and eventually ends up with Naoya from his father Naobito, and the whole idea is to chop one second into twenty-four individual “frames,” almost like Naoya is turning reality into a flipbook. He sets a full sequence of movements into those frames ahead of time and then carries them out instantly in that single second. Because of that, he moves with this unnatural, jittery speed that even special grade curses struggle to track. The problem is that anything his palm touches also has to follow those twenty-four frames. If they fail to match the rule, they freeze in place for a full second, stuck in that single frame. That one second is more than enough for Naoya to completely shred someone, because a frozen target is basically a free hit, and breaking that frozen frame usually comes with severe injury. Projection Sorcery isn’t something Naoya can spam without consequences, though. The paths he sets cannot be adjusted once he commits, so any unexpected change in direction becomes a huge liability. If he ignores physics too heavily or pushes the movement too far, the technique locks him up as well and he freezes just like his victims. There’s also a limit to how fast his body can accelerate with the technique, although keeping it active lets his speed stack over time, making prolonged fights incredibly dangerous for anyone facing him. After he loses to Maki and ends up dying from a stab wound inflicted by Maki’s mother, Naoya reappears in the Culling Game as a vengeful cursed spirit — basically the worst-case scenario considering his personality and technique. In this new cursed form, Naoya pushes Projection Sorcery far enough to unlock his domain expansion, Time Cell Moon Palace. He uses it for the first time during his fight against Maki, Daido, and Miyo, and this is where his technique becomes genuinely lethal on a completely different level. When Time Cell Moon Palace unfolds, the domain creates a black void filled with just a few floating particles and a fleshy path stretching toward a giant eye behind Naoya. The environment itself is unsettling, but the danger comes from the domain’s guaranteed-hit rule. Instead of targeting the whole body like regular Projection Sorcery, the domain forces “film strips” into the necks of those caught inside. Once that happens, every single cell in the body has to follow the twenty-four-frame rule. Missing a frame on even a tiny cellular level creates countless tiny cuts across the body. Those cuts stack instantly, turning any movement into a self-inflicted massacre. Because the domain is so precise, the moment Daido and Miyo try to move normally, their bodies erupt with blood from all the micro-tears caused by failing the frame rule. Daido even loses his hand when he tries to swing the Split Soul Katana, since forcing the motion causes his arm to break the rule completely. Both fighters are immobilized almost instantly, showing just how brutal this domain is when its conditions are properly met. It basically weaponizes the target’s own biology against them, making the technique impossible to counter unless you already understand Projection Sorcery’s mechanics — and even then, actually reacting at twenty-four frames per second is basically impossible for a normal sorcerer. The only reason Maki survives inside the domain is because Naoya’s technique doesn’t acknowledge her. She has no cursed energy after her transformation, and Projection Sorcery depends on cursed energy interaction to properly enforce its rules. Without that, the domain can’t apply its auto-hit effect to her cells. She moves normally, unaffected by the animation frames, and closes in while Naoya is focused on Daido and Miyo. She stabs Naoya through the chest, seemingly killing him, but Naoya refuses to go down quietly. His cursed spirit body twists into an even more monstrous form as he taunts Maki for not finishing him. Maki doesn’t let him drag things out; she swings upward and completely cleaves him in half, finally exorcising him for good. Time Cell Moon Palace earns its spot among the deadliest domains because it removes almost any chance of survival once you’re caught inside. It forces every cell in the opponent’s body to obey Projection Sorcery’s twenty-four-frame system, and since no human body can consciously respond at that speed, victims end up shredding themselves simply by existing. The level of precision is far beyond what Naoya can achieve with regular Projection Sorcery, and the domain’s auto-hit condition bypasses defensive techniques by attacking the internal structure of the body rather than relying on brute force. But despite how dangerous it is, the domain still has limitations. The biggest one is that Naoya cannot enforce the rules on someone with no cursed energy, which is exactly why Maki walks through it untouched. It also inherits Projection Sorcery’s core restriction: the user needs predictable, pre-set movement patterns and must avoid breaking physics too severely. In other words, Naoya is still at risk of freezing himself if he miscalculates, even inside his own domain. And since the domain doesn’t automatically kill the target but instead punishes movement, someone who understands its conditions might be able to stall for a moment — though surviving long enough to turn that into a comeback is extremely unlikely for anyone except someone like Maki. But for anyone else? Getting caught inside this domain is basically a one-way trip to being sliced apart frame by frame.

4. **4th Place: Coffin of The Iron Mountain** 

Coffin of the Iron Mountain is Jogo’s signature domain expansion, and it takes everything about his Disaster Flames technique and pushes it to its most extreme form. Since Jogo can already manipulate fire and lava, his domain basically drops the target into the heart of an active volcano. The moment the domain forms, the environment turns into a sealed stone chamber filled with jagged fissures running across the walls and floor. Those cracks constantly spew magma, filling the air with so much heat that any ordinary sorcerer would ignite before they even get the chance to scream. This isn’t just uncomfortable heat — it’s a level of temperature meant to erase anything living. What makes Coffin of the Iron Mountain especially dangerous is its guaranteed-hit effect. Jogo can command molten rock from anywhere in the domain and direct it straight at his opponent with no chance of missing. It’s basically a constant threat of being smashed, burned, and melted all at once, delivered by a special grade cursed spirit who knows exactly how to weaponize volcanic heat. In a typical fight, this domain would be an instant death sentence. Even sorcerers with solid defensive techniques would struggle to survive a few seconds because the air alone cooks everything from the inside out. The first time Jogo uses this domain is during his fight against Satoru Gojo. Jogo is desperate at this point because he knows he needs a surefire way to bypass Infinity, and a guaranteed-hit domain is his best option. He expands his domain and tries to overwhelm Gojo with the heat and lava attacks that no normal sorcerer could deal with. But the “strongest sorcerer in the world” not only walks through the domain without taking damage — he casually protects Yuji from the heat too. Jogo fires off the domain’s sure-hit attack, only for Gojo to counter it instantly with his own technique. When Jogo tries to force even more power into the domain in hopes of reducing Gojo to ash, Gojo responds by unleashing Unlimited Void. The difference in refinement between the two domains is massive, and Gojo’s domain easily crushes Jogo’s, forcing it to collapse almost immediately. After getting completely overwhelmed by Gojo’s domain, Jogo realizes exactly how outclassed he is. Because of that, he doesn’t even bother trying to use Coffin of the Iron Mountain during his fight with Sukuna. He knows Sukuna’s domain would shut it down instantly, and considering the King of Curses’ attitude during their fight, he doesn’t get a second chance to reconsider. Despite that, Coffin of the Iron Mountain still earns its spot among the deadliest domains because of how lethal it is right from the moment it activates. The environment alone is enough to incinerate most sorcerers without needing a single follow-up attack. Combine that with a guaranteed-hit made of molten rock and you get a domain that can wipe out almost anyone who isn’t a top-tier monster, we’re talking about Gojo- or Sukuna-level of power here. It’s a brutally simple concept: extreme heat, constant magma pressure, and attacks you can’t dodge. But it’s not without its drawbacks. The biggest issue is refinement. Jogo’s domain is deadly, but it lacks the sophistication needed to compete with stronger or more precise domains. Against someone like Gojo or Sukuna, Coffin of the Iron Mountain doesn’t stand a chance in a clash. And since domain battles are often decided by refinement, Jogo ends up in a situation where his domain is terrifying to most opponents but completely ineffective against the strongest sorcerers and curses. Even with that flaw, the raw lethality of the environment and the guaranteed-hit effect make it one of the most dangerous domains ever shown — just not one that can climb any higher against the absolute top-tier monsters of the series.

5. **3rd Place: Self Embodiment Of Perfection**

Self-Embodiment of Perfection is Mahito at his absolute scariest, because it takes everything dangerous about Idle Transfiguration and pushes it into a space where you basically have no way out. Idle Transfiguration already lets him reshape souls with a single touch, and since the body follows the soul, that means one slip-up against him can turn you into a twisted mess instantly. When he expands his domain, he gets rid of his biggest limitation: the need for physical contact. Inside this domain, Mahito can alter your soul just by willing it, and once that happens, the fight is over. He activates Self-Embodiment of Perfection using tiny hands that form seals inside his mouth, and the moment the domain unfolds, the entire environment turns into a black void framed by giant hands clasped together like a blooming shape around the target. From these hands stretch countless arms that weave together into a net, completely surrounding anyone trapped inside. Nanami later describes the feeling as being literally in Mahito’s palm, and honestly, based on how the domain works, that description makes total sense. Inside this space, Mahito’s Idle Transfiguration gets cranked up to full power. He no longer needs to land a hit to reshape a soul; he just does it, and the guaranteed-hit effect makes sure the target’s soul is touched the moment they’re sealed inside. Nanami considers Self-Embodiment of Perfection to be one of the most unavoidable and lethal domains in existence, placing it on the same level as Gojo’s Unlimited Void in terms of guaranteed victory. Mahito’s first time using this ability happens during his fight with Nanami and Yuji, right when he’s pushed to the brink of death. The pressure unlocks his ability to form a Domain Expansion, and he immediately uses it to trap Nanami inside while locking Yuji out. Nanami is seconds away from being erased since Mahito doesn’t need physical contact anymore. But everything backfires the moment Yuji rips his way into the domain. Because Mahito is touching every soul within the domain by default, Yuji forcing his way in also forces Mahito to make unwanted contact with Sukuna’s soul. This is one of the worst things that can happen to him. Sukuna reacts instantly, carving open Mahito’s body with two massive slashes. The damage is so severe that Mahito loses control of his domain entirely, causing it to collapse as he drops to his knees. It’s the moment where he realizes the risk built into his own technique — anything that requires touching souls becomes dangerous when Sukuna is involved. Later, on October 21st, Mahito uses Self-Embodiment of Perfection again against Kokichi Muta. Kokichi’s puppet collapses as if Mahito successfully hit him with Idle Transfiguration, but Kokichi had prepared for this exact scenario. He used Ultimate Mechamaru: Mode Absolute, which includes a Technique Charge Simple Domain that neutralizes the domain’s guaranteed-hit effect. Kokichi fakes being taken out to get close enough to land a counterattack, catching Mahito off guard. It’s one of the few times someone manages to survive direct exposure to this domain without outside interference. Mahito keeps evolving later on, especially after landing a Black Flash on Yuji and gaining a deeper understanding of cursed energy. This growth lets him refine how he activates his domain. Normally, a Domain Expansion needs two steps: realizing the innate domain and then activating the technique inside it. After his experiences against Gojo and Yuji, Mahito learns how to combine these steps. The result is a 0.2-second domain activation, which is long enough for the guaranteed-hit effect to work but too short for the enemy to counter the domain itself. He uses this new technique against Yuji and Aoi Todo. In that brief moment, he applies Idle Transfiguration to Todo without touching him. Todo stops the transfiguration from spreading by immediately severing his altered hand, which saves his life. And because the activation window is so tiny, Mahito avoids the deadly consequence of touching Sukuna’s soul again, even while Yuji is present. Self-Embodiment of Perfection earns its place among the deadliest domains because of how oppressive it is. The moment it forms, Mahito gains direct access to the soul of anyone inside. It removes the biggest difficulty of his technique and turns every target into someone he can reshape at will. But it comes with a major weakness: Mahito is forced to touch all souls inside the domain, even unintentionally. This creates a catastrophic vulnerability whenever Sukuna is around. On top of that, skilled sorcerers with access to Simple Domains or similar counters can negate the guaranteed-hit, which removes the biggest advantage of Self-Embodiment of Perfection. Even with those drawbacks, the fact that Mahito can instantly rewrite someone’s soul inside this space makes his domain one of the most lethal techniques in modern jujutsu.

6. **2nd Place: Malevolent Shrine**

Malevolent Shrine is Sukuna’s domain expansion, and it’s basically the ultimate expression of his slashing technique, Shrine. His cursed technique lets him fire off invisible cuts at any distance or through direct contact, and when he opens his domain, that ability gets pushed to its absolute limit. The structure that appears is this warped, corrupted Buddhist shrine. The base is made of bovine skulls, the roof is a hip-and-gable design with a small skull perched on top, and horns jut out like the building itself is alive. Each gable has a smaller mouth, and even the doorframes have tiny fanged mouths in the corners. Human skulls hang from all four corners of the roof, like decorations from something that really shouldn’t exist. The entrances aren’t even doors but four huge, grotesque mouths with humanlike teeth and tongues. And around the shrine, there are these short, tree-like stumps with twisted branches sticking out. When it shifts into its alternate form, the thing goes full eldritch horror in the form of a huge mass of flesh, horns, teeth, and eyes, with one giant central eye and six smaller ones staring in different directions. It has hair-like patches, four curved horns on top, two giant humanoid hands at the bottom, and a long, spine-like appendage trailing behind it. Everything about it looks wrong on purpose. The real threat of Malevolent Shrine comes from its guaranteed-hit effect. Anything inside Sukuna’s effective range gets shredded nonstop until the domain ends, either because Sukuna turns it off or because he’s too injured to keep it going. He uses two types of slashes: Dismantle for inanimate objects and Cleave for beings with cursed energy. He can choose which one targets what, and the domain keeps attacking until nothing recognizable is left. Sukuna also has this rare divine technique that lets him expand his domain without forming a closed barrier, which is usually a core part of how domains function. Doing it this way creates a binding vow that actually makes the guaranteed-hit even deadlier, because the range can balloon up to nearly 200 meters. There’s technically an escape route, but it doesn’t matter when everything in that radius is being cut apart nonstop. And since the domain doesn’t have an outer shell, it can’t be destroyed in the usual way. Other domains collapse if you damage their barrier, but Malevolent Shrine doesn’t have one, so it can only be stopped by injuring Sukuna badly enough that he can’t maintain it anymore. His mastery over domain expansion lets Malevolent Shrine go toe-to-toe with Gojo’s Unlimited Void — basically the gold standard of domains — and that alone shows how refined it is. Even with only two fingers of his power, Sukuna casually shows off the domain’s potential when he destroys a Finger Bearer instantly. He opens the domain just to show what “real jujutsu” looks like, and the spirit gets erased before it can even react. Later, in Shibuya, he activates Malevolent Shrine during his fight with Mahoraga. Since Mahoraga adapts to everything, Sukuna tries to overwhelm it by bombarding it with a massive range of slashes over a 140-meter radius. He adjusts the conditions so only anything with cursed energy gets hit by Cleave, while objects get hit with Dismantle, all to make sure Megumi doesn’t get caught in the attack. Even with that control, the domain still kills tons of civilians and slices off Toge Inumaki’s arm. This is the level of destruction Sukuna casually works with. During Sukuna’s battle with Gojo, Malevolent Shrine gets pushed harder than ever. Sukuna expands his domain three times in that fight. In their first clash, Malevolent Shrine’s range reaches beyond Unlimited Void’s barrier, and because a domain barrier is weak from the outside, Sukuna’s slashes rip through it and hit Gojo immediately. Later on, Sukuna alters the conditions of his own domain through another binding vow, removing Malevolent Shrine’s guaranteed-hit effect inside Unlimited Void’s effective area and narrowing its range. He does that to boost the pressure on Unlimited Void’s reinforced outer shell, which he manages to collapse again. On their third exchange, Gojo tweaks his domain as well, so both domains fall apart at the same time after three minutes. Malevolent Shrine destroys Unlimited Void’s barrier from the outside, but Gojo manages to injure Sukuna enough that he can’t keep his domain up. They immediately jump into another round, and while both expand their domains at the same time, the result doesn’t change — they collapse together. Then they try again, but this time Sukuna activates his domain a fraction of a second late because he has to heal his body with Reverse Cursed Technique before restoring his cursed technique. That small delay lets Unlimited Void immobilize him, and Gojo uses the opening to wound him severely. The injury is bad enough that Sukuna can’t maintain Malevolent Shrine for more than two minutes and forty seconds, causing it to fall apart inside Unlimited Void. With his domain gone, he loses the protection that normally blocks Unlimited Void’s effect, leaving him completely stuck and forcing him to summon Mahoraga to break the domain. When Gojo tries to expand his domain a sixth time and fails, Sukuna aims to finish things by enclosing Malevolent Shrine with its own barrier to prevent Gojo from escaping. But the moment Sukuna activates it, the domain collapses instantly. Unlimited Void damaged his brain earlier since he was inside it for less than ten seconds total, but that was enough to leave lasting effects. Because of that damage, he physically can’t use domain expansion for the rest of the fight. In the end, Sukuna gets around Infinity by using Mahoraga’s adaptability and tweaking his slash so it cuts through the space itself. That “World Cutting Slash” is basically the key move that let him take down Gojo. Later, after Yuji lands seven consecutive Black Flashes, Sukuna gets desperate and pulls off an impromptu Malevolent Shrine. He changes the hand signs and uses a part of his brain that wasn’t affected by Unlimited Void, which lets him fire off the domain at its full range and power while being boosted by the Black Flash effects. He pulls this off by throwing in uncertain conditions and improvised binding vows. He also keeps the domain as an open barrier so he can catch Maki, who has no cursed energy and would normally slip through anything based on cursed energy detection. Even so, Sukuna’s condition is too unstable, and he can only keep the domain active for 99 seconds before it breaks. Near the end, when Yuta returns to the battlefield using Kenjaku’s cursed technique to control Gojo’s body, Sukuna expands his domain again to clash with Yuta’s version of Unlimited Void. To deal with the stronger durability of Yuta’s barrier, Sukuna shrinks his domain’s effective range so it only touches the outer shell of Unlimited Void. Doing this removes the time limit completely, but even then, both domains collapse at the same time when Yuta hits Sukuna with Hollow Technique: Purple. The attack massively injures Sukuna but destroys Yuta’s domain barrier as well. Malevolent Shrine earns its spot as one of the deadliest domain expansions because of how overwhelming and unavoidable it is. A guaranteed-hit effect that spans up to 200 meters is already terrifying, but pairing it with invisible slashes that never stop until the target is dust makes it an instant death zone for almost anything caught inside. The fact that it’s an open-barrier domain means it can slice apart other domains from the outside, something very few techniques can do. It’s versatile, customizable, and refined to a degree only someone like Sukuna could achieve. But even a technique this monstrous has weaknesses. Since there’s no barrier, the only way to stop the domain is to injure Sukuna himself, and we’ve seen that happen. Brain damage from Unlimited Void temporarily locked him out of using domains entirely. The technique also demands a huge amount of precision and control, and maintaining it in a weakened state drains him fast, cutting down the time he can keep it active. The wider he wants the range, the more binding vows and conditions he has to stack on himself, which comes with risks. And because Malevolent Shrine can’t filter out collateral damage unless Sukuna actively adjusts it, it’s almost impossible for him to use it without killing everything in the area. Even with these drawbacks, Malevolent Shrine is lethal because of its speed, range, flexibility, and the way it merges Sukuna’s innate technique with absolute control over the battlefield. It’s not just a domain that hits hard — it’s a domain that erases anything unlucky enough to be nearby.

7. **1st Place: Unlimited Void**

Unlimited Void is Satoru Gojo’s Domain Expansion, and it basically drags you straight into the heart of his Limitless technique. Since the Limitless brings the concept of “Infinity” into reality, Unlimited Void pushes that idea to the maximum by pulling Gojo and his target inside a space that reflects the technique itself. And the crazy part is that Gojo is the only sorcerer in the last four hundred years to be born with both the Limitless and the Six Eyes, so he’s literally the perfect user for it. Limitless works like a real-world application of convergent and divergent sequences in math. Instead of stopping things with some magical wall, it manipulates the space between Gojo and whatever is coming at him, slicing that distance into infinitely smaller pieces. So even if something tries to hit him, it slows down forever and never actually reaches him. Unlimited Void takes this idea and turns it into an entire world. The domain looks like an endless cosmic void, complete with galaxies, a giant black hole in the distance, and white patches floating around like fragments of impossible scenery. Inside this domain, Gojo forces infinite raw information straight into the mind of whoever’s caught in it. The target’s senses and thoughts get overwhelmed instantly, leaving them completely immobilized. They see everything, feel everything, know everything, yet can’t act on any of it, so they become stuck in their own mind and basically shut down. It’s a near-guaranteed win the moment someone gets trapped inside. That’s how devastating the sensory overload is. Strangely enough, anyone Gojo is physically touching — whether intentionally or even accidentally — gets exempted from the effects. It’s part of the technique’s structure, and that detail can be exploited by an enemy if they’re clever enough to force physical contact. Gojo likely perfected Unlimited Void right after unlocking his full potential during his rematch with Toji Fushiguro in the Star Plasma Vessel arc. After that point, his control over his inherited techniques skyrocketed. The first real demonstration of Unlimited Void in the story happens when he unleashes it against Jogo’s Coffin of the Iron Mountain. Even though Jogo’s domain is volcanic, intense, and lethal, Unlimited Void completely smothers it. Because domains collide based on refinement, understanding, and technique quality, Gojo’s Domain Expansion is so much more polished that it instantly suppresses Jogo’s. Once Jogo is pulled into the domain, he’s frozen — mentally trapped, unable to move, overwhelmed by the flow of information pouring into his head. He’s stuck in place, caught in the Limitless, unable to think clearly or act at all. Kenjaku later brings up a problem that makes Unlimited Void trickier to use around civilians. During the Shibuya Incident, thousands of non-sorcerers stood in a confined space under a curtain, and if Gojo expanded his domain normally, the pressure between the domain barrier and the curtain would have crushed them. Kenjaku even uses this as part of his strategy to seal Gojo. Since Gojo is so skilled at excluding certain targets from his Domain Expansion, Kenjaku figures he might still do it, but the physical setup of the battlefield makes it too risky. Because of this, they assume Gojo can’t afford to cast his domain at full size inside Shibuya. And they’re right — he technically can’t. But Gojo being Gojo, he takes the insane route and activates a 0.2-second version of Unlimited Void instead. That tiny moment is still enough to flood everyone’s minds with about half a year’s worth of information. All of them — including cursed spirits — get weakened or knocked out instantly, but they survive because the exposure time is so short. With everyone stunned, Gojo uses that window to eliminate all 1,000 transfigured humans without risking the lives of innocents. This moment shows off Gojo’s absolute precision with Domain Expansion. He tailors the effect to fit impossible situations. Gojo’s battle with Sukuna becomes the ultimate stress test for Unlimited Void. Throughout the fight, he expands his domain five times, and each clash pushes the limits of both sorcerers. Their first domain battle comes after a brief exchange of blows. Normally, when two domains collide, the more refined one overwrites the other. But here, Unlimited Void and Malevolent Shrine are so perfectly honed that their guaranteed-hit effects cancel each other out. The problem is that Malevolent Shrine has an open barrier and a massive attack range, while Unlimited Void still uses a traditional enclosed barrier. That means its outer shell is vulnerable. Malevolent Shrine hits it with nonstop slashes from the outside, and Unlimited Void collapses. Gojo expands his domain again after restoring his cursed technique with Reverse Cursed Technique. This time, he changes the internal and external structure of Unlimited Void’s barrier to protect it from outside attacks. It holds at first, but Sukuna answers by creating a binding vow that removes his sure-hit effect and narrows his domain’s range to strengthen his slashing output. The barrier breaks again. When Gojo expands Unlimited Void for the third time, he uses knowledge gained from his time trapped in the Prison Realm. He shrinks the barrier down to the size of a basketball, making it denser and tougher. Sukuna responds by compressing Malevolent Shrine’s range again and pushing its output even further. It still takes three full minutes of nonstop attacks to break through, and Gojo uses that time to injure Sukuna enough that he can’t maintain his own domain. Both domains collapse at the same moment. They restore their techniques again and clash a fourth time, resulting in another double collapse. For the fifth and final clash, Sukuna is only a fraction of a second slower in expanding his domain because he’s still healing himself. That delay is less than 0.01 seconds — but it’s enough for Unlimited Void to hit him first and immobilize him for a moment. Gojo takes that opportunity and deals a massive injury. They keep fighting for two minutes and forty seconds before Malevolent Shrine collapses due to Sukuna’s wounds. With Sukuna finally vulnerable inside Unlimited Void, the domain overwhelms him completely. But before Gojo can finish the fight, Sukuna summons Mahoraga. The moment Mahoraga appears, the shikigami destroys the domain. Sukuna also reveals a disturbing detail: he had Megumi Fushiguro’s soul take the brunt of all the previous Unlimited Void exposures while helping Mahoraga adapt. That sets up the final phase of their battle. Ultimately, Gojo can’t cast Unlimited Void a sixth time because repeatedly burning out his technique and regenerating it with Reverse Cursed Technique pushes him past his limit. But Unlimited Void still leaves a mark — its brief activations damaged Sukuna’s brain, preventing him from using Malevolent Shrine for the rest of the fight. There’s still a big debate over whether Sukuna could’ve beaten Gojo if it was just a straight-up domain clash. Sukuna might’ve had a shot if he was in his full power and true form. But in the end, he didn’t beat Gojo by raw domain strength alone — he needed the help of Megumi’s Ten Shadows technique and Mahoraga’s ability to adapt. Without Mahoraga adapting, he never would’ve unlocked the World-Cutting Slash, the boosted version of Dismantle that finally went through Gojo’s Infinity. That move bypassed the “everything slows to a stop” effect and sliced Gojo in half. Later, when Yuta takes over Gojo’s body by transplanting his brain into it using Kenjaku’s cursed technique, he gains access to both the Limitless and the Six Eyes. Facing Sukuna again, Yuta expands Unlimited Void while Sukuna uses Malevolent Shrine. Following Gojo’s own method, Yuta compresses the domain barrier to make it harder to break. But his control over Gojo’s techniques is imperfect, and when he uses Hollow Technique: Purple, he damages his own domain’s barrier while injuring Sukuna. Eventually, both domains collapse at the same time. Despite Gojo’s defeat at the hands of Sukuna, Unlimited Void is still considered the deadliest Domain Expansion in the series because there’s nothing else that can immobilize a target on both a physical and conceptual level. It doesn’t rely on physical harm, slashes, poison, or direct destruction. It attacks the target’s mind by drowning them in limitless information, making it impossible for them to function. Even an elite sorcerer can only endure it for a short time before collapsing. In a normal fight without outside variables, counters, or battlefield barriers, Unlimited Void ends everything instantly. Its only real weaknesses come from external factors. The enclosed barrier can be attacked from the outside, especially against open-barrier domains like Malevolent Shrine. Gojo also has to be extremely careful using it around civilians. There’s also the curse technique burnout issue — Gojo has to use Reverse Cursed Technique to recover his cursed energy after each domain use, and doing that repeatedly is exhausting, even for him. But despite those limitations, Unlimited Void remains the most lethal domain in *Jujutsu Kaisen*. It doesn’t matter how durable you are, how many abilities you have, or how strong your cursed energy is. If you’re caught inside Unlimited Void with no counter, you lose your ability to think, act, or even stay conscious. And once that happens, the fight is already over.
