**Most Underrated Arc in Hunter x Hunter Explained: 13th Hunter Chairman Election Arc**

*Hunter x Hunter* is famous for a lot of things — insane power systems, trauma disguised as a shonen anime, and a mangaka who goes on hiatus more than he publishes chapters. But one arc that fans constantly sleep on is the 13th Hunter Chairman Election Arc, the final arc of the 2011 anime. Today we're breaking the whole thing down, start to finish, so you finally understand what went down.

* **The Power Vacuum**

So let's set the stage. Isaac Netero, the 12th Chairman of the Hunter Association, is dead. He died fighting Meruem during the Chimera Ant crisis in East Gorteau (*Episode 126*), and the man went out like an absolute legend — but that's a story for another video. The point is, the Hunter Association just lost its chairman, and now someone has to step up and fill that seat. Netero's final will included one last ridiculous challenge: the election would only count if at least 95% of all licensed Hunters cast a vote. If turnout dropped even slightly below that number, the entire election would be invalid, and everything would have to start over from scratch. There’s also another rule in play: to officially become the 13th Chairman, the winning candidate has to get more than 50% of the votes. By demanding these conditions, Netero made sure the next Chairman couldn't simply rise to power with the support of a small, biased group of voters. The winner would need legitimacy from nearly the entire Hunter Association. Enter the Zodiacs (*Episode 137*). These are Netero's twelve most trusted Hunters, each named after a Chinese zodiac sign, and together they form basically his inner circle. We're talking Cheadle Yorkshire, Botobai Gigante, Ging Freecss — yes, Gon's dad is one of them — and everyone's favorite politician, Pariston Hill. After Netero's death, the Zodiacs gather at the Hunter Association building to figure out what comes next. When they draw lots to decide the election rules, the Association's secretary end up pulling Ging's paper. Ging's rules kept Netero's brutal requirements and added a few twists of his own. Every Hunter would be both a candidate and a voter at the same time, and everyone was required to write their name on their ballot. Any vote without a name would be automatically thrown out. Then Ging slipped in a fifth and final rule: he would personally serve as Chairman of the election committee. The Zodiacs immediately exploded in protest. They said they could tolerate all of his other rules, but there was no way they were accepting that last one. He casually agreed and dropped the rule without putting up any fight, making it pretty obvious that he had only included it to mess with them in the first place. What the rest of the Zodiacs don't realize is that Ging planned all of this in advance. He visited Beans, the Association's secretary, two days before the meeting and planted those rules in the basket. He knew exactly how the Zodiacs would bicker and how long it would take them to reach any decision.

* **The Political Chessboard**

Now here's where it gets genuinely fascinating. The election isn't just an election — it becomes this slow-burning political chess match, and the two main players are Pariston and Ging. Pariston is something else entirely. He shows up late to the Zodiac meeting, apologizes with the biggest smile on his face, and then immediately proposes to skip the whole election and declare himself Chairman. Of course, the other Zodiacs are furious and some are even threatening to kill him. And Pariston is just... smiling. It's the kind of smile that tells you this guy is running seventeen different calculations behind his eyes at all times. The thing about Pariston is that he enjoys the chaos. He enjoys watching people get angry at him, watching them scramble to counter his moves, watching the whole system bend itself into knots trying to deal with him. Ging, on the other hand, plays it completely differently. While everyone else in the room is losing their minds over Pariston, Ging is barely paying attention. He doesn't react to Pariston's provocations. Even when Pariston brings up the possibility of his son dying, he stays completely calm and confidently says that Gon will survive. His entire move during this arc is to use the election as a playground — set the rules so the election runs as chaotically as possible, and walk away when it stops being fun. He even tells Beans flat out that he has no intention of becoming the Chairman. He just wanted to continue Netero's legacy in his own weird, irresponsible way.

* **First and Second Rounds**

The first round of voting goes about as expected. Pariston comes in first with 249 votes, Cheadle trails way behind in second with 42 votes, and the rest of the field is basically irrelevant. The turnout doesn't hit 95%, so the whole thing gets redone. The second round comes and goes (*Episode 138*), and the top three — Pariston, Cheadle, and Ickshonpe Katocha — hold their positions. Still no 95% turnout. Pariston, sensing an opportunity to make things even more complicated, proposes that Hunters who abstain from voting should have their Hunter Licenses temporarily confiscated. This sends Kanzai — one of the Zodiacs — into a complete meltdown. He tries to physically attack Pariston and gets restrained by three of his own colleagues at the same time. Cheadle counters Pariston's proposal by suggesting that Hunters should be informed before new rules get added and that casting an empty vote should be treated as a violation. Pariston agrees with the first part, because of course he does — he agrees with just enough to seem reasonable while still causing maximum disruption. 

* **Alluka**

While the election circus is unfolding, Killua is dealing with something a lot more personal. Gon is in the hospital in critical condition after the events of the Chimera Ant crisis — he used every ounce of life force he had, and his body is basically shutting down. Killua decides he's going to fix this himself, which means going back home to the Zoldyck Estate and getting his little sister, Alluka. Now, Alluka Zoldyck is the most mysterious member of an already deeply unsettling family. The Zoldycks have kept her locked away in a sealed room, and the reason becomes clear pretty quickly once you understand what she can do. Alluka is possessed by a mysterious entity named Nanika who has the power to grant wishes — but it comes with a catch, and the catch is brutal. Here's how it works: Alluka will make three requests from a person. If that person fulfills all three, Nanika takes over and grants them one wish. Simple enough, right? Except the difficulty of those three requests scales with the size of the last wish that was granted. Someone wished for something minor? The next requests are easy. Someone wished to become a billionaire? The next requests are going to be impossible — things like "give me your liver" or "give me your spine." And if someone refuses her requests four times in a row, that person dies, along with the people they spent the most time with. After one of the butlers used Alluka's power to wish for a billion jenny, the price came due almost immediately. The next butler couldn't fulfill Alluka's requests, triggering a horrific backlash that resulted in 67 people dying at the same time. Killua goes to his father Silva and demands access to Alluka. Silva is deeply reluctant — he genuinely doesn't think of Alluka as family — but Killua reminds him of a promise Silva once made: never betray your friends. Killua insists that he's only doing this to save his friend, who's currently on the brink of death. It's enough to get the door open. Silva unlocks a series of doors to Alluka's room. Getting out of the estate is its own mission. Killua pulls a move that is both terrifying and kind of impressive — he instructs Nanika to kill their mother Kikyo if both of them haven't left the mountain within thirty minutes (*Episode 139*). With no other choice, Silva opens the gate to let them go. But they're joined by Gotoh, the senior butler, and Canary, with two additional butlers — Tsubone and her granddaughter Amane — added by Silva's orders (*Episode 140*). Out of nowhere, Alluka asks for Tsubone's pinky fingernail. Without hesitation, Tsubone tears it off and hands it over. Since she knows Alluka has to make three successful requests in a row before a wish can be granted, Tsubone immediately hides herself from view, preventing Alluka from making any further requests to her.

* **Third and Fourth Rounds**

Back at the election, the third round produced a minor shake-up: Botobai Gigante entered the top three, bumping down Ickshonpe. But the third round fails too, so they end up having to redo the whole voting process again (*Episode 139*). Pariston notices that more Hunters are abstaining now, spooked by the threat of losing their licenses, so he proposes that the Zodiacs run a lecture to remind Hunters why the election matters. The fourth round is when things genuinely lurch sideways (*Episode 140*). During a lecture event at the Hunter Association, Leorio Paradinight — yes, one of the four MCs, the one studying to become a doctor — gets up in front of the whole room and asks Ging a very simple question: why hasn't he visited Gon in the hospital? Ging's response is so infuriatingly nonchalant that Leorio just launches a full attack at him that sends him flying across the hall. The Hunters watching go absolutely wild cheering for him. The fourth round results come back with Pariston still in first, but Leorio has rocketed into third place purely off that one moment. This time, they actually hit the 95% turnout requirement (*Episode 141*). But Pariston still doesn’t manage to get a majority, only pulling in about 39% of the total votes. Still, since the turnout rule is satisfied, the election moves forward anyway, and the top 16 candidates advance to the next round.

* **The Chase**

Meanwhile, the road trip with Killua and Alluka turns dangerous fast. Illumi, Killua's oldest brother, has no intention of letting Alluka reach that hospital. He's been in contact with Hisoka this whole time — the two of them were at the first round of voting together (*Episode 137*) — and now Illumi puts his plan into motion. His goal is straightforward and deeply concerning: eliminate Alluka for good. Illumi figures that Killua's plan to heal Gon is such an unbelievably massive wish that the backlash afterward could end up costing thousands of lives (*Episode 139*). From Illumi's perspective, that means the entire Zoldyck family is doomed. Hisoka ends up in the mix too, since if the consequences really hit on a thousand-lives scale, he gets pulled into it because of how much time he spent around Illumi. Because of that, Illumi genuinely sees Killua's mission as a disaster waiting to happen. In his mind, Killua is charging straight into a situation that could get their whole family wiped out. That's why he's so determined to stop him. To Illumi, sacrificing Alluka is a small price to pay if it guarantees the survival of everyone else in the family. The first attack comes when Illumi has a truck slam into the car Killua's group is traveling in, sending them off a cliff (*Episode 141*). Hisoka moves in on the butlers while Tsubone chases after Killua and Alluka on foot. The ensuing fight between Hisoka and Gotoh is genuinely intense — Gotoh fires coins with enough force to deflect bullets, Hisoka's Bungee Gum catches them mid-flight, and the whole thing ends with Hisoka annihilating Gotoh in one of the more quietly devastating moments of the arc (*Episode 142*). Killua uses his Godspeed ability to get ahead of everyone and makes it to the airport, where he pulls a classic misdirection — he orders five airships prepared, boards a completely different one, and escapes his pursuers. But Illumi escalates. He deploys his Needle People — civilians he's secretly taken control of using his Nen-infused needles. Dozens of them start converging on the route to the hospital. This catches the attention of three Hunters: Teradein Neutral, Bushidora Ambitious, and Loupe Highland, who announce the revival of the Pure Paladin Squad — an elite Hunter task force that Netero himself once belonged to — specifically to hunt down Illumi and his controlled people. Teradein also uses this moment to campaign for votes, because in this arc even a manhunt becomes a political opportunity. The Pure Paladin Squad does not go well. Over a hundred Hunters get eliminated by Illumi's Needle People. Bushidora goes out himself to prove his worth as captain and gets eliminated by Hisoka who later slips into Teradein's room and takes him out too (*Episode 143*). The whole squad is dismantled before it ever really gets started. Illumi finally corners Killua on the road outside the hospital, surrounded by Needle People and backed by Tsubone carrying a surveillance camera connected to Kikyo. He demands Alluka but Killua refuses. Upon seeing Tsubone, Alluka requests for her middle and ring fingernails, and Tsubone complies immediately, which means Nanika can now grant a wish. Killua uses it to heal Tsubone's hand. Killua explains that healing wishes work differently. When a wish is used to heal or restore someone, Nanika doesn't follow it up with the usual horrifying demands or deadly consequences. Illumi watches this and recalculates. Killua can give Alluka multiple wishes and commands in a row. According to everything the Zoldyck family knows, the same person should never be able to make consecutive wishes like that. That's when Illumi realizes the truth: Killua has a unique level of authority over Alluka that nobody else possesses. He's the only person who can safely access and control this incredibly powerful ability. At that point, Illumi's priorities change. Instead of trying to eliminate Alluka, he decides it's far more valuable to observe and learn more about the relationship between Killua and Nanika. So rather than interfere, he lets them continue with healing Gon.

* **Fifth, Sixth and Seventh Rounds**

The fifth round delivers a surprise: Teradein has jumped all the way to second place, riding the wave of Pure Paladin Squad hype and people voting for the guy who promised to deal with the Illumi situation. Meanwhile, Cheadle and Leorio drop to third and fourth. The fifth round finally clears the 95% turnout requirement, but Pariston still doesn’t manage to secure a majority — he only gets about 41.4% of all possible votes. Even so, the election moves forward, and the top 8 candidates advance to the next round. The sixth round goes basically the same way, but even worse — it doesn’t hit the required turnout at all. So they’re forced to scrap it and run the whole vote again. By the seventh round, though, the situation has completely reversed. News breaks that every Hunter dispatched by the Pure Paladin Squad has been eliminated. Teradein is dead. Bushidora is dead. And Loupe is panicking. The seventh round ends with Pariston still in first gaining 46.1% of all possible votes, Leorio back in second, and Cheadle and Mizaistom holding third and fourth. Since the seventh round met the required turnout, the election now narrows to four candidates going forward.

* **Eighth and Final Rounds**

The eighth round kicks off with speeches from the four remaining candidates (*Episode 144*). Mizaistom gives a speech endorsing Cheadle, hoping to consolidate votes against Pariston. And then Cheadle completely blindsides everyone by endorsing Leorio instead. Leorio gets on stage and gives the most honest campaign speech in the history of campaign speeches. He admits he's not ready to be Chairman. He says he wants to make the whole Association his personal property and his first act as Chairman would be to save Gon. He starts crying onstage talking about how helpless he felt watching his friend deteriorate. Sympathy wins out over logic and the entire hall of Hunters basically loses it. The applause is thunderous. At this point, it’s starting to look like Leorio might actually end up winning the chairmanship. Cheadle seems pretty confident about it too — especially since she’s clearly expecting Pariston to take the L. The results come in, and even though they hit the required turnout, Leorio, now the top candidate, only manages to grab about 44.4% of the total votes. Because of that, the top two — Leorio and Pariston — move on to the next round. Meanwhile, at the hospital, Alluka finally wakes up and Killua explains that a friend is sick and he wants her to help (*Episode 145*). At first, she refuses, saying she wants to spend a little more time with him. But Killua promises her that they'll always be together from now on, whatever happens. And that's what it takes. Nanika comes out and touches Gon, and the amount of aura that releases is so massive it can be felt at the Hunter Association building. Everyone present at the hospital is stunned, all of them watch as Gon heals. And that's when Illumi, waiting outside the hospital, gets the confirmation he needed: Nanika's power is even greater than he thought. He's fully committed to acquiring it now by manipulating Killua. Back at the election, the ninth round starts off with a debate between Leorio and Pariston. Pariston tries to corner him by pointing out how inexperienced he is when it comes to running something as huge as the Hunter Association. But Cheadle jumps in and flips the situation a bit, saying she’ll act as Leorio’s adviser if he ends up winning the chairmanship. Pariston then asks Leorio to repeat his goals — and right as that's happening, Morel comes bursting through the door in tears, giving Leorio a thumbs up. And then Gon walks in like nothing happened. Leorio runs to him and hugs him as the whole hall erupts. And Cheadle finally understands what Pariston was doing this entire time — he timed the entire final round around Gon's arrival, knowing that once Gon appeared healthy, Leorio would lose his primary motivation for running. He trusted Ging's faith that Gon would survive, and he used that as his trump card. For a moment, the whole election drama fades into the background, and everything shifts to the moment everyone’s been waiting for — Gon finally meeting Ging (*Episode 146*). But instead of some emotional reunion hug, Gon just breaks down in tears right in front of him, apologizing over and over for what happened to Kite and how he ended up “turning into a girl.” Ging doesn’t really play along with the emotional scene. He basically tells Gon that he should be saying sorry to Kite, not him. After that, Gon leaves the hall, with Leorio walking out alongside him.

* **The 13th Chairman**

With Leorio dropping out of the race, the election ends with Pariston winning and he becomes the 13th Hunter Chairman of the Hunter Association. He gets on stage, announces that Cheadle will be his vice-chairman, and then he immediately resigns. Cheadle is furious. The Zodiacs are stunned. Even Ging admits he didn't see that coming. Cheadle chases after Pariston in the hallway to confront him. He tells her that he only ever wanted to play with Chairman Netero. Netero was the only opponent who made things interesting for him, and now that Netero is gone, the game Pariston was playing has no point. He tells Cheadle to reform the Hunter Association, improve the exam, make something worth topping. And if it ever gets dull under her leadership, he'll come back and start causing problems again. Over at the hospital, Illumi shows up to make one final play for Alluka. Killua tells him off — and then commands Nanika to teleport Illumi directly back to Kukuroo Mountain. Illumi vanishes instantly, landing right in front of Silva, Kikyo, and Milluki, who watched the whole thing happen live via camera. After visiting Kite (*Episode 147*), Gon heads back to the Hunter Association building — only to find a note from Ging, telling him to meet at the top of the World Tree.

* **Prelude to the Dark Continent**

Gon, Killua, and Alluka travel together to the base of the World Tree — a tree so massive it stands at 1,784 meters tall, making it the tallest living thing on the planet. Before they part ways, Killua tells Gon the truth: Alluka was the one who healed him. He introduces Gon to Nanika as well. And then they say goodbye. Not because they've grown apart — if anything, this arc is proof of exactly how much Killua would sacrifice for Gon — but because they both understand that the other needs space to find their own path. Killua has Alluka now. He has someone who needs him, and someone he genuinely wants to be there for. Gon needs to chase his own story, and that starts with climbing the World Tree. It's a quiet goodbye, but it lands hard. Gon climbs the tree (*Episode 148*) — it takes a while because it's nearly two kilometers tall — and at the top he finds a giant nest and his father sitting there waiting, tossing him an apple like this is the most casual reunion imaginable. Their conversation is the kind of talk that the whole series has been quietly building toward. Ging talks about his life philosophy: that the journey to a goal matters more than the goal itself. He became a Hunter, made connections with archaeologists all over the world, and pursued buried history not because he had to, but because not having something in front of him was more interesting than having it. When Gon asks what he wants now, Ging points out that the World Tree they're sitting on is actually a stunted sapling. The real World Trees grow where there's volcanic activity and mountain ranges, and they're enormous beyond comparison. And then Ging mentions the Outside World, the mysterious place where World Trees naturally grow and the very same place the terrifying Chimera Ants originally came from. This is where we learn about the Dark Continent — a place beyond the borders of what the Hunter Association currently considers the Known World. A place Ging hasn't earned the qualifications to explore yet, but intends to reach someday. It's the next horizon. The next impossible thing sitting just outside the frame of the world Gon has been living in his whole life. And the next generation of Hunters is about to find out just how small the map they've been working from actually is.
