**What if Meruem Had Lived**

*Hunter x Hunter* gave us one of the most terrifying villains in anime history, and then had the audacity to make us feel genuinely sad when he died — and honestly, that says a lot about how good the writing is. Meruem, the Chimera Ant King, came into this world with a plan so horrifying that it makes every other anime villain's scheme look like a mild inconvenience, and yet somewhere between the throne room and a board game, he started feeling things. But what if the story went differently — what if Meruem survived, kept his crown, and actually pushed his plans all the way through to the end? Because trust us, the answer to that is way more nightmare-inducing than you think, and we're going to break all of it down today.

* **Who is Meruem?**

Let's start from the top because some of you might not fully appreciate just how cooked humanity would be if this guy stayed alive. Meruem is the King of the Chimera Ants — a species of insect monsters from the Dark Continent that reproduce through a process called Phagogenesis (*Episode 77*), meaning the Queen eats other creatures and passes their traits down to her offspring. When the Queen started feasting on humans, her children began inheriting human intelligence, physical capabilities, and even the ability to use Nen. And then she gave birth to Meruem (*Episode 91*), her magnum opus, the absolute pinnacle of everything that process could produce. He burst out of her literally by force, tearing his way out before she was even done giving birth, and honestly that's just the mood he was going to carry for the rest of his life. From the moment he's born, Meruem is on a completely different level from everyone around him. His physical strength is downright absurd — he can casually tear people apart and barely even acknowledge that it happened. His most broken ability is called Aura Synthesis — a technique that lets him absorb the Nen abilities of anyone he devours, stacking their power on top of his own. So the stronger the person he eats, the stronger he gets (*Episode 128*). And the more he devours, the larger and more versatile his own aura pool becomes. He's literally a walking experience point grinder. On top of all of that, Meruem's intellect is off the charts. He can master virtually any discipline in an extremely short amount of time. Board games that take most people years to get competent at? He figures them out in minutes and immediately becomes the best in the world at them. If you dropped him into any competitive field, he'd dominate it before lunchtime. He views humanity the same way we view cattle: a resource to be used, not something worthy of real consideration, and perfectly fine to consume whenever it's convenient. Human lives mean absolutely nothing to him, and he doesn't spend even a second feeling guilty about taking one. The series gives us plenty of moments that drive this home. Right after literally bursting out of his mother’s womb, he casually decapitates a Squadron Leader just for prioritizing the Queen’s safety over his own needs (*Episode 91*). One of his very first acts after leaving the nest is casually slurping a little girl’s brain (*Episode 92*), eliminating every soldier guarding the palace in East Gorteau, and then finishing off the country's dictator just so he could use the palace as a base of operations. He eats a soldier who could use Nen, absorbs his power, and moves on like he ordered takeout (*Episode 93*). There's also the way he treats the champions he calls in for matches. He challenges the best players in East Gorteau at their own games, and when he wins, he eliminates them (*Episode 102*). To him, they were only useful as a way to pass the time and grow stronger. Once they stop being useful, they stop deserving to exist. That's his baseline worldview. Meruem is a creature who has no regard for human life, unlimited potential for growth, and a plan to take over the world. And that plan starts with something called the Sorting.

* **Sorting**

He and his Royal Guards — Neferpitou, Shaiapouf, and Menthuthuyoupi — had a very specific, very organized invasion program lined up, and the first phase of it is called the Sorting. Here's the basic idea. Not all humans are created equal in terms of their potential to use Nen. Most people go their entire lives without ever awakening Nen at all, but the capacity is there — it just needs to be triggered. The Sorting process is essentially a forced, mass Nen awakening program. And when we say forced, we mean it in the most brutal way possible. The plan works through a terrifying combination of logistics and brainwashing. First, Neferpitou uses her puppet-manipulation ability to control East Gorteau’s supreme leader and military officials (*Episode 95*). Through these puppets, the Chimera Ants issue a nationwide decree ordering the entire population of five million people to march to the capital city on foot (*Episode 100*). To prevent panic or rebellion during this massive migration, Shaiapouf uses his Spiritual Message ability to scatter hypnotic scales from his butterfly wings over the crowds. This passively hypnotizes the citizens, putting them into a compliant, trance-like state so they march willingly toward their doom (*Episode 110*). Once the population is gathered outside the royal palace under the pretext of a national celebration, the brutal sorting process begins (*Episode 111*). The plan is to systematically strike each citizen with a burst of malice-filled Nen. What this does to a person is basically force their aura nodes open. It's the same basic process Wing used on Gon and Killua when they reached the 200th floor of Heavens Arena and were at risk of running into other Nen users (*Episode 29*). For people who have the rare physical and mental strength to handle it, their Nen awakens and they survive. For everyone else — which is the vast majority — this forced rupture is fatal. Their bodies can't take the sudden loss of life energy, and they die. Before the plan was disrupted, the estimate was that only about one percent of the population would survive. In other words, for every hundred people forced through the Selection, ninety-nine wouldn't make it. The roughly 50,000 survivors who do make it through the initial awakening are then wrapped in Shaiapouf’s specialized Nen cocoons (*Episode 106*). Inside these cocoons, they undergo a terrifying metamorphosis that rebuilds them into Chimera Ant-human hybrids, creating a brainwashed, loyal, and devastatingly powerful Nen soldier army for the King. The end goal of the Sorting is to build an army of 50,000 Nen-capable hybrid soldiers. That number is what the Chimera Ants determine they need before they can move to phase two. 

* **Unification**

So the Sorting never gets to finish in the series, and because of that, the Unification never happens at all. But let's imagine the worst case scenario — the one where nobody stops it in time. Say the Sorting runs to completion across East Gorteau before Netero’s squad can intervene. Over five million people get processed, and somewhere around 50,000 of them survive and get turned into Nen-using hybrids through Shaiapouf's cocoon transformation. Once that number is reached, Meruem finally has the army he wants, and the Unification begins. This is the actual conquest phase. With 50,000 Nen-capable soldiers at his command, Meruem has no reason to rely on conventional warfare anymore. Modern militaries, no matter how advanced, don't have any real answer to Nen. Bullets aren't reliable against it, and conventional weapons quickly become ineffective (*you can use Uvogin in Episode 43 as an example here*). At this point, the only thing capable of stopping this hybrid army is something on the level of a bomb. After all, even Meruem was ultimately brought down by the Poor Man's Rose (*Episode 126*). The problem is that the military can't just start dropping these deadly weapons wherever they want, especially when civilians could end up caught in the blast zone. That's exactly why Netero hired Zeno in the first place. His job was to carry both Netero and Meruem far away from any towns or villages, ensuring that when the Poor Man's Rose finally detonated, no innocent people would be caught in its range. With bombs no longer an option, the hybrid army spreads across the world unchecked. One country after another falls as the invasion spreads across the globe at an alarming pace. Governments around the world, completely unprepared for threats like this, start collapsing almost immediately. Those who try to negotiate quickly learn that Meruem has no interest in diplomacy, while those who choose to fight discover that the gap in power is simply too massive for technology alone to overcome. The Hunter Association, despite having around 700 Nen-capable fighters who could potentially resist, eventually gets overwhelmed. Individual talent can only take you so far when you're facing 50,000 Nen-using hybrids. At the end of the day, that's an army, and armies win through sheer numbers and attrition. Within a matter of months, every government on Earth has either been eliminated or bent the knee. Meruem ends up at the top of a fully conquered planet, and the focus shifts from conquering the world to figuring out how to rule it. The surviving human population — the people who weren't consumed during the Sorting process, and who weren't fighters that resisted and paid for it — are now subjects. Not citizens. Not free people. Subjects, in the most literal, most chilling sense of that word. Their entire existence going forward is determined by what value they have to the new ruling class. And we're about to get into exactly what that looks like under Meruem's rule.

* **Change of Plans**

Now here's where the story gets genuinely interesting, because the Meruem we just described — the one who sees humans as livestock — isn't actually the Meruem who would be sitting on the throne at the end of it all. Because something happened in that palace that nobody predicted, and it changed him in ways that even he couldn't fully understand at the time. While Meruem is waiting for the Sorting to finish, his Royal Guards bring in the champions of East Gorteau's various games and disciplines for him to face as a way to keep him occupied. One by one he wrecks them all, no contest. And then they bring in Komugi (*Episode 102*). Komugi is a blind girl, not particularly impressive looking, who holds the national title in a board game called Gungi. Meruem expects to beat her in ten matches or less like everyone else. But he doesn't. He loses instead. And he loses repeatedly. He can't figure out why, which for a being of his intellect is genuinely maddening. He keeps calling her back, keeps playing, and something starts shifting in him without him even realizing it's happening. Because Komugi can’t see him and doesn’t really have much of a survival instinct, his overwhelming aura doesn’t scare her at all. Her whole world is Gungi, and she treats every match like she’s putting her life on the line (*Episode 104*). Since she’s already accepted the idea of dying if she loses, Meruem’s deadly threats don’t really faze her at all. Meruem has never once encountered anything that can challenge him and finds himself drawn to that. He rips off his own arm (*Episode 105*) as punishment for trying to mess with her mentally, because he decides winning through intimidation would be disrespecting the game itself. That’s how much she’s already started shifting what he considers important. Then Netero and Zeno arrive at the palace to kick off the Extermination plan (*Episode 111*). Komugi gets caught in the crossfire and gets badly hurt. And Meruem — this creature who has never cared about a single human life — orders Neferpitou to drop everything and heal her. He tells Netero and Zeno to wait. And just like that, the strongest fighters on both sides actually pause and let it play out, because seeing Meruem gently holding an injured girl completely freezes everyone in place. By the time Meruem goes off to fight Netero (*Episode 113*), he's already a different being from the one who entered the palace. When Netero tries to provoke him into a fight, Meruem keeps shutting it down. He’s not interested and he wants to talk. Meruem suggests taking over as a kind of ruler who would reshape the world, remove human inequality, and keep only a select group of exceptional people alive (*Episode 122*). He even talks about setting up a protected “special zone” where the surviving humans could live safely. From there, he’d personally decide who gets spared based on talent or value, while the rest of humanity would still be treated like livestock to sustain the Chimera Ants. Netero obviously rejects the idea of any future where humans are reduced to food for the Ants. Netero throws everything he’s got at the Ant King (*Episodes 123 \- 125*), but in the end he still has to pull out his final trump card, the Poor Man’s Rose (*Episode 126*), which is what ultimately brings Meruem’s life to an end. Meruem’s death — and especially how it plays out with him slowly succumbing to the poison while choosing to spend his final hours playing Gungi with Komugi (*Episode 135*) — hits hard for a lot of people. Some are even critical of Netero for taking out someone they see as a potential ruler who could’ve “fixed” a broken system. His idea of removing corrupt, incompetent leaders and protecting the weak sounds appealing on the surface. But even if the goal sounds ideal, it quickly becomes clear that a world ruled by a species that literally eats humans isn’t exactly the safe or comfortable future it might seem to be.

* **Fate of Humanity Under Meruem's Rule**

Alright. For everyone out there rooting for Meruem — and we know you exist — this part is specifically for you. We need to have a serious talk about what "Meruem wins" actually looks like for the seven billion people who aren't Komugi. So Meruem survives the poison bomb, his plans go through, and now he rules the world. His evolved worldview means he doesn't want to wipe out “all” of humanity anymore. Great news\! Truly wonderful. But here's the slightly less wonderful part: the system he designs for humanity is, in practical terms, one of the most horrifying things imaginable. Meruem and the Royal Guards establish what gets referred to as a preserve — a designated zone where humans are permitted to live. Think of it less like a city and more like a very large, very organized livestock facility. Because that's functionally what it is. All the surviving humans get moved into a designated preserve. You don’t get a choice in where you go, and there’s no opting out. You’re placed there, and from that point on, everything about your life is basically under constant evaluation. You soon get to realize that Meruem's idea of "coexistence" is not a partnership. It's a management system. The Chimera Ants run periodic screenings. Every person in the preserve gets assessed, and the assessment determines their status. Are you exceptionally talented at something? Do you have a rare skill, a high Nen potential, or some ability that the ruling class finds genuinely interesting? Congratulations, you get to keep living more. You might even get some privileges. Are you average? Are you just a normal person going about your life with no particularly impressive skills? Well — you're still in the preserve, for now. You're still being evaluated. You're in the undecided pile, which is a deeply uncomfortable place to be. Are you assessed as having no value to the new order? Then you get selected. And when we say selected, we mean that in the most grim, most permanent way possible. You become food. The next question you’d probably end up asking is whether the Ants are going to eat you alive or turn you into meatballs. And yeah, at that point, that whole setup suddenly doesn’t sound so great anymore. Under Meruem’s rule, the “softer” version of what the Chimera Ants originally had in mind is basically this: the people he considers valuable get to keep living. Everyone else? They’re just there to fill the Ant colony's stomach in the most literal way possible. This is where Meruem’s original ideology starts to fall apart and get pretty hypocritical. He basically made it clear that only people with talent, intelligence, or Nen potential would be considered worth keeping alive. So under his system, someone who doesn't have some kind of standout talent or doesn’t have any special abilities would basically just be labeled “useless.”  Realistically, most people on the planet are just average, and a lot of them are even born without the kind of physical potential needed to develop real talent — let alone something like Nen. And instead of helping them, his world order would just strip them of any value and push them into livestock status. Now let's actually put ourselves in this preserve for a second, because we think a lot of people gloss over just how unbearable this would be to live through. You don’t really know what the criteria are for being judged. That info isn’t even shared with the general population, since there’s no real reason to explain it to people whose choices don’t actually factor into the decision-making anyway. Maybe being able to cook well counts for something. Maybe being physically strong matters. Maybe having an IQ score of 140 is enough to get the overseers to bump you up a tier. You genuinely don't know. When the selection date rolls around, overseers move through the preserve sector by sector. People disappear during these screenings. You might see a neighbor get taken one morning, and that's the last you ever hear of them. They're just gone. And everybody in the preserve knows what that means, but nobody says it out loud because what is there to say. The mental pressure here is honestly insane. Humans are social by nature — we build communities, form bonds, and think long-term. But in the preserve, thinking about the future barely even makes sense anymore. You’re basically just planning for the next screening, figuring out how to make yourself look more valuable before the next round of evaluation. You watch your family members go through their own assessments and you hold your breath. When your kid goes through a screening and comes back, you feel relief so intense it's almost physical. When a parent goes in and doesn’t return, you grieve however you can, quietly and privately, and then you just keep going — because at that point, surviving is the only thing left to do. At its core, Meruem’s plan to end human inequality just replaces it with an even harsher kind of inequality — swapping social hierarchy for a brutal biological one, where the majority of ordinary people would basically be reduced to cattle. And here's the part that really should shake the Meruem fans in the room. Eventually, that kind of system would just lead to a major drop in the human population, which basically turns into a food shortage problem for the Chimera Ants. And the inevitable consequence of that? Forced reproduction. Yeah, exactly as messed up as it sounds — because a species that relies on humans for food would naturally try to keep the supply from running out. Now imagine being one of the women selected for that role, because let’s be real, not everyone is Komugi. While Komugi is safely in the palace playing Gungi with Meruem, thousands of others are being forced into that system just to keep the food supply going. And presiding over all of this is Meruem, sitting on his throne somewhere above it all, genuinely convinced that he has created something better than what came before. He ended wars, technically. He eliminated political corruption, technically. And you? You’re one of hundreds of millions of people stuck in the preserve, just waiting for the next screening and trying to figure out how to make yourself “useful” enough to matter to a being who already sees your life as just a number in an equation he’s basically solved. That’s the kind of world Meruem was building. And yeah, the fact that he eventually becomes capable of love, tenderness, and even something close to humility is genuinely moving — and the writing really earns that change. But at the same time, it doesn’t really erase what living inside that system would’ve actually felt like for everyone else. Netero's sacrifice didn't just stop a monster. It stopped a horrifying system.
