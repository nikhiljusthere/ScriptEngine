**All 7 Levels of Curses in Jujutsu Kaisen Explained**

**1\. Grade 4 – Weak Curses**

Grade 4 curses are the absolute bottom-feeders of the *Jujutsu Kaisen* world. Weak, mindless, and barely dangerous, they’re usually exorcised by first-year students before they’ve even had lunch. These are the kind of curses that pop up from small, everyday fears — like bugs, dirty toilets, or that creepy feeling of being alone at night. They’re nuisances more than threats, the kind of thing a rookie sorcerer uses for warm-ups. Canonically, their strength is described in the simplest way possible: “A wooden bat is enough.” That’s how little it takes to take them out. Fly Heads are basically the poster children for weak curses. These insect-like creatures are considered so small-time that sorcerers don’t even bother ranking them. They’re easily contained inside a sealed box wrapped in talismans, which is already a huge red flag for how harmless they are. In fact, Jujutsu High uses them to check if ordinary people can even see curses. If someone can spot a Fly Head, congratulations — they’ve got at least a bit of cursed perception. Individually, Fly Heads take the form of bug-like spirits with weird, distorted faces. None of them are particularly scary or special, and their biggest strength is usually just existing in large numbers. They tend to swarm together, which makes them look more intimidating than they actually are. Even someone without cursed energy can find creative ways to use them. Toji Fushiguro, the “Sorcerer Killer” himself, used to keep a swarm of Fly Heads stored in his inventory curse. During his fight with Gojo, Toji uses this trick perfectly — he releases a cloud of Fly Heads to confuse Gojo, throwing off his detection abilities just long enough to sneak in a clean strike. So yeah, Fly Heads are weak, but even weak curses can have their uses if you’re clever enough. Next up, we’ve got the curses that showed up during the very beginning of the series, when Megumi Fushiguro is sent to Sugisawa Municipal High School. His mission is to investigate rumors about a special grade cursed object. Megumi runs into Yuji Itadori, who tells him that his Occult Research Club found the cursed object and kept it around for fun. Yuji then tells him his friends are planning to peel the seal completely that night at the school. Megumi immediately warns him that doing so will get them all killed. Meanwhile, inside the school, Yuji’s friends start unraveling the talisman and discover a human finger inside — Sukuna’s finger. The second they do that, the place becomes a cursed energy nightmare. Cursed spirits start spawning all over, drawn to the finger’s overwhelming energy. By the time Yuji and Megumi arrive, the entire school is drenched in cursed energy, and several low-level curses — definitely around Grade 4 — are already attacking the students. Megumi sends out his Divine Dogs, which tear through the weaker spirits without much resistance. He continues cutting down curses one after another until he finds Yuji’s friends being consumed by a particularly nasty curse. Just when things are about to get worse, Yuji smashes through the fourth-floor window and dropkicks the curse mid-air. It’s a chaotic scene, but the important part is how weak these curses actually are in comparison to what comes later. They might look terrifying to regular people, but to trained sorcerers like Megumi, they’re nothing but pests. Another solid example of Grade 4-level curses shows up when Gojo decides to test Yuji and Nobara’s skills in an abandoned building in Roppongi. He tells them to exorcise every curse inside, using it as a real-world training mission. Yuji is given a cursed tool called the Slaughter Demon — a knife infused with cursed energy. As soon as they step inside, a curse jumps at him from above. Yuji reacts instantly, slicing it cleanly with his knife. The spirit goes down, which tells you everything you need to know about its level. This is exactly what “A wooden bat is enough” looks like in practice. Meanwhile, Nobara walks into a room filled with mannequins and feels cursed energy radiating from one in the center. Without hesitation, she hammers a nail into its head. The curse survives the first strike, but she channels her cursed energy through the nails and blasts its head apart. Another weak curse, another quick exorcism. In short, Grade 4 curses are the fodder curses in the jujutsu world. They’re the bugs of the cursed world — literally and figuratively — and a first-year’s cursed technique or even a simple swing from a wooden bat is more than enough to wipe them out.

**2\. Grade 3 – Lesser Curses**

Grade 3 curses are still on the lower end of the food chain, but unlike the Grade 4 bottom-feeders, these ones can at least fight back. They’re born from common, everyday anxieties — things like illness, job stress, social rejection, or that constant sense of failure people drag around. They’re not powerful, but they’ve got enough cursed energy to put up a fight before being exorcised. A normal person could die going up against one, but for a trained sorcerer, they’re solo missions. The official benchmark for their strength says it all: “If you have a handgun, you can rest easy.” They’re still weak compared to the heavy hitters, but Grade 3s mark the point where curses start showing signs of aggression, cunning, and a little bit of resilience. The Occult Club Curse is one of the earliest and best examples of what a Grade 3 looks like in action. It shows up back in Episode 1, when Megumi investigates rumors of a special grade cursed object at Sugisawa Municipal High School. The object turns out to be one of Sukuna’s fingers — an artifact so dangerous it attracts countless curses toward it. After Megumi and Yuji fight through several minor spirits to save Yuji’s Occult Club friends, a much larger cursed spirit suddenly bursts through the roof. This one isn’t your average fly-sized nuisance — it’s stronger, faster, and capable of grabbing Megumi mid-fight. Megumi summons Nue to retaliate, but the curse slams him into a wall with enough force to send him crashing onto a lower rooftop. His Divine Dogs vanish from the impact, leaving him temporarily vulnerable. Yuji, still new to all of this, doesn’t hesitate. He jumps down to help, landing a clean punch straight to the curse’s head. He follows up with several hits, showing off his physical power — but none of it works. The blows do nothing because cursed energy is the only thing that can actually harm curses. This is the wall every normal human hits when they face one of these things. Yuji gets knocked down fast, and Megumi, beaten and bruised, knows the only option left. He tells Yuji the truth — if he wants to fight this curse, he needs cursed energy, and the only way to get it is to consume Sukuna’s finger. Megumi expects Yuji to die instantly, but Yuji swallows it without hesitation. The moment Sukuna’s energy floods through him, Yuji tears through the curse with ease, completely obliterating it in seconds. That single fight shows the perfect range of a Grade 3’s threat: strong enough to crush a student without cursed energy, but weak enough to be vaporized by the raw power of Sukuna. It’s not the weakest kind out there, but it’s still no match for anyone who can actually use cursed techniques. This curse marks the start of Yuji’s sorcerer life — and the point where curses start feeling like real threats, not just minor annoyances. The Roppongi mission also gives us another clear picture of a Grade 3’s strength. After Gojo decides to test Yuji and Nobara, he sends them to an abandoned building crawling with curses. Most of them are weak, but one in particular — the Roppongi Curse — shows that some Grade 3s can be surprisingly tricky. Nobara is the first to encounter it. She enters a room expecting an easy exorcism, but things immediately get complicated when the curse grabs a young boy and uses him as a human shield. It’s a smart move, showing a level of intelligence that Grade 4s completely lack. Nobara hesitates because she can’t attack without risking the hostage’s life. This is the point where you realize that while Grade 3s aren’t physically overwhelming, their cunning makes them dangerous in the right setting. She drops her weapon to try to negotiate, but the curse doesn’t let the boy go. It’s buying time, holding the hostage as leverage until Yuji bursts through the wall behind it. In one clean motion, Yuji chops off the curse’s arm and grabs the boy, breaking its advantage instantly. The spirit panics and tries to flee, but Nobara isn’t done. She takes advantage of the severed arm and activates her Straw Doll Technique. Using the detached limb as a link, she channels her cursed energy through her voodoo-style doll and drives nails into it, transferring the damage directly to the fleeing curse. The attack works perfectly, exorcising the spirit before it can escape the building. The Roppongi Curse might have been clever, but it still falls into the same bracket: weak enough to be defeated by first-years, but strong enough to test their instincts. It couldn’t overpower Nobara or Yuji, but it managed to make them think strategically. Mahito’s transfigured humans also fall under this level. Using his Idle Transfiguration technique, he reshapes people into grotesque, curse-like monsters. Once they’re transformed, there’s no coming back — death is inevitable. From that point on, they’re completely under Mahito’s control, following his every command without question. But every so often, traces of their humanity leak through. Their souls “sweat,” as Mahito puts it, making them cry, beg for help, or even ask for death, showing that some part of them is still aware of what’s happening. A single transfigured human has enough strength to easily kill an average person with no cursed energy. Most of them would land somewhere between a Grade 3 and a weaker Grade 2 in terms of power. But Mahito can take it even further with a move called Soul Multiplicity, which fuses compatible souls together into one stronger body. The result is something called a Polymorphic Soul Isomer — a much more powerful version of a transfigured human. These monsters can casually send a Grade 1 sorcerer flying through a building with one hit and are durable enough to survive much longer than normal transfigured humans.

**3\. Grade 2 – Dangerous Curses**

Grade 2 curses are where things start getting serious. These spirits aren’t the mindless fodder that rookies warm up on anymore. They’re fully capable of killing trained exorcists if taken lightly. A Grade 2 can tear through a team of sorcerers who don’t know what they’re doing, and unlike the weaker grades, they often come with actual cursed techniques or special abilities. Compared to the lower ranks, Grade 2 curses possess a much higher concentration of cursed energy. Some even show enough intelligence to speak, strategize, and manipulate their surroundings. They’re dangerous, they hit hard, and they require an experienced sorcerer to take them down safely. The official measure of their power says it all: “Close call with a shotgun.” That means you’re not walking away from an encounter with one unless you know what you’re doing. Ko-Guy is the only officially ranked Grade 2 cursed spirit we’ve seen in the series so far, and he’s a great example of what makes this level so threatening. Born from humanity’s deep-seated hatred of locust plagues, Ko-Guy is a grasshopper curse that’s part of Kenjaku’s group during the Shibuya Incident. Yuji first encounters him inside Shibuya Station, at the B2F entrance. When Yuji arrives, Ko-Guy is in the middle of devouring a human corpse. Yuji asks him about Mahito’s whereabouts, and Ko-Guy actually responds. That’s already a major step up from the mindless spirits in earlier grades. He’s aware, talkative, and capable of holding a conversation. He even mentions that his role is to protect a curtain — a jujutsu barrier keeping people out of the area. Yuji decides to exorcise him, both to lift the curtain and to eliminate a threat. Ko-Guy claims to be superior to humans in every way, boasting about his arms, jaws, and eyes. And in a way, he’s right — his body is built for combat. All the physical traits of a grasshopper are enhanced through cursed energy. He can spit out black mucus from his mouth and expand his abdomen to deliver piercing strikes. His powerful legs let him zip around enclosed spaces at high speed, and his jaws are sharp enough to bite clean through a metal sign. When he gets aggressive, he darts across the area, biting and stabbing in every direction. Ko-Guy tries to take Yuji by surprise by extending his abdomen for a sneak attack. The move could easily impale a human, but Yuji reacts fast — he blocks it with his left hand and immediately counters. With a single ring-handed downward punch, Yuji severs the extended appendage and follows up by exorcising Ko-Guy completely. The Yasohachi Bridge Curse is another strong example of a Grade 2-level threat. This one wasn’t officially ranked, but the events surrounding it fit perfectly with the characteristics of a curse at this level — durable, tricky, and capable of creating its own barrier and domain. After several former students from Saitama Urami East Junior High die from a mysterious curse, Tokyo Jujutsu High sends its first-year trio — Yuji, Megumi, and Nobara — to investigate. Their search takes them to Yasohachi Bridge, a place rumored to be haunted by a curse strong enough to kill anyone who jumps from it at night. Their first attempt fails. Even after Yuji tries to bungee jump from the bridge, they can’t locate the curse. Megumi starts to suspect that the real cursed spirit is hiding within a barrier that only manifests under certain conditions. Later, they return at night, this time entering from beneath the bridge and crossing the river — a symbolic act in jujutsu that allows passage into hidden barriers. Their theory works. The moment they cross, they enter the curse’s Innate Domain. The environment changes, and the real enemy reveals itself — a mole-like curse that moves through holes it creates across the ground. The fight gets chaotic quickly. Yuji tells Megumi and Nobara to focus on the mole curse while he takes on an even bigger threat that appears behind them — one of the Death Painting Wombs. The bridge curse stays behind to deal with the remaining two sorcerers. Nobara immediately tries using her Resonance technique on the curse’s exit holes, blasting cursed energy through them to attack its main body. But the spirit moves too quickly between the tunnels, dodging her hits. Megumi joins in, attempting to slice the creature with his cursed tool, but the curse ducks back into its hole just in time. They realize its attacks are predictable but frustratingly fast — like a cursed version of whack-a-mole. Nobara starts crushing the exits while Megumi waits for an opening. They notice that the curse’s Expanded Cursed Technique, the number of its tunnels, and the barrier itself are all draining its energy. It’s strong, but maintaining the domain is wearing it down. Suddenly, Nobara gets dragged out of the barrier by an unknown force, leaving Megumi alone. He keeps the pressure on, watching for patterns in the curse’s movement. The mole switches between two tunnels in front of him, trying to bait him into attacking too early. But Megumi anticipates its next move, predicting that it’ll appear behind him instead. He turns swiftly and attacks, though the curse slips away again, smugly resurfacing just behind him. Before it can land a hit, Megumi’s Divine Dog: Totality strikes from the shadows and slashes the curse’s head in half. The mole spirit collapses instantly, and the domain fades as its main body disappears. The whole fight demonstrates how a Grade 2 curse can control space, defend itself, and still pose a legitimate threat even to skilled sorcerers. The curse had its own domain, a sign of immense cursed energy, and could fight tactically rather than blindly. They’re sentient enough to make decisions, and some can even form alliances like Ko-Guy did with Kenjaku. They’re not unstoppable, but they’re the kind of curses that demand focus, teamwork, and experience.

**4\. Semi-Grade 1 – High-Class Curses**

Semi-Grade 1 curses are spirits that can use cursed techniques, meaning they’re not just brutes relying on raw cursed energy — they actually fight smart. They’re capable of channeling energy into deadly attacks and using strategy during combat, which makes exorcising them a serious risk even for veteran sorcerers. A one-on-one battle with one of these curses isn’t something you take lightly. Curses that can communicate or display a clear sense of intelligence are typically considered to be at least Semi-Grade 1\. They’re strong, clever, and unpredictable. As of now, only two cursed spirits have been officially ranked as Semi-Grade 1 in the series, both leaving quite the impression. The first appears during the events of *Jujutsu Kaisen 0*, when Suguru Geto traps Yuta Okkotsu and Toge Inumaki inside another curtain. Out of nowhere, a powerful cursed spirit emerges from the darkness. With a single command, it releases a blinding flash that tears a hole straight through the ground. Toge reacts first, pushing Yuta out of harm’s way, though his finger snaps from the impact. Despite the injury, Toge quickly uses his Cursed Speech technique to counterattack, commanding the curse to “twist.” The attack partially works — the curse’s arm twists violently — but the backlash hits Toge hard, leaving him coughing from the recoil. Yuta jumps in to save him before the curse can crush them both, pulling Toge to a higher floor to regroup. Yuta quickly realizes this spirit is far stronger than the one they faced back at the elementary school — meaning it’s not even supposed to be part of their current mission. As Toge tries to fix his finger, Yuta notices the curse hasn’t followed them. He figures out that the only way to lift the curtain trapping them is to exorcise the spirit completely. Channeling Rika’s power into his katana, Yuta coats the blade with cursed energy and charges in. He moves with precision, dodging the curse’s crushing attacks through quick flips and acrobatic maneuvers. Yuta manages to land an upward slash, but the wound is too shallow — the curse’s skin is far tougher than expected. He takes a minor hit from its counterattack but slides underneath it, spotting Toge’s dropped cough syrup. With perfect timing, Yuta grabs it and tosses it to Toge, who leaps down from the floor above. As the curse remains focused on Yuta, Toge drinks the medicine to restore his voice and gives a final command — “crush.” The cursed spirit is instantly flattened by its own power, exorcised in one decisive blow. The second officially ranked Semi-Grade 1 curse shows up during the Kyoto Goodwill Event. Before the student battle begins, Principal Gakuganji authorizes Noritoshi Kamo to secretly release this curse with one goal — assassinate Yuji Itadori. The plan involves marking Yuji with a blood scent that will attract the cursed spirit, allowing it to hunt him down after his fight with Aoi Todo. Gakuganji believes Yuji will be weakened enough by then for the curse to finish him off. Later, the Semi-Grade 1 spirit actually appears before Toge. But before it can even make a move, Hanami instantly slices through it — its head is severed and the body drops lifelessly to the ground. The assassination plan never even gets the chance to play out. Although not officially listed as Semi-Grade 1, Kechizu — one of the Cursed Womb: Death Paintings — fits solidly within this level of power. He’s the weakest among the Death Painting brothers, but still a major threat. Kechizu can manipulate his blood, which is thick, corrosive, and laced with a deadly poison. He uses it as a weapon, spewing streams of rotten blood at opponents that burn flesh on contact. If his blood enters an enemy’s body through wounds or mucous membranes, it activates his Decay technique. Once Decay is active, the victim’s body starts to rot from the point of contact, creating a disturbing flower-like pattern on their skin. Within ten to fifteen minutes, their body completely decomposes, leaving behind only bones. During his fight with Yuji and Nobara, Kechizu uses this ability alongside his brother Eso, infecting both sorcerers with their cursed blood and activating Decay. The battle turns into a race against time, with their bodies slowly breaking down while they try to fight back. Despite being poisoned, Nobara manages to turn the tables with her Resonance technique. Using the shared blood inside her body as a link, she channels the pain back onto the brothers themselves, damaging both Eso and Kechizu at once. Kechizu, barely holding on, tries to attack again, but Nobara lands a Black Flash — her cursed energy perfectly aligned for an explosive impact. The nail strike pierces straight through Kechizu’s head, leaving him critically wounded. Even then, Kechizu refuses to die quietly. He drags himself up and tries to attack Nobara from behind while she’s turned away. But the nail embedded in his skull becomes his downfall — Nobara activates Hairpin, detonating cursed energy through it and blowing his head apart, ending him for good. Facing a Semi-Grade 1 means you’re dealing with a curse that doesn’t just attack blindly — it thinks, adapts, and strikes with intent. At this level, even the smallest mistake could be fatal.

**5\. Grade 1 – Borderline Disasters**

Grade 1 curses are that tricky middle ground between the average and the absurd. They’re not quite strong enough to be labeled Special Grade, but they’re definitely way beyond the lower ranks. These are the types of cursed spirits that even tanks might have trouble with — and that’s straight from the series itself. This rank usually covers curses with multiple techniques or a power so refined that it starts to look like a Domain Expansion, even if it technically isn’t one. Now, despite Grade 1 being such an important benchmark in the Jujutsu world — since a Grade 1 sorcerer is literally defined by their ability to defeat a Grade 1 curse — we’ve never actually seen a confirmed one in the series. Not a single official Grade 1 curse has been shown so far. But based on feats and context, there are a few that fit perfectly in this category. Let’s start with Eso, one of the infamous Cursed Womb: Death Paintings. These Death Paintings are technically labeled as “Unregistered Special Grades,” but that’s really just a blanket term. If we’re being specific, Eso feels much closer to the Grade 1 range. He’s noticeably stronger than his younger brother Kechizu, but not quite strong enough to touch the higher Special Grades, including the strongest among the Death Paintings \- Choso. Eso, along with his two brothers, was incarnated by Mahito’s group to help the curses take over Japan. He and Kechizu were tasked with retrieving one of Sukuna’s fingers — which brought them right into a clash with Yuji and Nobara. Eso’s cursed technique revolves around Rot and Decay, similar to Kechizu’s, but his version is way more advanced. His signature move, “Maximum: Wing King,” lets him reshape the blood flowing from his back into massive wasp-like wings. These aren’t just for show — they increase his mobility and let him fire off blood attacks from long range. Despite his monstrous appearance, Eso shows some emotion when Kechizu dies during their fight. In a desperate escape attempt, he takes a hostage in a passing car, trying to flee the scene. But Nobara uses her Resonance technique on his severed arm, throwing him completely off balance. Before he can recover, Yuji catches up and lands a devastating cursed energy punch straight to Eso’s abdomen, ending his life. Next up are the Finger Bearers. Officially, these creatures are also listed as “Unregistered Special Grades,” but when you compare them to the true Special Grades, they clearly sit a level lower. That makes them a perfect fit for the Grade 1 slot. Finger Bearers are born when a cursed spirit consumes one of Sukuna’s fingers. Each one carries a fragment of Sukuna’s power, which makes them way above the typical curse. They act on instinct and cruelty, often lingering near their place of origin and attacking anyone who enters their territory. Despite their raw nature, Finger Bearers show signs of intelligence and can use basic curse techniques, including forming barriers and launching unrefined blasts of cursed energy. The first Finger Bearer we see shows up at the Eishu Juvenile Detention Center. It’s born from a cursed womb found at the scene and immediately displays its danger by slicing off Yuji’s left hand. Even when Yuji tries to fight back, it’s clear that the curse is far too strong. When Sukuna takes control, he briefly considers teaming up with the curse — but the moment it attacks him, the fight is over. Sukuna completely destroys it using his Domain Expansion, Malevolent Shrine, and casually claims the finger afterward. Then there’s the second Finger Bearer, which Megumi encounters under the Yasohachi Bridge. This one is noticeably stronger than the first, and it completely overwhelms Megumi in their fight. It breaks his weapon and corners him, forcing Megumi to consider using a dangerous last-resort technique. Instead, he decides to test his incomplete Domain Expansion, Chimera Shadow Garden. The Finger Bearer manages to resist the full effects, using a burst of cursed energy to dispel the domain, but that ends up being its biggest mistake. Megumi uses the distraction to land a killing blow through his shikigami, Divine Dog: Totality, finally exorcising it. Finally, there’s the Rainbow Dragon, one of Suguru Geto’s most impressive curses during his time as a second-year student. Its official rank is unknown, but based on what we see, Grade 1 fits like a glove. The Rainbow Dragon is known for having the hardest skin among all of Geto’s cursed spirits, making it extremely durable in battle. It’s also fast, strong, and capable of flight — a rare trait for curses of its kind. During the Star Plasma Vessel Escort Mission, Toji kills Riko Amanai right in front of Geto. In a rage, Geto summons the Rainbow Dragon to attack Toji. The dragon’s raw power is undeniable; it crashes through stone walls with ease and dominates the battlefield. But even with its incredible defense, it doesn’t stand a chance against Toji. Using the Split Soul Katana, Toji slices the dragon clean in half. The weapon completely ignores physical durability by striking directly at the soul, making the Rainbow Dragon’s armor useless. The Rainbow Dragon may have been destroyed in seconds, but its toughness and sheer presence make it one of the strongest mid-tier curses we’ve seen — absolutely worthy of a Grade 1 classification. If we look at the overall picture, Grade 1 curses are dangerous but manageable threats. So even though the series hasn’t given us an official, named Grade 1 curse, these examples fill the gap perfectly.

**6\. Special Grade – Calamity-Class Curses**

Now we’ve reached the peak of cursed existence — the Special Grades. These are the monsters that redefine what a curse can be. They’re born from humanity’s deepest fears: death, hatred, disasters, and despair. A single one of these things can wipe out a city block or take on entire teams of sorcerers without breaking a sweat. They have unique cursed techniques, massive cursed energy, and in most cases, terrifying intelligence. In the world of jujutsu, anything beyond Grade 1 is automatically Special Grade. But that label covers a huge range of abilities. Some Special Grades specialize in brute strength, while others rely on technique and intelligence. One thing they all share is that only a Special Grade or top-tier Grade 1 sorcerer can even attempt to exorcise them. Special Grade curses are so strong that the official explanation for dealing with them is basically, “Yeah… maybe cluster bombs could work.” By 2017, there were sixteen officially recognized Special Grade cursed spirits — these are known as “Registered Special Grades.” They include terrifying entities like Tamamo-no-Mae, the Smallpox Deity, Kurourushi, Ganesha, Akuro-o-Otake, and the Queen of Curses herself, Rika. Many of them are Imaginary Vengeful Spirits or curses born not from individuals, but from the collective fear of humanity itself. Tamamo-no-Mae is an Imaginary Vengeful Spirit controlled by Geto using his Cursed Spirit Manipulation technique. She was so powerful that Geto didn’t even release her during the Night Parade of a Hundred Demons — he saved her for the end of his fight against Yuta Okkotsu. During that battle, he summoned Tamamo-no-Mae Incarnate as his trump card alongside his Maximum: Uzumaki technique. In raw strength, Tamamo-no-Mae Incarnate was about equal to Rika, which says everything you need to know. If Rika was tied up fighting Tamamo-no-Mae, Yuta would have been completely open to Geto’s Maximum: Uzumaki, which could have killed him instantly. To counter this, Yuta made a Binding Vow, staking his life to fully unleash Rika’s power in exchange for his own. The gamble worked, and while Geto survived the clash for a few moments, he ended up dying shortly after. Tamamo-no-Mae hasn’t been seen since that fight, and it’s unclear if she still exists as an active curse after Geto’s death. The Smallpox Deity is another Special Grade curse under Kenjaku’s control, born from humanity’s ancient terror of plagues and disease. During the clash in Shibuya, Kenjaku didn’t want to personally fight Mei Mei, so he sent this curse to deal with her instead. The moment it appeared, the Smallpox Deity activated its Domain Expansion, trapping Mei Mei and her brother Ui Ui inside. Its Domain automatically sealed its victims inside coffins before summoning a giant boulder to crush them. Once the coffin is hit, the curse starts a countdown from three — if it reaches one, the victim becomes infected with smallpox and dies instantly. Mei Mei shatters the boulder before the countdown ends, avoiding infection. The Smallpox Deity tries again, forcing Mei Mei and Ui Ui to think fast. Mei Mei discovers that the domain’s sure-hit effect targets the “strongest” one. When Ui Ui releases his cursed energy to assist, the curse shifts its attention to him, but he manages to block it using a simple domain. Mei Mei then creates an opening, allowing one of her crows to sacrifice itself and exorcise the Smallpox Deity. Kurourushi is a grotesque Special Grade curse born from humanity’s collective disgust toward cockroaches. It was released by Kenjaku to participate in the Culling Game as a player. Kurourushi lives for one thing — eating humans. It doesn’t care about power or dominance, only feeding its endless hunger. Its ability to produce and control massive swarms of man-eating cockroaches made it an absolute nightmare to deal with. Each insect is weak alone, but a swarm can strip a person to the bone in seconds. The curse’s ability called Earthen Insect Trance lets it summon flying insect curses that release a blinding liquid when destroyed, making it harder for opponents to see. Kurourushi also wields the Festering Life Sword, a cursed weapon with six small barrels that shoot insect eggs into its victims. These eggs hatch immediately, causing smaller curses to crawl out of the victim’s body from the inside. The mix of horror and strategy in its attacks shows just how creative Special Grade curses can get. When Yuta faces Kurourushi, he finds himself in a brutal fight. Kurourushi keeps regenerating and attacking relentlessly. But when it tries to devour Yuta, he grabs its face and blasts it with positive energy through Reverse Cursed Technique. Since curses are made of negative energy, the positive burst completely disintegrates Kurourushi. However, this wasn’t the end. The lingering fear of cockroaches across Japan caused Kurourushi’s child — created through parthenogenesis — to inherit its cursed energy and revive. Later, when Yuta, Ryu, and Takako expand their domains simultaneously, Kurourushi interrupts the clash and attacks again. Ryu’s Granite Blast heavily damages it, and Yuta finishes the job by crushing its head with Reverse Cursed Technique once more. Then there’s Rika Orimoto — the Queen of Curses. Once Yuta’s childhood friend, she was killed in a tragic accident and cursed herself into becoming one of the most powerful Special Grade vengeful spirits in the series. As a curse, Rika’s power is almost limitless. Even partially manifested, she can crush jujutsu sorcerers and non-sorcerers alike with ease. When fully released, her destructive potential was so immense that the higher-ups considered her a national-level threat. Her cursed energy is bottomless, and her bond with Yuta lets him draw on it whenever needed. When Rika fully manifests, she can even copy cursed techniques, allowing Yuta to use abilities that he normally couldn’t. She’s both weapon and shield — capable of fighting alongside Yuta, protecting him from attacks, and even carrying his injured allies to safety. During Yuta’s battle against Geto, Rika transforms into her most powerful state after Yuta makes a Binding Vow, promising his life to her so they can be together after death. This promise unleashes Rika’s full strength, letting her fire an energy blast made of pure cursed love. That single attack completely overpowers Geto’s Maximum: Uzumaki and his summoned Tamamo-no-Mae Incarnate, ending the fight. After the battle, Rika forgives Yuta for breaking their promise and allows her spirit to pass on peacefully. Even though her soul is freed, a manifestation of her curse remains by Yuta’s side — a lingering presence of her affection, symbolizing the love that fueled her transformation into a curse in the first place.

Now on to the unregistered Special Grade curses — the monsters who operated outside Jujutsu High’s ranking system but were terrifying enough to qualify anyway. Choso is one of the three Cursed Womb: Death Paintings, and like his brothers Eso and Kechizu, he’s half-human, half-curse. When he’s first introduced, he’s part of Kenjaku’s alliance alongside Mahito, but unlike the others, Choso has something unique going for him: he’s not just powerful, he’s experienced. Even before his incarnation, he was intelligent and self-aware, spending over 150 years honing his cursed technique. So by the time he’s finally given a body, he’s already mastered Blood Manipulation to a level far beyond Noritoshi Kamo. Choso’s control over his blood is ridiculous. He can fire sharpened streams that cut through targets with precision, shape his blood into long whips for wide-range attacks, or form rotating chakrams for single devastating hits. His technique Convergence lets him fire high-pressure blood beams that can bend midair or ricochet off surfaces, making them incredibly hard to dodge. He can even detonate multiple condensed blood orbs at once, sending reinforced projectiles in every direction — or fuse them into one massive sphere that explodes with lethal force. His Flowing Red Scale technique takes things even further by enhancing his physical stats. By pumping blood through his body faster, he boosts oxygen intake, speeds up his pulse, and strengthens his entire system. The result? Extreme increases in speed, power, and reflexes. When he fights Yuji in Shibuya, he completely dominates him, landing one of the most brutal beatdowns in the series. But when he’s about to land the finishing blow, he suddenly feels Yuji’s “death” the same way he felt the deaths of his brothers. That strange connection clues him in that Yuji might actually be related to him through Noritoshi Kamo’s lineage. From that point, Choso’s entire purpose flips. Instead of killing Yuji, he decides to protect him, declaring him his little brother. He shifts all his rage toward Kenjaku, the one who manipulated him into fighting his own family. From that moment on, Choso dedicates himself to fighting for Yuji and avenging his brothers’ suffering. Dagon is another member of Mahito’s cursed alliance, born from humanity’s fear of natural disasters — specifically water-related ones. For a long time, he existed only as a cursed womb, but during the Shibuya Incident, he evolved into his true form, a terrifying aquatic curse with immense control over water. Dagon doesn’t need a nearby source to manipulate water; he generates it directly from his cursed energy. He can summon massive waves that crash through entire areas, then retract the water to swallow any victims caught inside. His Domain Expansion, Horizon of the Captivating Skandha, appears as a peaceful tropical beach, complete with calm waves and sunshine. But don’t be fooled — this beautiful paradise doubles as a death trap. Inside, his shikigami automatically attack anyone caught within it. In his domain, Dagon can unleash an army of deadly sea creatures: eel-like serpents, swarms of piranhas, small sharks, and huge crustaceans that can crush sorcerers with their shells. These shikigami attack relentlessly, making it nearly impossible to survive for long. Even techniques like Falling Blossom Emotion can’t fully protect against his Death Swarm, which he can spread across multiple targets at once. When Dagon traps Naobito, Nanami, and Maki inside his domain, he completely dominates the battle. The three elite sorcerers struggle to even scratch him. But things turn around when Megumi arrives and opens his own incomplete domain inside Dagon’s, breaking the “guaranteed hit” effect. That gives the sorcerers a chance to escape — but instead of freedom, they end up with Toji, who bursts through the exit Megumi creates. Toji, being the walking disaster that he is, immediately identifies Dagon as the strongest in the room and goes for him first. The fight doesn’t last long — Toji overwhelms Dagon with sheer speed and strength, exorcising him completely. Jogo, on the other hand, is born from the collective fear of the earth itself — volcanoes, earthquakes, and all the fiery destruction that comes with them. He’s the most vocal of the curses about his belief that cursed spirits are the true humans and that humanity should be wiped out entirely. Because of that, he willingly joins forces with Kenjaku to bring that goal closer to reality. Jogo’s innate technique revolves around volcanic flames. He can shoot concentrated fire beams from his palms, summon miniature volcanic craters from the ground that erupt on command, or even generate massive heat rays strong enough to melt stone. He also has insect-like minions that fly out of the volcanic opening on his head and explode on impact. His Domain Expansion, Coffin of the Iron Mountain, traps opponents inside a molten hellscape where every inch of the environment becomes a weapon. The air itself burns, and the ground erupts under his control. Any normal sorcerer would be reduced to ash the moment they step in. Despite all this, Jogo learns early on that raw power doesn’t mean much when you’re up against Gojo Satoru. Their encounter in the series becomes legendary for how one-sided it is. Gojo casually activates Infinite Void — his Domain Expansion — for the first time in the series, completely overwhelming Jogo and crushing his confidence. If Hanami hadn’t saved him, Jogo would’ve been exorcised completely. But that defeat doesn’t define him. During the Shibuya Incident, Jogo gets a chance to redeem himself when he faces Sukuna. This time, he doesn’t hold back. He unleashes Maximum: Meteor, a technique that summons a colossal fireball from the sky and hurls it toward the target. The meteor’s impact is so massive that it devastates a large section of Shibuya. Even Sukuna admits that if it had hit directly, he wouldn’t have escaped unharmed. Unfortunately for Jogo, Sukuna is still Sukuna. The King of Curses completely outclasses him and ends their fight with his Divine Flame, reducing Jogo to ashes. Still, Jogo’s dedication to his ideals and his willingness to fight beings like Gojo and Sukuna head-on make him one of the most memorable Special Grades in the story. Mahito is the youngest of the Special Grade curses but easily the most terrifying conceptually. He’s born from humanity’s hatred and fear of each other, making him a walking embodiment of human cruelty. He leads his own group with Jogo, Hanami, and Dagon, aiming to replace all humans with cursed spirits. His cursed technique, Idle Transfiguration, is one of the most disturbing in the series. It lets him directly manipulate the souls of living beings. Since a body’s shape depends on the soul, He can distort people’s bodies however he likes — stretching, twisting, or fusing them into grotesque shapes. He uses this to disfigure victims, heal himself, or morph his limbs into deadly weapons mid-fight. His creativity with Idle Transfiguration is off the charts. He can create clones of himself that share most of his powers, merge multiple souls into one unstable mass, or compress several souls into an explosive attack through Soul Multiplicity. When he stabilizes those merges, he creates Polymorphic Soul Isomers, transfigured humans that are more durable and controlled than his usual ones. These creatures can hit hard enough to send a grade 1 sorcerer like Aoi Todo flying, though they’re still fragile enough to be destroyed with strong counterattacks. During his early fights, Mahito takes on Kento Nanami and completely dismantles his technique. Nanami recognizes just how dangerous Mahito’s potential is and warns that he needs to be exorcised before he becomes unstoppable. That warning turns out to be right. When Mahito fights both Nanami and Yuji together, he evolves mid-battle, gaining deeper understanding of his cursed energy and creating his Domain Expansion: Self-Embodiment of Perfection. Inside this domain, Mahito connects directly to the souls of everyone trapped within, allowing him to reshape them instantly. He’s even skilled enough to activate it for just 0.2 seconds to avoid leaving himself vulnerable — something only top-tier sorcerers like Gojo have done. As the Shibuya Incident unfolds, Mahito’s power keeps skyrocketing. He perfects his cloning, learns to manipulate souls on a near-instinctual level, and even lands Black Flashes on Yuji and Todo. The rush of cursed energy understanding he gains from that pushes him into a new form: Instant Spirit Body of Distorted Killing. In this state, his body becomes more monstrous and muscular, his face peels off, and his durability skyrockets. Yuji’s punches can’t even faze him at this point. In this evolved form, Mahito is far beyond normal Special Grade level. But even with his growth, the teamwork between Yuji and Todo finally corners him. Out of options and close to death, Mahito tries to escape, only to be caught by Kenjaku. Using Cursed Spirit Manipulation, Kenjaku absorbs Mahito into his collection, ending his short but devastating reign. Together, these unregistered Special Grades showcase just how terrifying curses can become when they break free from human control.

**7\. Divine Class – God-Tier Curses**

This is the “beyond Special Grade” territory — the point where curses break through every ceiling of power and enter divine status. These are entities that warp the rules of cursed energy itself, beings so overwhelmingly strong that they’re less like curses and more like natural disasters with a will. It’s the absolute peak, a rank so high that even Special Grades look tame in comparison. And while it’s not an official level in the series, there’s only one curse that fits the description perfectly: Ryomen Sukuna, the undisputed King of Curses. Sukuna’s origin goes all the way back over a thousand years to the Heian Era — the Golden Age of Jujutsu. He wasn’t born a curse; he was a human sorcerer, the strongest of his time, feared by everyone and known as the Disgraced One. No sorcerer could challenge him and live to tell the tale. His unmatched strength and cruelty made him a legend even before his death. But even death couldn’t stop Sukuna. Before his demise, he agreed to a proposal from Kenjaku to participate in the Culling Game, and later used Kenjaku’s help to turn himself into a cursed object. By splitting his soul into twenty pieces, he sealed himself inside his own fingers, each one becoming an indestructible cursed object that grew even stronger over the next thousand years. These fingers eventually ended up scattered around Japan, each one classified as a Special Grade cursed object. One of them was stored in a box at Sugisawa High School, where it would accidentally fall into the hands of Yuji Itadori. When Yuji swallowed the finger to save his friends from a cursed spirit, Sukuna’s soul was revived inside his body. From that moment, the King of Curses shared a vessel with a teenager who barely understood cursed energy. Even in this limited state, Sukuna’s cursed energy was monstrous. His reserves dwarfed every other sorcerer and cursed spirit, and his techniques were devastating in both power and precision. His innate technique, Shrine, gives him control over a series of slashing and fire-based attacks that can obliterate nearly anything in his path. His default attack, Dismantle, is a ranged slash that tears through targets effortlessly. Cleave, on the other hand, automatically adjusts its power depending on the target’s toughness and cursed energy, cutting down even the toughest enemies in one strike. He can also apply Cleave on a massive scale, like with Spiderweb, a ground-based version that collapses everything around him. And when he chants “Open,” Sukuna releases his Divine Flame — a fire attack so intense it eclipses Jogo’s strongest flames, burning through everything in its path. Then there’s Sukuna’s Domain Expansion: Malevolent Shrine. It manifests as a Buddhist shrine surrounded by skulls, unleashing Dismantle and Cleave across a massive area. Unlike most domains, it doesn’t create a separate space. Instead, it expands directly into the real world, forming a 200-meter radius of unavoidable slashes. By leaving an opening in the barrier, Sukuna formed a binding vow that drastically increased its range and lethality — a perfect mix of strategy and overwhelming force. As Sukuna regained more fingers through Yuji, his strength continued to grow. However, his interest shifted after witnessing Megumi’s Ten Shadows Technique. Seeing potential in Megumi, Sukuna plotted to take over his body instead. That plan succeeded during the Culling Game, when Sukuna incarnated fully into Megumi’s body, gaining access to the Zenin Clan’s prized technique. With it, Sukuna summoned multiple shikigami through shadows, including a custom fusion called Merged Beast Agito — a towering humanoid monster combining the powers of Nue, Great Serpent, Round Deer, and Tiger Funeral. Agito could generate electricity from its fists and even heal itself or others using reverse cursed technique. But Sukuna’s true weapon among the Ten Shadows was Mahoraga — the most powerful shikigami of the technique, armed with the Sword of Extermination and the terrifying ability to adapt to all phenomena. Sukuna became the first user to successfully tame Mahoraga after exorcising it during the Shibuya Incident. Once tamed, he could summon the eight-handled wheel above himself or others, sharing Mahoraga’s adaptation process with them. When fighting Gojo, Sukuna used Mahoraga’s adaptation to Limitless as inspiration to enhance Dismantle, creating slashes that could cut through the world itself. This evolution gave him the edge he needed to finally defeat Gojo — a feat that no one in the modern era thought possible. Although the battle left him battered and drained, Sukuna still had to deal with the remaining Culling Game players and jujutsu sorcerers. Kashimo, Higuruma, Yuji, Yuta, Maki, Kusakabe, Miguel, Larue, Ino, and Choso all took their shots at him, but one by one, they fell. Even then, Yuji refused to give up, landing multiple Black Flash attacks and slowly awakening his hidden potential. In the middle of the chaos, Sukuna noticed Yuji had not just incredible resilience but also inherited Blood Manipulation and Shrine — Sukuna’s own cursed technique. Yuji’s growth began to push Sukuna into a corner. To end things quickly, Sukuna activated Divine Flame, amplified by several binding vows that allowed him to maximize its speed and range — but only when he wasn’t outnumbered and only inside his Domain. Within that Domain, the flames reached destructive levels that could incinerate everything alive inside. The pressure and heat were enough to vaporize anything in moments, much like what happened with Mahoraga in Shibuya. But before the flames consumed everything, Choso sacrificed himself to shield Yuji. When Sukuna’s cursed energy finally ran dry, Yuji confronted him once more — this time with Todo backing him up, wielding a newly upgraded Boogie Woogie that used a Vibraslap to activate. During their fierce fight, Sukuna tried to use his Domain again, but was interrupted by someone who appeared to be Gojo. Upon closer look, Sukuna realized the truth — it was Yuta, having possessed Gojo’s body using Kenjaku’s technique. The rematch between Malevolent Shrine and Unlimited Void began. Sukuna quickly noticed Yuta struggling to control Limitless, deducing that he likely couldn’t access Gojo’s copied techniques besides Purple. Sukuna went all-in, refusing to give Yuta time to charge. But Yuta used a clever trick: a Cursed Speech recording from Toge that froze Sukuna for just long enough to fire off Hollow Purple. The clash destroyed both barriers, and although Sukuna survived with minimal damage, it was clear Yuta was nearing his limit. As Todo and Yuji regrouped, Hana Kurusu entered the fray, hitting Sukuna with a massive Jacob’s Ladder. Sukuna countered with a Black Flash aimed to kill her, but Todo intercepted it, saving them at the cost of being knocked out of the fight. Now alone, Yuji faced Sukuna one last time. To Sukuna’s shock, Yuji finally unlocked his Domain Expansion — a perfect recreation of his hometown. The domain’s sure-hit effect directly attacked Sukuna’s soul using a version of Dismantle that didn’t require physical contact. Meanwhile, Megumi’s soul, still buried within, managed to regain control for a brief moment, trapping Sukuna’s body with his shadows. As the two clashed, Yuji overpowered Sukuna blow for blow. At the same time, Nobara, from Jujutsu Headquarters, used Resonance on Sukuna’s final remaining finger, paralyzing him just long enough for Yuji to land several finishing hits. With one devastating Black Flash to the chest, Yuji finally knocked Megumi free and brought the King of Curses to his knees. For the first time, Sukuna felt fear — genuine fear of death. In desperation, he tried to crush Megumi’s spirit to regain his body, but Megumi rejected Sukuna completely. Without a vessel, Sukuna’s form began to unravel, breaking down into a grotesque heap of decaying flesh. Even then, Yuji offered him a chance to merge again, to live within him peacefully. But Sukuna refused. He praised Yuji — calling him by his full name for the first time — and told him not to underestimate him, even in death. And with that final word, Sukuna turned to dust, ending his thousand-year reign of terror. The King of Curses, feared by all, finally fell to the new generation he once mocked — a fitting end for a being who stood at the very peak of jujutsu sorcery.
